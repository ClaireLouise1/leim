Ser, estar & hay

Spot the pattern

  • Mi madre es profesora. Es de Cork. Es muy simpática. (My mum is a teacher. She's from Cork. She's very nice.)
  • Hoy mi madre está cansada. Está en el trabajo. Está hablando por teléfono. (Today my mum is tired. She's at work. She's talking on the phone.)
  • Hay un parque cerca de mi casa. Hay tres dormitorios. (There's a park near my house. There are three bedrooms.)

All three mean "is" or "are" in English. Look at the first two lines: which one tells you what someone is (always, or for a long time), and which one tells you where they are and how they are right now? And what does hay do? Think about it, then check.

Show me

Ser says what someone or something is: who they are, what they're like, their job, where they're from, what it's made of, the day, the date and the time. Estar says where something is and how it is right now: location, feelings, a state or condition, and actions happening now (estoy estudiando). Hay says something exists there: "there is / there are". Remember the rhyme: "How you feel and where you are, that's when you use estar."

SER = DOCTORDescription (alta, simpática) · Occupation (profesora) · Characteristic (es de madera) · Time, day & date (son las dos, es lunes) · Origin (es de Cork, es irlandés) · Relationship (es mi primo)
ESTAR = PLACEPosition (encima de la mesa) · Location (en Madrid, en casa) · Action now, -ing (está jugando) · Condition (cansado, enfermo, abierta) · Emotion (contento, nerviosa)

Hay never changes: hay un perro, hay dos perros. Don't forget the accents on estás, está, estáis, están: without them esta means "this".

Hay or está?

Hay (there is / are)Está / están (it is / they are, somewhere)
Hay un supermercado en mi calle.El supermercado está en mi calle.
Hay muchas tiendas en el centro.Las tiendas están en el centro.
¿Hay un baño por aquí?¿Dónde está el baño?

The trick is the little word before the noun: hay + un, una, unos, unas, a number, mucho / muchos or nothing (hay leche). Está(n) + el, la, los, las, mi, tu or a name (Madrid está en el centro). So never hay el… and never está un….

Words that change meaning

with serwith estar
aburridoes aburrido = he's boringestá aburrido = he's bored
listoes lista = she's cleverestá lista = she's ready
maloes malo = he's bad / naughtyestá malo = he's ill
ricoes rico = he's richestá rico = it's tasty (food)
verdees verde = it's greenestá verde = it's not ripe yet
guapoes guapa = she's beautifulestá guapa = she looks lovely (today)
Watch out: the time always uses ser: Es la una, Son las tres. Where an event takes place also uses ser: La fiesta es en mi casa (but Mi casa está en el centro). And some fixed phrases use estar: Estoy de vacaciones (I'm on holiday), Está nublado (it's cloudy), Estoy de acuerdo (I agree).
Compare your languages: English uses one verb, "to be", for everything, which is why ser and estar feel strange. But you already know a language with two! In Irish, Is múinteoir mé (I am a teacher: who I am, like ser) and Tá mé tuirseach (I am tired: how I am now, like estar). And Irish tá … ann / tá siopa sa bhaile works a bit like hay. French and Italian learners: Italian has essere and stare too.
Exam tip: In Writing, ser and estar mistakes are some of the most common slips, and examiners notice them. Before you finish, check every "is" and "are": who or what (ser), where or how right now (estar), there is or there are (hay). A good short description uses all three: Mi pueblo es pequeño y tranquilo. Está en el sur de Irlanda, cerca de la costa. Hay un parque, dos supermercados y una piscina, pero no hay cine. Hoy estoy muy contento porque es viernes. In Listening and Reading, watch for the words that change meaning: está aburrido is bored, not boring!
