Decimals, rounding & estimation

Spot the pattern

Look at what happens to the digits 3, 4 and 7.

3.47 × 1034.73.47 ÷ 100.347
3.47 × 1003473.47 ÷ 1000.0347
3.47 × 100034703.47 ÷ 10000.00347

Count the zeros in 10, 100 and 1000. How many places do the digits move each time, and which way? So what is 0.52 × 100? And 52 ÷ 1000?

Show me

The digits move one place for every zero: to the left when you multiply (the number gets bigger) and to the right when you divide (it gets smaller). The point stays put; empty places get a 0. 0.52 × 100 = 52 and 52 ÷ 1000 = 0.052. That's why "add a zero" only works for whole numbers: 3.47 × 10 is not 3.470.

Place value

TensUnits.TenthsHundredthsThousandths
25.473

In 25.473 the 7 is worth 7 hundredths (0.07). Comparing decimals: give them the same number of decimal places first. 0.5 = 0.50 and 0.45 = 0.45, so 0.5 is bigger. More digits doesn't mean bigger!

Fractions ↔ decimals: 1/2 = 0.5 · 1/4 = 0.25 · 3/4 = 0.75 · 1/5 = 0.2 · 1/8 = 0.125 · 1/10 = 0.1 · 3/20 = 0.15. To go from a decimal to a fraction, use the place value: 0.35 = 35/100 = 7/20.

Rounding

  1. Find the last digit you're keeping (the 1st decimal place, the hundreds, the 2nd significant figure…).
  2. Look at the next digit. 5 or more: round up. Less than 5: leave it.
  3. Drop the digits after it. In whole numbers, fill the gaps with zeros (48,372 to the nearest thousand is 48,000, not 48).

Significant figures: start counting at the first digit that isn't zero. 0.004637 to 2 significant figures = 0.0046 (the zeros at the front don't count). 3.0496 to 3 significant figures = 3.05 (a zero between digits does count).

Decimal sums

  • + and −: line up the decimal points. Fill gaps with zeros: 7 − 2.35 → 7.00 − 2.35 = 4.65.
  • ×: multiply without the points, then count the decimal places in the question: 0.3 × 0.4 → 3 × 4 = 12, two places → 0.12.
  • ÷ by a decimal: multiply both numbers by 10 (or 100) until you're dividing by a whole number: 4.8 ÷ 0.6 = 48 ÷ 6 = 8.

Is it reasonable? Estimate first

Round every number to 1 significant figure and work it out in your head. 4.87 × 21.3 ≈ 5 × 20 = 100, so 103.731 looks right and 10.3731 doesn't. If your answer is about 10 times too big or too small, check the decimal point.

Don't round too early. Keep every digit until the last line, then round once. 4.56 × 2.34 = 10.6704 → 10.7. Rounding first (4.6 × 2.3 = 10.58 → 10.6) gives the wrong answer.
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Almost every question with a number answer ends with an instruction like "give your answer correct to 2 decimal places" or "to 1 significant figure". Decimals also run through money (bills, change, best buy), measurement (area, volume, speed) and statistics (the mean). "Estimate…" questions test whether you can check an answer without a calculator.

How marks are won and lost

  • Round only at the end. Rounding a middle step changes the final answer and costs marks.
  • Read the instruction twice: decimal places and significant figures are not the same thing. 0.04637 to 2 decimal places is 0.05; to 2 significant figures it's 0.046.
  • Show the estimate when asked: "5 × 20 = 100" written down earns the marks; just the answer doesn't.
  • Money has 2 decimal places: write €9.80, not €9.8.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first.

  1. Round it. Write 2847.365 correct to (a) the nearest whole number (b) 2 decimal places (c) 2 significant figures (d) the nearest hundred.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) The digit after the units is 3, so leave it: 2847.
    (b) The digit after the 6 is 5, so round up: 2847.37.
    (c) Keep 2 and 8; the next digit is 4, so leave it and fill with zeros: 2800.
    (d) The digit after the hundreds is 4: 2800 (the same as (c) here).

  2. Estimate, then calculate. (a) Estimate (19.8 × 3.1) ÷ 0.49 by rounding each number to 1 significant figure. (b) Use a calculator to work it out correct to 2 decimal places. (c) Is your answer reasonable?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 20 × 3 ÷ 0.5 = 60 ÷ 0.5 = 120.
    (b) 19.8 × 3.1 = 61.38, and 61.38 ÷ 0.49 = 125.265… = 125.27.
    (c) Yes: 125.27 is close to the estimate of 120.

  3. Change. Seán buys 3 notebooks at €2.45 each and 2 pens at €1.39 each. How much change does he get from €20?
    Show the worked answer

    3 × €2.45 = €7.35 and 2 × €1.39 = €2.78. Total €7.35 + €2.78 = €10.13. Change: €20.00 − €10.13 = €9.87.

  4. Too early! A rug measures 4.56 m by 2.34 m. (a) Find its area correct to 1 decimal place. (b) Ciara rounds both lengths to 1 decimal place first. What answer does she get, and why is it wrong?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 4.56 × 2.34 = 10.6704 m² = 10.7 m².
    (b) 4.6 × 2.3 = 10.58 → 10.6 m². She rounded too early: each rounding adds a small error, so round only the final answer.
