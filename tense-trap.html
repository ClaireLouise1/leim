Tenses, agreement & pronouns

Three checks to make on every piece of writing: Is every verb in the right time? Does each verb agree with its subject? Is each pronoun the right one?

Spot the pattern

Yesterday I walked to school.
Every day I walk to school.
Tomorrow I will walk to school.
Every day Gran walks to Mass.
Every day my cousins walk to school.

Which word in each sentence tells you the time? When does the verb get an -s?

Show me

The time words (yesterday, every day, tomorrow) decide the tense: past walked, present walk, future will walk. In the present, the verb takes -s when the subject is one person or thing other than I or you: Gran walks, but my cousins walk.

Keep the tense steady

⏰ Pastyesterday, last week, two years ago → walked, saw, won
🔁 Presentevery day, always, usually, often → walks, plays
🔮 Futuretomorrow, next year, soon → will walk

A story in the past stays in the past. “I opened the door and see saw a fox.”

Make it agree

1️⃣ Singulareach, every, everyone, nobody, one of…, the box of apples → is, was, has, plays
👥 PluralTom and Aoife, the players, there are three… → are, were, have, play
💡 Watch out“One of my friends plays”: the subject is one, not friends.

Saw or seen?

VerbPast (no helper)After has / have / had / was
seeI saw itI have seen it
doI did itI have done it
goshe wentshe has gone
writehe wrotehe has written
breakit brokeit was broken

“I seen it” and “I done it” are common in spoken Irish English, but in standard written English (and in the exam) write I saw it, I did it.

I or me?

👤 Doing itI, he, she, we, they: “Tom and I went.”
🎯 Getting itme, him, her, us, them: “The coach picked Tom and me.”
✂️ The testTake away the other person. “picked Tom and I” → “picked I”? No: “picked me”.

Clear pronouns: if “he” could mean two people, use the name. “Darragh told Seán he was picked” → “Darragh told Seán, ‘You're picked.’”

Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

There is no separate grammar question, but every writing task is marked partly on accuracy: tense, agreement and pronouns. A story that drifts between past and present, or “we was” in a speech, costs marks. Some questions ask you to correct or edit a short passage.

How marks are won and lost

  • Choose a tense and stick to it: most stories work best in the past.
  • Read your work back and check every verb: one slip in each paragraph adds up.
  • Check “one of”, “each” and “everyone”: they take a singular verb.
  • Write standard English unless a character is speaking: I saw, not I seen.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). "Where the marks would go" is our guide, not an official marking scheme.

  1. This story opening slips between tenses. Rewrite it in the past tense.
    The ferry left Schull at ten. The sea is calm at first, but halfway across the wind picks up. Ciara held the rail and watches the island come closer.
    Show a model answer

    The ferry left Schull at ten. The sea was calm at first, but halfway across the wind picked up. Ciara held the rail and watched the island come closer.
    Where the marks would go: all three slipped verbs (is, picks, watches) changed to the past; nothing else changed.

  2. Correct the four mistakes in this notice.
    Each of the teams have a coach. The players was told to bring water. Me and Liam is collecting the money, so give it to Liam or I.
    Show a model answer

    Each of the teams has a coach. The players were told to bring water. Liam and I are collecting the money, so give it to Liam or me.
    Where the marks would go: one mark per fix: has (each = singular), were (players = plural), Liam and I are (subject, plural), me (after “to”). Putting yourself last (“Liam and I”) is good manners.

  3. Fill each gap with the right form of the verb in brackets: “I have ___ (see) that film, but I ___ (do) my homework first, then I ___ (go) to bed.”
    Show a model answer

    I have seen that film, but I did my homework first, then I went to bed.
    Where the marks would go: seen after “have”; did and went with no helper verb.

  4. Why is this sentence unclear? Rewrite it so it is clear: “When Mam met Gran at the station, she was crying.”
    Show a model answer

    We can't tell who “she” is: Mam or Gran. Gran was crying when Mam met her at the station.
    Where the marks would go: naming the problem (two women, one “she”); a rewrite that uses a name so only one reading is possible.
