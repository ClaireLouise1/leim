Reading for meaning: comprehension skills

Every reading question is a hunt. Know what you're hunting for, and you don't have to read every word five times.

Spot the pattern

📖 Story

1The wind howled around the caravan all night.

2Rua pulled the blanket over her head and lay still.

3Every time the roof rattled, her hands started shaking.

4At dawn the storm passed.

Three questions: (a) When did the storm pass? (b) How does Rua feel? (c) What's the best title? Which one can you answer by finding one word? Which one needs a clue? Which one needs the whole text?

Show me

(a) is a scan question: hunt for a time word. Line 4: At dawn.
(b) is an inference question: the text never says "scared". The clue is in line 3: her hands started shaking.
(c) is a big idea question: skim all four lines. "A frightening night in a storm" covers them all; "A day at the beach" only fits one line.

Four ways to read

🦅 Skimfor the big idea
Title, first line, last line. What's it about? Who's it for?
🔦 Scanfor one fact
When → time · How many → number · Who → name · Where → place
🕵️ Inferread between the lines
Shaking → scared. Face went red → embarrassed.
🔎 Prove itpoint + quote + explain
Rua is scared: “her hands started shaking”.

Fact or opinion?

📏 Factcan be checked
The tickets cost €12.
Croke Park is in Dublin.
💭 Opinionwhat someone thinks
The tickets are too expensive.
Clue words: best, boring, should, I think
  • Read the questions first, then the text. You'll know what to hunt for.
  • Watch for traps: the wrong answers are often real words from the text, from the wrong line.
  • Short quotes beat long ones: three to six words that prove your point.
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

The Junior Cycle paper is full of texts you've never seen: stories, articles, adverts, letters. Most questions ask what the text tells you, what it suggests, and how you know. You win the marks by backing up each point with the text.

How marks are won and lost

  • Answer the question asked. "Two reasons" means two separate points.
  • Quote or refer to the text for every point. A point with no proof is only half an answer.
  • Explain what the quote shows. Don't just copy a chunk of the text.
  • Use your own words for the point; save the text's words for the quote.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). "Where the marks would go" is our guide, not an official marking scheme.

The Night Bus
Niall hadn't meant to fall asleep. One minute he was watching the lights of Cork slide past; the next, the bus was dark and silent, parked in a yard full of empty buses. His phone showed 11.40 p.m. and 3% battery. He pressed the door button. Nothing. He knocked on the glass, softly at first, then harder, his breath coming in short gasps. At last a torch beam swung towards him. "You're the third one this month," the security guard said, grinning, as he pulled the doors open. Niall's cheeks burned all the way home.

  1. Find two details that show Niall was in a difficult situation.
    Show a model answer

    1. The bus was “dark and silent” and locked in a yard, so he was trapped.
    2. His phone had only “3% battery” late at night (11.40 p.m.), so he could hardly call for help.
    Where the marks would go: two separate details from the text, each with a word or two of explanation.

  2. How does Niall feel when he can't get off the bus? Support your answer with reference to the text.
    Show a model answer

    Niall feels panicked. He knocks “softly at first, then harder”, which shows his fear growing, and his “breath coming in short gasps” suggests he is close to panic.
    Where the marks would go: a clear feeling (point), a short quote (proof), and what the quote shows (explain).

  3. How does Niall feel at the end of the story? How do you know?
    Show a model answer

    He is embarrassed. The guard grins and says he's “the third one this month”, and Niall's “cheeks burned all the way home”: burning cheeks show he was blushing.
    Where the marks would go: the feeling, the clue, and the link between them.

  4. Is “You're the third one this month” a fact or an opinion? Explain.
    Show the answer

    A fact: it is something that could be checked (count the people found on buses this month). It doesn't say what anyone thinks or feels.
    Where the marks would go: the right answer plus a reason that uses the idea of checking.
