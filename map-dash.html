Directions and getting around

Spot the pattern

To a friend (tú)To an adult you don't know (usted)English
Sigue todo recto.Siga todo recto.Go straight on.
Gira a la derecha.Gire a la derecha.Turn right.
Toma la primera calle.Tome la primera calle.Take the first street.
Cruza la plaza.Cruce la plaza.Cross the square.

Look at the endings. What happens to the last vowel when you speak politely? Why does cruza become cruce? Think about it, then check.

Show me

1. For tú, the order looks just like the "he / she" form of the present: él sigue → ¡sigue!, él gira → ¡gira! 2. For usted, the vowel swaps: -ar verbs take -e (gire, tome, cruce, baje) and -er / -ir verbs take -a (coja from coger, suba from subir). Seguir keeps its e → i change: sigue / siga. 3. Cruzar changes z → c before e (cruce), just as coger becomes coja to keep the sound. 4. Use usted with a stranger, a police officer or an older person: Perdone, ¿para ir a la estación?

Asking the way

SpanishEnglish
Perdone, ¿dónde está la estación?Excuse me, where is the station?
¿Para ir al museo, por favor?How do I get to the museum, please?
¿Por dónde se va a la playa?Which way is it to the beach?
¿Hay un banco por aquí?Is there a bank near here?
¿Está lejos? · ¿Está cerca?Is it far? · Is it near?

Watch out: a + el = al (al museo) and de + el = del (enfrente del cine), but a la and de la never join. Use está for where a place is (¿Dónde está la farmacia?) and hay for "is there a…?" (¿Hay una farmacia por aquí?).

Understanding the answer

Which wayWhere exactlyHow far

a la izquierda (on the left)entre… y… (between… and…)Está a cinco minutos andando. (5 minutes' walk)

Primera, segunda, tercera agree with la calle. Listen out for cruza (cross), pasa los semáforos (go past the traffic lights), en la rotonda (at the roundabout), sube / baja la calle (go up / down the street).

Getting around

TransportAt the stationEnglish
Voy en autobús / tren / metro / coche / bici.Un billete de ida, por favor.A single ticket, please.
Voy a pie (or andando).Un billete de ida y vuelta.A return ticket.
Cojo el autobús número tres.¿A qué hora sale el próximo tren?What time does the next train leave?
Me bajo en la próxima parada.¿De qué andén sale?Which platform does it leave from?
Hay que cambiar en Zaragoza.¿Cuánto tarda el viaje?How long does the journey take?

It's always en + transport, except a pie (on foot). At the station look for: SALIDAS (departures), LLEGADAS (arrivals), TAQUILLA (ticket office), ANDÉN (platform), CONSIGNA (left luggage), SALA DE ESPERA (waiting room). In the street: PARADA DE AUTOBÚS, PASO DE PEATONES (pedestrian crossing), CALLE PEATONAL (pedestrian street), PROHIBIDO APARCAR (no parking), OBRAS (roadworks), DESVÍO (diversion).

Culture: most Spanish towns have a plaza Mayor (main square) and a casco antiguo (old town) with narrow pedestrian streets. Spain's fast trains are called the AVE (Madrid to Seville in about two and a half hours), run by Renfe. Big cities like Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia, Bilbao and Sevilla have a metro. Irish link: ar dheis (on the right), ar chlé (on the left), díreach ar aghaidh (straight ahead): just like Spanish, Irish says "on the" side, a la derecha.
Exam tip: In Listening, directions are short and fast: write R / L / ↑ and the number of the street (1ª, 2ª, 3ª) as you hear them, and wait for the last detail (está enfrente del banco). Station announcements test times, platforms and delays (con veinte minutos de retraso). In Reading, signs and notices come up: learn the station and street signs above. In Writing, tell a visitor how to get to your house: Coge el autobús número 208 en el centro y bájate en la segunda parada. Mi casa está enfrente del parque.
