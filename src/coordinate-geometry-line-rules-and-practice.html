1. Slope

The slope m is rise ÷ run: how far the line goes up for each step across. A(−1, −1) to B(2, 1): run 3, rise 2, slope 2/3.

m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁)

Rises left to right: positive. Falls: negative. Flat: zero.

In a real graph the slope is the rate (€ per km, litres per minute) and the point on the y-axis is the starting value (a fixed charge).

2. Midpoint and distance

Midpoint((x₁ + x₂) ÷ 2, (y₁ + y₂) ÷ 2)
add, then halve
Distance√((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²)
it's Pythagoras

(1, 2) to (4, 6): across 3, up 4, distance √(9 + 16) = 5. Midpoint (2.5, 4).

3. The equation of a line

y = mx + c: m is the slope, the line meets the y-axis at (0, c). Get y on its own first: y = 6 − 3x has slope −3.

y − y₁ = m(x − x₁): one point and the slope. Through (2, 5), slope 3: y − 5 = 3(x − 2) → y = 3x − 1.

Two points: find the slope first, then use either point.

On the line? Put in x; if you get the point's y, yes. Axes: x = 0 on the y-axis, y = 0 on the x-axis.

4. Parallel, perpendicular, meeting

Parallel lines have the same slope. Where lines meet: read the crossing point, then check it in both equations.

Higher Level: perpendicular slopes multiply to −1 (flip and change the sign: 2/3 → −3/2). ax + by + c = 0: get y on its own; slope = −a ÷ b.

Classic mistakes: run ÷ rise · losing a minus (5 − (−2) = 7) · subtracting for the midpoint · 3 + 4 instead of √(3² + 4²) · reading c as the slope · not getting y on its own first.
Exam tip: write the formula, label (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂), use brackets for negatives, and check your equation with the points you were given.
