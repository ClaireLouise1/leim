Statistics: questions, data & tables

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): SP.3a, SP.3b, SP.3c (graphs, averages and comparing data sets are taught later, in Statistics 2)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can classify data as categorical (nominal, ordinal) or numerical (discrete, continuous), pose a statistical question, tell a population from a sample, spot biased samples and leading questions, design fair answer boxes, and organise raw data in a tally and frequency table. Good preparation for CBA 2.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Data Sort cards + Fix the Survey · p.3 Data Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: Two questions. Board: "How tall am I?" and "How tall are the students in this class?" Which one needs data? Why? Elicit "the answers vary": a statistical question.
8 minGuided discovery: counted or measured? Ask three students their number of siblings and their height. "Could anyone have 2.5 siblings? Could anyone be 1.555 m?" Elicit discrete v continuous, then words v numbers, and order v no order.
12 minData Sort (p.2, groups of 3–4). Lay out the four header cards; groups sort the 24 cards into the four types and must agree a reason for each. Check against the key. Feedback: which cards caused arguments? (Usually star ratings and grades.)
10 minFix the Survey (p.2, pairs). Find the six problems with the class's first questionnaire and plan, and rewrite each one. Feedback: build a class checklist for a good survey.
10 minData Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start at START, answer the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, build the chain to FINISH. Chain below.
12 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs, then the exam-style questions alone. Fast finishers: ask the class the fixed lunchtime question and make a tally and frequency table.
3 minExit ticket. "Type of data: the time each student takes to get to school? Who is left out if you only ask the students on the bus?" (Numerical continuous; students who walk, cycle or come by car.) Homework: play Data Detective at leim.ie/data-detective.html (Fair or biased? round).
Data Sort key: Nominal: 1, 3, 9, 12, 13, 14 · Ordinal: 2, 10, 15, 16, 20, 24 · Discrete: 4, 6, 7, 11, 17, 22 · Continuous: 5, 8, 18, 19, 21, 23.
Errors to look for: "categorical" or "numerical" with no second word · star ratings and grades called numerical · shoe size and age (leave them out of first lessons: textbooks differ) · "biased" with no reason · frequencies that don't add up to the number asked · overlapping answer boxes.

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. Name the type of data: (a) the number of emails a teacher gets each day (b) the time a candle takes to burn down (c) each student's favourite fruit (d) the level of each Irish exam paper taken (Higher, Ordinary, Foundation)
  2. The heights (cm) of 20 students: 148, 152, 139, 161, 155, 143, 158, 150, 147, 163, 141, 156, 152, 149, 160, 145, 154, 151, 138, 157. (a) Make a grouped frequency table with classes 135–139, 140–144, … 160–164. (b) Which class has the most students? (c) What fraction of the students are 155 cm or taller?
  3. Twenty students said what they do at lunchtime (S = sport, H = homework club, C = chat): S, H, S, C, S, H, S, S, C, H, S, C, S, S, H, S, C, S, S, H. Make a frequency table. What type of data is this?
  4. A gym owner wants to know how often adults in his town exercise. He asks 25 people at his gym. Give two reasons why his results may be misleading, and suggest a better sample.
  5. Write a statistical question you could investigate about your class, a fair survey question for it with answer boxes, and say what type of data you would collect.

Fix the Survey

1Leading question. Fair: "How would you rate the canteen food?" □ Very good □ Good □ OK □ Poor □ Very poor
2Boxes overlap (13, 14 fit twice) and older students have no box. Use □ 12 □ 13 □ 14 □ 15 □ 16 or older
3Vague: "a little" means different things. Use minutes: □ 0 □ 1–10 □ 11–20 □ 21–30 □ more than 30
4Not needed and too personal. Leave it out (keep surveys anonymous)
5No box for 0 or for more than 4. Use □ 0 □ 1–2 □ 3–4 □ 5 or more
PlanBiased sample: friends are alike and only one year is asked. Pick a random sample from every year group

A. Error Hunt

Number of brothers and sisters → numerical continuousYou can't have 2.5 siblings: it's counted, so numerical discrete
Star rating of a film (1 to 5 stars) → numerical discreteStars are ordered categories (a rating, not a count): categorical ordinal
Asking the school choir about favourite music → a fair random sampleBiased: choir members like singing. Pick students at random from the whole school
20 students asked; frequencies 5, 8, 6 → table complete5 + 8 + 6 = 19: one student is missing, so recount
Answer boxes 0–5 · 5–10 · 10–15 → good boxes5 and 10 fit two boxes and there's no box above 15: 0–4 · 5–9 · 10–14 · 15 or more
Liam asks 40 of 600 students. Population? → the 40 studentsThat's the sample. The population is all 600 students

B. Exam-style questions

  1. (a) numerical discrete (b) numerical continuous (c) categorical nominal (d) categorical ordinal
  2. (a) 135–139: 2, 140–144: 2, 145–149: 4, 150–154: 5, 155–159: 4, 160–164: 3 (total 20) (b) 150–154 (5 students) (c) 4 + 3 = 7: 7/20
  3. Sport 11, homework club 5, chat 4 (total 20) · categorical nominal
  4. People at a gym exercise more than most adults (biased), and 25 is a small sample; the town's adults who don't go to his gym are left out. Better: a bigger random sample of adults from the whole town (e.g. from a full list), asked at different places and times.
  5. Any sensible answer, e.g. "How long do students in my class spend on homework each night?", "How many minutes did you spend on homework last night? □ 0–29 □ 30–59 □ 60–89 □ 90 or more": numerical continuous (grouped).

Marking tip

As in the exam, a type of data needs both words, and "biased" needs a reason (who is left out, or how the question pushes an answer). Accept any fair rewording of survey questions. A frequency table earns full marks only if the frequencies add up to the number asked.
