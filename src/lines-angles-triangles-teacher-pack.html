Lines, angles & triangles

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): GT.3b, GT.3c (constructions are taught next; congruent and similar triangles and the other theorems come in Geometry 2)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can name and classify angles and triangles, use the angle facts (straight line, point, vertically opposite, triangle, isosceles, exterior angle, alternate, corresponding, co-interior) to find missing angles, and give the correct reason for every step, as the exam asks.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Angle Sort cards · p.3 Angle Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

8 minLead-in: Torn corners. Each student draws any triangle, colours the corners, tears them off and lines them up. "What shape do they make? Did it matter which triangle?" Elicit: the angles of every triangle add to 180° (Theorem 4).
8 minGuided discovery: parallel lines. Students draw a transversal across two lines of their lined copy and measure all eight angles. "Which angles are equal? Which pairs add to 180°?" Name them: vertically opposite, alternate, corresponding, co-interior.
12 minAngle Sort (p.2, groups of 3–4). Cut out the five headers and 20 cards. Groups sort each card by the pair of angles marked a and b, and must say whether a = b or a + b = 180°. Check against the key.
10 minAngle Dominoes (p.3). Start at START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, build the chain to FINISH. Chain below.
17 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs, then the exam-style questions alone, writing a reason for every step. Fast finishers: invent a two-step chase for a partner.
5 minExit ticket. Draw two parallel lines and a transversal; mark one angle 70°. "Mark an angle equal to it and name the reason. Mark an angle of 110° and name the reason." Homework: play Angle Chase at leim.ie/angle-chase.html (Name the reason round).
Angle Sort key: Vertically opposite: 5, 9, 11, 14 · On a straight line: 2, 16, 17, 18 · Alternate: 12, 13, 19, 20 · Corresponding: 3, 8, 10, 15 · Co-interior: 1, 4, 6, 7.
Errors to look for: 360° used for a straight line · adding the two given angles instead of subtracting from 180° · "the angles are equal" in an isosceles triangle without saying which ones · alternate and co-interior mixed up · "Z angles" / "F angles" written as reasons · measuring a diagram that isn't drawn to scale.

Angle facts

Every step needs a reason!

Angles on a straight line add to 180°
a + b = 180°
Angles at a point add to 360°
a + b + c = 360°
Vertically opposite angles are equal (Theorem 1)
a = b
Angles in a triangle add to 180° (Theorem 4)
a + b + c = 180°
Isosceles triangle: base angles are equal (Theorem 2)
a = b
Exterior angle = sum of the two interior opposite angles (Theorem 6)
e = a + b
Alternate angles are equal (Theorem 3)
a = b
Corresponding angles are equal (Theorem 5)
a = b
Co-interior angles add to 180°
a + b = 180°

Say alternate and corresponding, not "Z" and "F" · Exam diagrams aren't to scale: don't measure!

A. Error Hunt

70° and x on a straight line → x = 360 − 70 = 290°A straight line is 180°, not 360°: x = 110°
Triangle with angles 45°, 75° and x → x = 45 + 75 = 120°Added instead of taking away: x = 180 − 120 = 60°
Isosceles triangle, apex 50°: find a base angle → 50° (the angles are equal)The two BASE angles are equal: (180 − 50) ÷ 2 = 65°
x and 64° are alternate angles → x = 180 − 64 = 116°Alternate angles are equal: x = 64° (co-interior angles add to 180°)
Give the reason for x = 64° above → Z anglesWrite the proper name: alternate angles
Exterior angle; interior opposite angles 40° and 70° → 180 − (40 + 70) = 70°70° is the interior angle at that corner. The exterior angle = 40 + 70 = 110°

B. Exam-style questions

  1. a = 180 − 68 = 112° (straight line) · b = 68° (corresponding) · c = 68° (vertically opposite to b)
  2. x = 180 − (48 + 70) = 62° (angles on a straight line, at the top) · y = 48° (alternate angles). Check: the angle at the bottom right is 70° (alternate), and 48 + 70 + 62 = 180 (angles in a triangle) ✓
  3. No. Two right angles make 90 + 90 = 180°, which leaves 0° for the third angle, but the three angles of a triangle add to exactly 180°.
  4. w = 114 − 38 = 76° (exterior angle = sum of the two interior opposite angles). Or: the inside angle next to the 114° is 180 − 114 = 66° (straight line), then w = 180 − (38 + 66) = 76° (angles in a triangle) ✓

Marking tip

As in the exam, a correct angle with no reason (or "Z angles") should not get full marks when a reason is asked for. Accept any correct chain of facts, e.g. "co-interior" instead of "corresponding then straight line". Give follow-through marks for later steps done correctly from an earlier slip.
