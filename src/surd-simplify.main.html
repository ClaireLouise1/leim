Surds

A surd is a square root that never comes out exact: √2, √3, √5, √12 …  √9 = 3 and √25 = 5 are not surds. Index rules are on Power Up.

Spot the pattern

  • √4 × √9 = 2 × 3 = 6  ·  √36 = 6
  • √4 × √25 = 2 × 5 = 10  ·  √100 = 10
  • √9 × √16 = 3 × 4 = 12  ·  √144 = 12

What do you notice? Now try: is √9 + √16 the same as √25?

Show me

Multiplying works inside one root: √a × √b = √(ab).
Adding does NOT: √9 + √16 = 3 + 4 = 7, but √25 = 5. So √a + √b ≠ √(a + b).

The rules

Multiply√a × √b = √(ab)
√2 × √8 = √16 = 4
Divide√a ÷ √b = √(a ÷ b)
√50 ÷ √2 = √25 = 5
Square(√a)² = a
(√7)² = 7  (3√2)² = 9 × 2 = 18

Simplifying a surd

Find the biggest square number that goes into it: 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64 …

  • √72 = √(36 × 2) = √36 × √2 = 6√2
  • √50 = √(25 × 2) = 5√2
  • √48 = √(16 × 3) = 4√3  (√(4 × 12) = 2√12 is not finished: 12 still has a square in it.)
  • Backwards: 3√5 = √9 × √5 = √45

Adding and subtracting

Only like surds (same number under the root) can be added. Treat √2 like a letter: 3√2 + 5√2 = 8√2, just like 3x + 5x = 8x.

  • √8 + √18 = 2√2 + 3√2 = 5√2  (simplify first!)
  • √50 − √8 = 5√2 − 2√2 = 3√2
  • √2 + √3 can't be simplified. It is not √5.

Multiply the numbers in front and the roots separately: 2√3 × 5√2 = 10√6.

Higher Level Brackets with surds

Expand like algebra, and remember √3 × √3 = 3.

  • √3(4 + √3) = 4√3 + 3 = 3 + 4√3
  • (5 + √2)(5 − √2) = 25 − 5√2 + 5√2 − 2 = 23  (the middle terms cancel)
  • (1 + √2)² = 1 + √2 + √2 + 2 = 3 + 2√2  (not 1 + 2 = 3!)
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Mostly Higher Level: a short "simplify" or "show that" part, or a surd answer inside a Pythagoras or area question ("give your answer in the form a√b").

How marks are won and lost

  • Use the biggest square. 2√12 is not fully simplified.
  • Show the step: √72 = √36 × √2 = 6√2.
  • Exact answer means leave the √. Don't write 8.49.
  • √a + √b is not √(a + b).

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first.

  1. Simplify √48 + √27. Give your answer in the form a√b.
    Show the worked answer

    √48 = √16 × √3 = 4√3. √27 = √9 × √3 = 3√3.
    4√3 + 3√3 = 7√3.

  2. Show that √2 × √18 is a whole number.
    Show the worked answer

    √2 × √18 = √36 = 6, a whole number.

  3. A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 2 cm and 4 cm. Find the longest side. Give your answer in the form a√b.
    Show the worked answer

    c² = 2² + 4² = 4 + 16 = 20. c = √20 = √4 × √5 = 2√5 cm.

  4. Higher Level Expand and simplify (3 + √2)(3 − √2) + (1 + √2)².
    Show the worked answer

    (3 + √2)(3 − √2) = 9 − 2 = 7.
    (1 + √2)² = 1 + 2√2 + 2 = 3 + 2√2.
    Total: 7 + 3 + 2√2 = 10 + 2√2.
