Fractions

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): N.1a, N.1b, N.2a (fractions come back in ratio, probability and algebra)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can find and explain equivalent fractions, simplify using the HCF, switch between mixed numbers and improper fractions, compare fractions, and add, subtract, multiply and divide fractions and mixed numbers, including fractions of amounts and word problems.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Fraction Wall + Find Your Match cards · p.3 Fraction Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: Fold it. Each student folds a strip of paper in half, then in half again, then again. Shade half the strip. How many eighths is that? Quarters? Elicit numerator, denominator, equivalent.
10 minGuided discovery: the Fraction Wall (p.2, one per student). Pairs answer the wall questions using only the wall (no rules given yet). Feedback: what do you do to the top and bottom to get an equivalent fraction? Why is 1/8 smaller than 1/6 even though 8 is bigger? Then board the rules from the poster (p.4): simplest form with the HCF, mixed ↔ improper.
10 minFind Your Match (p.2, cut up; one card each, print twice for big classes). Students mingle, work out their card and find the partner card with the same value. Pairs stand together and explain their match to the class.
5 minModel the four operations on the board with the poster: common denominators (LCM) for + and −; tops × tops for ×; keep, change, flip for ÷; improper first for mixed numbers. Ask: why does 1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5 have to be wrong? (2/5 is less than 1/2.)
12 minFraction Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left and build the chain to FINISH. Answer chain below.
15 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name the rule, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone. Feedback on question 2: why is it "of what is left"?
3 minExit ticket. 2 3/4 + 1 2/3 and 3/5 ÷ 9/10 (4 5/12; 2/3). Homework: play Fraction Match at leim.ie/fraction-match.html (Exam mix round) and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Find Your Match key: A: picture = 3/4 · B: picture = 1/3 · C: picture = 2/3 · D: picture = 2/5 · E: 11/4 = 2 3/4 · F: 1/2 + 1/3 = 5/6 · G: 3/4 of 20 = 15 · H: 2/3 ÷ 1/6 = 4.
Errors to look for: adding the bottoms (1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5) · finding a common denominator for × · forgetting to flip, or flipping the first fraction, for ÷ · multiplying the wholes and the fractions separately in mixed numbers · stopping before simplest form (6/9) · 2 1/3 = 5/3 · "of the rest" worked out on the whole amount · thinking 1/8 > 1/6. Support: keep the Fraction Wall on the desk. Fast finishers: find a fraction exactly halfway between 1/3 and 1/2 (5/12); is there always one between any two fractions?

Fractions

Equivalent: × or ÷ the top and bottom by the same number · Simplest form: ÷ by the HCF · 2 1/3 = 7/3 (2 × 3 + 1)

+ and −Same bottom first (use the LCM)
1/3 + 1/4 = 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12
Never add the bottoms!
×Tops × tops, bottoms × bottoms
2/3 × 3/5 = 6/15 = 2/5
÷Keep, change, flip
3/4 ÷ 1/2 = 3/4 × 2/1 = 1 1/2
of÷ by the bottom, × by the top
3/5 of 40 = 40 ÷ 5 × 3 = 24

Mixed numbers? Change to improper fractions before × and ÷.

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. Work out, giving each answer as a mixed number or a whole number: (a) 4 1/6 − 2 3/4 (b) 1 3/5 × 2 1/2 (c) 2 2/3 ÷ 1 1/9
  2. A prize of €3600 is shared. Áine gets 1/3, Brian gets 2/5 and Ciara gets the rest. (a) What fraction does Ciara get? (b) How much money does Ciara get?
  3. A water tank is 3/8 full. After 330 litres are added, it is 5/6 full. How many litres does the tank hold when it is full?
  4. Which is bigger, 7/9 or 4/5? Show how you decided.
  5. How many 2/5 kg bags of sugar can be filled from 6 kg?

Answers

↗ Léim

Fraction Wall questions

1. 3 · 2. e.g. 2/4, 3/6, 4/8 (also 5/10, 6/12) · 3. 3/4 · 4. 4/10 · 5. 1/5, 1/4, 1/3: the bigger the bottom, the smaller the piece · 6. 3/4; 1/2 · 7. 8

A. Error Hunt

1/2 + 1/3Added the bottoms. Common denominator: 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6
3/4 ÷ 1/2Multiplied without flipping: 3/4 × 2/1 = 6/4 = 1 1/2
12/18 in simplest formNot finished: divide by the HCF, 6 → 2/3
2 1/3 as an improper fraction2 × 3 + 1 = 7 → 7/3
3 1/5 − 1 3/5Took 1/5 from 3/5 the wrong way round. 16/5 − 8/5 = 8/5 = 1 3/5
2/5 of 30Divided by 2. Divide by the bottom, times the top: 30 ÷ 5 × 2 = 12

B. Exam-style questions

  1. (a) 25/6 − 11/4 = 50/12 − 33/12 = 17/12 = 1 5/12 (b) 8/5 × 5/2 = 40/10 = 4 (c) 8/3 ÷ 10/9 = 8/3 × 9/10 = 72/30 = 2 2/5
  2. (a) 1 − 5/15 − 6/15 = 4/15 (b) 3600 ÷ 15 × 4 = €960 (check: €1200 + €1440 + €960 = €3600)
  3. 5/6 − 3/8 = 20/24 − 9/24 = 11/24 of the tank is 330 litres. 1/24 = 30 litres, so the tank holds 720 litres.
  4. 7/9 = 35/45 and 4/5 = 36/45, so 4/5 is bigger (just!).
  5. 6 ÷ 2/5 = 6 × 5/2 = 15 bags.

Marking tip

Give credit for each correct step, as the exam does: a correct common denominator or a correct flip with one arithmetic slip should still earn most of the marks. Insist on a sense check: is the answer bigger or smaller than they expected?
