Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

Sentences, fragments and run-ons

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): W10, W11, W12

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can tell a complete sentence from a fragment or a run-on, fix comma splices and fragments in their own writing, name the four sentence types, join ideas with the right joining word, and say whether a sentence is simple, compound or complex.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Sentence Sort cards (cut-up) · p.3 Fix the Story relay cards · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (useful revision in 2nd and 3rd). Time: about 60 minutes. All sentences are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Read aloud: "The bus was late." / "Because the bus was late." / "The bus was late we missed the film." Which one sounds finished? Elicit: who + verb + a complete thought.
8 minGuided discovery. Pairs: what's missing from the second? What's too much in the third? Board the words fragment and run-on and the two fixes (full stop + capital, or comma + and / but / so). Then the four sentence types.
12 minSentence Sort (p.2, groups of 3–4, one set of 24 cards). Sort into three piles: sentence, fragment, run-on. Then each group fixes two fragments and two run-ons on mini-whiteboards. Feedback: why is card "Because it was raining, we stayed indoors." a sentence?
15 minFix the Story relay (p.3, groups). Each group gets one card, finds the 2 mistakes and writes the fixed lines on a group sheet, then passes the card on (2 min per card). At the end, groups read the whole story in order and compare fixes.
15 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find it, say what's wrong, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone.
5 minExit ticket. Write one sentence of each type about your weekend, and one complex sentence starting with "When". Homework: play Sentence Surgeon at leim.ie/sentence-surgeon.html.
Sort key: sentences: cards 1, 4, 7, 9, 14, 15, 19, 23 · fragments: cards 3, 8, 10, 11, 12, 17, 18, 21 · run-ons: cards 2, 5, 6, 13, 16, 20, 22, 24.
Errors to look for: comma splices ("It was late, we went home") · "Because…" sentences that never finish · thinking a long sentence must be a run-on (length isn't the test: two sentences with no joining word is) · thinking "Ciara and Jack walked to school" is compound (two names, one clause: simple). Support: the poster (p.4) on the desk. Fast finishers: rewrite the Lost Phone story with one short sentence for effect.

Relay answers (any correct fix is fine; a comma + and / but / so also fixes a run-on):

  1. My birthday started badly. I woke up late. My alarm hadn't gone off. I came rushing down the stairs. (run-on (late, my) · fragment (Rushing…))
  2. Mam was in the kitchen. She was making pancakes because it was my birthday. (run-on (kitchen she) · fragment (Because…))
  3. After breakfast, I looked for my phone. It wasn't in my room. It wasn't in my bag. I had lost the phone that Gran gave me. (run-on (room, it) · fragment (The phone that…))
  4. I searched the whole house. Nobody had seen it. My little brother was grinning in the corner. (run-on (house nobody) · fragment (…grinning))
  5. “Where is it?” I asked him. He just laughed. He pointed at the fridge. (end mark (it? not it.) · run-on (laughed, he))
  6. My phone was in the fridge, next to the butter. What a strange birthday! It was a strange birthday, although it ended well. (end mark (birthday!) · fragment (Although…))

B. Exam-style questions

Night fell. The wind rose, rattling the windows and howling down the chimney as if it wanted to get in. Ellen lay awake. Something was moving downstairs.
  1. How does the writer use sentence length to build tension? Refer to the text.
  2. Write one statement, one question, one command and one exclamation about a school tour.
  3. Join into one complex sentence starting with "When": "The referee blew the final whistle. The fans ran onto the pitch."
  4. "Ciara and Jack walked to school." Simple or compound? Explain.
  5. Write a short paragraph (3–4 sentences) about a storm. Use at least one very short sentence for effect.

Answers

↗ Léim

Sentence Sort

Sentences: cards 1, 4, 7, 9, 14, 15, 19, 23 · Fragments: cards 3, 8, 10, 11, 12, 17, 18, 21 · Run-ons: cards 2, 5, 6, 13, 16, 20, 22, 24. Watch for "Because it was raining, we stayed indoors." and "Oisín forgot his lunch, so he borrowed a sandwich.": both are complete sentences.

Fix the Story relay

  1. My birthday started badly. I woke up late. My alarm hadn't gone off. I came rushing down the stairs. (run-on (late, my) · fragment (Rushing…))
  2. Mam was in the kitchen. She was making pancakes because it was my birthday. (run-on (kitchen she) · fragment (Because…))
  3. After breakfast, I looked for my phone. It wasn't in my room. It wasn't in my bag. I had lost the phone that Gran gave me. (run-on (room, it) · fragment (The phone that…))
  4. I searched the whole house. Nobody had seen it. My little brother was grinning in the corner. (run-on (house nobody) · fragment (…grinning))
  5. “Where is it?” I asked him. He just laughed. He pointed at the fridge. (end mark (it? not it.) · run-on (laughed, he))
  6. My phone was in the fridge, next to the butter. What a strange birthday! It was a strange birthday, although it ended well. (end mark (birthday!) · fragment (Although…))

A. Error Hunt

It was late, we went home.Comma splice (run-on): It was late. We went home. / It was late, so we went home.
Because I was tired.Fragment: add a main part. I went to bed early because I was tired.
Are you coming to the party.It's a question: Are you coming to the party?
The rain stopped. we went outside.Capital letter after a full stop: We went outside.
My brother plays the drums he is very loud.Run-on: My brother plays the drums. He is very loud.
Sitting on the bus for two hours.Fragment (no helper verb): We were sitting on the bus for two hours.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. e.g. Very short sentences ("Night fell." "Ellen lay awake.") slow us down and feel tense. The long middle sentence lets the wind build up. The last short sentence, "Something was moving downstairs.", is a shock that leaves us waiting. (Credit: point about length + quote + effect.)
  2. e.g. The bus leaves at eight. Where are we stopping for lunch? Bring a packed lunch. What a brilliant day!
  3. When the referee blew the final whistle, the fans ran onto the pitch.
  4. Simple: there are two names, but one clause (one verb: walked).
  5. Any paragraph of correct sentences with at least one short sentence used for tension or surprise.

Marking tip

In students' own writing, comma splices and fragments are the most common accuracy slips. In questions on a writer's style, credit a clear point about sentence length or type, a short quote and the effect on the reader.
