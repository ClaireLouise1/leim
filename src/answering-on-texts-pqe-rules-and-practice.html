Point, quote, explain

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R3, R4, R7, W7, W8. Play the game: leim.ie/pqe-builder.html

Almost every question on a text (an unseen extract, a poem, your novel, play or film) wants three things: a point that answers the question, a quote that proves it, and an explanation of how the words prove it. Some schools call it PQE, PEE or PIE.

1. The three parts

PointAnswer the question in your own words, in one sentence. Use the question's key word: Cian is lonely at his new school.
QuoteA few words, copied exactly, in quotation marks, built into your sentence: On his first day he “ate his lunch alone”.
ExplainZoom in on one word and say how it proves the point: The word ‘alone’ suggests nobody wants to know him yet.

2. Quoting well

Short2–6 words is usually enough
Exact“hands were shaking” is not “hands trembled”
Markedquotation marks round the writer's words
Built inSaoirse “clenched” her fists when the ref gave a free.

3. Explaining well

Starters: The word ‘…’ suggests … · This shows … · The verb ‘…’ makes … sound … · This tells us …

Weak explanationWhat's wrong
This means her hands were trembling while she opened the case.Retelling: it only says the quote again.
‘Trembled’ shows she is angry.Off the point: it argues a different feeling.
This is a good quote because it shows what she is like.Vague: which word? Like what?

4. A full PQE

Aoife is nervous before her audition. As she opens the case, “her hands trembled”. The word ‘trembled’ shows how anxious she is: her body gives her away.

Two or three short PQEs beat one long paragraph. Don't retell the story.

Exam tip: “Use evidence from the text” or “Support your answer” means: quote (or refer to) the words, then explain them.

A. Write the short quote (2–6 words) that proves each point.

The New School
On his first day in Athlone, Cian ate his lunch alone on the wall.
The yard was grey and puddles covered the cracked concrete.
Then a girl called Hana sat down and offered him half her orange.
Cian smiled for the first time that week.
Next Door
Every Tuesday, Mr Daly walked to the shop in the drizzle.
Since his wife died, he spoke to no one all week.
One day Ellen from next door helped him carry his bags home.
Now he waits at the gate, grinning.
  1. Cian feels lonely at his new school.
  2. The yard is a dreary place.
  3. Hana is kind to Cian.
  4. Cian is happy at the end.
  5. The weather is dreary.
  6. Mr Daly is lonely.

B. Point, quote or explain? Write P, Q or E.

  1. On his first day he “ate his lunch alone on the wall”. ___
  2. Leah will not take no for an answer. ___
  3. This suggests she is trying to hold her temper in. ___
  4. Saoirse is furious with the referee. ___
  5. The word ‘alone’ suggests that nobody wants to know him yet. ___
  6. She tells us she “asked again and again”. ___
  7. The repeated word ‘again’ shows she is stubborn. ___
  8. When he gives a free, she says, “I clenched my fists”. ___
  9. Cian feels lonely at his new school. ___

C. Which is the best explanation?

  1. Aoife is nervous. “Her hands trembled as she undid the clasp”
    1. The word ‘trembled’ shows that she is angry with her teacher.
    2. This means that her hands were trembling while she was undoing the clasp of her case.
    3. The word ‘trembled’ shows how nervous she is: her body gives her away.
    4. This quote is a good one because it shows what Aoife is like.
  2. Cian feels lonely. “ate his lunch alone on the wall”
    1. This tells us that on his first day Cian ate his lunch on the wall by himself.
    2. The word ‘alone’ suggests he feels lonely and shut out.
    3. The word ‘wall’ shows that Cian is proud of his new school.
    4. This is a really good quote and it proves my point about Cian.

D. Quote it short and exact.

  1. Oisín is furious. Oisín stormed into the kitchen and threw his bag on the floor.
  2. The opening is peaceful. The water lapped gently against the reeds at the edge of the lake.
  3. Kevin is worried. Every rock on the ridge looked the same, and his stomach churned.

E. Which point answers the question?

  1. How does the writer show that Cian is lonely?
    1. Cian is angry with his parents for moving house.
    2. Cian is proud of his new uniform on his first day.
    3. Cian is new in Athlone.
    4. Cian is isolated: he eats his lunch on the wall by himself.
  2. How does the poet create a peaceful mood at the start?
    1. The rain at the end makes the whole lake feel gloomy.
    2. The heron standing in the reeds makes the lake feel tense.
    3. Gentle sounds make the lake calm.
    4. It is set at Lough Gur.

F. Exam-style: an original extract

The Bake SaleCiara set her lemon cake on the table and stepped back. Around her the hall buzzed with parents and pushing children. She checked the price card for the third time and smoothed her apron. When a woman finally bought a slice, Ciara's face lit up, and she counted the coins twice before dropping them into the tin.
  1. How does Ciara feel before anyone buys her cake? Make a point, use a short quote and explain it.
  2. How does Ciara feel at the end? Write a full PQE.

Answers

A1. “ate his lunch alone on the wall” · 2. “The yard was grey” · 3. “offered him half her orange” · 4. “smiled for the first time” · 5. “in the drizzle” · 6. “he spoke to no one”
B7. Q · 8. P · 9. E · 10. P · 11. E · 12. Q · 13. E · 14. Q · 15. P
C16. c · 17. b (the others retell the quote, argue a different feeling or name no word)
D18. “stormed into the kitchen” · 19. “lapped gently” · 20. “his stomach churned” (or another 2–6 exact words that prove the point)
E21. d · 22. c
F23. Nervous / anxious: she “checked the price card for the third time” and “smoothed her apron”; ‘third time’ suggests she can't stop worrying that something is wrong. · 24. Delighted and proud: her “face lit up”; ‘lit up’ suggests a sudden, bright smile, and counting the coins “twice” shows how much the sale means to her. Reward a clear point + a short, exact quote + an explanation of one word.
