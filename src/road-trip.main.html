Speed, distance, time & currency

Spot the pattern

A car drives along the motorway at a steady speed. Here is how far it has gone at different times.

TimeDistance
30 min50 km
1 hour100 km
1 h 30 min150 km
2 hours200 km

Divide each distance by its time in hours. What do you get every time? What would you do to the speed to get the distance for 3 hours? And to get the time for 350 km?

Show me

50 ÷ 0.5 = 100 ÷ 1 = 150 ÷ 1.5 = 200 ÷ 2 = 100 km/h every time: that's the speed. So distance = speed × time (100 × 3 = 300 km) and time = distance ÷ speed (350 ÷ 100 = 3.5 hours = 3 h 30 min). Careful: 30 min is 0.5 of an hour, not 0.3.

The three formulas

Speed = D ÷ Thow fast
Distance = S × Thow far
Time = D ÷ Show long
  • Units must match: km and hours give km/h; metres and seconds give m/s.
  • Minutes → hours: divide by 60. 45 min = 0.75 h; 2 h 24 min = 2.4 h. 2 h 30 min is 2.5 hours, not 2.3.
  • Hours → minutes: only the decimal part × 60. 1.35 h = 1 h + 0.35 × 60 min = 1 h 21 min.
  • Arrival time = leaving time + journey time. Leave 09:40, drive 2 h 35 min → 12:15.
  • km/h ↔ m/s: 1 km/h = 1,000 m in 3,600 s, so km/h ÷ 3.6 = m/s and m/s × 3.6 = km/h (20 m/s = 72 km/h).

Average speed

Average speed = total distance ÷ total time, and the total time includes any stops.

The trap: Niamh drives 120 km to Galway at 60 km/h (2 hours) and back at 40 km/h (3 hours). Her average speed is 240 km ÷ 5 h = 48 km/h, not (60 + 40) ÷ 2 = 50. She spends longer at the slower speed, so the average is pulled down.

Distance–time graphs

  • Going up = moving away from home; flat = stopped; going down = coming back.
  • Steeper = faster. Speed on a part = distance covered ÷ time taken. Cian: O to A is 40 km in 1 h = 40 km/h; he stops from A to B for 30 min; B to C is 40 km in 30 min (11:30 to 12:00) = 80 km/h, the steepest part.
  • Average speed for the whole trip: total distance travelled (there AND back: 80 km) ÷ total time (2 hours, stop included) = 40 km/h.

Currency

Euro → otherMultiply by the rate. €1 = £0.86: €250 × 0.86 = £215.
Other → euroDivide by the rate. £215 ÷ 0.86 = €250. £100 ÷ 0.86 = €116.28 (nearest cent).
CommissionA bank takes 2% of €500 = €10 first, then changes €490: × 1.08 = $529.20.

Sense check: €1 buys less than £1, so you get fewer pounds than euro; €1 buys more than $1 (at $1.08), so you get more dollars. Finding the rate: €400 gave £344, so £344 ÷ 400 = 0.86. Where is it cheaper? Change both prices into the same currency first.

The classic mistakes: 2 h 30 min as 2.3 hours; dividing when you should multiply (S × T for distance); taking the mean of two speeds; leaving the stop out of the total time; on a graph, reading the distance instead of working out the speed; multiplying by the rate to get back into euro.
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Journeys and holidays are a favourite real-life setting: a family trip with a timetable or a departure time, a distance–time graph of a walk or a cycle, or a holiday budget with an exchange rate. It is often one part of a longer question that also uses percentages, ratio or money. The later parts usually ask for an average speed for the whole trip, a comparison, or an explanation.

How marks are won and lost

  • Change the time into hours first and write it down (2 h 15 min = 2.25 h). Most lost marks come from 2.15.
  • Write the formula (time = distance ÷ speed), then the numbers, then the answer with its units.
  • Average speed: add up ALL the distance and ALL the time, stops included.
  • Graphs: read the scale on both axes carefully; one small square may not be 1.
  • Currency: say which way you're going (× into the foreign money, ÷ back into euro) and round money to the nearest cent at the end.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first.

  1. The family trip. The Murphys drive 210 km to a holiday park. They leave at 09:15 and drive at an average speed of 84 km/h. (a) How long does the journey take? (b) At what time do they arrive? (c) On the way home they stop for 20 minutes, and the whole trip home takes 3 hours. Find their average speed for the trip home. (d) Find their average speed on the way home when they were actually driving.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) Time = 210 ÷ 84 = 2.5 hours = 2 h 30 min.
    (b) 09:15 + 2 h 30 min = 11:45.
    (c) 210 ÷ 3 = 70 km/h.
    (d) Driving time = 3 h − 20 min = 2 h 40 min = 2⅔ hours. 210 ÷ 2⅔ = 78.75 km/h.

  2. The cycle. The graph shows Aoife's cycle from home.
    (a) How far from home was she at 09:30? (b) What was she doing between A and B? (c) Find her speed on each of the parts OA, BC and CD. Which part was fastest? (d) Find her average speed for the whole trip.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 12 km.
    (b) The line is flat: she stopped for 30 minutes (09:30 to 10:00).
    (c) OA: 12 km in 30 min = 24 km/h. BC: 18 km in 1 h = 18 km/h. CD: 30 km in 1 h = 30 km/h, the fastest (the steepest part of the graph).
    (d) Total distance 12 + 18 + 30 = 60 km in 3 hours (09:00 to 12:00): 60 ÷ 3 = 20 km/h.

  3. Holiday money. Seán is going to London. The rate is €1 = £0.86. (a) He changes €600 into sterling. How much does he get? (b) He spends £430 and changes what's left back into euro at €1 = £0.88. How many euro does he get, to the nearest cent? (c) A pair of runners costs £68 in London and €82 in Cork. Using €1 = £0.86, where are they cheaper, and by how much?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 600 × 0.86 = £516.
    (b) £516 − £430 = £86. 86 ÷ 0.88 = 97.727… = €97.73.
    (c) £68 ÷ 0.86 = €79.07 (nearest cent). London is cheaper by €2.93 (€82 − €79.07).

  4. There and back. Darragh cycles 24 km to his grandmother's house at 16 km/h and cycles home at 24 km/h. (a) How long does each part take? (b) Find his average speed for the whole trip. (c) His sister says "(16 + 24) ÷ 2 = 20 km/h". Explain why she is wrong. (d) A sprinter runs 100 m in 10 seconds. Write her average speed in km/h.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 24 ÷ 16 = 1.5 h = 1 h 30 min; 24 ÷ 24 = 1 h.
    (b) Total 48 km in 2.5 h: 48 ÷ 2.5 = 19.2 km/h.
    (c) He spends longer (1 h 30 min) at the slower speed than at the faster one (1 h), so the average is not halfway between them. Average speed = total distance ÷ total time.
    (d) 100 ÷ 10 = 10 m/s; 10 × 3.6 = 36 km/h.
