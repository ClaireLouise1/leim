Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

Vocabulary and dictionary skills

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R10, OL13, W5

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can put near-synonyms in order of strength and replace “very” + a weak word with one precise word, tell synonyms from antonyms, use prefixes (un-, dis-, im-, il-, ir-, re-, mis-) and suffixes to build words, choose the right word from a word family, and use ABC order and guide words to find words fast.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Ladder Sort cards + Guide Word Swap A/B cards · p.3 Prefix & Family Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (useful revision in 2nd and 3rd). Time: about 60 minutes. All sentences are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Board: “The soup was very hot.” Pairs find one word to replace “very hot”. Collect answers on a ladder drawn on the board: warm → hot → boiling.
8 minGuided discovery. Board: impossible, impolite, illegal, illogical, irregular, irresponsible. Pairs: all mean “not”, so why does the prefix change? (look at the first letter of the root). Then: why two l's in illegal?
10 minLadder Sort (p.2, 24 cards = 8 ladders of 3; groups of 3–4). Sort the words into 8 families, then order each from bottom (weakest) to top (strongest). Groups compare: did anyone order a ladder differently? Can they defend it?
12 minGuide Word Swap (p.2, pairs, don't show your card). A reads a word; B says which of their four dictionary pages it goes on and writes it there. 8 words each, then swap. Check with the key; any mistakes, say the ABC order out loud.
10 minPrefix & Family Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, make the word the clue asks for, find the domino with that word on its left. Build the chain to FINISH.
12 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs, then the exam-style questions alone.
3 minExit ticket. Write the opposite: possible, legal, regular. Replace: “very tired”. Homework: play Word Ladder at leim.ie/word-ladder.html.
Ladder Sort key (bottom → top): warm → hot → sweltering · chilly → cold → freezing · pleased → happy → overjoyed · annoyed → angry → furious · sleepy → tired → exhausted · peckish → hungry → starving · soft → quiet → silent · breeze → wind → gale.
Errors to look for: unlegal / inpossible · ilegal (one l) · mispell (one s) · “happy” for “happily” · the strongest word in the wrong place (“furious” about a lost pencil). Support: the poster (p.4) on the desk. Fast finishers: make your own ladder of 4 words and test a partner.

Worksheet

Error Hunt + exam-style questions

↗ Léim

A. Error Hunt

Each sentence has one weak or wrong word in bold. Say what's wrong and write a better word.

SentenceWhat's wrong and the fix
The film was very funny, so we laughed all the way through.
It is unlegal to drive without a licence.
She sang happy in the shower.
The deep of the pool is two metres.
I mispelled my own name!
Thanks for being so helpless with my project.

B. Exam-style questions

The climb up Croagh Patrick was very hard. Halfway up, the rain turned into a very heavy shower and we were very wet. My little brother was very scared of the loose stones, so Dad held his hand. At the top, the view over Clew Bay was very beautiful, and we were all very happy.
  1. Replace each underlined phrase with one stronger word that keeps the meaning.
  2. Write the opposite of each word with a prefix: possible, patient, legal, responsible, lucky.
  3. Put these words on a ladder, bottom → top: soaked, damp, wet.
  4. “My little brother clung to Dad's hand.” What does “clung” suggest about how he felt? Why is it better than “held”?
  5. Find a word in the passage that has more than one meaning. Write two sentences that show two different meanings.

Answers

↗ Léim

Ladder Sort

warm → hot → sweltering · chilly → cold → freezing · pleased → happy → overjoyed · annoyed → angry → furious · sleepy → tired → exhausted · peckish → hungry → starving · soft → quiet → silent · breeze → wind → gale. Accept a different order if students can defend it (e.g. chilly and cold are close).

Guide Word Swap

A's words on B's pages: dentist → page 1 · hockey → page 2 · mango → page 3 · thunder → page 4 · dog → page 1 · honey → page 2 · medal → page 3 · tent → page 4.
B's words on A's pages: bed → page 1 · chip → page 2 · pencil → page 3 · seal → page 4 · basket → page 1 · city → page 2 · panda → page 3 · salt → page 4.

A. Error Hunt

The film was very funny, so we laughed all the way through.Weak: use one stronger word → hilarious
It is unlegal to drive without a licence.Wrong prefix: legal starts with l → illegal
She sang happy in the shower.It says how she sang, so an adverb → happily
The deep of the pool is two metres.After “the … of” we need a noun → depth
I mispelled my own name!mis + spelled: keep both s's → misspelled
Thanks for being so helpless with my project.Wrong word in the family: helpless = can't help → helpful

B. Exam-style questions

  1. very hard → gruelling · very heavy shower → downpour · very wet → soaked · very scared → terrified · very beautiful → stunning · very happy → overjoyed. Accept other strong words that keep the meaning (tough, drenched, petrified, breathtaking, delighted).
  2. impossible, impatient, illegal, irresponsible, unlucky
  3. damp → wet → soaked
  4. “Clung” suggests he held on tightly because he was frightened and needed to feel safe. It is better than “held” because it shows his fear without saying “he was scared”.
  5. e.g. shower: “a heavy shower of rain” / “I had a shower after the climb”. Also hand, top, loose, held. Any two sentences that show two clearly different meanings.

Marking tip

For “what does this word suggest?” questions, reward the meaning AND the effect (feeling, picture, tone). In students' own writing, reward precise words that fit the context over long or rare words used loosely.
