1. Six techniques to spot

TechniqueWhat it isExample
Similecompares two things using like or asRain fell like tiny needles.
Metaphorsays one thing is anotherThe classroom was a zoo.
Personificationa thing does something only a person can doThe wind whispered.
Alliterationwords close together start with the same soundFierce flames flickered.
Onomatopoeiaa word that sounds like the noise it namessizzle, crash, creak
Rhymewords end with the same soundnight / bright

2. Simile or metaphor?

Look for the small word. Her eyes shone like stars → simile. The stars were diamonds → metaphor. A metaphor is stronger: it doesn't say "a bit like", it says "is".

3. Rhyme is about sound, not spelling

wood / good rhyme. wood / food look alike but don't. Say them out loud. A poem that rhymes in pairs (side / tide, eye / sky) is in rhyming couplets.

4. Imagery: the five senses

Sightcolours, light, shapes: golden, glittered, shadows
Soundroared, echoed, rang, rumbled
Smellscent, stank, perfume, reeked
Tastesour, salty, bitter, spicy
Touchrough, icy, soft, soggy

5. Name it, quote it, explain it

Name: The poet uses personification.
Quote: "The wind whispered secrets to the trees."
Explain: It makes the wind seem gentle and mysterious, as if it is sharing a secret. The mood is quiet and calm.

6. Words for the effect

The technique creates a picture (it helps us imagine…), a mood (calm, eerie, lonely, joyful, tense) or a sound (harsh, soft, fast, slow).

Classic mistakes: naming a technique and stopping · "it makes the poem more interesting" (say HOW) · calling a simile a metaphor · quoting the whole verse · "rhymes" that only look the same.
Exam tip: two techniques, each explained well, beat a list of five names. Always ask "So what? What does it make me see, hear or feel?"

E21. line 4 (metaphor) · 22. line 3 (onomatopoeia) · 23. line 1 (personification) · 24. line 2 (simile)
F25. "The rain is a drummer on our roof": the rain makes a steady, loud beat, like someone playing drums. It suggests the rain is heavy and keeps going all night.
26. Onomatopoeia: "tap, tap, tapping" lets us hear the rain; repeating it gives a steady rhythm. Alliteration + onomatopoeia: "gutters gurgle, gulp and gush": the repeated g sound is thick and watery, like water rushing down the drain.

Worked examples

Simile or metaphor?"The frost was as white as a sheet." — it uses like or as, so it's a simile. The frost is compared to a sheet.
PersonificationAsk: could this thing really do it? An alarm clock can't shout and a kettle can't sing. Only people can.
AlliterationThree or more words close together with the same first sound: "Fierce flames flickered". One word said three times (crunch, crunch, crunch) is repetition, not alliteration.
Explaining the effectNot "it makes it interesting" but "it makes the rain sound heavy and steady, like a drum".
