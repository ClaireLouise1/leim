1. Talking to a group

Do itAvoid it
Eyeslook up, around the roomstare at the floor or the page
Voiceloud enough for the back row; stress key wordsmumble; one flat voice
Paceslow down; pauserush; "em" and "like"
Bodystand straight, feet stillfidget, sway, turn your back
Notescue cards with key wordsread every word

2. Plan your talk

Hook (first line): a question to the audience, a surprising fact or number, or a short story from your life. Never "Today I'm going to talk about…".
Plan: tell them how many points (three reasons…). End strongly: sum up and leave one last thought.
StartFirst of all · To begin with · Firstly
Next pointMoving on · Secondly · Another reason · On top of that
EndTo sum up · In conclusion · To finish · All in all

3. Listening well

Nod, let them finish, then reply to what they said: ask a question about it.
“I went to Paris.” → What was your favourite part of Paris?
Not: changing the subject · talking about yourself · putting them down.

4. Discussing in a group

AgreeThat's a great point. · I agree with…
Disagree politelyI see what you mean, but… · With respect, I disagree.
Join inCan I come in here? · Could I say something?
Ask for moreCan you give us an example? · Why do you think that?
Classic mistakes: reading a script word for word · no hook · no signposts, so listeners get lost · "That's it, thanks" as an ending · interrupting · "That's stupid" instead of a polite disagreement.
Exam tip: in the written paper you may have to write a talk. Use the same tools: a hook, "you" and "we" to speak to the audience, signposts for each point, and a strong ending.

D. What is it for?

  4. “I agree with Aoife on that.”

E. Listen well: circle the best reply

  1. Your friend says: “My team won the county final.”
    We'd have beaten you easily.  /  That's not a big deal.  /  Did you watch the new film last night?  /  Well done! Who scored in the final?
  2. Your friend says: “I'm minding my cousins every Saturday.”
    I don't have any cousins. I just have a brother.  /  Where did you get that jacket?  /  What do you do with your cousins on Saturdays?  /  Sounds like a pain.
  3. Your friend says: “I've started learning the guitar.”
    Have you done the maths homework?  /  Ugh, that sounds boring.  /  I play the piano and I'm way better at it.  /  What song are you learning on the guitar first?

Worked examples

Writing a hookTopic: recycling. Question: What happens to your crisp packet after the bin? Fact: A plastic bottle can take hundreds of years to break down. Story: Last summer, I found a plastic bag on Inch beach.
Planning with signpostsHook → "three reasons" → First of all … → Moving on … → Another reason … → To sum up … → one last thought.
A good listening replyPick a word they used and ask about it: “My team won the final.” → Who scored in the final?
Disagreeing politelyShow you heard them (I see what you mean…), then but + your reason. Never "That's stupid".
