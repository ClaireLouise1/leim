Drama 1: how plays work

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R6, R7, R9, OL8. Play the game: leim.ie/stage-it.html

A play is written to be seen and heard. When you read one, picture it on a stage: What do we see? What do we hear? How do the characters move and speak?

1. Reading a script

(A kitchen, late at night. Rain against the window. DAN sits at the table.)
NUALA: (entering, out of breath) Did you hear that?
DAN: (not looking up) Hear what?
Character namesin capitals, before each line
Dialoguethe words the actors say (no speech marks)
Stage directionsin brackets and italics, present tense: where we are, how to say it, where to move, what to do, what the face shows

2. The stage

Upstage = the back. Downstage = nearest the audience. Stage left / right = the actor's left / right as they face the audience.

3. Five backstage jobs

Setthe place: backdrop, walls, furniture
Costumewhat the actors wear
Lightingspotlights, blackouts, a glow
Soundeffects and music: thunder, a doorbell
Propsobjects actors hold: a letter, keys, a cup

4. Lighting and sound set the mood

MoodLightingSound
Spookydim, cold blue lightan owl and a creaking door
Happybright, warm lightcheering and lively music
Sadsoft, grey lighta slow, sad violin
Dangerflashing red lighta fast, pounding drum
Peacefulgentle golden lightbirdsong and gentle waves

A blackout ends a scene with a shock. A spotlight on one character shows they matter most, or that they're alone.

5. Conflict drives every play

Person vs persontwo characters clash
Person vs selfguilt, doubt, a hard choice
Person vs naturea storm, a flood, the sea
Person vs societyrules, laws, tradition

6. Same words, new meaning

(smiling, hugging her mam) I'm fine. = happy
(slamming the bedroom door) I'm fine. = angry

In a play, what characters do often tells us more than what they say.

Exam tip: use the stage directions as evidence, not just the dialogue, and give a reason for every staging choice: "a single spotlight, to show how alone he is".

F. Exam-style

THE MATCH (an extract from an imaginary play)
(A dressing room, minutes before the county final. Bright, harsh light. CIARÁN, sixteen, sits alone, his helmet in his hands. His coach, DEIRDRE, enters.)
DEIRDRE: (gently) They're waiting for you, Ciarán.
CIARÁN: (staring at the floor) What if I miss again? Like last year?
DEIRDRE: (kneels beside him) Then you pick up the next ball.
(Outside, the crowd begins to roar. CIARÁN stands up slowly and pulls on his helmet.)
  1. Where and when is this scene set? Use two details from the stage directions.
  2. What is Ciarán's conflict? Quote one line that shows it.
  3. Choose the lighting and one sound effect for the last moment. Explain your choices.
