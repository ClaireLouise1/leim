The novel 1: character

Writers rarely tell you "Mia is kind". They show you, and leave the clues for you to find. Your job is to spot them.

Spot the pattern

📖 Story · Mia

1Mia gave half of her lunch to the new boy, who had forgotten his.

2The boy stared at her, as if nobody had ever done that before.

3“It's only a sandwich,” Mia said, going red.

4Later, she wondered why nobody else had noticed him sitting alone.

The word "kind" isn't there. So how do we know? In each line, where does the clue come from: something Mia does, says, thinks, or someone else?

Show me

Line 1 is an action: she shares her lunch → kind.
Line 2 is the effect on others: the boy can't believe it → kindness is rare here.
Line 3 is speech: she plays it down and goes red → modest, a bit shy.
Line 4 is her thoughts: she notices people who are left out → caring.
Every line is a clue. Put them together and you know Mia without being told.

STEAL: five ways writers show a character

💬 SpeechWhat do they say, and how?
“It should have been me.”
💭 ThoughtsWhat goes on in their head?
He wished he had trained harder.
👥 Effect on othersHow do other people react?
The others groaned but did what she said.
🎬 ActionsWhat do they do?
He handed the wallet in.
👀 LooksWhat do they look like or wear?
His jumper was creased.

Words for what a character is like

🤝 kind🦁 brave🙈 shy📣 bossy🙅 selfish🛋️ lazy💯 honest😒 jealous😂 funny🧠 clever🙄 rude
  • A trait lasts, a feeling passes. "Scared" is how someone feels right now; "brave" is what they are like.
  • Characters can change. Leah is shy in class but brave on stage. Saoirse is rude, then kind. Say what they're like at the start AND at the end.
  • Prove it: point + quote + explain. Ciarán is selfish (point): he “grabbed the biggest slice” (quote), before anyone else had sat down, so he only thinks of himself (explain).
  • Short quotes win. Three to six words that show the trait, not the whole sentence.
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Character questions come up on the novel, the short stories and the unseen extracts. You might be asked what a character is like, how they change, how the writer shows them, or whether you admire them. Every time, the marks go to points backed up with the text.

How marks are won and lost

  • Name the trait. "Lorcan is shy" is a point; "Lorcan stared at the floor" is only a quote.
  • One trait per paragraph, each with its own quote. "Two qualities" means two separate points.
  • Explain the quote. Say what the words show about the character.
  • Look for change. Many questions reward noticing how a character is different at the end.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). "Where the marks would go" is our guide, not an official marking scheme.

The New Captain
Lorcan had never wanted to be captain. When Coach Byrne pinned the armband on him, he stared at the floor and mumbled, “Are you sure?” At the first training session he arrived twenty minutes early to put out the cones, and he stayed late to help the youngest player, Dara, with his free kicks. In the county final, with his team two points down, Lorcan was knocked flat. He picked himself up, wiped the mud from his knees and shouted, “Heads up, lads! We're not done yet!” The others stared at him. Nobody had ever heard Lorcan raise his voice before. Five minutes later, they were level.

  1. What kind of person is Lorcan? Give two qualities and support each with reference to the text.
    Show a model answer

    1. Lorcan is hard-working. He “arrived twenty minutes early to put out the cones”, doing extra jobs nobody asked him to do.
    2. He is also kind. He “stayed late to help the youngest player”, giving up his own time for someone who needed it.
    Where the marks would go: two different qualities, each with its own short quote and a few words explaining what it shows.

  2. How does the writer use other characters to show us what Lorcan is like?
    Show a model answer

    When Lorcan shouts, “The others stared at him” and “Nobody had ever heard Lorcan raise his voice before.” Their surprise tells us he is normally quiet, so this moment shows how much he has grown as a leader.
    Where the marks would go: a reaction from another character (quote), and what that reaction tells us about Lorcan.

  3. Does Lorcan change during the extract? Explain your answer with reference to the text.
    Show a model answer

    Yes. At the start he is shy and unsure: he “stared at the floor and mumbled”. By the end he is confident: he picks himself up and shouts, “Heads up, lads!” to lead his team back.
    Where the marks would go: what he is like at the start (with proof), what he is like at the end (with proof), and the word "change" made clear.

  4. Would you like Lorcan to be your team captain? Give a reason based on the text.
    Show a model answer

    Yes. He doesn't boss people around; he leads by example, arriving early and helping Dara. And when things go wrong he doesn't give up: “We're not done yet!” That is the kind of captain who makes a team better.
    Where the marks would go: a clear yes or no, a reason, and proof from the text. Either answer can score full marks if it is backed up.
