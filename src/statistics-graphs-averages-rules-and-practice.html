1. Three averages and the range

Meantotal ÷ how many
3, 5, 8, 8 → 24 ÷ 4 = 6
Medianin ORDER, the middle
3, 5, 8, 8 → 6.5
Modemost often
3, 5, 8, 8 → 8
Rangebiggest − smallest
8 − 3 = 5 (spread)

Missing value: mean 7 of 5 numbers → total 35; subtract the ones you know. New value: new total ÷ new count.

2. From a frequency table

Goals (x)0123
Matches (f)2521

Mean = Σfx ÷ Σf = 12 ÷ 10 = 1.2. Mode = the value with the biggest frequency (1, not 5). Median: count down to values 5 and 6 → 1.

Grouped data (0–10, 10–20…): use the mid-interval values (5, 15…) to estimate the mean. "10–20" means at least 10, less than 20.

3. Which display?

Bar chart: compare categories. Pie chart: parts of one whole. Line plot: a few small numbers, one dot each. Histogram: lots of numerical data in equal classes (bars touch). Stem-and-leaf: keeps every value, in order, with a key. Back-to-back: compares two groups.

4. Pie chart angles

360° ÷ total = degrees for one person. 20 students: 18° each, so Bus 9 × 18° = 162°. Angles add up to 360°.

5. Stem-and-leaf plots

BoysGirls
8 4 116
5 5 223 7 9
6 132 4 4 8
 45

Key: 5 | 2 | 3 = 25 for Boys and 23 for Girls

Tens on the stem, units as leaves, in order, with a key. In a back-to-back plot the left leaves get bigger going outwards: 5 | 2 is 25. Boys' median 23.5, Girls' 32.

6. Comparing two groups

Give an average and the range for each, then say what they mean in words: "The girls did better on average, and their marks were less spread out."

7. Misleading charts

Watch for: a vertical axis that doesn't start at 0, uneven gaps on a scale, a pie chart whose parts don't add to 100%, 3-D slices, pictures that grow both ways.

8. Samples have limits

"8 of my 10 friends like it" describes your 10 friends, not all students in Ireland. A small or non-random sample can't support a big claim. Say what limits your conclusion.

Classic mistakes: median without ordering · the median's position instead of its value · the frequency as the mode · Σfx ÷ number of rows · reading the left side of a back-to-back plot backwards · pie angles that don't make 360°.
Exam tip: show the total and what you divide by; when you compare, use numbers AND words; give every chart a title, labelled axes and (for stem-and-leaf) a key.
