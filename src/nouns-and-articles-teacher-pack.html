Junior Cycle Spanish · Teacher pack

Nouns & articles: gender and plurals

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes: 2.1, 2.5 (2017 spec, exams 2027–2028) · 2.1, 2.4 (2025 spec, 1st years from Sept 2026)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can predict a noun's gender from its ending (-o, -a, -ción, -dad, -or, -ón, -ma), know the common exceptions (el día, el mapa, la mano, la foto), choose el, la, los, las, un, una, unos, unas, form plurals (including -z → -ces and accent changes) and spot article slips in writing.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Grammar Auction (two team sheets) · p.3–4 Gender Dominoes (cut-up) · p.5 Wall poster · p.6 Worksheet + answers. Level: 1st–3rd year. Time: about 60 minutes.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: what's in my bag? Take objects out of a bag one by one: un libro, una llave, unos lápices, una foto, un mapa… Students say the English. Write each noun with its article in two columns (el / la) without labels.
10 minGuided discovery. "Why are these in two groups? Look at the last letters." Elicit -o → el, -a → la. Then add el mapa, la foto, la canción, la ciudad, el ordenador: "Which ones break your rule? Which new endings can you see?" Show lápiz → lápices, canción → canciones: "What happens to the z? Where did the accent go?" Concept checks: "Una problema: right or wrong?" "Mis padres: two dads?"
5 minPoster (p.5). Chant the endings, then the exceptions with gestures (a hand for la mano, a sun for el día). Rule for life: never write a noun without its article in your copy.
15 minGrammar Auction (p.2), teams of 3–4, one sheet per team. Read each sentence aloud; teams tick ✓ or ✗ and bid (minimum €50) only on sentences they think are correct. Reveal after each one. Correct: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 10. Wrong: 2 un problema, 4 la mano, 6 exámenes, 8 unos plátanos, 11 flores, 12 al cine. The richest team wins; every team must then correct the wrong sentences out loud and say the rule.
10 minGender Dominoes (p.3–4), groups of 3–4. Players match the English to the Spanish noun with the right article. Answer chain below.
10 minWorksheet (p.6) A–C in pairs. Feed back on C: students say why (ending or exception?).
5 minExit ticket. Write five things in your schoolbag with un, una, unos, unas. Homework: play Gender Sort at leim.ie/gender-sort.html and copy five lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Errors to listen for: una problema / la día / el mano (exceptions) · el canción · los ciudades · lápizs (lápices) · canciónes (no accent) · examenes (exámenes) · a el / de el (al / del) · no article where Spanish needs one: Me gusta fútbol (el fútbol).
Language awareness: Irish students already know gender: an fear but an bhean. Ask "how do you know bean is feminine in Irish?" It makes the idea feel normal. Support: give weaker students the poster as a mat. Fast finishers: find five nouns in the worksheet email and explain each article.

¿El o la?

EL · UN-o · el libro
-or · el ordenador
-ón · el avión
-ma · el problema
LA · UNA-a · la casa
-ción / -sión · la canción
-dad / -tad · la ciudad
-e or consonant? Learn it!

Exceptions: el día · el mapa · el sofá · el idioma  |  la mano · la foto · la moto · la radio

thea / some
masculineel · losun · unos
femininela · lasuna · unas

vowel + s · consonant + es · lápiz → lápices · canción → canciones · el lunes → los lunes

Learn it WITH the article: la casa, not casa!

Worksheet

Nerea's new room in Gijón

↗ Léim

A. Read Nerea's email

¡Hola, Aisling! ¿Qué tal? Te escribo desde mi casa nueva en Gijón, una ciudad en el norte de España, al lado del mar. El piso es pequeño, pero tiene unas vistas preciosas: desde la ventana de mi habitación veo la playa de San Lorenzo. En mi habitación hay una cama, un escritorio, dos estanterías llenas de libros y un sofá azul. Todavía hay unas cajas en el suelo, ¡qué desastre! Mi hermano pequeño tiene la habitación al lado de la mía; tiene tres peces en un acuario y muchos peluches. Mañana empiezo en el instituto nuevo. Tengo un problema: no conozco a nadie y estoy un poco nerviosa. Mis padres dicen que las clases aquí son muy buenas. ¿Y tú? ¿Qué hay en tu habitación? Mándame una foto. Un abrazo, Nerea
  1. Where is Gijón?
  2. What is the flat like?
  3. What can Nerea see from her bedroom window?
  4. Name four things in her room.
  5. Why does she say "¡qué desastre!"?
  6. Where is her little brother's room, and what does he have?
  7. What happens tomorrow? How does she feel, and why?
  8. What does she ask Aisling to do?

B. Find the Spanish in the email (with the article)

  1. some boxes
  2. a problem
  1. the window
  2. three fish

C. Fill in el, la, un, una, unos or unas

Mi habitación es pequeña. Hay (13, a) ______ cama y (14, a) ______ armario. (15, the) ______ ventana es grande. En (16, the) ______ estantería tengo (17, some) ______ libros y (18, some) ______ fotos de mis amigos. Mi gata siempre duerme en (19, the) ______ sofá. ¡Tiene (20, a) ______ vida muy fácil!

D. Now you

Write Aisling's reply to Nerea (50–70 words). Answer her question: what's in your room? Use at least six nouns with el, la, un, una, unos or unas.

Answers. A: 1 In the north of Spain, beside the sea. 2 Small, but it has lovely views. 3 San Lorenzo beach. 4 Any four: a bed, a desk, two bookshelves full of books, a blue sofa. 5 There are still boxes on the floor. 6 Next to hers; three fish in an aquarium and lots of soft toys. 7 She starts at her new school; a bit nervous, because she doesn't know anyone. 8 Send her a photo. · B: 9 unas cajas 10 un problema 11 la ventana 12 tres peces. · C: 13 una 14 un 15 La 16 la 17 unos 18 unas 19 el 20 una.
Watch out: el piso = the flat; las vistas = the views; el escritorio = the desk; la estantería = the bookshelf; los peluches = soft toys; la mía = mine (feminine, because it means my room). Un problema: masculine, even though it ends in -a.
