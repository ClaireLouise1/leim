Pythagoras' theorem

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): GT.3b, GT.3e, U.6. Play the game: leim.ie/ladder-lab.html

The hypotenuse c is opposite the right angle: c² = a² + b²
3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5²
Isosceles: use HALF the base. x² = 13² − 5², x = 12 cm

1. The theorem (Theorem 14)

In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle, always the longest) is the sum of the squares of the other two sides: c² = a² + b².

2. Finding a side

Hypotenuse: ADD
5² + 12² = 169
√169 = 13
Shorter side: SUBTRACT
13² − 5² = 144
√144 = 12
Then √
√52 = 7.21… = 7.2 (1 d.p.)

Sense check: the hypotenuse is longer than both other sides; a shorter side is shorter than the hypotenuse.

3. Square roots

Know 1² … 15². √50 lies between 7 (7² = 49) and 8 (8² = 64).

4. Is it right-angled? (Theorem 15)

If (longest side)² = the sum of the squares of the other two, the triangle IS right-angled, and the right angle is opposite the longest side. 8, 15, 17: 64 + 225 = 289 = 17², yes. 6, 9, 11: 36 + 81 = 117 ≠ 121, no.

Pythagorean triples: 3, 4, 5 · 5, 12, 13 · 8, 15, 17 · 7, 24, 25 · 20, 21, 29, and their multiples (6, 8, 10).

5. Real life

Ladder = hypotenuse · diagonal of a rectangle = hypotenuse · north then east: the distance back is the hypotenuse · builder's check: 3 m, 4 m, gap 5 m.

Classic mistakes: adding the sides (5 + 12) instead of the squares · forgetting the √ · adding when you need a shorter side · the whole base of an isosceles triangle instead of half · rounding too early.
Exam tip: write the formula with the numbers in it (x² = 6.5² − 2.5²), round only at the end, and give the units.
