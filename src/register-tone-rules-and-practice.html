1. Register: who is it for?

Register is how formal your words are. It changes with your reader and your purpose.

FormalInformal
a principal, a manager, an editor, the council, an audience, judges, strangersfriends, family, your team chat
I am, I cannot, I would likeI'm, can't, I'd love
I wish to complain about…It's a joke!
no slang, no "!!"slang and "!" are fine

2. Same news, two readers

Text to a friendMatch's off Saturday, pitch is flooded. Gutted!!
Email to the principalI am writing to let you know that Saturday's match has been cancelled because the pitch is flooded.

The facts stay the same. The words change.

3. Polite or blunt?

Blunt (an order)Polite (a question)
Give me that book.Could you pass me that book, please?
Turn the TV down.Would you mind turning the TV down?
Move!Excuse me, could I get past?
Watch out: "Move, please!" is still an order. Polite tools: Could you…? Would you mind…? Excuse me · Sorry · Thank you.

4. Tone: the feeling in the words

Cheerfulbrilliant, thrilled, can't wait
Sadmiss, lonely, tears, goodbye
Angryfurious, fed up, unacceptable
Worriedwhat if, nervous, anxious, afraid

5. Slang to swap in formal writing

deadly / epic → excellent · loads → many · wrecked → exhausted · gonna → going to · asap → as soon as possible
Exam tip: every writing task names a reader and a purpose. Underline them before you write. For a tone question: name the tone, quote the words that create it, and explain.

F. Exam-style

  1. The school disco has been cancelled. Write it as (a) a message to your class group chat and (b) a notice for the school website. Then name two differences.
  2. What is the tone of this line? Quote two words that show it: "I'm fed up with this. Three times the bin wasn't collected, and nobody answers the phone. It's ridiculous."

Answers

A1. Formal (email to the county council) · 2. Informal (text to your friend) · 3. Formal (letter to the company) · 4. Informal (call to gran) · 5. Informal (chat with your brother) · 6. Formal (radio announcement for listeners)
(Any reader of the right kind is fine.)
B7. Polite · 8. Polite · 9. Polite · 10. Blunt · 11. Blunt · 12. Blunt
C13. Sorry, would you mind not kicking my chair?
14. Could I have a glass of water, please?
15. Would you mind putting your phone away?
(Other polite versions are fine: a question, softening words, no "!" or "now".)
D16. Sad (empty) · 17. Cheerful (Great news) · 18. Angry (Never again) · 19. Worried (keep thinking) · 20. Sad (lonely) · 21. Worried (anxious) · 22. Cheerful (Hooray) · 23. Angry (furious)
E24. loads → many · 25. epic → very enjoyable · 26. savage → excellent · 27. tons → thousands
F28. Sample: (a) Disco's off Friday, the hall's flooded. Gutted!! (b) Please note that Friday's school disco has been cancelled because the hall is flooded. Tickets will be refunded. Differences: short forms and slang in (a); full words and extra details (refunds) in (b).
29. Angry: "fed up" and "ridiculous" show the writer is annoyed; "Three times" shows it keeps happening.
