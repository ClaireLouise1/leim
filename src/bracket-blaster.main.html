Expanding & simplifying

Spot the pattern

Here are some double brackets, multiplied out. Look at the numbers in the brackets and the numbers in the answer:

  • (x + 2)(x + 3) = x² + 5x + 6
  • (x + 4)(x + 1) = x² + 5x + 4
  • (x + 5)(x + 2) = x² + 7x + 10
  • (x + 6)(x − 2) = x² + 4x − 12

Where do the middle number and the last number come from? Can you write (x + 7)(x + 3) straight away? And what about (x + 3)²: is it x² + 9?

Show me

The last number is the two numbers multiplied (2 × 3 = 6, 6 × −2 = −12). The middle number is the two numbers added (2 + 3 = 5, 6 + −2 = 4). So (x + 7)(x + 3) = x² + 10x + 21. And (x + 3)² = (x + 3)(x + 3) = x² + 6x + 9, not x² + 9: the middle term 6x is the one people forget. Check with x = 1: (1 + 3)² = 16, and 1 + 6 + 9 = 16 ✓, but 1 + 9 = 10 ✗.

Multiplying terms

  • Numbers × numbers, letters × letters: 3x × 4x = 12x²; 2a × 5b = 10ab; −2x × 3 = −6x.
  • A letter times itself makes a power: x × x = x², x² × x = x³ (add the powers: 2 + 1 = 3).
  • Signs: same signs give +, different signs give −: (−3) × (−4) = +12.

One bracket: multiply EVERY term inside

  • 3(x − 4) = 3 × x + 3 × (−4) = 3x − 12. Not 3x − 4: the 3 multiplies both terms.
  • −2(x − 5) = −2x + 10. Minus × minus = plus.
  • x(x + 3) = x² + 3x. 2x(3x − 1) = 6x² − 2x.
  • −(x − 6) = −x + 6. A minus in front of a bracket changes the sign of every term inside.

Expand, then simplify

Expand each bracket first, then collect like terms.

3(x + 2) − 2(x − 4)
= 3x + 6 − 2x + 8   (−2 × −4 = +8)
= x + 14

Double brackets: every term × every term

Draw a box (the area model): one bracket along the top, the other down the side. Fill each box with row × column, then add the boxes.

(x + 3)(x − 5) = x² − 5x + 3x − 15 = x² − 2x − 15

  • With letters in both brackets: (2x + 1)(x − 4) = 2x² − 8x + x − 4 = 2x² − 7x − 4.
  • Different letters: (a + 2)(b − 3) = ab − 3a + 2b − 6. No like terms, so 4 terms stay.
  • The area of a rectangle with sides (x + 5) and (x + 2) is (x + 5)(x + 2) = x² + 7x + 10.

Squares and special products

  • (x + 4)² = (x + 4)(x + 4) = x² + 4x + 4x + 16 = x² + 8x + 16.
  • (x − 3)² = x² − 3x − 3x + 9 = x² − 6x + 9. The last term is +9: (−3) × (−3).
  • (2x + 5)² = 4x² + 10x + 10x + 25 = 4x² + 20x + 25.
  • (x + 7)(x − 7) = x² − 7x + 7x − 49 = x² − 49. The middle terms cancel: a difference of two squares.
Higher Level: a bracket with three terms works the same way. (x + 2)(x² − x + 3) = x³ − x² + 3x + 2x² − 2x + 6 = x³ + x² + x + 6. Each term of the first bracket multiplies all three terms of the second.
The classic mistakes: 3(x + 4) = 3x + 4 (the 3 must multiply both), −2(x − 5) = −2x − 10 (minus × minus = plus), and (x + 3)² = x² + 9 (the middle term is missing). Check with a number: put x = 1 into the question and into your answer. They must give the same value.

Factorising (putting the brackets back) comes later, in Algebra 3. Dividing and algebraic fractions come in Algebra 4.

Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

"Expand and simplify" is one of the most common short parts in the paper, often as a first part worth a few easy marks. Expanding also turns up inside bigger questions: the area of a rectangle with sides like (x + 5) and (x − 2), showing two expressions are equal, and as the first step in solving equations. A sign slip early on carries through the whole question.

How marks are won and lost

  • Multiply every term in the bracket, not just the first one.
  • Watch the sign in front of the bracket: −3(x − 2) = −3x + 6.
  • Write (x + 3)² as (x + 3)(x + 3) before you start. It stops you writing x² + 9.
  • Show the line before you collect (all four terms of a double bracket): it can earn marks even if you slip later.
  • Check with x = 1 or x = 2: the question and your answer must give the same number.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first.

  1. Expand and simplify. (a) 4(2x − 3) − 3(x − 5)  (b) (x + 4)(x − 6)  (c) Higher Level: (x + 2)(x² − x + 3)
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 8x − 12 − 3x + 15 = 5x + 3 (−3 × −5 = +15).
    (b) x² − 6x + 4x − 24 = x² − 2x − 24.
    (c) x³ − x² + 3x + 2x² − 2x + 6 = x³ + x² + x + 6.

  2. The patio. A rectangular patio is 2x + 3 metres long and x − 1 metres wide. (a) Write the area of the patio without brackets. (b) Write the perimeter in its simplest form. (c) Find the area when x = 5, using both the bracket form and your answer to (a).
    Show the worked answer

    (a) (2x + 3)(x − 1) = 2x² − 2x + 3x − 3 = 2x² + x − 3 m².
    (b) 2(2x + 3) + 2(x − 1) = 4x + 6 + 2x − 2 = 6x + 4 m.
    (c) Brackets: (13)(4) = 52. From (a): 2(25) + 5 − 3 = 52. Both give 52 m² ✓.

  3. Consecutive squares. (a) Show that (n + 1)² − n² = 2n + 1. (b) Use this to work out 51² − 50² without a calculator.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) (n + 1)² = (n + 1)(n + 1) = n² + 2n + 1. Take away n²: n² + 2n + 1 − n² = 2n + 1 ✓.
    (b) Here n = 50, so 51² − 50² = 2(50) + 1 = 101.

  4. Spot the mistake. Seán wrote (x − 3)² = x² − 9. (a) Show that he is wrong by putting in x = 5. (b) Write the correct expansion.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) (5 − 3)² = 2² = 4, but 5² − 9 = 16. They aren't equal, so he is wrong.
    (b) (x − 3)(x − 3) = x² − 3x − 3x + 9 = x² − 6x + 9. Check: 25 − 30 + 9 = 4 ✓.
