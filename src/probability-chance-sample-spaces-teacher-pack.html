Probability: chance & sample spaces

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): SP.1a, SP.2a, SP.2b (relative frequency, experiments and the fundamental principle of counting are taught later)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can place events on a 0–1 probability scale, use the words impossible, unlikely, even chance, likely and certain, find probabilities of equally likely outcomes, use P(not A) = 1 − P(A), and list sample spaces systematically with lists, two-way tables and tree diagrams.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Probability Line-Up cards + Fair Game? cards · p.3 Probability Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: Would you rather? "I roll a die. You win €1 if it's a 6, or €1 if it's a prime number. Which bet do you take?" Vote, then ask how they could be sure. Elicit "count the outcomes".
12 minProbability Line-Up (p.2 cards; one each). Stretch a string or draw a long line on the board from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain). Students stand (or stick their card) where their event belongs, then justify it with a number. Equal probabilities stand together. Check against the key. Feedback: which cards share a place? Why is CORK's vowel at 1/4?
8 minGuided discovery: two coins. Board "Toss two coins. P(one head and one tail)?" Many say 1/3 (HH, HT, TT). Hold up a 1c and a 2c coin: is "1c head, 2c tail" the same as "1c tail, 2c head"? Pairs list the outcomes in order: HH, HT, TH, TT, so the answer is 2/4 = 1/2.
12 minFair Game? (p.2, pairs, one game card each). Fill in a two-way table for two dice, count the cells for A and for B and decide whether the game is fair. Pairs who finish swap cards. Feedback: which games are fair? How would you fix the others?
10 minProbability Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left and build the chain to FINISH. Answer chain below.
10 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name the rule, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone.
3 minExit ticket. A bag has 4 red and 6 blue counters: P(blue)? P(not blue)? (3/5; 2/5). Homework: play Spin & Predict at leim.ie/spin-and-predict.html (Tables & trees round) and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.

Errors to look for: dividing by "the rest" (3 red, 5 blue → 3/5) · HT and TH counted once · counting each repeated letter once · answers as "3 out of 8" or ratios · treating the 11 totals of two dice as equally likely · probabilities above 1. Fast finishers: invent your own fair game for two dice, then explain why 7 is the most likely total.

Probability

P(event) = outcomes that fit ÷ total outcomes

"Not"P(not A) = 1 − P(A)
P(rain) = 0.3
P(no rain) = 0.7
Sample spaceevery possible outcome
Two coins: HH, HT, TH, TT
HT ≠ TH!
Two-way tabletwo dice: 6 × 6 = 36 cells
P(total 7) = 6/36 = 1/6
Tree diagramcoin, then spinner R, B, G
2 × 3 = 6 outcomes
P(H and G) = 1/6

Divide by the total, list outcomes in order, and check: 0 ≤ P ≤ 1.

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. A spinner has 8 equal sectors: 3 red, 4 blue and 1 green. Find (a) P(red) (b) P(not blue) (c) P(yellow).
  2. Cards numbered 1 to 30 are shuffled and one is picked. Find (a) P(a multiple of 5) (b) P(a square number).
  3. A coin is tossed and a die is rolled. (a) List the sample space. (b) Find P(a tail and an odd number).
  4. A bag has 40 counters. The probability of picking a blue counter is 0.35. How many counters are blue? How many are not blue?
  5. Two dice are rolled and the smaller score is taken from the bigger one. (a) Draw a two-way table. (b) Find P(the difference is 0). (c) Which difference is most likely? (d) Find P(a difference of 3 or more).
For the teacher

Answers

↗ Léim

Fair Game?

Game 1: A 16, B 20: not fair: B is more likely to win · Game 2: A 18, B 18: fair · Game 3: A 27, B 9: not fair: A is more likely to win · Game 4: A 15, B 15: fair. Possible fix for Game 1: A wins with a total of 2, 6, 7, 8 or 12 (18 cells each). Game 3: any rule that gives each player 18 cells.

A. Error Hunt

3 red and 5 blue counters: P(red) = 3/5Divided by the rest, not the total: 3/8
Toss two coins: the sample space = {HH, HT, TT}TH is missing (HT and TH are different): {HH, HT, TH, TT}
P(rain) = 0.3. P(no rain)? = 0.6P(not) = 1 − P: 1 − 0.3 = 0.7
Roll a die: P(an even number) = 3That's the number of outcomes, not a probability: 3/6 = 1/2
Letters of TIPPERARY: P(P) = 1/7Counted each different letter once. TIPPERARY has 9 letters and 2 are P: 2/9
Two dice: P(a total of 4) = 1/11The 11 totals aren't equally likely. Use the table: 3 of 36 cells, 1/12

B. Exam-style questions

  1. (a) 3/8 (b) 4 of 8: 1/2 (c) 0 (impossible)
  2. (a) 5, 10, …, 30: 6 of 30 = 1/5 (b) 1, 4, 9, 16, 25: 5 of 30 = 1/6
  3. (a) H1, H2, H3, H4, H5, H6, T1, T2, T3, T4, T5, T6 (12) (b) T1, T3, T5: 3 of 12 = 1/4
  4. 40 × 0.35 = 14 blue; 40 − 14 = 26 not blue
  5. (a) 36 cells (b) 6 of 36 = 1/6 (c) 1 (10 cells) (d) 3, 4, 5: 6 + 4 + 2 = 12 cells, 1/3

Marking tip

Give credit for each step, as the exam does: a correct list or table with one miscount still earns most of the marks. Insist on probabilities written as fractions, decimals or percentages (never "3 out of 8" or a ratio), and on fractions in their simplest form when the question asks for it.
