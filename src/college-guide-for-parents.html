The big picture in one page

↗ Léim

Most school leavers in Ireland apply to college through the CAO (Central Applications Office). Your child lists the courses they want in order of preference, sits the Leaving Cert, and is offered the highest course on their list that their points (and subjects) qualify them for.

1. Points

Each Leaving Cert grade is worth points. The best six subjects are added up. The maximum is 625.

2. Requirements

Points alone aren't enough. Every college has minimum entry requirements, and many courses need particular subjects.

3. Dates

Apply by 1 February of 6th year. Final choices can be changed up to 1 July. Offers come out in late August.

Three things many parents only find out too late

Subject choices made in 3rd year can close doors. Dropping a foreign language, or a science, or taking ordinary-level Maths can rule your child out of some courses, no matter how many points they get. See page 6.
Points for a course aren't set in advance. The "points" for a course are simply the score of the last person offered a place that year. They go up and down with demand, so last year's figure is a guide, not a promise.
The CAO isn't the only way in. Reduced-points places (HEAR and DARE), further education (PLC) courses, apprenticeships, the new tertiary degrees and mature entry all lead to college qualifications. See pages 7 and 8.

Section 1

How CAO points work

↗ Léim

Leaving Cert grades run from H1 to H8 at higher level and O1 to O8 at ordinary level. Each grade is a 10% band (H1 = 90–100%, H2 = 80–89%, and so on). This is the official CAO points scale, used since 2017:

MarkHigher levelPointsOrdinary levelPoints
90–100%H1100O156
80–89%H288O246
70–79%H377O337
60–69%H466O428
50–59%H556O520
40–49%H646O612
30–39%H737O70
0–29%H80O80

The rules in plain English

  • Best six subjects from one sitting of the Leaving Cert are added up. A 7th or 8th subject is a safety net, not extra points.
  • A H5 is worth the same as an O1 (56). Higher level pays off even with a middling grade.
  • A H7 (30–39%) still earns 37 points, so trying higher level is low-risk.
  • Leaving Cert Vocational Programme (LCVP) Link Modules can count as a subject: Distinction 66, Merit 46, Pass 28.

The higher-level Maths bonus

A H6 or better in higher-level Maths (40%+) earns 25 bonus points. A H1 in higher Maths is worth 125; a H6 is worth 71.

The bonus only counts if Maths is one of the best six subjects (it usually is). It's why the maximum is 625, not 600.

There's ongoing debate about changing the bonus, but it still applies for 2027 entry.

Worked example

Aoife sits seven subjects. Her results: English H2 (88), Irish H3 (77), higher Maths H4 (66 + 25 = 91), Spanish H3 (77), Biology H5 (56), Geography O1 (56), Business H6 (46).

Best six: 91 + 88 + 77 + 77 + 56 + 56 = 445 points. Business (46) is her 7th subject, so it isn't counted.

Section 1 (continued)

Ranking courses, offers and rounds

↗ Léim

How the course list works

Your child can list up to 10 Level 8 courses (honours degrees) and 10 Level 6/7 courses (higher certificates and ordinary degrees). The two lists are separate, so they can get one offer from each.

The golden rule: list courses in order of genuine preference. Don't leave a course off, or put it lower down, because you think the points will be too high. If they don't get it, the CAO simply moves on to the next course. Putting a "safe" course first only means they might get the safe course and miss the one they really wanted.

What "the points" for a course means

  • The CAO fills each course by points, highest first. The cut-off points are the score of the last person offered a place.
  • They change every year with the number of applicants and places. Use the last 2–3 years as a rough guide.
  • An asterisk (*) next to the points means not everyone on that score got a place: some were chosen by random selection.
  • Some courses add marks from a test, portfolio, interview or audition (e.g. the HPAT for undergraduate Medicine). These "restricted" courses usually must be on the CAO list by 1 February.

Offers and rounds

RoundWho it's for
Round A (early July)Mature applicants and some other non-school-leaver groups.
Round Zero (early Aug)Mainly applicants with further education (QQI Level 5/6) awards, and more mature applicants.
Round One (late Aug)Most Leaving Cert students: offers come a few days after results. In 2026, results came out on 21 August and Round One offers on 26 August.
Round Two and laterPlaces that weren't accepted are offered on, so points can drop a little. If your child accepts an offer and later gets a higher preference, they can still take the new one.

Offers must be accepted online by the deadline (usually within a week). A missed deadline means losing the offer.

The Leaving Cert is changing. Under Senior Cycle reform, every subject is being rewritten, and each new subject has an Additional Assessment Component (a project, investigation or practical) worth at least 40% of the marks, done during the two years. The first redeveloped subjects (including Biology, Chemistry, Physics and Business) started in September 2025 and are examined for the first time in 2027. The new Maths and English courses have been delayed until at least 2028. More subjects follow each year. Ask your child's school which of their subjects are on the new course. The CAO points scale itself hasn't changed.

Section 3

Minimum entry requirements

↗ Léim

To be offered a place, your child must have enough points and meet the entry requirements. There are two layers:

1. College minimum ("matriculation")

The basic standard for any course at that college. For example, the NUI universities (UCC, UCD, University of Galway, Maynooth) ask for six subjects, with at least two H5s and four at O6/H7, and English and Irish among them. Many technological universities ask for five or six passes (O6/H7), usually including Maths and English or Irish.

2. Course requirements

Extra subjects or grades for a particular course, e.g. a third language, a laboratory science (Biology, Chemistry, Physics…), or a set grade in higher-level Maths. Engineering at UCC, for example, asks for H4 in higher Maths; Dentistry and Pharmacy ask for H4 in Chemistry and in Physics or Biology.

The NUI "third language" rule

At the four NUI universities, many courses need a third language (a language as well as English and Irish, e.g. Spanish, French, German) among the six subjects. Examples from NUI's published rules:

Course area (NUI universities)Language needed as well as English and Irish
Arts, Human Sciences, LawA third language
CommerceA third language and Maths
Social ScienceA third language at UCC, Galway and Maynooth (not at UCD, which asks for Maths instead)
Science, Engineering, NursingUsually no third language, but often Maths and/or a lab science

Rules differ by college and course and can change: always check the course page and nui.ie.

Why this matters in Junior Cycle. Leaving Cert subjects and levels are usually chosen in 3rd year or Transition Year, and schools often use Junior Cycle results to decide who takes higher level. A 14-year-old who drops their foreign language after Junior Cycle can find, at 17, that Arts, Law or Commerce at an NUI university is closed to them. Keeping a language, a science and the highest Maths level they can manage keeps the most doors open.

Exemptions from Irish or a third language

Some students can be exempt, for example if they were born outside the Republic of Ireland, received much of their education abroad, or have a specific learning difficulty (such as dyslexia) or another condition affecting language learning. For NUI universities, apply to NUI for the exemption (exemptions.nui.ie) and tick the box on the CAO form. An exemption from studying Irish in school doesn't count automatically: your child still has to apply to NUI, so check early. Some courses (e.g. primary teaching) need Irish whatever the exemption.

Section 4

Other ways in (1): HEAR and DARE

↗ Léim

HEAR and DARE are national access schemes run through the CAO. Colleges keep places for eligible applicants at reduced points, and offer extra support once they start. Your child must still meet the minimum entry and subject requirements. Both are for school leavers under 23 (on 1 January of the year they start). Find out more at accesscollege.ie.

HEAR: Higher Education Access Route

For school leavers from backgrounds that are under-represented in college. Applicants must meet a low-income limit and are then scored on:

  • a medical card or GP visit card in the family
  • a means-tested social welfare payment for 26+ weeks
  • living in an area of concentrated disadvantage
  • five years in a DEIS secondary school

Care-experienced, Traveller and Roma applicants and young parents are priority groups.

DARE: Disability Access Route to Education

For school leavers whose disability has had a negative effect on their second-level education: for example autism, ADHD, dyslexia, a mental health condition, a physical or sensory disability, or a significant ongoing illness.

They need both evidence of the disability (a form or report from a professional) and an Educational Impact Statement completed with the school.

Even students who aren't eligible for DARE can get disability supports in college.

How to apply (both schemes)

By 1 FebruaryApply to the CAO and say yes to HEAR and/or DARE.
By 1 MarchComplete the online form in the CAO account.
By 10 MarchPost the supporting documents (signed and stamped) to the CAO.
Late JuneThe outcome is emailed. Eligible students compete for reduced-points places in Round One.
Start gathering documents in the autumn of 6th year. Reports, school forms and income documents take time, and late documents aren't accepted. Your child's guidance counsellor can help.
Section 4 (continued)

Other ways in (2): more routes to a degree

↗ Léim

PLC courses → college (QQI Level 5 or 6)

Post-Leaving Cert (PLC) courses run in colleges of further education, usually for one year. A full QQI Level 5 or 6 award can be used to apply through the CAO under the Higher Education Links Scheme. Results are scored to a maximum of 390 points, and colleges keep places for these applicants, including on some high-points courses. A PLC can also be a way to try a subject area first, or to get around a missing subject requirement where the course accepts a linked award. Apply directly to the further education college (most open applications in spring).

Tertiary degrees: start local, finish at university

A newer route: a full bachelor's degree that starts in a local further education college and finishes in a university or technological university. No CAO points are needed and there are no fees for the first year or two. There are 78 for 2026/27 (e.g. Business, IT, Nursing, Occupational Therapy, Agricultural Science and a new Primary Teaching route). Apply through nto.hea.ie or fetchcourses.ie, not the CAO.

Apprenticeships: earn while you learn

There are 87 apprenticeships in Ireland, from electrician and chef to accounting, software, insurance and paramedic, leading to qualifications up to degree level and beyond. The apprentice is employed and paid, with time in college as well. The key step is getting hired by an approved employer: jobs are listed on apprenticeship.ie. Entry rules vary. Craft trades start at 16 with Junior Cycle results; many newer apprenticeships ask for five Leaving Cert passes including Maths and English or Irish.

Access and foundation courses

Many colleges run free or low-cost access programmes (often one year) that prepare students for degree study and can lead to a reserved place. Check the "access" pages of the colleges near you.

Mature entry

Anyone who is 23 or older on 1 January of the year they start can apply as a mature student through the CAO. Places are offered on experience, motivation and interview or test, not just Leaving Cert points. It's worth knowing: missing out at 18 doesn't close college for good.

The bottom line: if your child doesn't get the points they hoped for, there is almost always another route to the same career. A good guidance counsellor can map it out with them.
Section 5

What to do when: 2nd year to 6th year

↗ Léim

Stick this on the fridge. Tick as you go.

2nd year
  • Chat about what they enjoy and are good at. No decisions yet, just curiosity.
  • Try a careers interest quiz together on careersportal.ie.
  • Notice which subjects feel like "keepers" (a language, a science, Maths level).
3rd year
  • Before Leaving Cert subject choices: look up 3–4 courses they might like on qualifax.ie or college sites and check the required subjects.
  • Think hard before dropping a foreign language (NUI third language rule, page 6) or all the sciences.
  • Aim for higher level in Junior Cycle Maths, English and Irish where possible: it keeps higher level open.
  • If your child may need an exemption from Irish, find out how it works now.
  • Decide on Transition Year.
TY / 5th year
  • Use work experience to test-drive careers.
  • Go to college open days (autumn and spring) and CAO/careers fairs.
  • Watch levels: switching to ordinary level is easy to do but can close courses.
  • If HEAR or DARE might apply, read accesscollege.ie now.
6th year: autumn
  • CAO opens 5 November: register early (it's only €35 before 20 January).
  • Start gathering HEAR/DARE documents. Book any HPAT, portfolio or audition.
  • Look at PLC courses, tertiary degrees and apprenticeships as real options, not just back-ups.
6th year: Jan–Mar
  • 20 Jan discounted fee · 1 Feb CAO closes (tick HEAR/DARE, list restricted courses).
  • 1 Mar HEAR/DARE forms · 10 Mar documents to the CAO.
  • Apply to PLC colleges and apprenticeship employers if interested.
6th year: May–July
  • Use the free Change of Mind to put courses in genuine order of preference.
  • Check the list is final before 1 July at 5pm.
  • Apply for a SUSI grant if eligible.
August
  • Results day: celebrate whatever happens, then look at the options calmly.
  • Accept the offer online by the deadline. Watch for Round Two.
  • No offer? Check available places on cao.ie, PLCs and apprenticeships.
