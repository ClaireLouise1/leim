Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

Reading for meaning

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R1, R2, R3, OL4

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can skim a text for its main idea, scan for a fact, infer a feeling from clues, tell fact from opinion, and back up a point with a short quote (point, quote, explain).

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Proof Match cards (mingle) · p.3 Scan Swap A/B cards (pairs) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Answer + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (useful revision in 2nd and 3rd). Time: about 60 minutes. All texts are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Board three questions about the storm text (poster or game): When did the storm pass? How does Rua feel? Best title? Pairs: which needs one word, which needs a clue, which needs the whole text? Elicit scan, infer, skim.
8 minGuided discovery. Show a point with no quote and a point with a whole-paragraph quote. Which is better? Neither: build point + short quote + explain together.
12 minProof Match (p.2, mingle). Hand out one card each (24 cards: 12 points, 12 proofs). Students read their card aloud to each other and find the partner card. Pairs then explain the link in one sentence ("Shaking shows she's scared").
12 minScan Swap (p.3, pairs, A/B). Each student has one text and questions about the partner's text. They take turns asking; the partner scans and reads the answer aloud. No peeking at each other's card.
18 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Answer in pairs (what's missing? improve it), then the exam-style questions alone.
5 minExit ticket. One point + quote + explain about Aunt Gráinne. Homework: play Clue Hunt at leim.ie/clue-hunt.html.
Proof Match pairs (point–proof): 17–18 · 19–9 · 6–16 · 11–24 · 7–20 · 2–10 · 14–5 · 3–15 · 1–4 · 12–8 · 13–23 · 22–21.
Errors to look for: points with no proof · long copied quotes · quotes with no explanation · grabbing a word from the wrong line (the trap) · treating "lots of people think so" as a fact. Support: the poster (p.4) on the desk. Fast finishers: write a 4-line text of their own with one hidden feeling for a partner to infer.

B. Exam-style questions

The Jump
Every New Year's Day, hundreds of people gather at the Forty Foot in Dublin for a swim. The sea is usually around nine or ten degrees. This year, my aunt Gráinne dragged me along. Standing on the steps, I couldn't feel my toes, and my teeth were chattering. “Just jump!” she shouted, already laughing in the water. I counted to three, held my nose and leapt. For one second I couldn't breathe. Then I surfaced, gasping and grinning, and I understood why people come back every year.
  1. Find two facts about the New Year's Day swim.
  2. How does the writer feel on the steps? Support your answer with reference to the text.
  3. What impression do you get of Aunt Gráinne? Point, quote, explain.
  4. How do the writer's feelings change by the end? Quote to show the change.
  5. Suggest a better title for this text and explain why it fits the whole text.

A. Fix the Answer

Rua is scared.No proof. Add a quote: “her hands started shaking”.
Rua is scared because “Every time the roof rattled, her hands started shaking. At dawn the storm passed, and the beach was covered in seaweed.”Quote far too long. Keep 3–6 words: “her hands started shaking”.
Rua is scared: “her hands started shaking”.No explanation. Add: shaking hands are a sign of fear.
The camp costs €10.Trap: 10% is the discount. Line 3: “€95 a week”.
“Pizza is delicious” is a fact because lots of people like pizza.It's an opinion: it can't be checked. Liking something is an opinion.
Two reasons Aoife was late: she was on her phone.Only one reason. Add a second: she got on the wrong bus / went 20 minutes the wrong way.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. Any two: it happens every New Year's Day · it's at the Forty Foot in Dublin · hundreds of people take part · the sea is around nine or ten degrees.
  2. Cold and nervous: “I couldn't feel my toes” and “my teeth were chattering”; chattering teeth show cold and fear.
  3. e.g. She is bold and fun-loving: she “dragged me along” and was “already laughing in the water”, so she isn't afraid of the cold and enjoys it.
  4. From scared and frozen (“teeth were chattering”, “couldn't breathe”) to delighted (“gasping and grinning”); now the writer understands “why people come back”.
  5. e.g. “My First New Year's Swim” or “Just Jump!”: it covers the build-up, the jump and the happy ending, not just one detail.

Marking tip

Credit a clear point in the student's own words, a short accurate quote, and a sentence explaining how the quote proves the point. A point without reference to the text is usually worth about half.
