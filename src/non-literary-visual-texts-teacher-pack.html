Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

Non-literary & Visual Texts

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R3, R9, R12, OL8

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can tell fact from opinion and explain how they know, read a bar chart accurately and quote its numbers, name the parts of a poster or leaflet, and explain how headlines, pictures and layout work on the reader.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Fact or Opinion Sort (24 cards) · p.3 Chart Swap A/B (pair listening task) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Answer + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 3rd year (works from 1st year), Higher and Ordinary. Time: about 60 minutes. All texts and charts are original, with invented brands only.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Bring in (or project) a real local leaflet. Students shout “Fact!” or “Opinion!” as you read lines aloud. Ask: how did you know?
12 minFact or Opinion Sort (p.2, groups of 3–4). Sort the 24 cards into two piles. Then each group turns three opinion cards into facts (“The skate park is far too small” → “The skate park has 6 ramps”) and underlines the judgement words.
15 minChart Swap (p.3, pairs). Each reads their hidden data aloud; the partner draws the bars on the blank grid. Compare, then ask each other the questions on the card.
5 minPoster walk. Show the poster (p.4) or a real one: name each part and what it does.
18 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Answer in pairs, then the exam-style questions on the Riverside Cinema Club leaflet alone.
5 minExit ticket. One fact and one opinion from any advert, with a reason for each. Homework: play Fact or Opinion at leim.ie/fact-or-opinion.html.
Errors to look for: “it's in the leaflet, so it's a fact” · calling a fact an opinion because they disagree with it · reading a bar from the wrong gridline · adding instead of subtracting for “how many more” · naming a feature without saying what it does · no quote or number as evidence. Support: give a list of judgement words. Fast finishers: design a poster for a school event with all five parts, one fact-based line and one opinion line.

A. Fix the Answer

The poster is good because it has a picture.names a feature but not what it does: say what the picture shows and why it was chosen
“The café makes the tastiest scones in Ireland” is a fact because it is in the leaflet.being printed doesn't make it a fact: “tastiest” is a judgement word, so it's an opinion
The chart shows lots of people like PE.no number: “PE was the favourite, with 45 votes”
The headline is big.say what it does: big, bold capitals grab the reader's attention first
“Tickets cost €9 on Tuesdays” is an opinion because I think it's too dear.the line itself can be checked, so it's a fact; your view about it is the opinion
The leaflet is persuasive.no evidence: quote the words or name the feature that persuades (“the friendliest in the Midlands”, the photo)

B. Exam-style questions

  1. Facts: “Every Friday at 7 p.m. in the Riverside Hall”; “Tickets €4, popcorn included”; “30 films a year”. Opinion: “the best night out in Leitrim”.
  2. 45 − 15 = 30 more members.
  3. It is growing every year (15 → 25 → 40 → 45), so the club is popular, which makes a new member trust it.
  4. “FILM NIGHTS ARE BACK!”: capitals and an exclamation mark sound exciting; the photo of teenagers with popcorn shows people the reader's age having fun; the caption makes it a real event.
  5. “It is the best night out in Leitrim, with the comfiest seats in the county!”: “best” and “comfiest” are judgement words; nobody could prove them.

Marking tip

Answers on non-literary and visual texts are rewarded for naming the feature, quoting the words or numbers, and explaining the effect on the reader. Our guide, not an official marking scheme.
