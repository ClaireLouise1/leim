1. Adding endings

Double itshort vowel + one consonant, ending starts with a vowel: hop → hopping, stop → stopped, big → biggest. Two syllables, stress at the end: begin → beginning. (Not w, x, y: snowing, fixing.)
Drop the ebefore a vowel ending: hope → hoping, make → making. Keep it before a consonant: careful, lovely.
y → iconsonant + y: happy → happiness, carry → carried. Keep the y before -ing (crying) and after a vowel (played).
hopping = jumping · hoping = wishing. One letter, a different word.

2. Plurals

+ smost words, vowel + y: days, keys, photos, pianos
+ esafter s, x, ch, sh: buses, boxes, churches, wishes · and potatoes, tomatoes, heroes
y → iesconsonant + y: babies, cities, parties
f → vesknives, leaves, wolves, loaves, shelves
learnchildren, mice, teeth, feet, women, geese, sheep
Never an apostrophe for a plural: potato's → potatoes.

3. Memory tricks

i before e, except after c: believe, friend, receive (weird is weird) · hidden words: definitely, sepa rate, environment · silent letters: knife, island, answer, Wednesday · doubles: necessary, embarrassed, address, tomorrow

4. Commonly confused words

there / their / they'reover there · their boots · they're = they are
your / you'reyour phone · you're = you are
to / too / twogo to school · too hot, me too · two sisters
could havecould have, should have, must have (never “could of”)
where / were / wearwhere is it? · we were late · wear a coat
than / thentaller than me · and then we left
quiet / quitebe quiet · quite hard
lose / looselose the match · a loose tooth
accept / exceptaccept a gift · everyone except me
affect / effectrain affects the match (verb) · special effects (noun)
weather / whethersunny weather · whether to go
past / passedwalked past, half past three · she passed the test
bought / broughtbought = buy (money) · brought = bring
hear / herehear with your ear · come here
know / no · new / knewI know · no milk left · a new bike · I knew it
our / areour teacher · we are going
The swap testSwap in the long form: “they are”, “you are”. If it makes sense, use the short form with the apostrophe. If not, use their / your.

Worked examples

10. They're going to be late again.Swap test: “They are going to be late” makes sense, so the short form they're.
13. I could have won that race.After could, would, should, must, might it's always have. “Could of” is what “could've” sounds like.
27. hop + ing = hoppingShort vowel (o) and one consonant (p): double it. Compare 28: hope ends in e, so drop the e: hoping.
33. cry + ing = cryingUsually consonant + y changes to i, but not before -ing (criing would have two i's). Keep the y.
