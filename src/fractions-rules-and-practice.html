1. Equivalent fractions

Multiply or divide the top and the bottom by the same number: 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 = 4/8. The pieces get smaller but there are more of them.

= =

Simplest form: divide top and bottom by their HCF. 12/18 → HCF 6 → 2/3.

2. Mixed numbers

2 1/3 → 2 × 3 + 1 = 7 → 7/3. Back again: 17/5 → 17 ÷ 5 = 3 rem 2 → 3 2/5.

3. Comparing

Same bottom first, then compare the tops. 3/4 or 5/8? 3/4 = 6/8, so 3/4 is bigger.

4. Add and subtract

Same bottom: add the tops, keep the bottom. 2/9 + 5/9 = 7/9.

Different bottoms: use the LCM. 1/3 + 1/4 = 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12.

Never add the bottoms: 1/2 + 1/3 is not 2/5. It's 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.

Mixed numbers: wholes, then fractions. 2 1/3 + 1 3/4 = 3 + 13/12 = 4 1/12. If a subtraction won't go, use improper fractions: 3 1/5 − 1 3/5 = 16/5 − 8/5 = 1 3/5.

5. Multiply and divide

×tops × tops, bottoms × bottoms: 2/3 × 3/5 = 6/15 = 2/5
÷keep, change, flip: 3/4 ÷ 1/2 = 3/4 × 2/1 = 1 1/2
of÷ bottom, × top: 3/5 of 40 = 40 ÷ 5 × 3 = 24

Mixed numbers? Change to improper fractions first.

Exam tip: show the common denominator (part marks!), finish in simplest form or as a mixed number if asked, and do a sense check: × a fraction less than 1 makes it smaller; ÷ makes it bigger. "2/5 of what is left" means of the remainder, not of the start.

E. Exam-style questions (show your work)

  1. Cian gets €60 for his birthday. He spends 1/4 of it on a book and 2/5 of what is left on a jersey. How much money has he left?
  2. A plank is 4 1/2 m long. How many shelves, each 3/4 m long, can be cut from it?
  3. In a class, 2/5 of the students play GAA, 1/3 play soccer and the rest don't play a sport. (a) What fraction don't play a sport? (b) There are 30 students. How many don't play a sport?
  4. A recipe for 4 people uses 1 2/3 cups of rice. How much rice is needed for 6 people?
