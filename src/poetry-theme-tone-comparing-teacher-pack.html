Poetry 2: theme & mood

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R7, R8, R9, R4, W8

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can name the theme of a short poem (and tell it apart from the topic), name its mood and the words that create it, say where and how a mood turns, and compare two poems on the same theme using point, quote, explain.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Mood Sort cards (cut-up) · p.3 Compare Swap A/B (speaking and listening) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Answer + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 2nd year. Time: about 60 minutes. All poems are written by Léim, so they work alongside whatever poems your class studies.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Read the two sea couplets (free sheet, box 3). Same theme? Same mood? Which words make the difference? Board: theme / mood / tone / topic.
8 minGuided discovery. Read "The Beach" aloud (free sheet, box 4). Where does the mood turn? Which word signals it? Build a list of turn words (But, Then, Now, Until).
12 minMood Sort (p.2, groups of 3, one set of 24 cards). Sort the couplets into six moods and underline the one word that sets each mood. Groups that finish early rank the cards in each pile from strongest to weakest.
12 minCompare Swap (p.3, pairs). A reads two pairs of short poems; B fills the grid (shared theme, mood of each, a word that proves it). Then A reads a poem with a turn; B names the turn word and the change. Swap.
18 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Answer in pairs, then the exam-style questions on "The Kitchen at Christmas" and "The Kitchen in March" alone.
5 minExit ticket. Write one sentence comparing two poems from your anthology using "both" and "whereas". Homework: play Mood Match at leim.ie/mood-match.html.
Errors to look for: naming the topic instead of the theme · "happy / sad" with no quote · retelling the poem line by line · comparing by writing about one poem, then the other, with no linking words · quoting a whole stanza instead of a word or phrase · confusing sad and nostalgic (nostalgic looks back fondly). Support: the poster (p.4) on the desk. Fast finishers: rewrite one Mood Sort card with a different mood word.

B. Exam-style questions

A · The Kitchen at Christmas
Every Christmas Eve the kitchen fills,
cousins squashed around the table,
Gran's laughter over the radio,
the windows steamed and gold.
B · The Kitchen in March
I remember the noise, the crowded table.
Now Gran's chair is pushed against the wall
and the kitchen is quiet,
just the clock and the kettle.
  1. What theme do both poems share?
  2. What is the mood of poem A? Support your answer with two quotations.
  3. Where does the mood change in poem B, and how? Refer to the poem.
  4. Pick one word from poem B and explain how it creates a feeling.
  5. Which poem do you prefer? Compare both poems in your answer.

Compare Swap

A1 growing up: A tense (trembling), B nostalgic (remember) · A2 nature: A peaceful (calm), B angry (raging) · B1 friendship: A joyful (giggled), B angry (glared) · B2 loss: A sad (wept), B joyful (laugh).

Mood turns: A joyful → sad, turn word: But · B angry → joyful, turn word: Then.

A. Fix the Answer

The poem is about a man in a shop.That's the topic, not the theme. Name the big idea: The theme is how places we love change over time.
The mood is happy because it is a happy poem.No evidence. Quote and explain: The mood is joyful: the friends are "laughing at nothing at all", which shows how carefree they feel.
First the poet says the market fills the square. Then he says there are awnings. Then ...Retelling line by line. Make a point about the poem instead: The poet fills the market with movement and sound to make it feel alive.
Poem A is joyful. Poem B is sad.Two statements, not a comparison, and no quotes: Both poems are about a town, but A is joyful ("laughing") whereas B is sad and empty ("nobody rings the bell").
The word "raging" is a good word.Say what the word DOES: "Raging" makes the sea sound like a furious person, which creates an angry, threatening mood.
I like the poem because it is nice.Give a real reason with a reference: I like the poem because the sign that "still says OPEN" on a closed shop is a sad, clever detail.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. Home and place: the family kitchen, and how it changes.
  2. Joyful and warm: "Gran's laughter over the radio", "cousins squashed around the table", "the windows steamed and gold".
  3. Line 1 is nostalgic ("I remember the noise"); the turn comes with "Now": Gran's chair is "pushed against the wall" and the kitchen is quiet. The mood becomes still and empty, suggesting Gran is gone.
  4. E.g. "quiet": after the noise of Christmas, the silence shows how much is missing; "just the clock" makes the time feel slow and lonely.
  5. Both poems are about the same kitchen, but A is joyful and crowded whereas B is quiet and nostalgic. Reward a clear preference, reference to both poems and comparing words.

Marking tip

Reward a clear point, a short accurate quote and an explanation of how the word creates the mood; when comparing, reward answers that discuss both poems together. Our guide, not an official marking scheme.
