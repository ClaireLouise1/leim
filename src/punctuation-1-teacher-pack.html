Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

Punctuation 1

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): W11, W6

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can use capitals, full stops, question and exclamation marks correctly, put commas in lists, after opening words, around extra information and around names, and use apostrophes for missing letters and belonging (including its / it's).

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Fix-It Match mingle cards + Dictation Swap A/B cards · p.3 Punctuation Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (useful revision in 2nd and 3rd). Time: about 60 minutes. All sentences are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Board two lines: “Let's eat, Grandad.” / “Let's eat Grandad.” What's the difference? Elicit that punctuation changes meaning.
8 minGuided discovery. Board “the dog's bone / the dogs' bone / the children's toys”. Pairs: how many owners in each? What's the rule? Then the it is test for its / it's. Quick board review of capitals and the five comma uses.
10 minFix-It Match mingle (p.2, 24 cards = 12 mistakes + 12 corrected versions; one card each). Find your partner, then tell them the rule. Feedback: which rule caught most pairs?
12 minDictation Swap (p.2, pairs, don't show your card). A reads their text aloud twice, pausing at commas; B writes it with all the punctuation. Swap. Pairs compare with the original and score one point per mark.
10 minPunctuation Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the answer to the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left. Build the chain to FINISH.
12 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs, then the exam-style questions alone.
3 minExit ticket. “its the girls first match” (many girls) → It's the girls' first match. Homework: play Comma Catch at leim.ie/comma-catch.html.
Mingle key (mistake card → corrected card): 8 → 18 · 13 → 15 · 21 → 23 · 19 → 14 · 1 → 6 · 4 → 11 · 22 → 9 · 12 → 20 · 2 → 10 · 3 → 5 · 16 → 24 · 7 → 17.
Errors to look for: comma between two sentences · apostrophes in plurals · “childrens'” · it's / its, your / you're · lower-case i and names. Support: the poster (p.4) on the desk. Fast finishers: write one sentence that uses all five comma rules.

B. Exam-style questions

my friend niamh lives in cork her house is beside the river lee on fridays we play football eat pizza and watch films niamhs dog sleeps on the sofa its huge
  1. Rewrite the passage with capitals, full stops, commas and apostrophes.
  2. Why does “niamhs” need an apostrophe? Where does it go?
  3. “its huge”: it's or its? Explain how you decided.
  4. Write a question you would ask Niamh about her dog. Use the right end mark.
  5. Write one sentence about something that belongs to two or more of your friends. Use an apostrophe.

A. Error Hunt

My brothers bike is red. (one brother)One owner: add 's → brother's bike
Whose coming to the match?Who is coming → Who's
The match starts at six?A statement, not a question → full stop: six.
Their going to win.They are going → They're
We need bread milk and eggs.List commas → bread, milk and eggs
I love Summer.Seasons take no capital → summer

B. Exam-style questions

  1. My friend Niamh lives in Cork. Her house is beside the River Lee. On Fridays we play football, eat pizza and watch films. Niamh's dog sleeps on the sofa. It's huge. (A comma after “On Fridays” is fine too.)
  2. The dog belongs to Niamh (one owner), so add 's: Niamh's dog.
  3. It's: “it is huge” makes sense, so it's the short form.
  4. e.g. What is your dog's name? (capital at the start, question mark at the end)
  5. e.g. My friends' bikes were locked outside. (more than one friend → friends')

Marking tip

In students' own writing, punctuation is part of accuracy: look first for full stops between sentences and capitals for names and I, then apostrophes. A paragraph of comma-joined sentences is harder to follow and loses more than a single missing comma.
