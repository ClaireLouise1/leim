Junior Cycle Maths · Teacher pack

Surds

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): N.1b, U.13

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can say what a surd is, simplify surds using the biggest square factor, add and subtract like surds, and multiply, divide and square surds. Higher Level: expand brackets with surds, including (a + √b)(a − √b) and (a + √b)².

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Surd Match mingle cards + Surd Swap A/B cards · p.3 Surd Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 3rd year (mostly Higher Level). Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: which is bigger? Write 3√2 and 2√3 on the board. Students guess, then square both (18 and 12). Ask: why do calculators show √2 as 1.414213…? Elicit "it never ends": a surd.
8 minGuided discovery. Pairs work out √4 × √9 and √36, √4 × √25 and √100, then √9 + √16 and √25. Elicit √a × √b = √(ab), but √a + √b ≠ √(a + b). Then use it backwards: √72 = √36 × √2 = 6√2.
10 minSurd Match mingle (p.2, 24 cards = 12 question cards + 12 answer cards; one card each). Find your partner. Pairs then explain which square number they used.
12 minSurd Swap (p.2, pairs, don't show your card). Each measurement is on only ONE card. Ask for what you're missing, then fill in the blanks together. Feedback: garden → 5√2, 2√2, 14√2 m, 20 m²; ramp → 3√5 cm, 9 cm².
10 minSurd Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, and build the chain to FINISH.
12 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name it, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone. Question 5 is Higher Level.
3 minExit ticket. Simplify √98 and √12 + √75. (7√2; 7√3.) Homework: play Surd Simplify at leim.ie/surd-simplify.html.
Mingle key (question card → answer card): 6 → 7 · 19 → 9 · 22 → 17 · 16 → 4 · 12 → 20 · 5 → 3 · 11 → 2 · 14 → 23 · 1 → 10 · 13 → 24 · 15 → 21 · 18 → 8.
Errors to look for: not the biggest square (2√12) · √a + √b = √(a + b) · 3√5 × 2√5 = 6√5 · (√6)² = 36 · forgetting the middle terms of (a + √b)². Support: a list of square numbers 4 to 100 on the desk. Fast finishers: which is bigger, 5√2 or 4√3? Explain without a calculator.

A. Error Hunt

Simplify √7218 still has a square in it. Use 36: √36 × √2 = 6√2
Simplify √2 + √8√a + √b ≠ √(a + b). √8 = 2√2, so √2 + 2√2 = 3√2
Work out 3√5 × 2√5√5 × √5 = 5, so 6 × 5 = 30
Work out (√6)²Squaring a root removes it: 6
Simplify √50 − √2Simplify first: 5√2 − √2 = 4√2
Higher Level: expand (2 + √3)²Forgot the middle: 4 + 2√3 + 2√3 + 3 = 7 + 4√3

B. Exam-style questions

  1. (a) side = √50 = √25 × √2 = 5√2 cm (b) 4 × 5√2 = 20√2 cm
  2. 2√3 + 3√3 + 5√3 = 10√3 ✓
  3. 4√3 = √48 and 3√5 = √45. 48 > 45, so 4√3 is bigger.
  4. c² = (√5)² + (√11)² = 5 + 11 = 16, so c = 4 cm, a whole number.
  5. (a) (2√3)² − 1² = 12 − 1 = 11 (b) 5 + 2√5 + 2√5 + 4 = 9 + 4√5

Marking tip

Give credit for each step, as the exam does: the square factor shown (√36 × √2), the surds simplified before adding, the final answer in the form asked for. An answer like 8.49 instead of 6√2 is not exact: ask for surd form.
