1. Show, don't tell

Tell: I was scared.
Show: My heart pounded. I froze at the bottom of the stairs.
Don't name the feeling. Give the body clues and let the reader work it out.
FeelingBody clues
Happygrinned, beamed, laughed
Excitedbounced, squealed, couldn't sit still
Scaredfroze, gasped, heart pounded
Nervousbit my nails, fidgeted, butterflies
Angryclenched my fists, slammed, glared
Sadtears, a lump in my throat, slumped
Boredyawned, doodled, watched the clock
Embarrassedcheeks burned, blushed, stared at my shoes

2. Use all five senses

Sight colours, light · Sound roared, hummed · Smell scent, reeked · Taste salty, sour · Touch icy, rough, damp. Most students only write what they see: one sound or smell makes the scene real.

3. Strong verbs

walkedtrudged (tired) · crept (quietly) · raced (fast) · strolled (relaxed) · stormed (angry)
saidwhispered · shouted · muttered · sobbed · giggled · stammered
lookedglanced (quick) · stared (long) · peered (hard to see) · glared (angry)
ategobbled (hungry) · nibbled (not hungry) · slurped (soup)
One strong verb beats a weak verb + adverb: walked slowly → trudged.

4. Zoom in

a dog → a scruffy brown terrier · food → a warm cheese and ham toastie. Cut nice, good, thing, stuff, lots.

5. Personal or descriptive?

Personal writing: a real moment from your life, told with "I", with how you felt and what you learned. Descriptive writing: paints a place or a moment so the reader feels they are there. Less story, more detail.

Classic mistakes: naming feelings (I was so happy) · only sight · nice / good / things · five adjectives in a row · "and then… and then…" · ending with "it was all a dream".
Exam tip: quality beats length. One vivid paragraph that shows a feeling, uses two senses and has sharp verbs earns more than a page of "it was nice".

Worked examples

Turning tell into show"I was nervous." Ask: what would my body do? → I bit my nails and checked the clock again. Then add where you are: outside the principal's office.
Choosing a strong verbFind the clue in the sentence. "Exhausted after training, I ___ up the hill": exhausted → slow and heavy → trudged, not raced or tiptoed.
Zooming inAsk: which one could I draw? "a nice car" (no) → "Dad's rusty red Fiesta" (yes). Colour, size, age, a name or a place make it specific.
Using the sensesWrite one sentence per sense, then keep the best two or three. Don't list all five in one sentence: spread them out.
