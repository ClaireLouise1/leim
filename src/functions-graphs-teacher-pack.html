Junior Cycle Maths · Teacher pack

Functions & graphs

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): AF.7a–d, AF.1c, U.4

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can say what a function is, use f(x) notation to find outputs and inputs, read roots, turning points and solutions of f(x) = k from a quadratic graph, match graphs to real situations, and tell linear, quadratic and exponential functions apart from a rule, a table or a graph. Higher Level: f(x) = g(x) from two graphs, exponential functions and "find k".

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Function Match mingle cards + Story Graph Swap A/B cards · p.3 Function Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 3rd year (Higher and Ordinary). Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: a function machine. "Give me a number." Apply a secret rule (×3, then +2) and write input → output on the board. Students guess the rule, then write it as f(x) = 3x + 2. Ask: "Could 4 ever give two different answers?" (No: that's what makes it a function.)
8 minGuided discovery. Pairs make tables for x = 0 to 4 for f(x) = 3x + 1, g(x) = x² and h(x) = 2ˣ, then look at the jumps. "What happens each time x goes up by 1?" Elicit: the same jump (linear), jumps that grow evenly (quadratic), multiply by the same number (exponential). Sketch the three shapes.
10 minFunction Match mingle (p.2, 24 cards = 12 rule/table pairs; one card each). Rule card? Work out f(0) to f(3) and find your table. Table card? Find your rule. Warn them: several tables start the same way, so check every value.
12 minStory Graph Swap (p.2, pairs, don't show your card). Each fact is on only ONE card. Ask your partner for what you're missing, then fill every blank together and sketch both graphs. Feedback: taxi f(x) = 2x + 4, 6 km costs €16, €20 goes 8 km, linear; f(x) = (x + 1)(x − 3) = x² − 2x − 3, minimum point (1, −4), f(5) = 12.
10 minFunction Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, answer the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, and build the chain to FINISH. Two answers are written "−1 and 4".
12 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name it, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone: question 1 needs the grid. Question 4 is Higher Level.
3 minExit ticket. f(x) = x² − 5. Find f(−3), and say whether 1, 3, 9, 27 is linear, quadratic or exponential. (4; exponential.) Homework: play Graph Match at leim.ie/graph-match.html and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Mingle key (rule card → table card): 24 → 21 · 19 → 12 · 13 → 9 · 22 → 23 · 20 → 8 · 6 → 7 · 4 → 1 · 16 → 18 · 2 → 14 · 3 → 17 · 10 → 5 · 15 → 11.
Errors to look for: (−3)² = −9 · points written (y, x) · 2x and 2ˣ mixed up · a quadratic graph drawn with straight lines · one answer when the curve gives two · a rule checked with the first value only. Support: a card with the three shapes and "same jump / even change / × same number". Fast finishers: invent a story for a graph that goes up, stays flat, then goes down.

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. f(x) = x² − 4x. (a) Complete a table for −1 ≤ x ≤ 5. (b) Draw the graph on the grid. (c) Write down the minimum point and solve f(x) = 0. (d) Estimate where f(x) = 3.
  2. A ball's height is h = 30t − 5t² metres after t seconds. (a) Find h when t = 1 and t = 3. (b) When does it land? (c) When is it 40 m high?
  3. Is each one linear, quadratic or exponential? (a) f(x) = 7 − 2x (b) 4, 8, 16, 32 (c) f(x) = x(x + 5).
  4. Higher Level: g(x) = x. On your grid, draw g and solve f(x) = g(x). Check with algebra.

Marking tip

Give credit for a correct table, plotted points and a smooth curve, as the exam does. When reading a graph, ask students to draw the line across (or up) so you can see their method; accept estimates within half a square.
