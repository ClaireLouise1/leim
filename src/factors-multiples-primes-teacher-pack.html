Junior Cycle Maths · Teacher pack

Factors, multiples & primes

↗ Léim

Learning outcome (2018 specification): N.1d (also used in N.1a fractions and AF.3 factorising)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can find all the factors of a number in pairs, list multiples, explain what a prime is (and why 1 isn't one), write a number as a product of primes in index form using a factor tree, find the HCF and LCM of two numbers, and decide which one a word problem needs.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Sieve of Eratosthenes + Number Detective cards · p.3 HCF & LCM Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: Rectangles. Pairs use 12 counters (or squares on grid paper) to make every possible rectangle: 1 × 12, 2 × 6, 3 × 4. Now try 7, then 9. Which numbers make only one rectangle? Those are the primes. Elicit factor, factor pair, prime.
10 minGuided discovery: the Sieve (p.2, one per student). Cross out 1. Circle 2 and cross out every other multiple of 2; circle 3 and cross out its multiples; then 5, then 7. Everything left is prime (25 primes). Ask: why can we stop at 7? (11 × 11 = 121 > 100.) Why is 2 the only even prime?
12 minNumber Detective line-up (p.2, cut up). One card per student (or one per pair). Each works out their mystery number, checks it with a neighbour, then the class lines up in order of the numbers. The class reads the clues out in order to check. Model the factor tree for 360 and HCF/LCM with the Venn diagram on the poster (p.4) before the next step.
12 minHCF & LCM Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left and build the chain to FINISH. Answer chain below.
15 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name the rule, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone. Feedback: which questions needed the HCF and which the LCM, and how could you tell?
5 minExit ticket. Write 72 as a product of primes in index form and find the HCF of 72 and 60 (2³ × 3²; 12). Homework: play Prime Smash at leim.ie/prime-smash.html (HCF & LCM round) and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Number Detective key: A 29 · B 12 · C 24 · D 14 · E 25 · F 30 · G 72 · H 2 · I 97 · J 21 · K 36 · L 16. Line-up order: 2 (H) → 12 (B) → 14 (D) → 16 (L) → 21 (J) → 24 (C) → 25 (E) → 29 (A) → 30 (F) → 36 (K) → 72 (G) → 97 (I).
Errors to look for: calling 1 prime · thinking every odd number is prime (51, 57, 87, 91) · leaving a composite in the "product of primes" (2² × 15) · 2³ = 6 · mixing up HCF and LCM, or giving a common multiple that isn't the lowest (48 for 6 and 8) · missing 1 and the number itself from a factor list · in word problems, giving "60" when the question asks for a time. Support: multiplication square on the desk. Fast finishers: find two primes that differ by 2 (twin primes) above 50; or find every number below 100 with exactly 3 factors (4, 9, 25, 49: squares of primes). Why does that work?

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. (a) Write 180 and 168 as products of primes in index form. (b) Find the HCF of 180 and 168. (c) Find the LCM of 180 and 168.
  2. Two lighthouses flash together at 9 p.m. One flashes every 15 seconds and the other every 25 seconds. (a) After how many seconds do they next flash together? (b) How many more times will they flash together in the next 10 minutes?
  3. A patio is 360 cm long and 210 cm wide. It will be covered with identical square slabs, with no cutting. Find the largest slab that can be used, and how many slabs are needed.
  4. Ciarán says, "Every prime number is odd." Is he right? Give a reason.
  5. Find the number between 100 and 120 that has both 3 and 7 as factors.

2³ × 52³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8, so 40

B. Exam-style questions

  1. (a) 180 = 2² × 3² × 5 and 168 = 2³ × 3 × 7. (b) HCF = 2² × 3 = 12. (c) LCM = 2³ × 3² × 5 × 7 = 2520. Check: 12 × 2520 = 30 240 = 180 × 168.
  2. (a) LCM of 15 and 25 = 75 seconds. (b) 10 minutes = 600 seconds; 600 ÷ 75 = 8 times.
  3. HCF of 360 and 210 = 30 cm slabs; 12 × 7 = 84 slabs.
  4. No: 2 is prime (factors 1 and 2) and it is even.
  5. It must be a multiple of 3 × 7 = 21: 21, 42, 63, 84, 105.

Marking tip

Give credit for each correct step, as the exam does: a correct factor tree with one slip should still earn most of the marks. In word problems, ask students to write "HCF" or "LCM" and why before they calculate.
