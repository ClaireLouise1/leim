Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

The novel: setting, plot & theme

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R6, R7, R9, W8

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can name the five stages of a plot, say what mood a setting creates and quote the details that create it, tell a theme from a topic, and describe how a character changes from the start to the end of a novel, with evidence.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Plot Jigsaw cards (cut-up) · p.3 Novel Swap A/B (speaking + listening) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Answer + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 2nd year (works alongside any novel on your course). Time: about 60 minutes. All texts are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Draw a mountain on the board. Ask: where on it is the most exciting moment of the novel you are reading? Where does it start? Where does it end?
8 minGuided discovery. Elicit the five stages and the signal words for each (Suddenly / After that / In the end). Then mood (tense, peaceful, joyful, gloomy) and the difference between a topic and a theme.
12 minPlot Jigsaw (p.2, groups of 3–4, one set of 20 cards). Rebuild the 4 mini-novels in order: exposition → rising action → climax → falling action → resolution. Fast groups: give each story a theme.
15 minNovel Swap (p.3, pairs). A reads a novel summary aloud; B fills the grid (setting, mood, climax, resolution, change, theme). Swap. Check together.
15 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Answer in pairs, then the exam-style questions on "The Market" alone.
5 minExit ticket. Your class novel: name its climax in one sentence and one theme with a quote. Homework: play Plot Line at leim.ie/plot-line.html.
Errors to look for: retelling the plot instead of making a point · naming a topic as a theme · "the setting is a house" with no mood · calling the last chapter the climax · points with no quote. Support: the poster (p.4) on the desk; give weaker groups the 4 exposition cards first. Fast finishers: map your class novel onto the five stages, one sentence each.

Student A · Novel Swap

1. Read the summary of your novel aloud twice, slowly. Your partner can't see it.

"The Lighthouse Summer"
Small waves lapped gently against the harbour wall below the lighthouse. It was the first day of the summer holidays, and Ruth had been sent to stay with her aunt on Inis Mór. She missed her phone signal, her friends and her bed, and she refused to leave her room. Suddenly, a storm blew up, and the island's only ferry was stranded at the pier with a group of tourists on board. After that, Ruth helped her aunt carry food and blankets down to the pier for a week. In the end, Ruth cried when the ferry came to take her home. At the start, Ruth wouldn't share her room with her cousin. By the end, she gave her cousin her favourite jumper. For the first time, Ruth was trusted to row the boat out on her own.

B. Exam-style questions: "The Market"

The Saturday market in Ennis had always been full of music and laughter, but this year half the stalls stood empty. Cathal's mam had run the bakery stall for twenty years, until the new supermarket opened and her customers stopped coming. Cathal had always been embarrassed by the stall and hid behind it when his friends walked past. Now, watching his mam pack away the unsold loaves, he felt something he couldn't name. On the Monday, he set up a stand at school selling her bread at break time. By Friday, there was a queue out the door, and his friends were the ones serving.
  1. How has the setting changed? Quote two details.
  2. What is the problem in this extract?
  3. Which sentence is the turning point for Cathal? Explain your choice.
  4. How does Cathal change? Use the text.
  5. Name one theme of the extract and explain how it is shown.

The Night Match: a pitch in Clones, a November evening · Gloomy ("grey clouds", "empty pitch") · Climax: Grandad walks onto the pitch with a whistle · Resolution: the team wins and carries Grandad round the pitch · shy → confident · Belonging ("he was one of them again").

A. Fix the Answer

“The novel is about a boy who moves house and then he goes to school and then he meets a dog and then…”Retelling the plot. Pick one key moment and say why it matters.
“The theme of the novel is a dog.”That's the topic. A theme is an idea: friendship can be found in unexpected places.
“The setting is a house.”No detail, no mood. Say where and when, quote a detail and name the mood it creates.
“Nell is brave.” (nothing else)A point with no proof. Add a quote and explain it: her hands were shaking, but she struck the match.
“The climax is at the end of the book.”Too vague. Name the moment and say why it is the turning point.
“In the book the girl changes.”Name the novel, the author and the character, and say from what to what, with a quote for each.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. It used to be lively ("full of music and laughter") but now it is gloomy and quiet ("half the stalls stood empty").
  2. The new supermarket has taken his mam's customers, and her bakery stall is failing.
  3. "Now, watching his mam pack away the unsold loaves, he felt something he couldn't name." Seeing his mam's loss changes how he feels, and everything after it follows from that moment.
  4. From embarrassed (he "hid behind" the stall) to proud and active: he sets up a stand at school, and his friends end up "serving".
  5. Credit family, loyalty, change or growing up, with a quote and a sentence on how it is shown (e.g. Cathal takes responsibility for helping his mam).

Marking tip

Reward a clear point + a short quote or close reference + a sentence on why it matters, more than length. Retelling the plot with no point earns little. Our guide, not an official marking scheme.
