Junior Cycle Spanish · Teacher pack

Food, drink & eating out

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes: 1.11, 1.12, 1.14, 3.4, 3.9 (2017 spec, exams 2027–2028) · 1.10, 3.4, 3.9 (2025 spec, 1st years from Sept 2026)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can order food and drink politely (quisiera, ¿me pone…?, para mí), handle the waiter's questions (¿Qué va a tomar? ¿Algo más?), ask and understand prices (¿Cuánto cuesta / cuestan?), name the courses and meals, and compare meal times in Spain and Ireland.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Café Luna menu info gap + role-play (two cards) · p.3–4 Food Dominoes (cut-up) · p.5 Wall poster · p.6 Worksheet + answers. Level: 1st–3rd year. Time: about 60 minutes.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Mime walking into a café: ¡Tengo mucha hambre! ¿Qué hay? Students call out every food and drink they know in Spanish; write them up in three columns: comida / bebidas / postres (with un, una, unos, unas).
10 minGuided discovery. Board: Quiero un bocadillo. / Quisiera un bocadillo, por favor. / ¿Me pone un bocadillo, por favor? / Para mí, un bocadillo. Ask: "Which is the most polite? Which sounds blunt? Which would you say in a group?" Then waiter vs customer lines and ¿Cuánto cuesta el flan? / ¿Cuánto cuestan las croquetas?: "Why two forms?" (one thing / more than one, like gusta / gustan). Concept checks: "At the end of the meal, la carta or la cuenta?" "The waiter says ¿Qué quieres? or ¿Qué quiere?" (usted).
5 minPoster + meal-times survey. Put up the poster (p.5). Drill the courses and prices. Quick hands-up survey: ¿A qué hora cenas? Compare with Spain (cena at 9–10 pm, comida at 2–3 pm).
15 minCafé Luna (p.2), pairs A and B. Step 1, info gap: each card is missing six prices; students ask ¿Cuánto cuesta…? and fill them in without showing their menu. Step 2, role-play: A (customer, €10) orders a meal from B (waiter), who writes the order and gives the bill (Son… euros). Swap roles (B has €12). Confident pairs perform for the class.
10 minFood Dominoes (p.3–4), groups of 3–4. Deal out the dominoes; players match a Spanish phrase to its English. First to place all their dominoes wins. Answer chain below.
10 minWorksheet (p.6) A and B in pairs. Feed back on the error correction.
5 minExit ticket. Order a three-course meal and a drink in Spanish (De primero… de segundo… de postre… para beber…). Homework: play Café Order Rush at leim.ie/cafe-order-rush.html and screenshot your best score.
Errors to listen for: Quiero un patatas fritas (unas) · ¿Cuánto cuesta las croquetas? (cuestan) · la carta for the bill (la cuenta) · Estoy hambre (Tengo hambre) · Tengo mucho hambre (mucha) · waiter saying ¿Qué quieres? to a customer (quiere) · La paella está muy rico (rica). Note: un agua is correct (agua is feminine, but takes el / un because it starts with a stressed a).
Culture note: in Spain lunch (la comida, 2–3:30 pm) is the main meal and dinner is late (9–10 pm), with a merienda around 6 pm. On weekdays many restaurants serve a menú del día: a fixed price for a starter, main, dessert or coffee, bread and a drink. Tapas are small plates to share. Tipping is small (rounding up or a few coins). Support: give weaker students the poster as a mat. Fast finishers: add a problem to the role-play: Lo siento, no hay churros.

Domino answer chain:

  1. I love churros. = Me encantan los churros.
  2. Nothing else, thanks. = Nada más, gracias.
  3. For the starter, the soup. = De primero, la sopa.
  4. It costs €3.50. = Cuesta tres euros con cincuenta.
  5. I'm thirsty. = Tengo sed.
  6. I have dinner at half past nine. = Ceno a las nueve y media.
  7. Can I have a cheese roll? = ¿Me pone un bocadillo de queso?
  8. The bill, please. = La cuenta, por favor.
  9. I'm very hungry. = Tengo mucha hambre.
  10. I have toast and a juice for breakfast. = Desayuno tostadas y un zumo.
  11. It's delicious. = Está muy rico.
  12. I'd like a white coffee. = Quisiera un café con leche.
  13. A table for four, please. = Una mesa para cuatro, por favor.
  14. I'm a vegetarian. = Soy vegetariana.
  15. For dessert, a crème caramel. = De postre, un flan.
  16. For the main course, chicken and chips. = De segundo, pollo con patatas.
  17. Anything else? = ¿Algo más?
  18. How much are the croquettes? = ¿Cuánto cuestan las croquetas?
  19. What is there for dessert? = ¿Qué hay de postre?
  20. What will you have? = ¿Qué va a tomar?

Worksheet

Meals at Carmen's house

↗ Léim

A. Read Carmen's email

¡Hola! ¿Qué tal? En tu último correo me preguntaste por las comidas en España. En mi casa desayunamos a las siete y media: yo tomo tostadas con tomate y un vaso de leche, y mis padres toman café. En el instituto, a las once, como un bocadillo en el recreo. La comida es a las tres, cuando vuelvo a casa: normalmente hay un primero, como sopa o ensalada, y un segundo, como pescado o carne. A las seis meriendo fruta o un yogur. Cenamos tarde, a las nueve y media, algo ligero: una tortilla o una ensalada. A veces, entre semana, mi madre come en un restaurante cerca de su trabajo: tiene un menú del día por doce euros. Los domingos comemos en casa de mi abuela. ¡Su paella es riquísima! Pero no me gustan nada las aceitunas. ¿Y tú? ¿A qué hora cenas en Irlanda? ¿Cuál es tu comida favorita? Un beso, Carmen
  1. What time does Carmen's family have breakfast, and what does Carmen have?
  2. What do her parents have for breakfast?
  3. What does Carmen eat at eleven, and where?
  4. What time is lunch? Give an example of a starter and a main course.
  5. What does she have at six?
  6. What time is dinner, and what is it like?
  7. Where does her mother sometimes eat on weekdays, and how much does it cost?
  8. What is Carmen's favourite dish, and what does she not like at all?

B. Find and fix the mistake

  1. Quiero un croquetas, por favor.
  2. ¿Cuánto cuesta los churros?
  3. La sopa está muy rico.
  4. Me gusta las patatas fritas.
  1. La carta, por favor. ¿Cuánto es?
  2. Yo desayuna a las ocho.
  3. Tengo mucho hambre.
  4. Camarero: ¿Qué quieres tomar, señora?

C. Now you

Write a reply to Carmen (40–60 words). Answer her questions: when you eat each meal in Ireland, what you usually eat, your favourite food (and why), and one food you don't like.

Answers. A: 1 At half past seven; toast with tomato and a glass of milk. 2 Coffee. 3 A roll (sandwich), at school at break time. 4 At three; soup or salad, then fish or meat. 5 Fruit or a yogurt. 6 At half past nine; something light (an omelette or a salad). 7 In a restaurant near her work; €12 (set menu). 8 Her gran's paella; olives. · B: 9 unas 10 cuestan 11 rica 12 gustan 13 La cuenta 14 desayuno 15 mucha 16 quiere (usted).
Watch out: tostadas con tomate = toast rubbed with tomato, a typical Spanish breakfast; el recreo = break time; algo ligero = something light; riquísima = really delicious; las aceitunas = olives.
