Junior Cycle Maths · Teacher pack

Transformations & symmetry

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): GT.6a, GT.6b

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can find and draw the image of points and shapes under translation, axial symmetry (in the axes and in other lines), central symmetry and rotations of 90° and 180°, name the transformation that maps one shape onto another, and find the axes and centres of symmetry of common shapes.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Mirror Move mingle cards + Grid Swap A/B cards · p.3 Transformation Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 2nd year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original. Tracing paper helps.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: human transformations. One student stands at the front facing the class. "Slide two steps left." "Turn a quarter turn clockwise." "Become your mirror image in the door frame" (which hand is raised now?). Elicit the words slide / flip / turn and what stays the same (size and shape).
8 minGuided discovery. Pairs plot A(3, 2) and find its image in the x-axis, the y-axis and the origin by counting squares, then repeat with (−4, 1) and (2, −5). "What happens to the numbers each time?" Build the three rules. Then turn (3, 2) a quarter turn anticlockwise about (0, 0) with tracing paper: (−2, 3). Can they spot the pattern?
10 minMirror Move mingle (p.2, 24 cards = 12 question/answer pairs; one card each). Question card? Work it out and find the person with your answer. Answer card? Find your question. Pairs check each other's working on mini-whiteboards.
12 minGrid Swap (p.2, pairs, don't show your card). Each fact is on only ONE card. Ask your partner for what you're missing ("Where is corner Q?"), plot it, then both fill every blank. Feedback: K after move 1 (−2, 1), (−5, 1), (−3, 4); after move 2 (−1, 2), (−1, 5), (−4, 3); M → (4, −5), (2, 0); the point (−4, 3).
10 minTransformation Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, and build the chain to FINISH.
12 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name the rule, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone.
3 minExit ticket. The image of (−2, 5) in the y-axis? Under central symmetry in (0, 0)? ((2, 5); (2, −5).) Homework: play Mirror Move at leim.ie/mirror-move.html and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Mingle key (question card → answer card): 22 → 18 · 3 → 19 · 23 → 1 · 13 → 14 · 12 → 17 · 5 → 9 · 6 → 21 · 11 → 7 · 4 → 2 · 24 → 10 · 8 → 15 · 16 → 20.
Errors to look for: changing the wrong sign · mixing clockwise and anticlockwise · thinking central symmetry flips the shape · translating backwards · counting to a line m along the grid · 4 axes for a rectangle. Support: tracing paper and a card with the three sign rules. Fast finishers: which capital letters have exactly one axis of symmetry? Which have a centre of symmetry?

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. Triangle T is shown. (a) Write the corners of T′, its image under axial symmetry in the y-axis. (b) Write the corners of T″, its image under central symmetry in the origin. (c) Which single transformation maps T′ onto T″? (d) Write the corners of the image of T under the translation that maps (1, 2) onto (−3, 0).
  2. P is (5, 2). Find its image under a rotation about (0, 0) of (a) 90° anticlockwise, (b) 180°, (c) 90° clockwise. (d) Join P and the three images in order. What shape do you get?
  3. The line m (a diagonal) is to be an axis of symmetry of the pattern below. How many more squares must be shaded? Shade them.
  4. Copy and fill in the table for a square, a rectangle, a rhombus, a parallelogram and a kite: the number of axes of symmetry, and yes/no for a centre of symmetry.
  5. A is (−1, 3) and B is (3, 1). (a) Find the image of A under central symmetry in B. (b) The translation maps A onto B. Find the image of (2, 2). (c) Which point does this translation map onto (0, 0)?

Marking tip

Give credit for each step, as the exam does: each correct image point, the label, and a fully named transformation each earn marks. "A reflection" without the line, or "a rotation" without the angle, direction and centre, is incomplete. For "explain" parts insist on a sentence with a reason in it.
