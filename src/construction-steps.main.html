Constructions

Spot the pattern

Draw a line segment [AB] and construct its perpendicular bisector (the steps are in the viewer below). Now pick any point on the bisector, call it X, and measure |XA| and |XB|. Try two more points.

  • What do you notice about |XA| and |XB|, wherever X is on the line?
  • The arcs you drew from A and B had the same radius. How does that explain where C and D are?
  • So why does the construction need the compass to be more than half of |AB|?
Show me

Every point on the perpendicular bisector is the same distance from A as from B. C and D are on arcs of the same radius from A and from B, so each of them is that same distance from both ends, and the line through them is the bisector. If the compass is open to half of |AB| or less, the two arcs never meet, so you need more than half.

The tools

Compass: draws circles and arcs, and copies a length. Straight edge: draws straight lines (a ruler you don't measure with). Ruler: measures lengths. Protractor: measures and draws angles. Set square: draws right angles and parallel lines.
Constructions 1 and 2 (the bisector of an angle and the perpendicular bisector of a line segment) must be done with a compass and straight edge only: no protractor, no measuring.
Golden rules: use a sharp pencil · leave every construction arc showing (it's your proof, and it earns marks) · label every point · set the compass carefully against the ruler, starting at 0 · keep the compass width the same when the step says so.

The 15 Junior Cycle constructions

  • 1. Bisector of an angle (compass and straight edge only)
  • 2. Perpendicular bisector of a line segment (compass and straight edge only)
  • 3. Line perpendicular to l, through a point not on l HL only
  • 4. Line perpendicular to l, through a point on l
  • 5. Line parallel to l, through a given point
  • 6. Dividing a line segment into 2 or 3 equal parts, without measuring
  • 7. Dividing a line segment into any number of equal parts, without measuring HL only
  • 8. Line segment of a given length on a given ray
  • 9. Angle of a given size with a given ray as one arm
  • 10. Triangle, given three sides (SSS)
  • 11. Triangle, given two sides and the angle between them (SAS)
  • 12. Triangle, given two angles and the side between them (ASA)
  • 13. Right-angled triangle, given the hypotenuse and one other side (RHS)
  • 14. Right-angled triangle, given one side and one of the acute angles
  • 15. Rectangle, given the side lengths

Triangles: which data?

SSS: all three sides. Draw one side, then an arc from each end with the other two lengths. SAS: two sides and the angle between them. ASA: two angles and the side between them. RHS: a right angle, the hypotenuse and one other side.
Can it be a triangle? The two shorter sides must add up to more than the longest side. With 3 cm, 4 cm and 8 cm the arcs never meet (3 + 4 = 7, less than 8). And always sketch the triangle first, with the measurements on it, before you construct it.
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Constructions are on the course for both Higher and Ordinary level (constructions 3 and 7 are Higher level only). Expect a part of a geometry question that asks you to construct something accurately in the space given, and sometimes to measure a length or angle on your construction or explain why it works.

How marks are won and lost

  • Leave all your construction arcs showing. If the examiner can't see the arcs, it looks measured, not constructed.
  • Be accurate. Lengths and angles are checked against the true values, so small errors can cost marks. Use a sharp pencil and set the compass against the ruler.
  • Use the right instruments. "Using only a compass and straight edge" means no protractor and no measuring.
  • Label the points the question names, and finish the job: join up the triangle, draw the final line.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Do each one on paper with a compass, ruler and protractor first.

  1. Perpendicular bisector. Draw a line segment [AB] of length 9 cm. Using only a compass and straight edge, construct the perpendicular bisector of [AB]. Label the point where it meets [AB] as M. What should |AM| be? Measure to check.
    Show the worked answer

    Open the compass to more than 4.5 cm (say 6 cm). With the point on A, draw arcs above and below [AB]; do the same from B with the same width. Join the two crossing points. Leave all the arcs showing. M is the midpoint, so |AM| = 9 ÷ 2 = 4.5 cm.

  2. A triangle (SAS). Construct the triangle PQR with |PQ| = 8 cm, |PR| = 6 cm and ∠QPR = 55°. Then measure |QR|, correct to one decimal place.
    Show the worked answer

    Sketch it first. Draw [PQ] 8 cm long. At P, use a protractor to draw an angle of 55° with [PQ] as one arm. Set the compass to 6 cm, put the point on P and mark R on the new arm. Join R to Q. |QR| should measure about 6.7 cm (anything from 6.6 cm to 6.8 cm shows an accurate construction).

  3. Equal parts. Draw a line segment [CD] of length 10 cm. Without measuring, divide [CD] into three equal parts. Show all your construction lines. How long should each part be?
    Show the worked answer

    Draw a ray from C at an acute angle to [CD]. With any compass width, step off 3 equal marks along the ray from C. Join the third mark to D. Through the other two marks draw lines parallel to that join. They cut [CD] into three equal parts, each 10 ÷ 3 ≈ 3.3 cm.

  4. Bisect an angle. (a) Draw an angle ∠XYZ of 70°. (b) Using only a compass and straight edge, construct the bisector of ∠XYZ. (c) What size should each half be? Measure to check. (d) Explain why the bisector cuts the angle exactly in half.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) Use a protractor, starting at 0° on [YZ. (b) Compass point on Y: an arc crossing both arms. From each crossing point, with the same width, draw arcs that cross inside the angle. Join Y to that crossing point. (c) 70° ÷ 2 = 35°. (d) The two triangles formed have all three sides equal (two equal radii from Y, two equal arcs and the shared side), so they are congruent and the two angles at Y are equal.
