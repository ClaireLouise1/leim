Point, Quote, Explain

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R3, R4, R7, W7, W8

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can make a point that answers the question, choose a short, exact quote that proves it, explain how one word in the quote proves the point, and spot the three common weak explanations (retelling, off the point, vague).

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 PQE Jigsaw (24 cards = 8 answers) · p.3 Evidence Swap A/B (pair listening task) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Answer + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 3rd year (works from 2nd year), Higher and Ordinary. Time: about 60 minutes. All texts are original and the skill works with any studied text.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Show two answers to “How does Aoife feel?”: one that retells the story, one with a point, “her hands trembled” and an explanation of ‘trembled’. Ask: which gets more marks, and why? Name the parts: point, quote, explain (poster, p.4).
12 minPQE Jigsaw (p.2, groups of 3–4). Cut up the 24 cards. Groups sort them into 8 answers, each in the order point → quote → explain, and label each card P, Q or E.
15 minEvidence Swap (p.3, pairs). Each reads a short text aloud twice; the partner listens for the evidence and writes the quote that proves each point. Then each says one explanation aloud.
5 minQuote surgery. Write a long quote on the board; students cut it to the 2–6 words that matter.
18 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Answer in pairs, then the exam-style questions on “The Lifeboat” alone.
5 minExit ticket. One PQE on a character from the studied novel or play. Homework: play PQE Builder at leim.ie/pqe-builder.html.
Errors to look for: retelling the story · a quote with no explanation · whole sentences quoted · misquoting (learn a few words exactly) · explanations that argue a different point · “This shows what she is like” (vague) · a point that doesn't use the question's key word. Support: give the sentence starters on the poster; let students highlight the evidence before writing. Fast finishers: write a second explanation for the same quote, on a different word.

Point. Quote. Explain.

PointAnswer the question in one sentence. Use its key word.
Quote2–6 words. Exact. In quotation marks.
ExplainZoom in on one word. How does it prove the point?
Point: Aoife is nervous before her audition.
Quote: As she opens the case, “her hands trembled”.
Explain: The word ‘trembled’ shows how anxious she is: her body gives her away.

The word ‘…’ suggests … · This shows … · The verb ‘…’ makes … sound …

Prove it. Don't retell it.

B. Exam-style questions: an original extract

The LifeboatThe pager went off at midnight, and Dara was out of bed before it stopped beeping. At the pier the sea heaved and the rain stung his face. His hands fumbled with the straps of his lifejacket, and he had to try three times. Out past the harbour wall, a small torch flashed and flashed. When they finally pulled the soaked fisherman on board, Dara gripped his shoulder and grinned until his cheeks hurt.
  1. Find one short quote that shows the weather is wild. Explain one word in it.
  2. How does Dara feel at the pier? Write a full PQE.
  3. “a small torch flashed and flashed”: why do you think the writer repeats “flashed”?
  4. How does Dara feel at the end? Use a quote and explain it.
  5. Choose a character from a novel or play you studied. Write two PQEs about them, each on a different quality.

A. Fix the Answer

Aoife is nervous. She goes into the music room and plays her violin and then she is happy.retells the story and has no quote: add “her hands trembled” and explain ‘trembled’
Cian is lonely: “On his first day in Athlone, Cian ate his lunch alone on the wall.”the quote is the whole sentence: cut it to “ate his lunch alone” and explain ‘alone’
Saoirse is angry because she “clenched her hands”.misquoted: the text says “clenched my fists”; copy the words exactly
Kevin is worried. “his stomach churned”a quote dropped in with no explanation: say what ‘churned’ suggests (feeling sick with worry)
Hana is kind. She “offered him half her orange”. This shows she is shy.the explanation argues a different point: link ‘offered’ back to kindness
The poem is good because the poet uses lots of good words.vague: no point about the poem, no quote, no word explained

B. Exam-style questions

  1. “the sea heaved” or “the rain stung his face”: ‘heaved’ makes the sea sound alive and powerful; ‘stung’ suggests the rain hurts.
  2. Nervous / tense: “His hands fumbled” and he “had to try three times”; ‘fumbled’ suggests his fingers won't do what he wants, because of nerves (and the cold).
  3. Repetition makes the signal feel urgent and desperate: someone out there is calling for help again and again, and the reader feels the tension.
  4. Relieved and happy: he “grinned until his cheeks hurt”; ‘until his cheeks hurt’ suggests his relief is so strong he can't stop smiling.
  5. Reward two clear points on different qualities, each with a short, exact quote and an explanation of a word; no retelling.

Marking tip

Answers on texts are rewarded for a clear response to the question, supported by reference to the text and explained. Our guide, not an official marking scheme.
