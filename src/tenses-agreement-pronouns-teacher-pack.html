Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

Tenses, agreement and pronouns

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): W10, W12, OL12

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can use time words to choose the past, present or future, keep a story in one tense, make verbs agree with tricky subjects (each, one of, and), use the past and past participle of common irregular verbs, and choose I or me.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Time Match cards (cut-up) · p.3 Slip Detective A/B (speaking) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Sentence + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 2nd year. Time: about 60 minutes. All texts are original; names and places are made up.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Write “Yesterday I seen a fox.” Is it OK? Where would you hear it? Where would you not write it? Elicit spoken Irish English vs standard written English.
8 minGuided discovery. Show “Yesterday I walked / Every day I walk / Tomorrow I will walk / Every day Gran walks / Every day my cousins walk”. Which word tells you the time? When does the verb get an -s? Elicit the rules (poster, p.4).
10 minTime Match (p.2, groups of 3, one set of 24 cards). Match each sentence to the verb that fits its time word. Then sort the pairs into past, present and future.
12 minSlip Detective (p.3, pairs). A reads a past story with 3 slipped verbs; B shouts “Slip!” and gives the past form. Swap. Then retell both stories in the past.
20 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Sentence in pairs, then the exam-style questions on “The Cliff Walk” alone.
5 minExit ticket. Write three sentences about last weekend with one “Each of…”, one “…and I” and one “have + seen / done / gone”. Homework: play Tense Trap at leim.ie/tense-trap.html.
Errors to look for: drifting into the present in a past story · “each of them have”, “one of my friends play” · “I seen / I done / we was” · “Me and him went” · “between you and I” · a “he” that could mean two people. Note: treat “I seen” and “youse” as spoken Irish English, not as “wrong English”; the point is choosing standard English for writing. Support: the poster (p.4) on the desk. Fast finishers: write a 5-line story about a storm with no tense slips.

B. Exam-style questions: "The Cliff Walk"

Last Sunday Fiona and me walk the cliff path at Ballycotton. The sea was rough and the wind blows so hard that each of us were holding the rail. Halfway along, we seen a seal on the rocks below. Fiona took a photo and sends it to Gran, who loved it.
  1. The writer meant to write this in the past. Find three verbs that slipped into the present and correct them.
  2. Find and correct the mistake with “I / me” in the first sentence. Explain the fix.
  3. Find and correct the agreement mistake (subject and verb) in the second sentence.
  4. Find and correct the “saw / seen” mistake. Why is it wrong in standard written English?
  5. Write two more sentences to finish the story, in the past, with one “and I” and one “has / have + seen / done / gone”.

pa = past, pr = present, fu = future. Each verb card fits only its sentence: check the time word first, then the subject (he / she / it → -s in the present).

Slip Detective

A “The Lost Kayak”: picks → picked, drifts → drifted, tells → told · B “The Talent Show”: shake → shook, walks → walked, claps → clapped

A. Fix the Sentence

Me and Ciara was late for the bus.Ciara and I were late for the bus. (I = subject; two people = plural)
Each of the players have a new jersey.Each of the players has a new jersey. (each = singular)
We done the project last week.We did the project last week. (no helper verb → did)
Yesterday I walk to the shop and buy milk.Yesterday I walked to the shop and bought milk. (yesterday → past)
The teacher gave Seán and I a merit.The teacher gave Seán and me a merit. (take away Seán: gave me)
There is three cakes left.There are three cakes left. (three cakes = plural)

B. Exam-style questions

  1. Any three of: walk → walked, blows → blew, sends → sent (the story is set “Last Sunday”).
  2. “Fiona and me walk” → “Fiona and I walked”: the pronoun is part of the subject (take away Fiona: “I walked”).
  3. “each of us were” → “each of us was”: each = one person at a time, so singular.
  4. “we seen” → “we saw”: there is no helper verb (has / have / had), so the past form “saw” is needed. “We seen” is spoken Irish English, not standard written English.
  5. Sample: Fiona and I stopped for chips in the village. I have never seen the sea so wild. Credit past tense throughout and the two required forms.

All six fixes in the passage: me → I (subject: take away Fiona: “I walked”) · walk → walked (Last Sunday → past) · blows → blew (keep the past) · were → was (each of us = singular) · seen → saw (no helper verb) · sends → sent (keep the past).

Marking tip

In writing tasks, reward one steady tense and correct agreement as part of accuracy; quote the student's own slips back to them so they learn to spot them. Our guide, not an official marking scheme.
