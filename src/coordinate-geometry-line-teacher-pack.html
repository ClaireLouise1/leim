Junior Cycle Maths · Teacher pack

Coordinate geometry of the line

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): GT.5a, GT.5b, GT.5c

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can find the midpoint, length and slope of a line segment, interpret slope as a rate of change and the y-intercept as a starting value, find and use the equation of a line (y = mx + c and y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)), recognise parallel lines, read where two lines meet, and (Higher Level) use perpendicular slopes and ax + by + c = 0.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Line Lab mingle cards + Line Swap A/B cards · p.3 Line Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 3rd year (Higher and Ordinary). Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original. Squared paper helps.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: which is steeper? Show two ramps drawn on the board (or two ski slopes): one rises 2 for every 3 across, one rises 3 for every 5. Pairs decide which is steeper and how they know. Elicit "rise ÷ run".
8 minGuided discovery. Pairs fill in a table for y = 2x + 1 and y = 2x − 3 (x = −2 to 2), plot both on one grid and compare. "What's the same? What's different? Where do the 2 and the −3 show up on the graph?" Build y = mx + c, then ask what y = −2x + 1 would look like.
10 minLine Lab mingle (p.2, 24 cards = 12 question/answer pairs; one card each). Question card? Work it out and find the person with your answer. Answer card? Find your question. Pairs check each other's working on mini-whiteboards.
12 minLine Swap (p.2, pairs, don't show your card). Each fact is on only ONE card. Ask your partner for what you're missing ("Where is point B?"), then both fill every blank. Feedback: midpoint (1, 6), slope 2, |AB| 8.9, y = 2x + 4; k: y = −x + 7; meet at (2, 5); y = 2x − 2.
10 minLine Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, and build the chain to FINISH.
12 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name it, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone. Questions 2(d) and 5 are Higher Level.
3 minExit ticket. The slope and midpoint of (1, 2) and (5, 10)? (2; (3, 6).) Homework: play Line Lab at leim.ie/line-lab.html and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Mingle key (question card → answer card): 7 → 11 · 5 → 16 · 13 → 17 · 24 → 9 · 23 → 14 · 20 → 22 · 12 → 6 · 19 → 21 · 8 → 3 · 1 → 2 · 10 → 18 · 4 → 15.
Errors to look for: run ÷ rise · losing a minus with negatives · subtracting for the midpoint · adding instead of squaring for distance · reading c as the slope · not getting y on its own first · parallel slope with the wrong sign. Support: a card with the formulas and (x₁, y₁), (x₂, y₂) labels. Fast finishers: find two points whose midpoint is (0, 0) and whose distance apart is 10.

Lines: the key facts

Slope = rise ÷ run
A(−1, −1) to B(2, 1):
run 3, rise 2, slope 2/3
Rises: positive · falls: negative · flat: 0
Slopem = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁)
Midpoint((x₁ + x₂) ÷ 2, (y₁ + y₂) ÷ 2)
Distance√((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²)
Equationy = mx + c
y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)

Parallel: same slope  ·  Perpendicular (HL): m₁ × m₂ = −1

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. A is (−1, −3) and B is (3, 5). Find (a) the midpoint of [AB], (b) the slope of AB, (c) |AB| to 1 decimal place, (d) the equation of AB.
  2. The lines l and k are shown. (a) Write the equation of each line. (b) Where do l and k meet? Check it in both equations. (c) Where does k cross the x-axis? (d) Higher Level: show that l and k are perpendicular.
  3. A barrel is leaking. A straight-line graph of the water in it goes through (0, 12) and (6, 3), with x in hours and y in litres. (a) Find the slope and say what it means. (b) Write the equation. (c) When will the barrel be empty?
  4. Find the equation of the line through (4, 1) with slope −½. Is (−2, 4) on this line?
  5. Higher Level: write 4x + 6y − 12 = 0 in the form y = mx + c, and show that (3, 0) is on the line.

A. Error Hunt

Slope through (1, 3) and (5, 11)? 1/2Run ÷ rise. Rise ÷ run: 8 ÷ 4 = 2
Midpoint of (−2, 6) and (4, 0)? (−3, 3)Subtracted the x's. Add, then halve: (1, 3)
Distance from (0, 0) to (5, 12)? 17Added 5 + 12. √(5² + 12²) = √169 = 13
Slope of y = 8 − 3x? 8Read the number on its own. y = −3x + 8: slope −3
Slope through (−1, 4) and (3, −4)? 0Lost the minus: −4 − 4 = −8, run 3 − (−1) = 4: −2
Line through (2, 7) parallel to y = 3x + 5? y = 3x + 5That's the same line. Same slope through (2, 7): y = 3x + 1

B. Exam-style questions

  1. (a) (1, 1) (b) 8 ÷ 4 = 2 (c) √80 = 8.9 (d) y − (−3) = 2(x − (−1)) → y = 2x − 1
  2. (a) l: y = ½x − 1, k: y = −2x + 4 (b) (2, 0): ½ × 2 − 1 = 0 ✓, −2 × 2 + 4 = 0 ✓ (c) (2, 0) (the same point) (d) ½ × (−2) = −1 ✓
  3. (a) (3 − 12) ÷ 6 = −3/2: the barrel loses 1.5 litres every hour (b) y = −(3/2)x + 12 (c) 0 = −1.5x + 12 → after 8 hours
  4. y − 1 = −½(x − 4) → y = −½x + 3; −½ × (−2) + 3 = 4 ✓ yes
  5. 6y = −4x + 12 → y = −⅔x + 2; −⅔ × 3 + 2 = 0 ✓

Marking tip

Give credit for each step, as the exam does: the formula, the numbers substituted correctly (with brackets for negatives), and the answer. An equation that is right but not tidied into the form asked for usually loses only the last mark. In context questions insist on units and a sentence: "€2 per km", not just "2".
