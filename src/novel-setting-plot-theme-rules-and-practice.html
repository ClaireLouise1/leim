The novel: plot, setting & theme

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R6, R7, R9, W8. Play the game: leim.ie/plot-line.html

1. The five stages of a plot

StageWhat happensLook for
Expositionmeet the characters, place, timea frosty January morning in Tralee
Rising actiontrouble starts and gets worselost, broken, missed, ruined, refused
Climaxthe turning pointSuddenly · Just then · At that moment
Falling actionwhat happens after itAfter that · Afterwards · Slowly · Bit by bit
Resolutionhow it all endsIn the end · At last · From that day on

In a novel each stage can last for chapters. The climax is the moment the whole book builds towards.

2. Setting and mood

The setting is where and when. The mood is the feeling it creates. Look at sounds, weather and light:
tense (creaked, shadow, footsteps) · peaceful (gently, still, softly) · joyful (music, laughed, bunting) · gloomy (grey, drizzle, empty)

3. Theme

The topic is what the novel is about (a dog, a war, a new school). The theme is the big idea about life it shows (friendship can be found anywhere). Common themes: friendship, courage, loss, belonging, growing up, fairness, forgiveness.

4. Character change

Compare the start with the end. Start: Leah whispered her answers and hid at the back. End: she volunteered to give the speech. Leah changes from shy to confident. Then ask: what changed her?
Classic mistakes: retelling the whole plot · no quote or reference · calling the topic a theme · "the setting is a house" with no detail or mood · forgetting the name of the novel and the author.
Exam tip: for every key moment and theme in your novel, learn two short quotes. A good paragraph = the point, a quote, and a sentence on why it matters.

A. Which stage of the plot?

Exposition · Rising action · Climax · Falling action · Resolution
  1. At half-time they were losing by three goals, and their captain was injured.
  2. Bit by bit, the two of them painted over the damage together.
  3. It was a grey September morning in Dungarvan, and Maeve was starting at a brand-new school.
  4. All at once, the fog lifted and they saw the path below them.
  5. At last, he opened the box, and the robin flew to the top of the hedge.
  6. After that, the girls asked her to show them how she did it.

C. Which theme?

Belonging · Courage · Forgiveness · Growing up · Loss
  1. Her legs were shaking, but Emma climbed the ladder to reach the trapped kitten.
  2. At the end of the match, Ella finally felt that she belonged in her new town.
  3. Every Christmas, Gran still set a place at the table for Grandad, who had died in the spring.
  4. Kate decided to give her brother a second chance.
  5. For the first time, Mam let Orla take the bus into the city on her own.

D. Which mood?

Tense · Peaceful · Joyful · Gloomy
  1. The beach was empty, and a grey sea rolled in.
  2. Somewhere in the darkness, footsteps stopped right outside the door.
  3. Everyone was dancing on the green as the sun went down.
  4. Snow fell softly on the quiet street.

Answers

↗ Léim
A1. Rising action · 2. Falling action · 3. Exposition · 4. Climax · 5. Resolution · 6. Falling action
B7. D → A → C → B · 8. D → C → A → B (rising action → climax → falling action → resolution)
C9. Courage · 10. Belonging · 11. Loss · 12. Forgiveness · 13. Growing up
D14. Gloomy · 15. Tense · 16. Joyful · 17. Peaceful
E18. scared → brave · 19. selfish → generous
F20. Gloomy: "rain dripped from the bare trees", "rusty doors". Themes: loss (Grandad has died, the cinema is going), courage or growing up (Dara stands up although he is shy). The last sentence feels like the climax or the start of it: the moment Dara finally acts.

Worked examples

Naming the stage"Just then, Mr Flynn's dog slipped its lead and ran towards the busy road." This is the climax: "Just then" signals the turning point, and after it Tadhg and Mr Flynn are never enemies again.
Setting and mood with evidenceThe lighthouse is empty and cold. "Grey rain dripped from the bare trees" creates a gloomy, lonely mood that matches Nell's grief.
Theme, not topicNot: "The theme is a lighthouse." Better: "One theme is courage: Nell goes in although her hands are shaking, because the boats need the light."
Writing about change1. Name the character and the novel. 2. Before, with a quote. 3. The key moment. 4. After, with a quote. 5. What caused it and what it shows.
