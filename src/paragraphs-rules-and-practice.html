Paragraphs and topic sentences

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): W1, W10, W3. Play the game: leim.ie/paragraph-builder.html

1. What is a paragraph?

A group of sentences about one main idea. One paragraph = one idea. A sentence about something else doesn't belong: move it or cut it.

2. How a paragraph is built

Topic sentencethe main idea, usually first: Dogs are loyal and loving pets.
Supporting sentencesdetails, facts, examples, reasons: A dog will often wait by the door for its owner to come home.
Linka last line that sums up or leads into the next paragraph

3. Spot the topic sentence

It could be a title for all the other sentences, and every other sentence links back to it: Puffins are some of the most unusual birds in Ireland. → puffins arrive every spring · these birds dig burrows · their beaks look unusual.

4. When to start a new paragraph: TiPToP

Timetime moves on: The next morning, … · Two hours later, …
Placea new place: Back at home, … · Inside the stadium, …
Topica new point or idea: Another reason … · On the other hand, …
Persona new person comes in or speaks: Mr Byrne stood up.

5. Linking words

OrderFirst, At first, Next, Then, After that, Finally, In the end
AddingIn addition, Also, What's more
ContrastHowever, On the other hand
ExampleFor example, For instance
ResultAs a result, Because of this

Put a comma after a linking word at the start of a sentence: However, the rooms were not clean. One or two per paragraph is plenty.

6. A paragraph that argues a point

Point: School uniforms save families money.
Support: A uniform costs less than the clothes students would want every week. For example, a jumper and two shirts can last a whole year.
Link: For these reasons, uniforms should stay.

7. Showing paragraphs

In handwriting, leave a line between paragraphs or start the first line further in (indent). Be consistent.

Classic mistakes: one long block with no paragraphs · a new paragraph for every sentence · a topic sentence with nothing after it · a sentence that wanders off the point · "However" where there's no contrast.
Exam tip: before you write, jot down one word for each paragraph's main idea. Then each paragraph starts with a topic sentence about that word.

A. Which is the topic sentence?

  1. 1 A good morning meal could be porridge, fruit or toast. 2 Breakfast is the most important meal of the day. 3 After a long night, your body needs fuel to start the day. 4 Students who eat breakfast find it easier to concentrate. → Topic sentence:
  2. 1 This danger grows every year as more rubbish ends up in the ocean. 2 Many birds at sea feed tiny pieces of rubbish to their chicks. 3 Plastic is a huge danger to sea life. 4 Turtles often mistake plastic bags for jellyfish. → Topic sentence:

B. Which sentence doesn't belong?

  1. 1 Hurling is one of the fastest field games in the world. 2 The sliotar can travel at over 150 kilometres an hour, so the game never slows down. 3 Hurling players need quick hands to catch the ball in mid-air. 4 My sister prefers reading comics on a rainy afternoon. 5 Teams race from one end of the field to the other in seconds. → Doesn't belong:
  2. 1 Learning an instrument has many benefits for young people. 2 Learning music helps your memory. 3 Playing in a band is a great way for young people to meet friends. 4 Another benefit is the confidence you get from performing on stage. 5 The price of bread went up last month. → Doesn't belong:

C. Put A, B and C in the right order after the topic sentence

  1. Making a cup of tea is easy. A Next, pour the water over a tea bag in a mug. B First, boil the water in the kettle. C Finally, add milk if you like it. → Order:
  2. The fire drill went smoothly. A Then, we lined up and walked out to the yard. B Finally, the teachers called the roll. C First, the alarm rang loudly. → Order:

D. A new paragraph starts at // . Why? Write Time, Place, Topic or Person.

  1. … The rest of the class went quiet. // Mr Byrne cleared his throat and stood up.
  2. … The car park was packed with buses. // Inside the stadium, the noise was deafening.
  3. … Mam waved goodbye as the bus pulled away. // Two hours later, the bus finally stopped for lunch.
  4. … School uniforms save families money. // Another reason for uniforms is that they stop bullying about clothes.

E. Fill each gap with a linking word (use each once)

As a result · Because of this · For example · However · In addition · On the other hand
  1. Heavy snow fell all night. _____________, the school was closed the next day.
  2. The hotel looked lovely in the photos. _____________, the rooms were not very clean.
  3. The film got great reviews. _____________, I found it boring and could not wait for it to end.
  4. Walking is good for your body. _____________, it gives your mind a rest.
  5. The bus broke down on the motorway. _____________, we had to walk the rest of the way.
  6. Ireland has many beautiful islands. _____________, Achill has huge cliffs and empty beaches.

F. Write a topic sentence for these details

  1. The museum has a dinosaur skeleton. Visitors can try on a suit of armour. Entry is free on Sundays.
  2. We sang songs the whole way there on the bus. Every student in our class won a medal. The rain held off until we got home.

G. Exam-style

  1. This should be three paragraphs. Write the first two words of each new paragraph, and the TiPToP reason.
    We woke up to a white world. Snow covered every car and every roof on our road. School was closed, so we raced outside in our pyjamas. An hour later, our fingers were frozen. We had built a snowman taller than Dad. Granny phoned to say she was snowed in. So we made her a tray of hot scones and carried them through the snow.
  2. Write one paragraph: "Every town should have a skate park." Use a topic sentence, two supporting sentences and a linking word.

A1. sentence 2 (Breakfast is the most important meal of the day.) · 2. sentence 3 (Plastic is a huge danger to sea life.)
B3. sentence 4 (My sister prefers reading comics on a rainy afternoon.) · 4. sentence 5 (The price of bread went up last month.)
C5. B A C · 6. C A B
D7. Person · 8. Place · 9. Time · 10. Topic
E11. As a result · 12. On the other hand · 13. However · 14. In addition · 15. Because of this · 16. For example
(However / On the other hand and As a result / Because of this are in the same family, so either is right in those gaps. For instance / What's more also work for For example / In addition.)
FAny sentence that sums up all three details, e.g. 17. Our local museum is a brilliant day out. · 18. Our school tour was a day to remember.
G19. New paragraphs start at "An hour" (Time) and "Granny phoned" (Person).
20. e.g. Every town should have a skate park. Teenagers need somewhere safe to meet in the evenings. For example, the skate park in Clonakilty is busy every night. As a result, our council should build one this year.
