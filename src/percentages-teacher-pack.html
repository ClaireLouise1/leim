Percentages

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): N.2a, N.2b (percentages run through every money, statistics and probability question; VAT, interest and tax come later under N.2c)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can switch between fractions, decimals and percentages, order them, find a percentage of an amount without a calculator, write one amount as a percentage of another, increase and decrease by a percentage using a multiplier, find a percentage change, and work back to the original amount.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Percent Trios cards + Sale Shop A/B cards · p.3 Percentage Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: Which is bigger? Board 3/8 and 38%. Hands up for each. Ask both sides why. Don't settle it yet.
10 minPercent Trios (p.2 cards; one each, 24 cards = 8 trios). Students mingle and find the two people whose cards are worth the same as theirs (fraction, decimal, percentage). Trios stand in order from smallest to biggest. Feedback: how did you know 0.12 and 3/25 match? Revisit the lead-in (3/8 = 37.5%).
8 minGuided discovery: the swap trick. Board 8% of 25, 12% of 50, 36% of 75. Pairs work them out, then swap the numbers (25% of 8…). What do they notice? Why does it always work (both are x × y ÷ 100)? Then build 35% of 80 from 10% and 5%.
12 minSale Shop (p.2, pairs: Student A gets Shop A, Student B gets Shop B; don't show your card). Each works out their own sale prices, then they ask each other ("How much are the runners in your shop?") and decide where to buy each item. Feedback: which deal type ("% off" or "€ off") is better for cheap items? For dear ones?
10 minPercentage Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, and build the chain to FINISH. Answer chain below.
12 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name the rule, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone. Feedback on question 4: why can't you add 15% back on?
3 minExit ticket. 15% of €60, and the price before 10% off if it now costs €45 (€9; €50). Homework: play Percent Pop at leim.ie/percent-pop.html (Back to the start round) and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Percent Trios key: 1/4 = 0.25 = 25% · 3/5 = 0.6 = 60% · 1/8 = 0.125 = 12.5% · 7/20 = 0.35 = 35% · 9/10 = 0.9 = 90% · 3/25 = 0.12 = 12% · 3/2 = 1.5 = 150% · 1/20 = 0.05 = 5%.
Errors to look for: 6% = 0.6 · 3/8 = 38% · "10% more" = add €10 · dividing by the new amount for a percentage change · adding the % back on to find an old price · multiplying by 0.15 to "take 15% off" · €46.5 instead of €46.50. Support: a 100-square grid on the desk and the 10% / 5% / 1% ladder. Fast finishers: a price goes up 10% then down 10%: is it back where it started? Which is better for the shopper, 20% off then 10% off, or 30% off?

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. A €48 concert ticket goes up by 15%. Find the new price.
  2. In a survey of 120 students, 36 said maths is their favourite subject. What percentage is that? Show it on a 100-square or a bar.

  4. After a 15% discount, a game costs €34. (a) Find the price before the discount. (b) Niamh says "34 × 1.15". What answer does she get and why is it wrong?
  5. Of the €240 Darragh earns in a month, he saves 35% and spends 25% on his phone and bus. How much is left for everything else?

A. Error Hunt

6% as a decimal = 0.6÷ 100 moves the digits 2 places: 0.06
3/8 as a percentage = 38%3 ÷ 8 = 0.375, × 100: 37.5%
Increase €70 by 10% = €80Added 10 euro, not 10%. 10% of €70 = €7: €77.00
€40 → €50: % increase = 20%Divided by the new price. 10 ÷ 40 × 100 = 25%
After 25% off it's €60. Old price? = €75Added 25% of €60 back on. €60 = 75%, so 1% = €0.80: €80.00
To decrease by 15%, multiply by… = 0.15You keep 85%: 0.85

B. Exam-style questions

  1. 48 × 1.15 = €55.20 (10% = €4.80, 5% = €2.40, so +€7.20)
  2. 36/120 × 100 = 30% (30 squares shaded)
  3. 2400 × 0.96 = €2,304; 2304 × 0.96 = €2,211.84 (not €2,208: the second 4% is of €2,304)
  4. (a) €34 = 85%, 1% = €0.40, 100% = €40.00 (b) 34 × 1.15 = €39.10: the 15% was of the old price, not €34
  5. Saves 35% = €84, spends 25% = €60; left: 240 − 84 − 60 = €96.00 (or 40% of €240)

Marking tip

Give credit for each step, as the exam does: "10% = €4.80" written down earns marks even if the final line slips. Insist on 2 decimal places for money and on a sense check (a sale price is lower; an increase gives a bigger number).
