1. The time word decides the tense

Time wordsTenseExample
yesterday, last week, two years agopastI walked to school.
every day, always, usually, oftenpresentGran walks to Mass.
tomorrow, next year, soonfutureI will walk to school.

2. Keep it steady

A story in the past stays in the past. Check every verb when you read your work back.

I opened the door and see saw a fox.

3. Make it agree

4. Saw or seen?

“I seen it” and “I done it” are common in spoken Irish English. In standard written English (and in the exam) write I saw it, I did it.

5. I or me?

Doing itGetting it
I · he · she · we · theyme · him · her · us · them
Tom and I went.The coach picked Tom and me.
The test: take away the other person. “picked Tom and I” → “picked I”? No: “picked me”.

Clear pronouns: if “she” could mean two people, use a name.

Exam tip: there is no separate grammar question, but every writing answer is marked partly on accuracy. Save two minutes to read your answer back and check every verb and pronoun.

F. Exam-style

  1. Rewrite this so every verb is in the past: “The bell rang and everyone runs outside. Aoife grabbed her coat and drops her phone. It is smashed.”
  2. Correct the mistakes: (a) My friends and me is going to the cinema. (b) I seen it last week. Explain each fix in a few words.

Answers

A1. moved (past) · 2. walks (present) · 3. closes (present) · 4. will fly (future) · 5. will tidy (future) · 6. watched (past)
B7. knocks → knocked · 8. takes → took · 9. picks → picked
C10. go · 11. are · 12. was · 13. is · 14. keeps · 15. are · 16. was · 17. has
D18. given · 19. begun · 20. forgotten · 21. saw · 22. came · 23. went · 24. sung · 25. did
E26. I · 27. he · 28. them · 29. her · 30. they · 31. me
F32. The bell rang and everyone ran outside. Aoife grabbed her coat and dropped her phone. It was smashed.
33. (a) My friends and I are going to the cinema. (“I” because it is doing the going; “are” because two people is plural; putting yourself last is good manners.) (b) I saw it last week. (no helper verb, so “saw”; “seen” needs has / have.)
