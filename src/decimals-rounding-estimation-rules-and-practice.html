Decimals, rounding & estimation

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): N.1e, N.2a. Play the game: leim.ie/round-it.html

1. Place value

TensUnits.TenthsHundredthsThousandths
25.473

In 25.473 the 7 is worth 0.07. To compare decimals, give them the same number of places: 0.5 = 0.50 > 0.45.

2. × and ÷ by 10, 100, 1000

The digits move one place for every zero: left for × (bigger), right for ÷ (smaller). 3.47 × 100 = 347; 3.47 ÷ 100 = 0.0347.

3. Fractions ↔ decimals

1/2 = 0.5 · 1/4 = 0.25 · 3/4 = 0.75 · 1/5 = 0.2 · 1/8 = 0.125 · 1/10 = 0.1. Decimal → fraction: 0.35 = 35/100 = 7/20.

4. Decimal sums

+ − line up the points (7 − 2.35 → 7.00 − 2.35 = 4.65). × multiply without the points, then count the places: 0.3 × 0.4 = 0.12. ÷ make the divisor whole: 4.8 ÷ 0.6 = 48 ÷ 6 = 8.

5. Rounding

Find the last digit you keep. Look at the next digit: 5 or more round up, less than 5 leave it. Fill whole-number gaps with zeros.

2 decimal places6.28

Significant figures: count from the first digit that isn't zero. 0.004637 to 2 s.f. = 0.0046; 48372 to 2 s.f. = 48000.

6. Estimate first

Round every number to 1 significant figure: 4.87 × 21.3 ≈ 5 × 20 = 100. If your answer is about 10 times too big or small, check the decimal point.

Round at the end, not the middle: 4.56 × 2.34 = 10.6704 → 10.7. Rounding first (4.6 × 2.3 = 10.58) gives 10.6: wrong.
Exam tip: read the instruction twice. Decimal places and significant figures are different (0.04637: 2 d.p. = 0.05, 2 s.f. = 0.046). Money always has 2 decimal places: €9.80, not €9.8. When the question says "estimate", write the rounded sum down.
