Poetry 2: theme, tone & comparing poems

In 1st year you learned how poets write: similes, metaphors, sound. In 2nd year the questions go deeper: What is the poem really about? How does it make you feel? How does it compare with another poem?

Spot the pattern

Two short poems about the same thing.

AThe sea is calm this morning,
a silver sheet out to the island.
BThe sea is raging at the harbour,
spitting foam across the pier.

What do the two poems have in common? What is different? Which words make the difference?

Show me

Both are about the sea, so they share a theme: nature. But the mood is opposite: A is peaceful (“calm”, “silver sheet”), B is angry (“raging”, “spitting”). One or two words change everything. That is how you compare poems: same theme, different mood, and you quote the words that prove it.

Theme, mood and tone

💡 Themethe big idea the poem is about: friendship, loss, nature, home, growing up. Say it in a few words.
🎭 Moodthe feeling the poem creates in you, the reader: joyful, peaceful, tense…
🗣️ Tonethe poet's attitude, like a tone of voice: fond, angry, sad, playful.

The topic is what the poem describes (a dog, a kitchen). The theme is what it says about life (“losing a pet shows how much we loved it”).

Six moods and the words that make them

😊 Joyfullaughter, danced, cheered, giggled
😢 Sadtears, lonely, sighed, wept
😌 Peacefulcalm, hush, softly, gentle
😨 Tenselurking, pounding, dread, crept
😠 Angryslammed, raging, glared, snarled
🕰️ Nostalgicremember, memories, recall: looking back fondly

When the mood turns

Many poems change mood halfway. Watch for a turn word at the start of a line: But, Then, Now, Until. In “The Beach”, the kites are dancing… “But then the tide came rushing in / and fear went through me like ice.” Joyful → tense. Say where the turn comes and quote the words on each side.

Comparing two poems

Use a frame: Both poems are about … However, poem A feels …, because “…”, whereas poem B feels …, because “…”. I prefer … because … Always quote from both poems.

Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

The paper usually prints an unseen poem (sometimes two) and asks about its theme, mood and language, and there is often a question on a poem you studied: what it is about, how it makes you feel, why you like it. Comparing two poems, or a poem and another text on the same theme, comes up too. Marks go to a clear point, a short quote, and an explanation of how the words create the effect.

How marks are won and lost

  • Name the mood or theme in one word, then prove it with a quote.
  • Explain the word: “raging” makes the sea sound like a furious person.
  • When comparing, talk about both poems in every paragraph, with words like both, whereas, however.
  • Don't just retell the poem line by line.
  • Don't confuse topic (a shop) with theme (how places change).

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). "Where the marks would go" is our guide, not an official marking scheme.

A · Saturday in Ennis
The market fills the square by nine,
striped awnings flapping in the breeze,
a man selling cheese and jokes,
a girl with a fiddle by the bank.
We buy warm bread and eat it walking,
laughing at nothing at all.
This town on a Saturday
is the best place in the world.
B · The Shop
Mr Kelly's shop is closed now.
The sign still says OPEN,
faded to the colour of tea.
I remember the jars of sweets,
the bell that rang above the door,
the way he called me “young lad”.
Now dust settles on the counter
and nobody rings the bell.
  1. What theme do both poems share? Explain briefly.
    Show a model answer

    Both poems are about home and place: what a town and its shops mean to the people who live there. Poem A celebrates the town as “the best place in the world”, while poem B shows how a place we love can change.
    Where the marks would go: one clear theme named; a link to both poems; a brief reference to each.

  2. What is the mood of poem A? Support your answer with two quotations.
    Show a model answer

    The mood is joyful and lively. The market is full of movement and fun: “striped awnings flapping in the breeze” and “a man selling cheese and jokes”. The friends are “laughing at nothing at all”, which shows a carefree happiness.
    Where the marks would go: a mood word; two short, accurate quotes; a sentence explaining how each one creates the mood.

  3. How does the mood change in poem B? Refer to the poem.
    Show a model answer

    The middle of the poem is nostalgic: the speaker fondly remembers “the jars of sweets” and the bell above the door. The turn comes with “Now”: “dust settles on the counter / and nobody rings the bell”. The mood becomes sad and empty, because the bell that once welcomed customers is silent.
    Where the marks would go: both moods named; the turn word or line identified; a quote from each part.

  4. Which poem do you prefer? Compare both poems in your answer.
    Show a model answer

    Both poems are about a town, but I prefer poem B. Poem A is cheerful and full of sounds, whereas poem B is quieter and more moving. The detail of the sign that “still says OPEN” is clever, because the shop is closed, and it made me think of shops in my own town. Poem A is fun, however poem B stays with you after you read it.
    Where the marks would go: a clear preference; reference to BOTH poems; comparing words (both, whereas, however); a personal response with reasons.
