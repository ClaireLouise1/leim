Integers & the order of operations

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): N.1a, N.1b, N.1c, U.1, U.2. Play the game: leim.ie/integer-rush.html

1. Spot the pattern

3 × (−2) = −6 · 2 × (−2) = −4 · 1 × (−2) = −2 · 0 × (−2) = 0 · −1 × (−2) = ?

Each answer goes up by 2, so the next one is +2. That's why a negative × a negative is positive.

2. The number line

Integers (ℤ) are … −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3 … Numbers get bigger to the right, so −2 > −8 (−2 °C is warmer than −8 °C).

3. Adding and subtracting

Add a positive → hop right−5 + 8 = 3
Subtract a positive → hop left2 − 7 = −5
− − side by side → +4 − (−3) = 4 + 3 = 7
+ − side by side → −6 + (−9) = 6 − 9 = −3
Both negative: add, keep the −−4 − 6 = −10

4. Multiplying and dividing

SignsAnswer
Same (+ +, or − −)positive: (−6) × (−3) = 18
Different (+ −)negative: 20 ÷ (−4) = −5

Lots of numbers? Count the minus signs: even → +, odd → −. (−2) × (−3) × (−1) = −6.

Squares: (−4)² = (−4) × (−4) = 16.

Watch out: these rules are for × and ÷. (−3) + (−5) = −8, not 8.

5. BIMDAS: the order of operations

Brackets(2 + 3) × 4 = 5 × 4 = 20Indices2 × 3² = 2 × 9 = 18
M D × ÷ left to right12 ÷ 3 × 2 = 4 × 2 = 8A S + − left to right10 − 4 + 3 = 9

2 + 3 × 4 = 2 + 12 = 14 (not 20). Laws: commutative (7 + 2 = 2 + 7, 4 × 5 = 5 × 4) · associative ((2 × 5) × 9 = 2 × (5 × 9)) · distributive (3 × (40 + 2) = 3 × 40 + 3 × 2 = 126).

Exam tip: integers are inside almost every question on the paper. Show every step (examiners give part marks for correct work), put negatives in brackets when you substitute ((−3)² = 9), and check your answer makes sense.
