Register and tone

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): OL12, R12, R13, W12

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can choose a formal or informal register to suit a reader and purpose, turn a blunt order into a polite request, name the tone of a short text with evidence, and swap slang for formal words.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Reader Match cards (cut-up) · p.3 Polite Swap A/B (speaking) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Register + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 2nd year. Time: about 60 minutes. All texts are original; names and places are made up.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Say “Give me your pen.” in a grumpy voice, then “Sorry, could I borrow your pen, please?” Which would you say to the principal? Why? Elicit register (how formal) and tone (the feeling).
8 minGuided discovery. Show the same news three ways (text to a friend, email to the principal, announcement: the game's “Spot the pattern”). What stays the same? What changes? Elicit: the facts stay, the words change with the reader.
12 minReader Match (p.2, groups of 3, one set of 24 cards). Match each reader card to the line that suits it. Then sort the pairs into formal and informal, and say which words gave it away.
12 minPolite Swap (p.3, pairs). A barks an order; B finds and says the polite version; then swap. Together they circle the polite tools (could you, would you mind, sorry…).
18 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Register in pairs, then the exam-style questions on the Tidy Towns notice alone.
5 minExit ticket. Write one line about a lost dog for (1) your best friend and (2) a local radio announcement. Homework: play Register Switch at leim.ie/register-switch.html.
Errors to look for: one slang word (“loads”, “deadly”) in a formal piece · “please!” added to an order and called polite · stiff formal language to a friend · naming a tone with no quote · confusing tone (the feeling) with register (how formal). Support: the poster (p.4) on the desk. Fast finishers: rewrite the Tidy Towns notice as a text to a friend.

B. Exam-style questions: "Ballinora Tidy Towns: Clean-Up Day"

Ballinora Tidy Towns: Clean-Up Day
We are delighted to announce that our autumn clean-up will take place on Saturday, 10 October, from ten o'clock. Volunteers are asked to meet at the community hall, where gloves and bags will be provided. Sadly, last year's flower beds were damaged by litter, so we would be grateful for every pair of hands. Refreshments will be served afterwards.

A reply posted under the notice:

omg I'm so in!! Can I bring my dog? He's deadly at finding cans lol
  1. Who is the notice written for, and what is its purpose?
  2. Is the notice formal or informal? Give two features that show it.
  3. Find one cheerful word and one sad word in the notice. Why does the writer use both?
  4. Name three things that make the reply informal.
  5. Rewrite the reply so it would suit an email to the Tidy Towns committee.

A. Fix the Register

To the county council: “Hey, the road outside our school is a total disaster!!”Too casual for the council: I am writing to report the dangerous state of the road outside our school.
Text to your friend: “I am writing to inform you that I will be late.”Too stiff for a friend: Running late, soz! Be there in ten.
To a hotel manager: “Gimme a refund asap.”Blunt and slangy: I would be grateful for a full refund.
Speech to the judges: “Cheers, lads, this is deadly!”Too casual for judges: Thank you very much for this award.
Note to a neighbour: “Move your car now!”Blunt: Would you mind moving your car? It is blocking our gate.
Letter to the editor: “Loads of people are well fed up with the buses.”Slang: Many people in our town are unhappy with the bus service.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. People in Ballinora (local residents); to invite volunteers to the clean-up day and give the details.
  2. Formal: full words (“We are delighted to announce”, “we would be grateful”); polite requests (“Volunteers are asked”); exact details (date, time, place); no slang or “!”.
  3. Cheerful: “delighted”; sad: “Sadly”. The sad detail (damaged flower beds) shows why help is needed; the cheerful words make people want to come.
  4. Slang and text speak (“omg”, “deadly”, “lol”); short forms (I'm, He's); “!!”; no capital letter at the start.
  5. Sample: Thank you for organising the clean-up. I would be happy to help on Saturday. Would it be possible to bring my dog? Credit full words, a polite question and no slang.

Marking tip

Reward the right register for the reader first, then polite, clear requests, then evidence (quoted words) for any tone named. Our guide, not an official marking scheme.
