Variables & expressions

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): AF.2a, AF.2b, AF.3a, U.4 (expanding brackets comes later in Algebra 2, AF.3b; forming and solving equations in Linear equations, AF.2c and AF.4)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can use the words variable, term, coefficient and constant; write expressions the short way; collect like terms (including x² terms and negatives); write expressions from words and real-life contexts; and substitute positive and negative numbers into expressions.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Think of a Number + Algebra Tiles Match cards · p.3 Algebra Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

6 minLead-in: Think of a Number. Everyone thinks of a number: add 4, double it, subtract 8, halve it. "Put your hand up if you got back to your own number!" How does it work? Don't explain yet.
8 minGuided discovery: like terms. Pairs test 3x + 2x and 5x with x = 2, 7 and 10, then 3x + 2y and 5xy with x = 2, y = 5. Which pair is always equal? Why? (3 lots of x plus 2 lots of x; x and y are different things.) Then x + x v x² with x = 3.
8 minBack to the trick. Board: n → n + 4 → 2n + 8 → 2n → n. Pairs prove the other two tricks on p.2 the same way (the second one always gives 5), then invent their own.
10 minAlgebra Tiles Match (p.2, 24 cards = 12 pairs; one card each). Tile pictures and word cards find their expression. Pairs check each other and sit together. Feedback: who had "5 less than n"? Why isn't it 5 − n?
12 minAlgebra Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4: start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left and build the chain to FINISH. Watch the substitution dominoes: brackets for negatives.
13 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name the rule, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone.
3 minExit ticket. Simplify 4a − 3b + 2a + 5b; find 3 − 2x when x = −4 (6a + 2b; 11). Homework: play Like-Term Sweep at leim.ie/like-term-sweep.html (Substitution Slot Machine round) and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Algebra Tiles Match key: tiles x² + 2x + 3 → x² + 2x + 3 · tiles 2x² + x → 2x² + x · tiles 4x + 2 → 4x + 2 · tiles x² + 5 → x² + 5 · tiles 2x² + 3x + 1 → 2x² + 3x + 1 · tiles 2x + 6 → 2x + 6 · 5 less than n → n − 5 · double n, then add 1 → 2n + 1 · subtract n from 5 → 5 − n · n squared, plus 5 → n² + 5 · half of n → n/2 · 5 more than double n → 2n + 5.
Errors to look for: 3x + 2y = 5xy · x² + x² = x⁴ · 7a − a = 7 · losing the sign in front of a term · "5 less than n" = 5 − n · (−3)² = −9 · 2x² with x = −3 worked out as (2 × −3)² · 2x with x = 3 read as 23. Support: use the tiles and real numbers to test every answer. Fast finishers: write a Think of a Number trick that always ends on 7.

Think of a Number. Try each trick with three different numbers. What do you notice? Then call your number n and write what happens at each step. Can you prove it always works?
  1. Think of a number. Add 4. Double it. Subtract 8. Halve it.
  2. Think of a number. Double it. Add 10. Halve it. Take away the number you first thought of.
  3. Think of a number. Multiply by 3. Add 6. Divide by 3. Take away 2.

Now invent your own trick that always gives back the number you started with.

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. Simplify 4x + 7 − x − 9 + 2y.
  2. a = 4 and b = −3. Find the value of (a) 2a + 3b (b) a² − b² (c) 5 − ab
  3. Concert tickets cost €25 each, plus a €4 booking fee for each order. (a) Write an expression for the cost, in euro, of n tickets in one order. (b) Find the cost of 6 tickets.
  4. A rectangle has length 2x + 3 and width x. (a) Write its perimeter in its simplest form. (b) Find the perimeter when x = 5.
  5. Think of a number, double it, add 10, halve it, then take away the number you first thought of. Use algebra to show that the answer is always 5.
For the teacher

Answers

↗ Léim

Think of a Number

  1. n → n + 4 → 2n + 8 → 2n → n (always your number)
  2. n → 2n → 2n + 10 → n + 5 → 5 (always 5)
  3. n → 3n → 3n + 6 → n + 2 → n (always your number)

A. Error Hunt

Simplify 3x + 2y = 5xyUnlike terms can't be added: 3x + 2y (it stays as it is)
Simplify x² + x² = x⁴Adding doesn't change the power: 2x²
Simplify 7a − a = 7The a doesn't disappear: 7a − 1a = 6a
Write "4 less than n" = 4 − nStart with n and take 4 away: n − 4
x² when x = −3 = −9(−3)² = (−3) × (−3) = 9
2x when x = 5 = 252x means 2 × x: 10

B. Exam-style questions

  1. x terms: 4 − 1 = 3; numbers: 7 − 9 = −2: 3x + 2y − 2
  2. (a) 2(4) + 3(−3) = 8 − 9 = −1 (b) 16 − 9 = 7 (c) 5 − (4)(−3) = 5 + 12 = 17
  3. (a) 25n + 4 (the fee is paid once per order) (b) 25(6) + 4 = €154
  4. (a) 2(2x + 3) + 2x = 6x + 6 (b) 6(5) + 6 = 36
  5. n → 2n → 2n + 10 → n + 5 → n + 5 − n = 5, whatever n is.

Marking tip

Give credit for each step, as the exam does: the line with the numbers in brackets earns marks even if the arithmetic slips. In "simplify" questions, accept the terms in any order (5 − n = −n + 5), but not unlike terms joined together.
