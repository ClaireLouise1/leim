1. The shape of a story

PartWhat it doesLook for
Settingwhere and whena time and a place: a wet Saturday morning on Kilkee beach
Problemsomething goes wronglost, missing, stuck, locked, broken, couldn't
Climaxthe big moment where everything turnsSuddenly · Just then · At that moment · All at once
Endinghow it works outIn the end · Finally · At last · From that day on

Your teacher may call these exposition, rising action, climax and resolution.

2. A strong first line

Dialogue“Don't open that door!” screamed Tara.
ActionOisín dived into the freezing lake before anyone could stop him.
MysteryThere was something strange about the new boy's shadow.

Not: This is a story about… · Hi, I'm going to tell you a story…

3. Twist endings

A twist surprises the reader but was set up by a clue earlier. When you look back, it makes sense.
(1) A parcel arrived. (2) It ticked quietly. (3) Grandad had been talking about his broken alarm clock.
Twist: It turned out to be a new alarm clock that Grandad had ordered online. (clue: line 3)
Cheats: an ending that nothing set up · It was all a dream.

4. Setting clues

Writers rarely say "at the beach". They show it with clue words: sand, gulls, the tide, seaweed. In an answer, quote the clue words and say what they help you picture.
Classic mistakes: too many characters for a short story · no real problem · "It was all a dream" · an ending from nowhere · telling the reader the place instead of showing it · retelling the whole plot when the question asks about one part.
Exam tip: when you write a short story, plan the ending first, then plant one clue for it early on. One problem, two or three characters, a strong first line.

Worked examples

Naming the part"Suddenly, a muddy brown head popped up from behind a rock pool." This is the climax: "Suddenly" signals the big moment, and the problem (the missing dog) is about to be solved.
Setting with evidenceThe story is set at a beach on a summer day. "Gulls screamed overhead" and "our sandcastle" help me hear and see the seaside.
Spotting the twistRead the ending, then look back: which line does it reach back to? That line is the planted clue. An ending that reaches back to nothing is not a twist.
Writing a twist1. Decide the surprise. 2. Plant one small clue early (an object, a habit, a remark). 3. Distract the reader with a more obvious explanation. 4. Reveal it in one or two sentences.
