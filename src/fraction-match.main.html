Look at the top and bottom numbers as you go down. What happens to both of them? Would 5/9 belong in this list? What about 5/10?

Show me

Each time, the top and the bottom are multiplied by the same number (1/2 → 2/4 is × 2, 1/2 → 3/6 is × 3). The pieces get smaller, but there are more of them, so the amount stays the same. These are equivalent fractions. 5/10 belongs (× 5); 5/9 doesn't: 9 is not 5 × 2.

Comparing fractions

Change them to the same denominator, then compare the tops. Which is bigger, 3/4 or 5/8? 3/4 = 6/8, and 6/8 is bigger than 5/8, so 3/4. For a list, use the LCM of all the bottoms (for 2/3, 3/5, 7/12 and 5/8 that's 120).

Add and subtract: same bottom first

  • Same denominator: add the tops, keep the bottom. 2/9 + 5/9 = 7/9.
  • Different denominators: change both to the LCM of the bottoms. 1/3 + 1/4 = 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12.
  • Mixed numbers: add the wholes, then the fractions: 2 1/3 + 1 3/4 = 3 + 4/12 + 9/12 = 3 + 13/12 = 4 1/12. If a subtraction won't go (3 1/5 − 1 3/5), change both to improper fractions first: 16/5 − 8/5 = 8/5 = 1 3/5.
Never add the bottoms. 1/2 + 1/3 is not 2/5 (2/5 is smaller than 1/2, so it can't be right). It's 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.

Multiply and divide: no common denominator needed

Multiplytops × tops, bottoms × bottoms2/3 × 3/5 = 6/15 = 2/5
Dividekeep, change, flip: keep the first, change ÷ to ×, flip the second3/4 ÷ 1/2 = 3/4 × 2/1 = 6/4 = 1 1/2
Mixed numberschange to improper fractions first1 1/2 × 2 2/3 = 3/2 × 8/3 = 24/6 = 4
"Of"means ×: divide by the bottom, times by the top3/5 of 40 = 40 ÷ 5 × 3 = 24
Sense check: multiplying by a fraction smaller than 1 makes a number smaller; dividing by it makes it bigger (how many halves in 3? 6).
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Fractions rarely get a question to themselves: they run through the whole paper. Expect them in Number questions (mixed numbers, fractions of amounts, "what fraction of the class…"), in money and ratio problems, in probability (answers are often fractions in simplest form), and in algebra (equations with fractions in them). No calculator is allowed on some questions, so you need the methods by hand.

How marks are won and lost

  • Show the common denominator (4/12 + 9/12), not just the answer. A correct method with one slip still earns part marks.
  • Finish the job: "simplest form" or "as a mixed number" means exactly that. 10/12 isn't finished; 5/6 is.
  • Read "of what is left" carefully: the second fraction is of the remainder, not of the original amount.
  • Answer the question asked. 5 ÷ 2/3 = 7 1/2, but if the question asks how many full batches, the answer is 7.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first.

  1. Mixed numbers. Work out (a) 2 1/3 + 1 3/4 and (b) 3 1/2 − 1 4/5. Give each answer as a mixed number.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) Wholes: 2 + 1 = 3. Fractions: 1/3 + 3/4 = 4/12 + 9/12 = 13/12 = 1 1/12. Total: 3 + 1 1/12 = 4 1/12.
    (b) 1/2 − 4/5 won't go, so use improper fractions: 7/2 − 9/5 = 35/10 − 18/10 = 17/10 = 1 7/10.

  2. Getting to school. A school has 840 students. 3/7 of them come by bus. 1/4 of the rest walk. (a) How many students walk? (b) What fraction of the whole school walks? Give your answer in its simplest form.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 3/7 of 840 = 840 ÷ 7 × 3 = 360 by bus. The rest: 840 − 360 = 480. 1/4 of 480 = 120 walk.
    (b) 120/840: divide top and bottom by 120 (the HCF) → 1/7.

  3. Baking. One batch of scones uses 2/3 of a cup of flour. Niamh has 5 cups of flour. How many full batches can she make?
    Show the worked answer

    5 ÷ 2/3 = 5/1 × 3/2 = 15/2 = 7 1/2. She can't make half a batch count, so 7 full batches. (Check: 7 × 2/3 = 14/3 = 4 2/3 cups, and 5 − 4 2/3 = 1/3 cup left over, not enough for another.)

  4. Order them. Write these fractions in order, starting with the smallest: 3/5, 2/3, 7/12, 5/8.
    Show the worked answer

    The LCM of 5, 3, 12 and 8 is 120. 3/5 = 72/120, 2/3 = 80/120, 7/12 = 70/120, 5/8 = 75/120.
    Smallest first: 7/12, 3/5, 5/8, 2/3.
