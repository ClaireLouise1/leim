The Junior Cycle English exam is one paper, 2 hours, 180 marks, at Higher and at Ordinary level. Every question links to the paper's theme, and the sections and marks change from year to year. You can't learn the paper off, but you can learn how to read a question and how to use your time.

1. Command words: the first word is the job

The question says…So you…
aIdentify, name, list, write down, pick out, findfind it in the text: short and exact
bDescribesay what it is like, with details
cExplain, why, howsay why or how, and back it up
dCompare, contrastshow how they are alike AND different
eDo you agree, do you think, in your opiniongive your view clearly, then reasons
fSummarise, sum upgive the main points only, short

2. Read the question twice

  • Underline the command word.
  • Circle how many: two reasons, three details.
  • Box the focus: which character, paragraph or feeling.
  • “With reference to the text” = point, quote, explain.

3. Time it by the marks

120 minutes ÷ 180 marks = ⅔ of a minute (40 seconds) a mark.

Marks152130456090
Minutes101420304060

When the time is up, finish your sentence and move on. Use the marks printed on your paper.

4. Writing tasks: form, reader, purpose

Write the text of a talk for your class about a place you love.Form: a talk (speak to them, “Thank you for listening”). Reader: your class (friendly, no slang). Purpose: to describe and share.

5. Read it back

Keep the last few minutes for missing words, spelling, full stops and capitals.

Exam tip: use the question's own words in your first sentence. It keeps you on the question, and the examiner sees it straight away.

E. Exam-style: plan the paper

A made-up paper (2 hours, 180 marks)Question 1 (A: Reading): 30 marksQuestion 2 (A: Reading): 30 marksQuestion 3 (B: Studied texts): 45 marksQuestion 4 (C: Writing): 75 marks
  1. Make a time plan: how many minutes for each question? Show how you worked it out.
  2. Read this question and say exactly what it asks you to do: “Explain two ways the writer makes the opening of the story exciting. Support your answer with reference to the text.”

Answers

Better: At the end, Seán feels relieved: he “let out a long breath” when his mam hugged him, which shows the worry has gone. 20. It only talks about ONE story. A comparison needs both, in every point. Better: Both stories open with a mystery, but the first one is set on a busy beach while the second starts alone in a dark house, so it feels more tense.
E21. Q1: 30 × ⅔ = 20 min · Q2: 30 × ⅔ = 20 min · Q3: 45 × ⅔ = 30 min · Q4: 75 × ⅔ = 50 min (total 120 min). In the real exam, keep a few of those minutes at the end to read back. 22. Command word: explain, so say how, not just what happens. How many: two ways, so two paragraphs. Focus: the opening only, and exciting. Reference: a short quote for each way, then what it does.
