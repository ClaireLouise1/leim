Junior Cycle Maths · Teacher pack

Quadratic & exponential patterns

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): AF.1a, AF.1b, AF.1c, N.4

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can tell linear, quadratic and exponential patterns apart (same jump, same 2nd difference, same multiplier), continue them, find far-away terms of growing tile patterns and solve doubling and tripling problems. Higher Level: find Tₙ = an² + bn + c and say which term has a given value.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Growth Match mingle cards + Pattern Swap A/B cards · p.3 Pattern Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 3rd year (Higher and Ordinary). Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: the rice and the chessboard. 1 grain on square 1, 2 on square 2, 4 on square 3 … Students guess square 10, then work it out (512). Compare with "add 100 each square". Which wins in the end?
8 minGuided discovery. Write 1, 4, 9, 16, 25. Pairs find the jumps, then the jumps of the jumps. Then 2, 5, 10, 17 and 3, 6, 12, 24 the same way. Elicit the three kinds: same jump (linear), same 2nd difference (quadratic), same multiplier (exponential).
10 minGrowth Match mingle (p.2, 24 cards = 12 pattern cards + 12 rule cards; one card each). Find your partner: the rule must fit all four terms. Pairs then say what kind of pattern they have.
12 minPattern Swap (p.2, pairs, don't show your card). Each number is on only ONE card. Ask for what you're missing, then fill in the blanks together. Feedback: tiles → quadratic, n² + 2, 102; bacteria → × 2, 1600, hour 9.
10 minPattern Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, and build the chain to FINISH.
12 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name it, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone. Question 5 is Higher Level.
3 minExit ticket. Linear, quadratic or exponential: 2, 6, 12, 20 and 2, 6, 18, 54? Next term of each? (Quadratic, 30; exponential, 162.) Homework: play Growth Spotter at leim.ie/growth-spotter.html.
Mingle key (pattern card → rule card): 22 → 5 · 23 → 4 · 20 → 9 · 6 → 3 · 15 → 18 · 17 → 11 · 12 → 1 · 16 → 2 · 10 → 7 · 21 → 19 · 24 → 8 · 14 → 13.
Errors to look for: adding the same jump again · stage 10 = 10 × stage 1 · + 2 instead of × 2 · a = the 2nd difference (it's half) · 2n, n² and 2ⁿ mixed up. Support: "Write the jumps under the terms." Fast finishers: invent a tile pattern with the rule n² + n + 1 and draw stages 1–3.

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. Fold a sheet of paper in half again and again. 1 fold → 2 layers. (a) How many layers after 3, 4 and 5 folds? (b) Write a rule for the layers after n folds. (c) How many folds until there are more than 1000 layers?
  2. Weeds in a garden triple every week. At the start there are 4. (a) How many after 4 weeks? (b) In which week are there first more than 2000?
  3. 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, … (a) Write the next two terms. (b) Linear, quadratic or exponential? Give a reason.
  4. A tile pattern has 3, 6, 11, 18 tiles in stages 1 to 4. Ciara says stage 8 has 6 × 8 = 48 tiles because stage 2 has 6. Is she right? Explain.
  5. Higher Level: 5, 12, 23, 38, … is quadratic. (a) Find Tₙ = an² + bn + c. (b) Which term is 107?

B. Exam-style questions

  1. (a) 8, 16, 32 (b) Tₙ = 2ⁿ (c) 2⁹ = 512, 2¹⁰ = 1024: 10 folds
  2. (a) 4 × 3⁴ = 324 (b) 4, 12, 36, 108, 324, 972, 2916: week 6
  3. (a) 21, 28 (b) jumps 2, 3, 4, 5; 2nd difference 1 (the same): quadratic
  4. No. The jumps grow (3, 5, 7), so it's quadratic, not × 3 each stage. Rule n² + 2: stage 8 = 64 + 2 = 66.
  5. (a) 2nd difference 4, a = 2; Tₙ − 2n² = 3, 4, 5, 6 = n + 2: Tₙ = 2n² + n + 2 (b) 2n² + n + 2 = 107 → 2n² + n − 105 = 0 → (2n + 15)(n − 7) = 0 → term 7

Marking tip

Give credit for each step, as the exam does: the differences written out, the kind of pattern with a reason, the rule, the checked answer. "It goes up" or "it gets bigger" is not a reason: look for the jumps or the multiplier.
