Indices & standard form

Spot the pattern

Write each one out in full and count the factors:

  • 2³ × 2⁴ = (2 × 2 × 2) × (2 × 2 × 2 × 2) = 2?
  • 3² × 3⁵ = (3 × 3) × (3 × 3 × 3 × 3 × 3) = 3?
  • x⁴ × x² = (x × x × x × x) × (x × x) = x?
  • 2⁶ ÷ 2² = (2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2) ÷ (2 × 2) = 2?

What happens to the indices when you multiply? And when you divide? Could you have got the answer without writing it all out?

Show me

2⁷, 3⁷, x⁶ and 2⁴. When you multiply powers of the same number you add the indices (3 + 4 = 7). When you divide, the 2s cancel in pairs, so you subtract (6 − 2 = 4). These are the laws of indices, and they only work when the base is the same.

Index notation

In 2⁵, 2 is the base and 5 is the index (or power). It means five 2s multiplied: 2⁵ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32, not 2 × 5 = 10.

  • Squares: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144. Cubes: 1, 8, 27, 64, 125.
  • Powers of 10: 10³ = 1,000 (3 zeros), 10⁶ = 1,000,000 (6 zeros).
  • Minus signs: (−3)² = (−3) × (−3) = 9, but −3² = −(3 × 3) = −9. The index only belongs to what it touches. (−2)³ = −8: an odd number of minuses gives a minus.

Square roots

√49 = 7 because 7 × 7 = 49. A power of ½ means the same thing: 49½ = √49 = 7.

  • Not a perfect square? Find the squares either side: 7² = 49 and 8² = 64, so √50 is between 7 and 8 (just above 7).
  • √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10. It is not √36 + √64 = 14: do the inside first.

BIMDAS with indices

Brackets, then Indices (and roots), then Divide/Multiply, then Add/Subtract.

5 + 3² × 2
= 5 + 9 × 2  (indices)
= 5 + 18  (multiply)
= 23  (not 8² × 2 = 128)

4 × 2³ = 4 × 8 = 32, not 8³. And (2 + 3)² = 5² = 25, not 4 + 9.

The laws of indices

ap × aq = ap+qMultiply: add the indices.
x³ × x⁵ = x⁸
ap ÷ aq = ap−qDivide: subtract the indices.
5⁹ ÷ 5³ = 5⁶
(ap)q = apqPower of a power: multiply.
(2³)⁴ = 2¹²
  • Numbers in front: deal with them separately. 3x² × 4x⁵ = 12x⁷ (3 × 4 = 12, 2 + 5 = 7). 12x⁷ ÷ 3x² = 4x⁵.
  • Different letters: one at a time. 2x²y × 3xy³ = 6x³y⁴.
  • The base never changes: 2³ × 2⁴ = 2⁷, not 4⁷.
  • Adding is different: x³ + x⁴ can't be made into one term. And 2³ × 5² has different bases, so just work it out: 8 × 25 = 200.
  • Find n: 2ⁿ × 2³ = 2¹⁰ means n + 3 = 10, so n = 7.

Zero, negative and fractional indices Higher Level

  • a⁰ = 1. Why? 5³ ÷ 5³ = 1, and by the law it's 5⁰. (Any number except 0.)
  • a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ. 2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8. A negative index means "one over"; it does not make the answer negative.
  • Fractional indices: the bottom is the root, the top is the power. 81/3 = cube root of 8 = 2. 272/3 = (cube root of 27)² = 3² = 9. Take the root first: the numbers stay small.
  • Brackets: everything inside gets the power. (2x³)² = 2²x⁶ = 4x⁶ (not 2x⁶). (2/3)² = 4/9.
  • Solving: make the bases the same. 2ˣ = 1/8 = 2⁻³, so x = −3. 9ˣ = 3 = 91/2, so x = ½.

Standard form

A number in standard form is a × 10ⁿ, where a is between 1 and 10 (1 is allowed, 10 isn't) and n is a whole number.

45,000 → put the point after the first digit: 4.5
The point moved 4 places → 4.5 × 10⁴
Back again: 3.07 × 10⁵ → move the point 5 places right → 307,000
  • Not standard form: 45 × 10³ (45 is too big), 0.45 × 10⁵ (0.45 is too small).
  • Comparing: look at the power of 10 first. 1.2 × 10⁵ is bigger than 9.8 × 10⁴.
  • Multiplying and dividing: numbers first, then the laws of indices, then tidy up. (4 × 10³) × (5 × 10²) = 20 × 10⁵ = 2 × 10⁶. (8 × 10⁹) ÷ (2 × 10³) = 4 × 10⁶.
  • Small numbers Higher Level: a negative index. 0.00036 = 3.6 × 10⁻⁴ (the point moved 4 places to the right).
  • Calculator: very big or very small answers may appear in standard form, sometimes shown like 2.4E9. Write it properly: 2.4 × 10⁹.

Simplifying surds like √12 = 2√3 comes later in Junior Cycle Maths (Higher Level).

Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Indices turn up as short parts ("write as a single power", "find n", "evaluate without a calculator") and inside bigger questions: algebra, patterns that double, and areas and volumes with squares and cubes. Standard form usually comes with a real-life context, such as distances in space, populations or computer storage, and often with a calculator step.

How marks are won and lost

  • Show the law you used: write 2⁵ × 2³ = 2⁵⁺³ = 2⁸, not just the answer.
  • Check the form asked for: "as a power of 2", "in standard form", "as a fraction". 20 × 10⁵ is the right value but not standard form.
  • Watch the minus signs: (−2)⁴ and −2⁴ are different, and 2⁻³ is not negative.
  • Calculator answers: copy standard form properly with × 10 and the index.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first.

  1. Powers of 2. (a) Write 2⁵ × 2³ as a single power of 2. (b) Hence write (2⁵ × 2³) ÷ 2⁶ as a power of 2 and work out its value. (c) Find n if 3ⁿ × 3² = 3⁹.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) Add the indices: 2⁵⁺³ = 2⁸.
    (b) 2⁸ ÷ 2⁶ = 2⁸⁻⁶ = 2² = 4.
    (c) n + 2 = 9, so n = 7. Check: 3⁷ × 3² = 3⁹ ✓

  2. Light from the Sun. The Sun is about 150,000,000 km from the Earth. Light travels about 300,000 km every second. (a) Write both numbers in standard form. (b) How many seconds does light take to reach the Earth? Give your answer in standard form, then in minutes and seconds.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 150,000,000 = 1.5 × 10⁸; 300,000 = 3 × 10⁵.
    (b) (1.5 × 10⁸) ÷ (3 × 10⁵): 1.5 ÷ 3 = 0.5 and 10⁸ ÷ 10⁵ = 10³, so 0.5 × 10³ = 5 × 10² seconds (500 s). 500 ÷ 60 = 8 remainder 20, so 8 minutes 20 seconds.

  3. No calculator. Work out (a) −2⁴ (b) (−2)⁴ (c) 3 + 2³ × 5 (d) √(6² + 8²). (e) Cian says √(6² + 8²) = 6 + 8 = 14. Explain his mistake.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) −(2 × 2 × 2 × 2) = −16. (b) (−2) × (−2) × (−2) × (−2) = 16.
    (c) Indices, then ×, then +: 3 + 8 × 5 = 3 + 40 = 43.
    (d) √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10.
    (e) The square root of a sum is not the sum of the square roots: the inside of the bracket must be worked out first (36 + 64 = 100).

  4. Higher Level. (a) Work out 272/3. (b) Write 2⁻³ as a fraction. (c) Simplify (2x³)² × x⁻⁴. (d) Solve 2ˣ = 1/16. (e) Write 0.00052 in standard form.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) Cube root of 27 = 3, then 3² = 9.
    (b) 1/2³ = 1/8.
    (c) (2x³)² = 4x⁶, then 4x⁶ × x⁻⁴ = 4x².
    (d) 1/16 = 2⁻⁴, so x = −4.
    (e) Move the point 4 places to the right: 5.2 × 10⁻⁴.
