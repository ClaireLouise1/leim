Poetry 2: theme & mood

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R7, R8, R9, R4, W8. Play the game: leim.ie/mood-match.html

In 1st year you learned how poets write: similes, metaphors, sound. Now the questions go deeper: What is the poem really about? How does it make you feel? How does it compare with another poem?

1. Theme, mood and tone

Themethe big idea: friendship, loss, nature, home and place, growing up. Say it in a few words.
Moodthe feeling the poem creates in you, the reader.
Tonethe poet's attitude, like a tone of voice: fond, angry, sad, playful.
Topicwhat the poem describes (a dog, a kitchen). Not the same as the theme!

2. Six moods and their words

Joyfullaughter, danced, cheered, giggled
Sadtears, lonely, sighed, wept
Peacefulcalm, hush, softly, gentle
Tenselurking, pounding, dread, crept
Angryslammed, raging, glared, snarled
Nostalgicremember, memories, recall: looking back fondly

3. Spot the pattern

AThe sea is calm this morning, / a silver sheet out to the island.
BThe sea is raging at the harbour, / spitting foam across the pier.

Same theme (nature, the sea), opposite mood: A is peaceful ("calm"), B is angry ("raging", "spitting"). One or two words change everything.

4. When the mood turns

Watch for a turn word at the start of a line: But, Then, Now, Until. We laughed and raced along the sand ... But then the tide came rushing in / and fear went through me like ice. Joyful → tense.

5. Comparing two poems

Both poems are about ... However, poem A feels ..., because "...", whereas poem B feels ..., because "...". I prefer ... because ...

Quote from both poems. Point, quote, explain: say how the word creates the mood.

Exam tip: name the mood or theme in one word, prove it with a short quote, then explain the word: "raging" makes the sea sound like a furious person. Don't just retell the poem.

F. Exam-style

A · The Pier at Dawn
Grandad and I on the pier at dawn,
the sea as calm as glass,
two rods, one flask of tea,
the gulls not even awake yet.
B · The Pier in January
The waves are raging at the pier,
spitting salt across the wall,
and I stand where we used to fish
with nobody beside me now.
  1. What is the mood of poem A? Support your answer with one quotation.
  2. Both poems are set on the same pier. Compare the moods of the two poems, quoting from both.

Answers

A1. tense · 2. nostalgic · 3. angry · 4. peaceful · 5. tense · 6. nostalgic · 7. joyful · 8. sad
B9. gentle (peaceful) · 10. softly (peaceful) · 11. laughter (joyful) · 12. memories (nostalgic) · 13. fists (angry) · 14. lonely (sad)
C15. friendship · 16. growing up · 17. friendship
D18. joyful → sad, turn word: But · 19. joyful → angry, turn word: Until
E20. theme: home & place; A joyful, B sad · 21. theme: home & place; A joyful, B sad
F22. Peaceful: "the sea as calm as glass", "the gulls not even awake yet". 23. Both poems are set on the same pier, but poem A is peaceful ("the sea as calm as glass") and close (Grandad and the speaker share "one flask of tea"), whereas poem B is angry and lonely: the waves are "raging" and "spitting", and the speaker stands "with nobody beside me now". The weather matches the feeling: B suggests a loss.
