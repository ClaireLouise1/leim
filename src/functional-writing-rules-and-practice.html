Functional writing

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): W4, W3, R13. Play the game: leim.ie/format-sort.html

Functional writing does a job for a real reader. Before you write, ask: What type of text is it? Who is reading it? What does it need to do? The answers decide your layout, your opening and your tone.

1. Five text types

TypeLook for
Report"Report on ..." title, terms of reference, findings (numbers), recommendations, signed and dated. Formal.
Reviewdetails (title, director or author, price), praise, criticism, a verdict or star rating. No spoilers.
Articleheadline, byline (By ...), who / what / where / when in the first paragraph, quotes, subheadings.
Blogtitle and date, first person, chatty, talks to readers, asks for comments, hashtags.
Speechgreets the audience, signposts, rhetorical questions, "Thank you for listening."

2. Headlines

Short (under eight words), the main fact, present tense, little words dropped: Bees Close Bray Playground. "Big News Today" tells the reader nothing.

3. Same news, four ways

Report: Of the 120 students surveyed, 85% said they would use a skate park.
Article: By Ellen Daly: Students in Carrick will skate at school from Friday, according to the principal.
Blog: Hi everyone! Our skate park opens on Friday. Comment below if you're coming!
Speech: Good morning, everyone. Fellow students, on Friday our skate park opens.

4. How a report is laid out

Report on Lunchtime Litter at St Brigid's College
Introduction: This report was requested by the principal, Ms Byrne, to find out why the yard is full of litter.
Findings: Of the 150 students surveyed, 90 said there are not enough bins.
Recommendations: I recommend that four more bins are placed in the yard.
Signed: Aoife Byrne, Student Council Secretary, 12 May 2026

5. How a review works

Detailstitle, director / author, length, price
Praisewhat's good, with a reason
Criticismwhat's weak, fairly
Verdictstars or a score; who would enjoy it

6. Tone for the reader

Formal for a principal, a board or a council (report, letter to a paper). Lively for a school magazine review. Chatty for a blog. A speech talks to the audience: "you", "we", "our".

Exam tip: show the format from the first line (a report title, a headline + byline, a greeting) and use the features the task names: if it asks for two recommendations, give two.

E. Exam-style

THE FACTS: BALLYDUFF COMMUNITY GARDEN
The old tennis court behind Ballyduff Community School has become a vegetable garden.
40 students and 12 neighbours helped to build it over the summer.
It opens to the public on Saturday 6 June at 11 a.m.
Of the 90 students surveyed, 71 said they would like to work in the garden at lunchtime.
Ms Daly, the science teacher: "The students have done something amazing."
  1. Write a headline and the first paragraph of an article about the garden for the local newspaper.
  2. The board of management wants a report. Write the title, the terms of reference and one recommendation.
  3. Write the opening of a blog post about the garden for the school website. Name two blog features you used.
