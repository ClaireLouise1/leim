Need a refresher?

Problem solving

Most exam questions join two or three topics: an area becomes a cost, a speed becomes a clock time. Each step on its own is easy. The skill is spotting the steps.

Spot the pattern

  • 🌱 Garden 10 m by 6 m · fence €20 per m → perimeter 32 m → €640
  • 🧱 Wall 8 m by 3 m · tin covers 5 m² → area 24 m² → 4.8 → 5 tins
  • 🚗 150 km at 60 km/h, leaves 10:15 → 2.5 h = 2 h 30 min → 12:45

What happens in the middle of each one? Why is it 5 tins and not 4.8?

Show me

Each answer from step 1 is the input to step 2. Find the hidden middle number first (perimeter, area, time).
Then check it makes sense: you can't buy 0.8 of a tin, so round up. And 2.5 hours is 2 h 30 min, not 2 h 50 min.

Four steps for any problem

1. ReadUnderline the numbers and the units. What is the question asking for?
2. PlanWhich topic? What do I need first? Draw a sketch.
3. DoOne step per line. Write what each number is.
4. CheckUnits? Sensible size? Round the right way? Answer the question asked.

Traps to watch for

  • Two discounts: 20% off then 10% off is NOT 30% off. €100 → €80 → €72.
  • Units: 1 litre = 1000 cm³. 1 m = 100 cm. Change them before you start.
  • Rounding: tins, buses, boxes → round up. "How many can you build/buy?" → round down.
  • Time: 1.25 h = 1 h 15 min. 0.1 h = 6 min.
  • Working backwards: mean 70 over 5 tests needs a total of 5 × 70 = 350.

Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Everywhere. The paper has about 13 compulsory questions, and the long ones (often the last few) are in context and join strands: a floor plan with a cost, a journey with a graph, a survey with a probability. Every part builds on the one before.

How marks are won and lost

  • Show every step. A right method with a slip still earns most of the marks. A bare wrong answer earns none.
  • Carry your answer on. If part (a) is wrong but you use it correctly in (b), you can still get (b).
  • Units and rounding as the question asks.
  • Never leave a part blank. Write down the formula or a first step.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first.

  1. A rectangular patio is 6 m by 4.5 m. Paving costs €38 per m². Find the total cost, including VAT at 13.5%.
    Show the worked answer

    Area = 6 × 4.5 = 27 m².
    Cost before VAT = 27 × €38 = €1026.
    VAT: €1026 × 1.135 = €1164.51.

  2. Orla drives 180 km. She drives the first 120 km at 80 km/h and the rest at 60 km/h. She leaves at 08:50. When does she arrive?
    Show the worked answer

    120 ÷ 80 = 1.5 h. 60 ÷ 60 = 1 h. Total 2.5 h = 2 h 30 min.
    08:50 + 2 h 30 min = 11:20.

  3. A bag has 6 red and some blue counters. The probability of picking red is 2/5. How many blue counters are there? Then 4 more red are added. Find the new probability of red.
    Show the worked answer

    6 is 2 parts out of 5, so 1 part = 3 and the total is 15. Blue = 15 − 6 = 9.
    New: 10 red out of 19. P(red) = 10/19.

  4. Higher Level A garden is a rectangle. Its length is 3 m more than its width, and its area is 70 m². Find the width. Then find the length of the diagonal path, correct to 1 decimal place.
    Show the worked answer

    x(x + 3) = 70 → x² + 3x − 70 = 0 → (x + 10)(x − 7) = 0 → x = 7 (a width can't be −10).
    Width 7 m, length 10 m.
    Diagonal = √(7² + 10²) = √149 = 12.206… = 12.2 m.
