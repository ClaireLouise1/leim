Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

Film: shots, sound & key scenes

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R9, R7, OL8, W8

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can name the five shot sizes and the main camera angles, tell diegetic from non-diegetic sound, recognise quick cuts, slow motion, flashback, fade and montage, and write about a key scene by naming a technique, describing the moment and explaining its effect.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Shot Sort (20 cut-up frames) · p.3 Storyboard Swap A/B (pair listening + sketching) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Answer + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 3rd year. Time: about 60 minutes. Works with any studied film: every frame and scene here is made up by Léim.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Make a frame with your hands and “film” a student from across the room, then from close up. What can we see each time? What do we feel? Introduce shot sizes and angles (poster, p.4).
12 minShot Sort (p.2, groups of 3, one set of 20 frames). Sort into five piles from furthest to closest: extreme long shot, long shot, medium shot, close-up, extreme close-up. 4 cards in each pile. Then pick one card and say what that shot is good for.
15 minStoryboard Swap (p.3, pairs). A reads a scene aloud shot by shot; B sketches it and labels the techniques and sounds. Swap. Which shot builds the most tension?
5 minSound. Play 30 seconds of the studied film with eyes closed. List every sound: diegetic or non-diegetic? What does the music tell us to feel?
18 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Answer in pairs, then the exam-style questions on “The Lighthouse” alone.
5 minExit ticket. One key scene from the studied film + one technique in it + its effect. Homework: play Shot Spotter at leim.ie/shot-spotter.html.
Errors to look for: “the camera shows” with no technique named · retelling the plot · mixing up low and high angle (low angle = camera low, looking up) · thinking diegetic means sad or happy music · naming a technique without its effect · not naming the film or director. Support: give the poster as a word bank. Fast finishers: storyboard the opening of their studied film in four shots.

B. Exam-style questions: a key scene

The Lighthouse (an imaginary film). Night. Ruth (15) climbs the lighthouse steps to light the lamp her grandfather always lit. Shot 1: a bird's-eye view of the spiral staircase, Ruth tiny at the bottom. Shot 2: quick cuts: her feet on the steps, her hand on the rail, the storm through a window. Shot 3: a flashback: Ruth, aged five, on her grandfather's shoulders at the top. Shot 4: a close-up of her face lit gold as the lamp turns on. Sound: wind and her breathing (diegetic); the soundtrack is silent until the lamp lights, then strings swell.
  1. Why do you think the director uses a bird's-eye view in Shot 1?
  2. How do the quick cuts in Shot 2 affect the audience?
  3. Why is the flashback placed here, in the middle of the climb?
  4. Explain the difference between the diegetic and non-diegetic sound in this scene. Why does the soundtrack stay silent until the lamp lights?
  5. Choose a key scene from a film you have studied. Name two techniques the director uses and explain the effect of each.

A. Fix the Answer

The camera shows the boy in the field.no technique named; which shot? (e.g. “an extreme long shot makes him look tiny and alone in the empty field”)
There is music in this scene and it is good.name the kind of music and its effect: “slow piano on the soundtrack makes the scene sad”
Then she goes home and then her dad is there and then they talk.plot retelling, no technique: one sentence of plot, then the film language
A close-up is when the camera is close.defines the term but says nothing about this scene: what does the close-up show us?
The low angle makes him look small.wrong effect: a low angle looks up, so he looks big and powerful
The music is diegetic because it is sad.diegetic means the characters can hear it, not the mood: is it a radio in the scene, or the soundtrack?

B. Exam-style questions

  1. Looking straight down the spiral makes the climb look long and dizzying, and Ruth tiny at the bottom: the task seems huge for one girl.
  2. The short, fast shots speed up the pace and build tension: we feel her rush and her nerves as the storm rages.
  3. It reminds us why the climb matters: she is doing it for her grandfather, so the moment becomes about memory and love, not just the storm.
  4. Diegetic: the wind and her breathing (Ruth can hear them); non-diegetic: the strings (added for us). The silence keeps the tension and makes us listen to her breathing; when the strings swell as the lamp lights, it feels like a release and a victory.
  5. Reward: a specific scene; two techniques correctly named; each described at a moment and linked to its effect on the audience; film title and director named; little plot retelling.
