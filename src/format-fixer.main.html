Show me

The formal letter adds their address on the left, opens with Dear Sir/Madam, says why it is written in the first line, ends Yours faithfully and gives the full name. The informal letter uses short forms (I'm, Can't), a “!” and a friendly sign-off with just a first name. Both put your address top right with the date under it.

The layout of a formal letter

🏠 Your addressTop right. No name.
📅 DateUnder your address: 3 March 2026.
🏢 Their addressOn the left, lower down: The Manager, …
👋 GreetingDear Sir/Madam, · Dear Ms Kelly,
🎯 Opening lineWhy you are writing: I am writing to…
🙏 Closing lineI look forward to hearing from you.
✍️ Sign-offYours faithfully, / Yours sincerely, then your full name.

Faithfully or sincerely?

No nameDear Sir/Madam, → Yours faithfully,
A nameDear Mr Byrne, → Yours sincerely,
A friendHi Aoife, → Talk soon, / Love,
💡 Memory trickSir and sincerely never go together.

Capital Y on “Yours”, small f or s on “faithfully” / “sincerely”, then a comma.

Formal or informal?

📨 FormalFull words: I am, I cannot, I would like. Polite and clear. No slang, no “!!”, no emoji.
😊 InformalChatty: I'm, can't, Guess what! Slang is fine. First names.
📧 EmailsA short subject line. Greeting, opening line, paragraphs, sign-off and full name, like a letter. No addresses or date needed.
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

The paper often asks you to write a letter or email: to complain, to apply, to ask, to thank, or to a friend. Some questions also ask you to read one and say who it is for and how you can tell. The marks reward the right layout and register (how formal you are) for your reader, as well as what you say.

How marks are won and lost

  • Read the question twice: who is it to, and why? That decides formal or informal.
  • Layout: addresses, date, greeting and sign-off in the right places (a subject line for an email).
  • Say why you are writing in your first line. One idea per paragraph.
  • Stay in one register: one “gonna” or “!!” in a formal letter loses marks.
  • Faithfully or sincerely: match it to your greeting.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). "Where the marks would go" is our guide, not an official marking scheme.

  1. You ordered a bike helmet online from Pedal Power and it arrived cracked. Write the subject line, greeting, opening line, closing line and sign-off of a formal email to the shop.
    Show a model answer

    Subject: Cracked helmet, order 4471
    Dear Sir/Madam,
    I am writing to complain about a bike helmet I ordered from your website, which arrived cracked on 9 May.
    …
    I would be grateful if you could send me a replacement or a full refund.
    Yours faithfully,
    Ella Byrne
    Where the marks would go: a short, clear subject line; Dear Sir/Madam with Yours faithfully (no name given); the reason for writing in the first line, with a detail (the date); a polite closing line that says what you want; your full name.

  2. Find and fix four mistakes in this letter to a manager.
    Hi Manager,
    I'm writing about the summer job in your window. I'd be well up for it!! I have helped in my aunt's shop for a year.
    Yours sincerely,
    Leah
    Show a model answer

    (1) “Hi Manager” → Dear Sir/Madam, (2) “I'm writing” / “I'd be well up for it!!” → I am writing to apply for… / I would be very interested in the job. (3) “Yours sincerely” → Yours faithfully, (no name was used in the greeting). (4) “Leah” → her full name, Leah Byrne. Also missing: her address and the date top right, the shop's address on the left.
    Where the marks would go: one mark for each mistake found and fixed, with the reason.

  3. Rewrite in a formal register: “Hey, the bus was super late, like an hour. Can ye sort it out asap?”
    Show a model answer

    “Your bus was an hour late on Tuesday morning. I would be grateful if you could look into this as soon as possible.”
    Where the marks would go: no slang (hey, super, like, ye, asap); full words; a polite request; the facts kept (an hour late).

  4. Write a short informal email inviting your cousin to your birthday party. Then name two things that make it informal.
    Show a model answer

    Subject: Party!
    Hi Sam,
    Guess what? I'm having a party on Saturday at the bowling alley in Tralee. Can you come? Mam says she'll collect you at two.
    Talk soon,
    Cian
    Two things: short forms (I'm, she'll) and a chatty greeting and sign-off with first names (Hi Sam, Talk soon, Cian).
    Where the marks would go: the details a guest needs (when, where, a question to answer), a friendly tone that suits a cousin, and two features named correctly.
