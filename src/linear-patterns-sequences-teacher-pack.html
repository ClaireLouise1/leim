Junior Cycle Maths · Teacher pack

Linear patterns & sequences

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): N.4, AF.1a, AF.1b, U.4, U.11 (quadratic and doubling patterns come later, AF.1c; function notation and graphs of functions in Functions, AF.7)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can continue a linear pattern from a list, a picture or a table; find the common difference and the general term Tₙ; find far-away terms; work backwards to find which term a value is (and whether it's in the pattern); and link tables, graphs and formulas using start value and rate of change.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Build It task + Pattern Detectives cards · p.3 Pattern Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Draw stages 1–3 of the matchstick squares on the board. "How many matchsticks for stage 4? For stage 100?" Collect guesses for stage 100 (expect 400). Don't judge yet.
12 minBuild It (p.2), groups of 3: build (or draw) stages 1–4 of the group's pattern with matchsticks, lollipop sticks or pencils, fill in the table, and explain in words why it goes up by the same amount each time. Find stage 10 and stage 100 and justify. Groups explain "where the + 1 comes from" (guided discovery: the first stick, counted once).
8 minPull it together. Board: common difference → dn → fix with T₁ → check T₂. Show the classic mistake ("n + 3") and test it with n = 1. Then working backwards: which stage uses 100 matchsticks?
10 minPattern Detectives (p.2, 24 cards = 12 pairs; one card each). Pictures, lists, a table and a story find their rule. Pairs check each other with two terms and sit together.
12 minPattern Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4: start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left and build the chain to FINISH.
10 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name the rule, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone.
3 minExit ticket. Find Tₙ for 8, 13, 18, … and say whether 100 is a term (5n + 3; no: 97 ÷ 5 = 19.4). Homework: play Pattern Builder at leim.ie/pattern-builder.html (Find the rule round) and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Pattern Detectives key: row of squares → 3n + 1 · row of triangles → 2n + 1 · row of houses → 5n + 1 · double row of squares → 5n + 2 · 5, 9, 13, 17, … → 4n + 1 · 2, 8, 14, 20, … → 6n − 4 · 10, 8, 6, 4, … → 12 − 2n · 7, 10, 13, 16, … → 3n + 4 · 1, 6, 11, 16, … → 5n − 4 · 9, 11, 13, 15, … → 2n + 7 · n: 1, 2, 3, 4; Tₙ: 3, 10, 17, 24 → 7n − 4 · €25 saved, plus €5 a week: savings after n weeks → 5n + 25.
Errors to look for: Tₙ = n + d (the jump added, not multiplied) · stage 10 = 10 × stage 1 · 10th term = T₁ + 10d · a positive common difference for a falling pattern · "is 50 a term?" answered from a rounded division · reading the graph's start value at x = 1. Support: build and count. Fast finishers: design a picture pattern with rule 4n + 3.

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. The sequence 3, 10, 17, 24, … (a) Find Tₙ. (b) Find T₁₅. (c) Is 150 a term? Explain.
  2. A row of matchstick houses: 1 house uses 6 matchsticks, 2 houses use 11, 3 houses use 16. (a) Write a rule for n houses. (b) How many matchsticks for 20 houses? (c) Tom has 200 matchsticks. How many houses can he make in one row, and how many matchsticks are left over?
  3. A gym charges a €40 joining fee plus €25 a month. (a) Write a formula for the total cost after m months. (b) Find the cost for 6 months. (c) After how many months will the total be €315?
  4. Ciara says the general term of 3, 7, 11, 15, … is n + 4. Explain her mistake and give the right rule.
  5. The points (0, 6), (1, 9), (2, 12) and (3, 15) lie on a straight line. (a) Write down the start value and the rate of change. (b) Write a formula for y in terms of x. (c) Find y when x = 10.

A. Error Hunt

Next term of 5, 9, 13, … = 18It goes up by 4, not 5: 17
Tₙ for 4, 7, 10, … = n + 3The jump goes in front of n: 3n + 1 (n + 3 gives 4, 5, 6, …)
10th term of 2, 6, 10, … = 2 + 10 × 4 = 42Only 9 jumps from T₁ to T₁₀: 2 + 9 × 4 = 38
Squares: stage 1 uses 4 sticks. Stage 10? = 10 × 4 = 40The squares share sides: 3(10) + 1 = 31
Common difference of 20, 15, 10, … = 5It goes down, so it's −5
Is 50 a term of 3, 7, 11, …? Yes, (50 − 3) ÷ 4 = 11.7511.75 is not a whole number, so no (T₁₂ = 47, T₁₃ = 51)

B. Exam-style questions

  1. (a) 7n − 4 (b) 7(15) − 4 = 101 (c) (150 + 4) ÷ 7 = 22, a whole number, so yes: T₂₂ = 150
  2. (a) 5n + 1 (b) 5(20) + 1 = 101 (c) (200 − 1) ÷ 5 = 39.8, so 39 houses, using 196 matchsticks: 4 left over
  3. (a) C = 25m + 40 (b) 25(6) + 40 = €190 (c) (315 − 40) ÷ 25 = 11 months
  4. n + 4 only says "add 4 to get the next term"; it gives 5, 6, 7, … The jump 4 goes in front of n: 4n gives 4 for n = 1, so take away 1: Tₙ = 4n − 1. Check: 4(2) − 1 = 7 ✓
  5. (a) start value 6, rate of change 3 (b) y = 3x + 6 (c) 3(10) + 6 = 36

Marking tip

Give credit for each step, as the exam does: a correct table or a correct common difference earns marks even if the rule slips. Accept equivalent forms of the rule (34 − 4n = −4n + 34) and any clear "working backwards" method.
