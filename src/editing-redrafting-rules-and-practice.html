Editing & redrafting

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): W1, W6, W13, W2. Play the game: leim.ie/proofread-patrol.html

Proofreading means reading your own work again to catch the slips. Redrafting means making it better. Every written answer is marked partly for accuracy, so both win marks.

1. Four passes

1. Spellingyour own tricky words: definitely, separate, beginning, until
2. Mix-upsthere / their / they're, to / too, then / than, should have (not of)
3. Grammarone tense all the way; each of them has; my friend and I went; I saw / I have seen
4. Punctuationcapitals, full stops (no run-ons), apostrophes, speech marks
Spot the slipsMy brother and me I went to the beach. Their There was nobody there. We should of have brought a coat.
A spell checker would miss all three: they're real words in the wrong place.

2. Tips

Read it in your head as if aloud. Check your own usual slips first. Cross out with one neat line and write the fix above. Leave two minutes at the end of every answer.

3. Fewer or less? Who or whom?

fewer / lessfewer for things you can count: fewer cars, fewer goals.
less for things you can't: less milk, less time.
who / whomwho does the action: the girl who won (she won).
whom has it done to them: the man whom we met (we met him), and after to / with / for.
Trick: him and whom both end in m.

4. Brackets and dashes

Our dog (a very lazy spaniel) slept through the storm.
Our dog – a very lazy spaniel – slept through the storm.
Always in pairs. Take the extra part out and the sentence still works.

5. Redrafting

Cut repeated words · swap weak words (nice, good, got) for exact ones · start sentences in different ways · one main idea per paragraph · keep your drafts for the Collection of Texts (CBA 2).

Exam tip: the students who keep their accuracy marks are the ones who read their answers back. Plan your time so you can.

F. Exam-style

  1. This paragraph from a student's diary has four slips. Find each one and write the correction.
    On Sunday we went to the open day at the coastguard station in Kilmore. My brother and me climbed into the rescue boat. The crew showed us how they done a rescue at sea. There was three helicopters on the pad, and we got to sit in one of them. I definately want to join the coastguard when I'm older.
  2. Redraft this paragraph to make it better. Keep the same day out.
    The day was good. We saw the boat and it was good. The helicopter was good too and the crew were nice.

Answers

A1. passed → past · 2. then → than · 3. here → hear · 4. went → gone · 5. wierd → weird · 6. suprise → surprise · 7. enviroment → environment · 8. me → I
B9. 3: durring → during, accross → across, nex → next · 10. 3: dinnar → dinner, me → I, Their → There
C11. less · 12. fewer · 13. less · 14. fewer · 15. fewer · 16. less
D17. who · 18. who · 19. whom · 20. whom · 21. who · 22. whom
E23. The island (only two miles long) has one shop. · 24. My sister (who hates spiders) screamed at the bath. · 25. Mr Byrne (our geography teacher) has a pet snake. · 26. The castle (built in the twelfth century) is still standing. (or dashes)
F27. me → I, done → did, was → were, definately → definitely · 28. For example: The open day was the best trip of the year. We clambered into the orange rescue boat, then sat in a real helicopter while the friendly crew explained every switch. Reward: no repeated "good", exact words, detail, varied sentence starts.
