1. Perimeter or area?

Perimeter = the distance around the edge, in m or cm (fencing, skirting, a frame). Area = the space inside, in m² or cm² (flooring, paint, tiles).

RectangleA = l × w · P = 2(l + w)
TriangleA = ½ × base × ⊥ height
ParallelogramA = base × ⊥ height
CircleC = 2πr = πd · A = πr²

⊥ height = the perpendicular height (at right angles to the base), never the sloping side. The formulas are in the Formulae and Tables booklet.

2. L-shapes

Missing sides first: 10 − 6 = 4 m, 7 − 4 = 3 m. Area = big rectangle − corner: 70 − 12 = 58 m². Perimeter = all six sides = 34 m.

End wall with a pointed roof = rectangle + triangle. Window with a round top = rectangle + semicircle.

3. Circles and π

d = 2r. Area uses the radius. Follow the instruction: π button, π = 3.14, π = 22/7, or in terms of π (r = 6: area 36π, circumference 12π). Back again: C = 18π → r = 9; A = 49π → r = 7.

Semicircle, d = 6 m: area ½ × π × 3² = 14.1 m²; perimeter ½ × π × 6 + 6 = 15.4 m (add the straight edge!). A quarter circle has two straight edges.

Wheels: one turn = one circumference (π × d).

5. Scale drawings

1 : 50 = 1 cm on the plan is 50 cm in real life. Plan → real × 50; real → plan ÷ 50; then ÷ 100 for m (÷ 100,000 for km). The scale: 6 m drawn 3 cm: 600 ÷ 3 → 1 : 200. Real area: change each length first, then multiply (9 cm × 7.2 cm at 1 : 50 = 4.5 m × 3.6 m = 16.2 m²), never plan area × 50.

Classic mistakes: the sloping side as the height · forgetting ½ · the diameter in πr² · no straight edge on a semicircle · m instead of m² · rounding packs down · mixing cm and m.
Exam tip: mark the missing lengths on the diagram, write the formula, put the numbers in, then give the answer WITH units. Round only at the end, the way the question asks.

E. Exam-style questions (show your work)

  1. The back garden. A rectangular lawn is 12 m by 8 m. A round pond with a radius of 1.5 m is dug in the middle of it. (a) Find the area of grass left, to 1 decimal place. (b) A box of lawn feed covers 20 m². How many boxes are needed for the grass? (c) A fence goes around the edge of the lawn except for a gate 1.2 m wide. How long is the fence?
  2. The running track. A track has two straights 85 m long and a semicircle at each end. The semicircles have a diameter of 64 m. (a) Find the length of one lap of the inside edge, to the nearest metre. (b) Find the area inside the track, to the nearest m². (c) How many complete laps must a runner do to run at least 5 km?
  3. The kitchen plan. Ella draws her kitchen to a scale of 1 : 20. The table is 9 cm by 4.5 cm on the plan. (a) Find the real length and width of the table. (b) Find the real area of the table top. (c) A round table with a diameter of 1.2 m is drawn on the same plan. What is its diameter on the plan? (d) Find the area of the round table top in terms of π.
  4. Same base, same area. A triangle and a parallelogram both have a base of 10 cm and an area of 40 cm². (a) Find the perpendicular height of each. (b) Ciara draws a triangle with a base of 10 cm and a sloping side of 8 cm. She says its area is ½ × 10 × 8 = 40 cm². Explain her mistake.
