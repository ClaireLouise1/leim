Statistics: graphs & averages

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): SP.3d, SP.3e, SP.3f, SP.3g, SP.3h (types of data, samples and frequency tables come first, in Statistics 1)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can find the mean, median, mode and range from lists and frequency tables (and estimate a grouped mean), read and draw bar charts, pie charts, line plots, histograms and ordered (back-to-back) stem-and-leaf plots, choose a suitable display and average, compare two groups in words, and spot misleading charts and claims. Good preparation for CBA 2.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Average Ace mingle cards + Class Data Swap A/B cards · p.3 Statistics Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 2nd year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: fair share. Four students hold up 2, 7, 3 and 4 cubes (or pens). "Make it fair without anyone leaving." Students move cubes until everyone has 4. Ask: what stayed the same? (The total.) Build mean = total ÷ how many.
8 minGuided discovery: which average? Board: pocket money €8, €10, €9, €12, €11, €90. Find the mean and the median. "Which tells a new student what's typical? Why?" Elicit outliers; then favourite colours: why only the mode works.
10 minAverage Ace mingle (p.2, 24 cards = 12 question/answer pairs; one card each). Question card? Work it out and find the person with your answer. Answer card? Find your question. Pairs check each other's working.
12 minClass Data Swap (p.2, pairs, don't show your card). Each fact is on only ONE card. Dictate your reaction times so your partner can build a back-to-back plot, then both fill every blank. Feedback: medians 35 and 29; ranges 23 and 18; Car = 12; angles 126°, 108°, 72°, 54°.
10 minStatistics Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, and build the chain to FINISH.
12 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name the rule, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone.
3 minExit ticket. Median of 6, 1, 9, 4? Pie chart of 36 people: angle for 5 people? (5; 50°.) Homework: play Average Ace at leim.ie/average-ace.html and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Mingle key (question card → answer card): 22 → 6 · 2 → 5 · 16 → 20 · 18 → 10 · 24 → 7 · 19 → 15 · 8 → 12 · 23 → 21 · 9 → 1 · 11 → 13 · 3 → 4 · 17 → 14.
Errors to look for: median without ordering · the position instead of the median · the frequency as the mode · Σfx ÷ number of rows · back-to-back left leaves read backwards · pie angles that don't add to 360° · "higher" with no numbers when comparing. Fast finishers: make up 5 numbers with mean 10, median 8 and mode 7. (e.g. 7, 7, 8, 13, 15)

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. Find the mean, median, mode and range of 12, 15, 9, 15, 20, 13, 14.
  2. The number of brothers and sisters of 20 students: 0: 4, 1: 7, 2: 6, 3: 2, 4: 1. Find the mean, the median and the mode.
  3. 36 students named their favourite subject: Maths 9, English 7, Science 12, Art 8. Find the angle for each sector of a pie chart.
  4. The ages of the players on two teams: Team A 18, 21, 23, 24, 27, 29, 31, 35, 38; Team B 19, 22, 25, 26, 26, 28, 30, 33, 34, 41, 44. (a) Draw a back-to-back stem-and-leaf plot with a key. (b) Find the median and range of each team. (c) Compare the two teams.
  5. The times (seconds) 20 students took to solve a puzzle: 0–20: 5, 20–40: 9, 40–60: 4, 60–80: 2. (a) Estimate the mean time. (b) What is the modal class? (c) Why is (a) only an estimate?

A. Error Hunt

Median of 9, 2, 7, 4, 5 → 7 (the middle one)Put them in order first: 2, 4, 5, 7, 9. Median = 5
Goals 0, 1, 2, 3 with frequencies 4, 9, 6, 1. The mode? → 99 is the frequency. The mode is the value: 1 goal
Values 1, 2, 3 with frequencies 2, 3, 5. The mean? → (1 + 2 + 3) ÷ 3 = 2Use Σfx ÷ Σf: (2 + 6 + 15) ÷ 10 = 2.3
Pie chart of 40 people: 12 chose the bus. Angle? → 12°Each person is 360° ÷ 40 = 9°: 12 × 9° = 108°

Range of 15, 8, 22, 11 → 22Range = biggest − smallest = 22 − 8 = 14

Marking tip

As in the exam, give credit for each step: the ordered list, the total and what it's divided by, the degrees for one person. "Compare" needs an average AND a measure of spread for both groups, with numbers and a sentence. A stem-and-leaf plot needs ordered leaves and a key.
