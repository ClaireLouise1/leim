At Higher Level you study one full Shakespeare play. For the 2028–2030 exams the list is As You Like It, Julius Caesar, The Merchant of Venice and Romeo and Juliet (check which list your year uses). Know the story, the characters, a few key scenes and some short quotes.

2. How Shakespeare writes

Verse and proseVerse has a beat (often 10 syllables): noble characters, big feelings. Prose is ordinary sentences: servants, jokes, characters who lose control.
Soliloquy · asideA soliloquy is a character alone on stage speaking their thoughts. An aside is a quick comment the others on stage can't hear.
Dramatic ironyWe know something a character doesn't.

3. Techniques to spot

Oxymoronopposites side by side: “sweet sorrow”
Similelike / as: “as boundless as the sea”
Rhyming couplettwo rhyming lines that close a scene
Rhetorical questionasked for effect: “Hath not a Jew eyes?”
Repetition“Brutus is an honourable man”, again and again
Puna play on words: the dying Mercutio's “a grave man”

4. Answering on Shakespeare

Point · short quote · explainCapulet is cruel to Juliet in Act 3, Scene 5: he calls her a “disobedient wretch” and threatens to throw her out, so we see how little her feelings matter to him.

Name the act and scene. Say how the audience feels. Mention staging when it helps: lighting, movement, tone of voice. Learn 3–4 short quotes per main character and theme.

Exam tip: a few words quoted accurately beat a long quote misremembered. Every quote needs an explanation.

E. Exam-style: Julius Caesar, Act 1, Scene 2

Caesar is talking to Antony about Cassius.Caesar: Let me have men about me that are fat; Sleek-headed men and such as sleep o' nights: Yond Cassius has a lean and hungry look; He thinks too much: such men are dangerous.Antony: Fear him not, Caesar; he's not dangerous; He is a noble Roman and well given.Caesar: Would he were fatter! But I fear him not: … Such men as he be never at heart's ease Whiles they behold a greater than themselves, And therefore are they very dangerous.
  1. What does Caesar think of Cassius, and why? Use two short quotes.
  2. Caesar says “I fear him not”. Do you believe him? How would you direct the actor playing Caesar to say these lines?

He doesn't trust him: Cassius has “a lean and hungry look” (hungry for power), “thinks too much”, and men like him are never happy while someone is “greater than themselves”, so they are “very dangerous”.
