Indices & standard form

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): N.1c, N.1f (zero, negative and fractional indices, (ab)ʳ and small numbers in standard form are Higher Level; surds come later, N.1b)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students move between whole numbers and index form; use the laws of indices for multiplying, dividing and powers of powers (with numbers in front); apply BIMDAS with indices and roots; and write numbers in and out of standard form, comparing and calculating with them.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Index Law Match cards + Standard Form Line-Up cards · p.3 Index Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 2nd year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: fold it. "A sheet of paper is folded in half again and again. How many layers after 1, 2, 3 … 10 folds?" Build the table 2, 4, 8 … and write the answers as 2¹, 2², 2³ … 2¹⁰ = 1,024. Base, index, and why 2⁵ ≠ 10.
8 minGuided discovery. Pairs write out 2³ × 2⁴, 3² × 3⁵, x⁴ × x² and 2⁶ ÷ 2² in full (as on the poster) and count. What happens to the indices? Then (2³)²: what is it as one power? Pairs state the three laws in their own words before you name them.
10 minIndex Law Match (p.2, 24 cards = 12 pairs; one card each). Question cards find the person holding their simplified answer. Pairs then explain which law they used. Feedback on 2⁵ × 2³ (the base stays 2) and (x⁴)² ÷ x⁵.
10 minStandard Form Line-Up (p.2, 16 cards). Students with a card line up across the room from smallest to biggest without talking, then each says their number in standard form. Discuss: why is 1.05 × 10⁵ bigger than 9.9 × 10³? (Look at the power of 10 first.)
10 minIndex Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4: start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left and build the chain to FINISH. No calculators.
14 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name the rule, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone. Higher Level groups do question 5.
3 minExit ticket. Work out (2⁶ × 2²) ÷ 2⁵ as a number; write 52,000 in standard form (8; 5.2 × 10⁴). Homework: play Power Up at leim.ie/power-up.html (Index laws and Standard form rounds) and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Index Law Match key (question card ↔ answer card): 14 ↔ 5 · 4 ↔ 23 · 3 ↔ 15 · 16 ↔ 13 · 10 ↔ 24 · 19 ↔ 18 · 12 ↔ 8 · 7 ↔ 1 · 17 ↔ 11 · 22 ↔ 20 · 6 ↔ 2 · 21 ↔ 9.
Standard Form Line-Up key (card numbers, smallest first): 5 < 11 < 6 < 12 < 3 < 14 < 1 < 13 < 2 < 7 < 4 < 15 < 8 < 9 < 10 < 16.
Errors to look for: 2⁵ = 10 · x³ × x⁵ = x¹⁵ · 3² × 3⁴ = 9⁶ · −4² = 16 · BIMDAS: 2 × 3² = 36 · 47 × 10⁵ left as the answer · 12 × 10⁷ not tidied up. Support: write powers out in full until the pattern is secure; a laws card on the desk. Fast finishers: which is bigger, 2¹⁰ or 10³? 2¹⁰⁰ or 10³⁰?

The laws of indices

2³ × 2⁴ = (2 × 2 × 2) × (2 × 2 × 2 × 2) = 2⁷

aᵖ × aq = ap+qMultiply: add the indices · x³ × x⁵ = x⁸
aᵖ ÷ aq = ap−qDivide: subtract the indices · 5⁹ ÷ 5³ = 5⁶
(aᵖ)q = apqPower of a power: multiply · (2³)⁴ = 2¹²

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. Write as a single power, then work out the value: (3⁵ × 3⁴) ÷ 3⁶.
  2. Simplify (a) 4a³ × 5a⁶ (b) 18b⁸ ÷ 6b³ (c) (y⁵)³ ÷ y⁷.
  3. Find n if (a) 5ⁿ × 5³ = 5¹⁰ (b) 2ⁿ = 128.
  4. A hard drive holds 2 × 10¹² bytes. A film uses 4 × 10⁹ bytes. (a) How many films fit on the drive? (b) Write 4 × 10⁹ as an ordinary number.
  5. Higher Level. (a) Work out 82/3 and 4⁻². (b) Solve 2ˣ = 1/32. (c) Simplify (2x⁴)³ × x⁻⁵.

A. Error Hunt

2⁴: 2⁴ = 2 × 4 = 8The index counts how many 2s are multiplied: 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 16
x³ × x⁵: x³ × x⁵ = x¹⁵Multiplying: ADD the indices: x⁸
3² × 3⁴: 3² × 3⁴ = 9⁶The base stays 3: 3⁶ (= 729)
−4²: −4² = 16The index belongs to the 4 only: −(4 × 4) = −16
4,700,000 in standard form: 47 × 10⁵47 is not between 1 and 10: 4.7 × 10⁶
(2 × 10³) × (6 × 10⁴): 12 × 10¹²Add the indices (3 + 4 = 7), then tidy: 12 × 10⁷ = 1.2 × 10⁸

B. Exam-style questions

  1. 3⁵⁺⁴⁻⁶ = 3³ = 27
  2. (a) 20a⁹ (b) 3b⁵ (c) y¹⁵ ÷ y⁷ = y⁸
  3. (a) n = 7 (b) n = 7 (2⁷ = 128)
  4. (a) (2 × 10¹²) ÷ (4 × 10⁹) = 0.5 × 10³ = 500 films (b) 4,000,000,000
  5. (a) (cube root of 8)² = 4; 1/4² = 1/16 (b) 1/32 = 2⁻⁵, so x = −5 (c) 8x¹² × x⁻⁵ = 8x⁷

Marking tip

As in the exam, give credit for showing the law (3⁵⁺⁴⁻⁶) even if the final value slips, and expect answers in the form asked for: a value of 12 × 10⁷ is correct but not in standard form.
