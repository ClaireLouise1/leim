Factorising

↗ Léim

Learning outcome (2018 specification): AF.3d (expanding was Algebra 2, AF.3b; solving quadratics by factorising comes in Quadratic equations, AF.4)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can find the HCF of terms and take it out; factorise four terms by grouping; factorise x² + bx + c; recognise and factorise a difference of two squares; factorise fully; and check every answer by multiplying out. Higher Level: ax² + bx + c and a²x² − b²y².

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Factor Relay + Factor Match mingle cards · p.3 Factorising Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 3rd year (Higher and Ordinary). Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

6 minLead-in: "undo it". Write 3(x + 4), x(x − 5), (x + 2)(x + 3) on the board and have pairs expand them. Then rub out the questions and leave only 3x + 12, x² − 5x, x² + 5x + 6: "Put the brackets back." Factorising is expanding backwards.
8 minGuided discovery. Pairs expand (x + 2)(x + 3), (x + 1)(x + 6), (x − 2)(x − 3), (x + 6)(x − 1). What do the bracket numbers do to make the middle and last numbers? (Add; multiply.) Then they factorise x² + 8x + 12 by listing the pairs that multiply to 12.
10 minFactor Match mingle (p.2, 24 cards = 12 pairs; one card each). Expression cards find their factorised form and vice versa. Pairs prove the match by multiplying out. Feedback: the three cards with 6 at the end, and why (x − 3)(x + 3) is only two terms.
10 minFactor Relay (p.2), teams of 4 in a line. Runner 1 does step 1 and passes the answer (not the card) to runner 2, and so on. The last runner brings the final number to you: if it's wrong, the team finds the broken step.
10 minFactorising Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4: start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left and build the chain to FINISH.
13 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name the rule, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone.
3 minExit ticket. Factorise fully 5x² − 20x; factorise x² − x − 20 (5x(x − 4); (x − 5)(x + 4)). Homework: play Factor Match at leim.ie/factor-match.html (Squares & checks round) and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Factor Match key (card numbers): 4↔6 · 8↔7 · 19↔5 · 11↔23 · 15↔2 · 14↔12 · 20↔18 · 9↔13 · 22↔24 · 21↔17 · 16↔10 · 3↔1.
Errors to look for: losing the 1 (3a(2b + 3c)) · not fully factorised (2(2x² − 5x)) · signs in x² − 5x + 6 · (x − 4)(x − 4) for x² − 16 · the second pair in grouping taken out with the wrong sign · x² + 9 "factorised" as (x + 3)(x + 3). Support: list the factor pairs in a table; draw the area model backwards; multiply out every answer. Fast finishers: 12x² − 27, x⁴ − 16, or explain why x² + 4 can't be factorised.

Factor Relay

  1. Relay A: 2x(x + 3) → x² + 6x + 9 → (x + 8)(x − 2) → 200 (x² + 6x + 9 − 25 = x² + 6x − 16; (12 + 8)(12 − 2) = 20 × 10)
  2. Relay B: (x − 4)(x + 4) → x² + 6x + 8 → 3(x + 2)(x + 4) → 240 (3x² + 18x + 24 = 3(x² + 6x + 8); 3 × 8 × 10)

A. Error Hunt

Factorise 6ab + 9ac − 3a = 3a(2b + 3c)3a ÷ 3a = 1, not 0: 3a(2b + 3c − 1)
Factorise fully 8x² + 12x = 4(2x² + 3x)x is still in both terms: 4x(2x + 3)
Factorise x² − 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3)they add to +5, not −5: (x − 2)(x − 3)
Factorise x² − 16 = (x − 4)(x − 4)that gives x² − 8x + 16: (x − 4)(x + 4)
Factorise x² + x − 12 = (x + 3)(x − 4)3 + −4 = −1, not +1: (x − 3)(x + 4)
Factorise xy − 2x − 3y + 6 = x(y − 2) − 3(y + 2)−3 × −2 = +6: x(y − 2) − 3(y − 2) = (y − 2)(x − 3)

B. Exam-style questions

  1. HCF = 5x: 5x(2x − 3)
  2. a(b − 5) + 2(b − 5) = (b − 5)(a + 2)
  3. (a) × to −30, + to 1: 6 and −5, (x + 6)(x − 5) (b) length x + 6 m
  4. (a) (97 − 3)(97 + 3) = 94 × 100 = 9,400 (b) 6(x² − 9) = 6(x − 3)(x + 3)
  5. × to −12, check the middle: (3x − 1)(x + 4) gives 12x − x = 11x ✓. (3x − 1)(x + 4). Check with x = 2: 12 + 22 − 4 = 30 and (6 − 1)(2 + 4) = 5 × 6 = 30 ✓

Marking tip

Give credit for each step, as the exam does: the middle line in grouping and a correct factor pair earn marks even if the final brackets slip. Accept the brackets in either order and (x + 3)² for (x + 3)(x + 3). "Factorise fully" needs the highest common factor out first.
