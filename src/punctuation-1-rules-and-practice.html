Punctuation 1: capitals, full stops, commas, apostrophes

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): W11, W6. Play the game: leim.ie/comma-catch.html

1. Capitals

Capitalthe first word · I · names · places · days · months · languages · festivals: On Monday, Aoife and I have French.
No capitalseasons · most school subjects · mum/dad after my or our: my mum loves summer and maths

2. Full stops and end marks

.ends a statement: We missed the bus.
?ends a question: Where is my phone?
!strong feeling: What a goal! How kind you are!
A reported question takes a full stop: She asked if I was hungry.
Two sentences need a full stop between them, not a comma.

3. Commas

Listseggs, milk and bread (no comma before the last “and”)
Opening wordHowever, the bus was late.
NamesLiam, are you coming? · Thanks, Dad.
Yes / No / WellYes, I'd love to.
Extra informationMr Byrne, our maths teacher, was off sick.

4. Apostrophes: missing letters

do not → don't · it is / it has → it's · we are → we're
cannot → can't · will not → won't · they are → they're

The apostrophe goes where the letters are missing.

5. Apostrophes: belonging

Write the owner first, then add the apostrophe.

One owneradd 's: the dog's bone · Sarah's phone
More than one, ends in sadd ': the dogs' kennel · the girls' changing room
More than one, no sadd 's: the children's toys · the men's team

6. It's or its?

Say “it is” or “it has”. Works → it's. Doesn't → its.
It's raining. The cat licked its paw.
Same test: you're / your · they're / their · who's / whose
Classic mistakes: no capital for I or names · a comma between two sentences · apostrophes in plurals (apple's for sale) · it's for its · “childrens'” (it's children's).
Exam tip: leave 3 minutes at the end of every writing answer to read it back for capitals, full stops and apostrophes.

Worked examples

11. How kind you are“How” can start a question (How old is your brother?) or an exclamation. Here it's strong feeling, and the words aren't in question order (not “are you”), so !
12. Have you finished your homeworkStarts with “Have you…” and asks something, so ? Compare 13: “She asked if…” only reports a question, so .
32. the children's toysOwner first: children (already plural, no s). No s at the end → add 's: children's.
35. The dog wagged its tail.Try “it is”: “the dog wagged it is tail” makes no sense, so no apostrophe: its.
