Sets & Venn diagrams

Spot the pattern

Here are three pairs of sets. Count the elements (# means "how many").

  • A = {1, 2, 3, 4, 6}, B = {2, 4, 6, 8}
    #A = 5 · #B = 4 · #(A ∩ B) = 3 · #(A ∪ B) = 6
  • A = {C, O, R, K}, B = {K, E, R, Y}
    #A = 4 · #B = 4 · #(A ∩ B) = 2 · #(A ∪ B) = 6
  • A = {3, 6, 9, 12}, B = {2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12}
    #A = 4 · #B = 6 · #(A ∩ B) = 2 · #(A ∪ B) = 8

Why isn't #(A ∪ B) just #A + #B? Can you find a rule that uses #(A ∩ B)?

Show me

#A + #B counts the elements in the overlap twice, so take them away once: #(A ∪ B) = #A + #B − #(A ∩ B). 5 + 4 − 3 = 6, 4 + 4 − 2 = 6 and 4 + 6 − 2 = 8. This is the idea behind every survey question.

The language of sets

A set is a well-defined collection of things, called elements. You can list it, A = {2, 4, 6, 8}, or give a rule: A = the even numbers from 1 to 9.

The four operations

U = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8}, A = {1, 2, 3, 4, 6}, B = {2, 4, 6, 8}

WriteMeansAnswer
A ∩ Bintersection: in A and in B{2, 4, 6}
A ∪ Bunion: in A or B (or both){1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8}
A \ Bdifference ("A less B"): in A but not in B{1, 3}
A′complement: in U but not in A{5, 7, 8}
Does the order matter? A ∩ B = B ∩ A and A ∪ B = B ∪ A, always (they are commutative). With three sets, (A ∩ B) ∩ C = A ∩ (B ∩ C), and the same for ∪ (they are associative). But A \ B is not the same as B \ A: above, A \ B = {1, 3} and B \ A = {8}.

Survey questions: fill from the middle out

  1. Draw the Venn diagram inside a box (U) and label the circles.
  2. Fill in the middle (the overlap) first. If it's unknown, call it x.
  3. For each circle, take the overlap away: if 15 play Gaelic and x play both, then 15 − x play Gaelic only.
  4. Put "neither" outside the circles but inside the box.
  5. All the regions add up to the total. Make an equation and solve for x.

With three sets there are 8 regions. Start with the centre (in all three), then the three "two only" parts, then the "one only" parts, then outside. "Exactly two" means the three "two only" parts, not the centre; "at least two" includes the centre.

Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Sets usually come up as a survey question: a group of students or customers, two or three things they like or do, and a Venn diagram to fill in. You may need to list sets like A ∩ B or (A ∪ B)′, use x for an unknown region and solve an equation, or shade a region and describe it in set notation.

How marks are won and lost

  • Draw the box. The universal set U is a rectangle; "neither" goes inside it, outside the circles.
  • Don't double count. "15 play Gaelic" includes the ones who play both, so Gaelic only is 15 − x.
  • Read the words carefully: "only", "exactly two", "at least two", "neither" all mean different regions.
  • Use set brackets { } and list each element once. An empty answer is ∅ (or { }), not 0.
  • Show your equation when you find x: it earns marks even if you slip later.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first.

  1. List them. U = {1, 2, 3, …, 12}. A = the factors of 12, B = the even numbers in U. (a) List A and B. (b) List A ∩ B, A ∪ B and B \ A. (c) List (A ∪ B)′. (d) Check that #(A ∪ B) = #A + #B − #(A ∩ B).
    Show the worked answer

    (a) A = {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12}, B = {2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12}.
    (b) A ∩ B = {2, 4, 6, 12}, A ∪ B = {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12}, B \ A = {8, 10}.
    (c) Everything in U not in A ∪ B: {5, 7, 9, 11}.
    (d) #(A ∪ B) = 8 and 6 + 6 − 4 = 8 ✓.

  2. Find x. 32 second-year students were asked if they use Instagram (I) or Snapchat (S). 19 use Instagram, 16 use Snapchat and 5 use neither. x students use both. (a) Draw a Venn diagram showing this. (b) Find x. (c) How many use exactly one of the two?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) Overlap x; Instagram only 19 − x; Snapchat only 16 − x; outside 5.
    (b) (19 − x) + x + (16 − x) + 5 = 32, so 40 − x = 32 and x = 8.
    (c) Instagram only 19 − 8 = 11, Snapchat only 16 − 8 = 8, so 19 use exactly one. Check: 11 + 8 + 8 + 5 = 32 ✓.

  3. Three clubs. 50 students were asked about the Chess (C), Drama (D) and Science (S) clubs. 4 are in all three. 9 are in Chess and Drama, 7 in Drama and Science and 6 in Chess and Science. 20 are in Chess, 18 in Drama and 17 in Science. (a) Draw the Venn diagram. (b) How many are in none of the clubs? (c) How many are in exactly one club?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) Centre 4. Two only: C and D 9 − 4 = 5, D and S 7 − 4 = 3, C and S 6 − 4 = 2. One only: Chess 20 − 5 − 2 − 4 = 9, Drama 18 − 5 − 3 − 4 = 6, Science 17 − 3 − 2 − 4 = 8.
    (b) Inside the circles: 9 + 6 + 8 + 5 + 3 + 2 + 4 = 37, so 13 are in none.
    (c) 9 + 6 + 8 = 23.

  4. Does the order matter? A = {1, 2, 3, 4}, B = {3, 4, 5} and C = {4, 5, 6}. (a) Describe the region A ∩ B′ in words and list it. (b) Is A \ B the same as B \ A? (c) Show that (A ∩ B) ∩ C = A ∩ (B ∩ C) for these sets.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) In A and not in B: {1, 2} (the same as A \ B).
    (b) A \ B = {1, 2} but B \ A = {5}, so no: set difference is not commutative.
    (c) A ∩ B = {3, 4}, then ∩ C = {4}. B ∩ C = {4, 5}, then A ∩ {4, 5} = {4}. The same ✓.
