Money: income tax, net pay & compound interest

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): N.2c, U.6. Play the game: leim.ie/payslip-puzzle.html

1. Gross pay

Gross pay = pay before anything is taken off. Hours × rate: 38 × €15.50 = €589.

Overtime: time-and-a-half = 1.5 × the normal rate; double time = 2 × the normal rate. Only the extra hours get it. 52 weeks or 12 months in a year.

2. Income tax (standard rate)

Gross tax20% of €40,000 = €8,000
− tax credit€8,000 − €3,750 = €4,250 tax payable
Net income€40,000 − €4,250 = €35,750

The tax credit comes off the tax, never off the income. Backwards: (tax + credit) × 5 = gross income.

3. Two rates Higher Level

Up to the standard rate cut-off point: 20%. Above it: 40%. €52,000 with a €44,000 cut-off: €8,800 + 40% of €8,000 (€3,200) = €12,000 gross tax, then take away the credits.

4. Net pay

Net pay = gross − income tax − USC − PRSI − pension (and any other deductions). Higher Level may give a deduction as a %: PRSI at 4% of €750 = €30.

5. Compound interest

Each year's interest is worked out on the amount at the start of that year.

YearStartInterest 5%End
1€4,000€200€4,200
2€4,200€210€4,410
3€4,410€220.50€4,630.50

Quick way: 5% a year → × 1.05 each year: €4,000 × 1.05³ = €4,630.50. Interest = €4,630.50 − €4,000 = €630.50.

Different rates: × 1.03 × 1.04 × 1.05. Loans: add the year's interest, THEN take off the repayment.

6. Working backwards Higher Level

Worth €4,410 after 2 years at 5%: €4,410 ÷ 1.05² = €4,000. Rate from €5,000 → €6,050 in 2 years: 6,050 ÷ 5,000 = 1.21, √1.21 = 1.1, so 10%.

Classic mistakes: credit taken off the income · 1.5 used for 5% · interest on the first amount every year · interest given when the value is asked · taking 5% off twice to go back.
Exam tip: label each line (gross tax, tax payable, deductions, net pay; end of year 1, 2, 3). Each step earns marks. Give money to the nearest cent.

A. Gross pay and payslips

  1. Aoife works 38 hours a week at €14.80 an hour. Find her gross pay for the week.
  2. Cian earns €16 an hour for a basic 39-hour week and time-and-a-half for overtime. One week he works 44 hours. Find his gross pay.
  3. Méabh works 4 hours on a Sunday at double time. Her normal rate is €15.50 an hour. How much does she earn for those hours?
  4. Tadhg earns €680 gross a week. Find his gross pay for a year (52 weeks).
  5. Ciara earns €42,000 gross a year. How much is that a month?
  6. A payslip shows gross pay €745.00, income tax €58.40, USC €19.65 and PRSI €29.80. Find the net pay.

B. Income tax

  1. Find 20% of a gross income of €36,000 (the gross tax).
  2. Seán earns €36,000 a year and has a tax credit of €4,000. The standard rate is 20%. Find the tax he pays.
  3. Find Seán's net income in question 8 (no other deductions).
  4. Róisín earns €52,500 a year with a tax credit of €3,750. Tax is 20%. Find her tax payable.
  5. Oisín earns €820 gross a week. His weekly tax credit is €75 and tax is 20%. Find his tax for the week.
  6. Niamh pays €5,100 income tax. Her tax credit is €3,900 and the rate is 20%. Find her gross income.
  7. Higher Level: Eoin earns €58,000. Cut-off point €44,000, rates 20% and 40%, tax credits €4,000. Find his tax.
  8. Higher Level: Gross pay €900 a week; tax €96, USC €27, PRSI 4% of gross. Find the net pay.

C. Compound interest

  1. €2,000 is saved at 5% compound interest a year. Find the interest for the first year.
  2. Find the value of the €2,000 in question 15 after 2 years.
  3. Find the value of the €2,000 after 3 years.
  4. How much interest is earned altogether in question 17?
  5. €8,000 earns 2% in year 1 and 3% in year 2 (compound). Find its value after 2 years.
  6. What do you multiply by to add 3.5% interest?
  7. Kate borrows €6,000 at 4% compound interest. She repays €2,000 at the end of year 1. How much does she owe at the end of year 2?
  8. Higher Level: After 2 years at 4% compound interest an investment is worth €10,816. How much was invested?

D. Putting it together

  1. Gross income €48,000; income tax €5,700, USC €1,050, PRSI €1,920. Find the net income.
  2. €10,000 for 2 years: Account A pays 4% each year; Account B pays 2% then 6%. Which is worth more, and by how much?
  3. Ella's gross pay of €600 a week goes up by 5%. Find her new gross pay.
  4. All of Ella's rise is taxed at 20%. How much more take-home pay does she get each week?
  5. Gross pay €2,900 a month; deductions €610 a month. Find the net pay for a year.
  6. Income €40,000, tax payable €4,000. What percentage of the income is the tax?
  7. €1,500 is saved for 3 years at 3% compound interest. Find its value to the nearest cent.
  8. Higher Level: €8,000 grows to €8,820 in 2 years at the same compound rate. Find the rate.

E. Exam-style questions (show your work)

  1. The payslip. Eoin earns €15.60 an hour for a basic 37-hour week and time-and-a-half for overtime. One week he works 44 hours. (a) Find his gross pay. (b) His weekly tax credit is €76 and tax is 20%. Find his tax. (c) He also pays USC of €18.35 and PRSI of €29.64. Find his net pay.
  2. Nóra's savings. Nóra invests €12,000. The account pays 2.5% compound interest in years 1 and 2 and 4% in year 3. (a) Find the value at the end of each year. (b) Find the total interest. (c) Is that more or less than 9% of €12,000? Why?
  3. The car loan. Aaron borrows €4,500 at 6% compound interest a year. He repays €1,500 at the end of year 1 and €1,500 at the end of year 2. How much does he owe at the end of year 3, to the nearest cent?
  4. Higher Level: Ronan earns €71,000 a year. Cut-off point €44,000, rates 20% and 40%, tax credits €4,000. He pays USC of €2,300 and PRSI at 4% of his gross income. (a) Find his income tax. (b) Find his net income. (c) What percentage of his gross income is taken off altogether?
