Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

Editing & redrafting

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): W1, W6, W13, W2

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can proofread their own writing in four passes (spelling, mix-up words, grammar, punctuation), explain each slip they fix, choose fewer / less and who / whom, use brackets and dashes in pairs, and redraft a sentence to make it better.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Slip Sort (24 cut-up cards) · p.3 Editor's Desk A/B (pair proofreading) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 3rd year. Time: about 60 minutes. All sentences are written by Léim.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Board: My brother and me went to the beach. Their was nobody there. We should of brought a coat. How many slips? Would a spell checker find them? Introduce the four passes (poster, p.4).
12 minSlip Sort (p.2, groups of 3, one set of 24 cards). Each card has one slip. Sort into three piles: spelling, mix-up word, grammar; write the fix on the back. 8 cards in each pile.
15 minEditor's Desk (p.3, pairs). Each student proofreads their own paragraph (4 slips), then the partner checks the fixes against the corrected version on their card, giving clues, not answers. Finish by redrafting one sentence.
5 minfewer / less, who / whom. Quick-fire on the board: ___ goals / ___ time / the girl ___ won / the man ___ we met. Teach the him / whom trick.
18 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs, then the exam-style questions on the Coastguard report alone.
5 minExit ticket. Students write their own top three slips on a sticky note to check in every answer. Homework: play Proofread Patrol at leim.ie/proofread-patrol.html.
Errors to look for: trusting the spell checker · reading too fast (read word by word, as if aloud) · should of · each of them have · me and him went · I seen / I done · less people · one bracket or one dash on its own · redrafting that only fixes slips without improving the writing. Support: give the number of slips per sentence. Fast finishers: write a paragraph with three hidden slips for a partner.

Editor's Desk A/B

↗ Léim

Proofread, check, redraft.

Student A · Editor's Desk

1. Proofread your paragraph. It has four slips. Circle each one and write the fix above it.

Last summer my cousin Ruth came to stay with us in Clare. We went swiming at Lahinch every day. Ruth surfed better then me. One day the waves were to big, so we built a sandcastle instead. Ruth and me called it Castle Clare.

2. Swap jobs. Your partner reads out the four fixes they made. Check them against the corrected version of their paragraph below. Tick each one they found; for any they missed, give a clue (which sentence?), not the answer.

Our school held a cake sale for the hospice in Ennis. Every class has to bake something. My friend and I made forty cupcakes with green icing. We sold them all before lunch, and there was a queue at the door. We raised more than last year.

3. Redraft one sentence of your paragraph to make it better. Read both versions to your partner: which is better, and why?

Each line has one slip. Say what's wrong and write the correct version.

B. Exam-style questions: a report for the local paper

The Coastguard Open Day was held in Kilmore Quay on Saturday. Hundreds of people came, despite the wheather. The crew showed visitors the rescue boat and there new drone. Each of the volunteers were wearing a bright orange jacket. The children, whom had never seen a lifeboat up close, loved every minute. Next year the crew hope to have less cancellations because of storms.
  1. The report has five slips. Find each one and write the correction.
  2. For two of your corrections, explain the rule in one sentence.
  3. Add a pair of brackets to this sentence: The rescue boat a gift from a local family was launched in 2019.
  4. Rewrite the second sentence so that it starts in a different way and gives more detail.
  5. The report ends flatly. Redraft the last sentence into a stronger ending of two sentences.

Answers

↗ Léim

Slip Sort (S = spelling, M = mix-up word, G = grammar)

1 S freind → friend · 2 M quite → quiet · 3 G have → has · 4 M then → than · 5 G less → fewer · 6 G is → are · 7 S beleive → believe · 8 G done → did · 9 G whom → who · 10 S suprise → surprise · 11 G are → is · 12 S definately → definitely · 13 M to → too · 14 G me → I · 15 M passed → past · 16 S Febuary → February · 17 G was → were · 18 M here → hear · 19 S occured → occurred · 20 M there → their · 21 S untill → until · 22 M your → you're · 23 M of → have · 24 S seperate → separate

Editor's Desk

A: swiming → swimming, then → than, to → too, me → I. B: have → has, fourty → forty, their → there, then → than.

A. Error Hunt

We should of booked the tickets online.should have booked (it only sounds like of)
Each of the girls have a locker.each = one, so has
I seen the new film on Friday.saw stands alone; seen needs have
There was less people at Mass this week.you can count people: fewer (and were)
My friend and me are going to Cork.take away "my friend": I am going
The match was brilliant, we won by a goal.a comma can't join two sentences: brilliant. We won… or brilliant; we won…

B. Exam-style questions

  1. wheather → weather, there → their, were → was, whom → who, less → fewer.
  2. For example: each means one, so the verb is singular (was); people can be counted, so fewer.
  3. The rescue boat (a gift from a local family) was launched in 2019.
  4. For example: Despite grey skies and a stiff breeze, hundreds of families queued along the pier.
  5. Reward a clear, confident close that looks forward or echoes the opening, e.g. The crew hope next year will bring calmer seas. Either way, the doors of Kilmore Quay will be open.

Marking tip

In the exam, spelling, grammar and punctuation are rewarded as part of the overall accuracy of each written answer rather than as a separate question. Our guide, not an official marking scheme.
