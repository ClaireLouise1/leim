Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

Narrative writing

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): W1, W3, W7, W9

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can plan a story in five minutes with a story spine, keep a story to one problem, recognise first- and third-person narrators, keep the tense and narrator steady, and choose an ending that solves the problem they set up.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Spine Jigsaw cards (cut-up) · p.3 Spine Swap A/B (speaking + listening) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Story + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (useful all through Junior Cycle). Time: about 60 minutes. All stories are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Ask: why do stories in exams run out of steam, or end with “I woke up”? Elicit: no plan. Write the five spine words on the board: Once upon a time · Every day · But one day · Because of that · Until finally.
8 minGuided discovery. Read one line at a time: Once upon a time, Fionn was afraid of the ball. Every day, he hid at the back of the pitch. But one day, the goalkeeper was sick. Because of that, Fionn had to play in goal in the final. Until finally, Fionn saved a penalty. What job does each line do? Could two lines swap? Which line solves the problem from line 1?
12 minSpine Jigsaw (p.2, groups of 3–4, one set of 24 cards). Rebuild 4 story spines in order. 4 cards belong to no story: find them and say why they'd be cut.
15 minSpine Swap (p.3, pairs). A reads a spine aloud; B fills the grid, including the narrator and the tense. Swap. Then each pair adds a “Because of that…” step to both stories.
15 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Story in pairs, then the exam-style questions on "The Green Gate" alone.
5 minExit ticket. A five-line spine for a story called "The Storm". Homework: turn it into a story, or play Story Spine at leim.ie/story-spine.html.
Errors to look for: spines with two problems · “Because of that” steps that don't follow from the step before · endings unrelated to the problem · I/she and past/present switches in students' own writing. Support: give weaker groups the 4 “Once upon a time” cards first. Fast finishers: retell one jigsaw spine in the first person, or in the present tense.

Spine Swap A/B

↗ Léim

Each of you has a story spine. Listen carefully: can you catch every step, the narrator and the tense?

Student A · Spine Swap

1. Read your story spine aloud twice, slowly. Don't show it to your partner.

"The Sponsored Swim"
Once upon a time, I was the only girl in my class who couldn't swim.
Every day, I sat on the bench at the pool in Tralee and watched the others swim.
But one day, our teacher announced a sponsored swim for the local hospice.
Because of that, I went to the pool every morning before school with my aunt.
Until finally, I managed ten lengths and raised two hundred euro for the hospice.

2. Then listen to "The Grumpy Farmer" and fill in the grid:

Who and where? (Once upon a time…)
Normal life (Every day…)
What changes? (But one day…)
What it leads to (Because of that…)
The ending (Until finally…)
Narrator: first or third person?
Tense: past or present?

3. Together: add one more "Because of that…" step to each story. Does the ending still work?

B. Exam-style questions: "The Green Gate"

Every Saturday, I walked Gran's dog, Bonnie, along the river in Fermoy. We always went through the green gate at the end of Gran's road. But that Saturday, the gate was locked with a shiny new padlock. Bonnie pulled at the lead and whined. Through the bars, I saw a small rowing boat drifting in the middle of the river, and a boy waving from the far bank.
  1. Is this story told in the first or the third person? Quote two words that show it.
  2. Which tense is it told in? Give two verbs from the text that show it.
  3. Fill in the first three steps of a story spine for this opening (Once upon a time… / Every day… / But one day…).
  4. Write the last two steps (Because of that… / Until finally…) so the ending solves the problem.
  5. Rewrite the last sentence in the third person (the narrator is a girl).

A. Fix the Story

“I started writing straight away and ran out of ideas after a page.”No plan. Spend five minutes on a story spine first: then you know where the story is going.
A story with a robbery, a football match, a birthday party and a trip to Spain.Too many events. Pick one problem and cut everything that isn't about it.
“I run to the door and I opened it.”Tense switch. Keep the past: I ran to the door and opened it.
“I grabbed my coat. Then she ran outside.” (the narrator is the same girl)Narrator switch. Keep first person: Then I ran outside.
“…and then I woke up. It was all a dream.”A cheat ending: nothing was at stake. Plan a real ending before you start.
The problem is a lost dog, but the ending is about winning a medal.The ending doesn't solve the problem. Go back to the spine: the ending must answer “But one day…”.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. First person: "I walked", "I saw" (also "We always went").
  2. Past tense: walked, went, was, pulled, whined, saw (any two).
  3. Once upon a time, a girl walked her gran's dog Bonnie by the river in Fermoy. Every day (Saturday), they went through the green gate. But one day, the gate was locked with a new padlock.
  4. Credit two steps that follow on: e.g. Because of that, she climbed the wall and ran along the bank to the boy. Until finally, she helped him pull the boat in with Bonnie's lead. The ending must link to the locked gate or the boat.
  5. Through the bars, she saw a small rowing boat drifting in the middle of the river, and a boy waving from the far bank.

Marking tip

In students' own stories, reward a clear shape (one problem, an ending that solves it) and control (steady tense and narrator) more than length. Our guide, not an official marking scheme.
