How to use this pack

↗ Léim

Junior Cycle Maths is one written paper in June of 3rd year, at Higher or Ordinary level. Recent papers at both levels have been 2 hours, 270 marks and 13 compulsory questions, each with a suggested time. The questions are not worth the same: some are worth 10 marks, others 30.

The two mock papers in this pack copy that format (the marks, parts and times of the 2026 papers) with original questions covering all four strands: Number, Algebra & Functions, Geometry & Trigonometry, and Statistics & Probability. The format can change from year to year.

Which level? Do your own level's paper first. If you're unsure which level to take, try a few questions from the other paper too and talk to your Maths teacher: the decision is usually made in 3rd year.
For parents: you don't need to know the Maths to help. Time your child, then use the solutions to check the method, not just the answer. Ask "Where did the marks go?" for every part they got wrong.

How the paper is marked

↗ Léim

Maths is not marked one mark per step. The examiner reads your answer to a part and decides which level it reaches on a scale. Each part in this pack shows its marks and scale, for example 10D (a 10-mark part on scale D).

ScaleHigher level marksOrdinary level marks
5B0, 3, 50, 3, 5
5C0, 2, 3, 50, 2, 3, 5
5D0, 2, 3, 4, 50, 2, 3, 4, 5
10C0, 3, 6, 100, 3, 7, 10
10D0, 3, 5, 7, 100, 4, 6, 8, 10
15D0, 4, 8, 12, 150, 4, 8, 12, 15

The scale values are the ones in the 2026 marking schemes. The SEC can change them from year to year.

Five things that win or lose marks

  1. One line of work is worth marks. The lowest mark above zero is usually 2 to 4 marks. A blank is always 0, so never leave a part empty: write the formula and put the numbers in.
  2. Show your work. A right answer with no work can get very low marks.
  3. A slip costs only 1 mark if everything else is right (for example 9 out of 10). Two or more slips in the same part cost more.
  4. Carry on with a wrong answer. If (a) went wrong, use your answer from (a) in (b). You can still get full marks for (b).
  5. Don't scribble out work. Put a single line through it: the examiner marks every attempt and gives you the best one.

How to use "where the marks would go"

Under each solution you'll see the part's scale, what earns full marks, and what kind of work earns partial credit. When you mark your own paper, find the description that best matches your answer and give yourself a mark from the scale. Be honest: it's practice.

Our guide, not an official marking scheme. "Where the marks would go" shows how partial credit usually works on these scales. The real marking scheme for each June paper is written by the SEC after the exam, and you can read past ones free on the SEC's Exam Material Archive (examinations.ie).

Exam day

The exam-day checklist

↗ Léim

The night before

  • Calculator, with a fresh battery, set to degrees
  • Geometry set: ruler, compass, protractor, set squares
  • Two pens, two pencils, an eraser and a sharpener
  • A clear pencil case and a watch (not a smartwatch)
  • Read your own list of "mistakes I make" one last time
  • Sleep: it's worth more than another hour of revision

In the exam

  • Ask for the Formulae and Tables booklet if it isn't on your desk (you can't bring your own)
  • Read the whole paper first (2 minutes)
  • Start with a question you like
  • Watch the suggested time on each question; move on if you go over
  • Write the formula, then the numbers, then the answer
  • Units on every answer (cm, m², €, km/h)
  • Round only at the end, and to what the question asks
  • Never leave a part blank
  • Last 10 minutes: check the parts you skipped
