Relative frequency & counting

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): SP.1b, SP.2c (sample spaces, two-way tables and equally likely outcomes come first, in Probability 1)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can find relative frequencies from experiments, explain why more trials give a better estimate of a probability, work out expected frequencies, judge whether a die or spinner is fair, and use the fundamental principle of counting (including choices that go down when nothing can be repeated).

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Class Experiment record + Coin & Dice Lab mingle cards · p.3 Probability Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 2nd year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: is it 50 : 50? Hold up a drawing pin (or a plastic cup). "It lands point up or point down. So P(point up) = 1/2?" Take a vote. Elicit that the outcomes are not equally likely, so we can't count: we have to experiment.
15 minClass Experiment (p.2 record, one per pair, a drawing pin or cup each). Pairs drop it 50 times, tally, and fill in the relative frequency after 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 drops. Pool the class results on the board (add the counts, then divide). Discuss: whose 10-drop result was furthest from the class value? Why is the class value the best estimate?
5 minGuided discovery: expected frequency. "If we dropped it 1,000 times, how many point up?" Students suggest a number and how they got it: relative frequency × 1,000. Then "a fair die, 300 rolls, how many sixes?" → 1/6 × 300 = 50, and "will it be exactly 50?"
12 minCoin & Dice Lab mingle (p.2, 24 cards = 12 question/answer pairs; one each). Question card? Work it out and find your answer. Answer card? Find your question. Pairs then explain their method to another pair. Board the counting rule from the meal-deal and lock cards: multiply the choices.
10 minProbability Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left and build the chain to FINISH. Answer chain below.
10 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name the rule, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone.
3 minExit ticket. 7 of 20 bottle flips land standing up: relative frequency? How many in 200 flips? (0.35; 70.) Homework: play Coin & Dice Lab at leim.ie/coin-dice-lab.html and throw a coin 10,000 times in the lab under the game.
Mingle key: Pin: 50 drops, 18 point up. Relative frequency? → 0.36 · Fair die, 600 rolls. Expected sixes? → 100 · 3 starters, 5 mains, 2 desserts. Meal deals? → 30 · Lock: 3 dials, each 0–9. Codes? → 1,000 · Coin: 400 tosses. Expected heads? → 200 · 4 friends in a row. How many ways? → 24 · P(late) = 0.15. Late days out of 60? → 9 · 12 of 40 seeds grew. Relative frequency? → 0.3 · Rel. freq. of red = 0.2. Reds in 350 spins? → 70 · Gold, silver, bronze from 6 runners? → 120 · P(red) = 1/4. Spins to expect 20 reds? → 80 · A letter A–E, then a digit 0–9. Codes? → 50.
Errors to look for: dividing by "the rest" instead of the trials · "you will get exactly 50" · trusting 10 trials over 500 · averaging two relative frequencies from different numbers of trials · adding the choices instead of multiplying · letting one runner win two medals. Fast finishers: design a "fair or biased?" test for a die: how many rolls would you do, and what results would convince you?

Probability from experiments

Relative frequency= times it happened ÷ number of trials
18 point up in 50 drops → 18 ÷ 50 = 0.36
Expected frequency= probability × number of trials
fair die, 300 rolls → 1/6 × 300 = 50 sixes
Countingm ways, then n ways → m × n ways
3 starters × 4 mains × 2 desserts = 24 meals
no repeats? the choices go down: 8 × 7 × 6

More trials → a better estimate of the probability

Results are never exactly what you expect. A coin has no memory.

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. A spinner is spun 250 times and lands on green 85 times. (a) Find the relative frequency of green. (b) Estimate how many greens in 1,000 spins.
  2. A fair spinner has 5 equal sectors: 2 red, 2 blue, 1 yellow. It is spun 300 times. How many times would you expect (a) red (b) yellow?
  3. A die is rolled 300 times: 1 → 52, 2 → 47, 3 → 50, 4 → 49, 5 → 53, 6 → 49. Is it fair? Give a reason with numbers.
  4. A school canteen offers 4 sandwiches, 3 drinks and 5 pieces of fruit. (a) How many lunches (one of each)? (b) Two more drinks are added. How many now?
  5. A code uses the letters A, B, C, D, E, each at most once, and has 3 letters. (a) How many codes? (b) A code is picked at random: P(it starts with A)?

Answers

Class Experiment

Answers depend on the class data. 1: the whole class (most trials). 2: relative frequency × 1,000. 3: drawing pins usually land point up well over or under half the time, so no; the class relative frequency is the evidence.

A. Error Hunt

A pin lands point up 18 times in 50 drops. Relative frequency? = 18/32Divided by the rest, not the trials: 18 ÷ 50 = 0.36
A fair die is rolled 60 times. How many sixes will you get? = exactly 10That's the expected number: you'll get about 10, rarely exactly
4 starters, 3 mains, 2 desserts. Meal deals? = 4 + 3 + 2 = 9Multiply, don't add: 4 × 3 × 2 = 24
Ciara: 12 of 30. Seán: 18 of 50. Together? = (0.4 + 0.36) ÷ 2 = 0.38Add the counts first: 30 ÷ 80 = 0.375
5 heads in a row. P(tail next)? = more than 1/2: it's dueA coin has no memory: still 1/2
Gold, silver, bronze from 8 runners? = 8 × 8 × 8 = 512No one can win two medals: 8 × 7 × 6 = 336

B. Exam-style questions

  1. (a) 0.34 (b) 340
  2. (a) 120 (b) 60
  3. Fair: expect 50 each; every result is within 3 of 50
  4. (a) 60 (b) 4 × 5 × 5 = 100
  5. (a) 5 × 4 × 3 = 60 (b) 4 × 3 = 12 of 60: 1/5

Marking tip

Give credit for each step, as the exam does: the division for a relative frequency, the multiplication for an expected frequency or a count. For "is it fair?" insist on a reason with numbers: the expected value and the result that is (or isn't) far from it. "Biased, because they're not all equal" earns little.
