Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

Parts of speech

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): W10, W12, R11, OL12

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can name the word class of a word from its job in the sentence (noun, verb, adjective, adverb, pronoun, preposition, conjunction), see that one word can change class, swap weak words for strong ones, and fix adjective/adverb slips in their own writing.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Upgrade Match mingle cards + Story Swap A/B cards · p.3 Word Class Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (useful revision in 2nd and 3rd). Time: about 60 minutes. All sentences are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: the jobs game. Write "The tired fox slept soundly under the old oak." Ask: which words could you take out and still have a sentence? Which ones can't go? Elicit that words do different jobs.
8 minGuided discovery. Pairs try the tests on each word: "the ___" (noun), "they ___" (verb), "very ___" (adjective or adverb). Then: does it describe a thing, or tell you how? Board the big four, then the glue words (pronoun, preposition, conjunction).
10 minUpgrade Match mingle (p.2, 24 cards = 12 weak phrases + 12 strong words; one card each). Find your partner, then say a sentence using the strong word. Feedback: which pairs were verbs, which adjectives?
12 minStory Swap (p.2, pairs, don't show your story). A asks B for each word in the list ("Give me an adjective"), writes it in, then reads the silly story aloud. Swap. Then pairs rewrite one story with the best words they can think of.
10 minWord Class Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, find the word the clue asks for, then find the domino with that word on its left. Build the chain to FINISH.
12 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find it, say what's wrong, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone.
3 minExit ticket. "Fear kept them silent." Name each word's class. (noun, verb, pronoun, adjective.) Homework: play Word Sort at leim.ie/word-sort.html.
Mingle key (weak card → strong card): 4 → 9 · 19 → 3 · 7 → 20 · 11 → 13 · 14 → 18 · 10 → 22 · 23 → 24 · 16 → 15 · 6 → 12 · 5 → 2 · 1 → 17 · 21 → 8.
Errors to look for: "quick" for quickly, "good" for well · every -ly word called an adverb (friendly, lovely are adjectives) · a word's class decided out of context (run, fast, light) · "Me and Tom went". Support: the poster (p.4) on the desk. Fast finishers: write one sentence where "light" is a noun, a verb and an adjective.

B. Exam-style questions

The old lighthouse stood alone on the cliff. Waves smashed against its walls, and the rusty door groaned whenever the wind blew.
  1. Find one example of each: noun, verb, adjective, preposition, conjunction.
  2. Pick out two verbs that make the lighthouse seem lonely or threatened. Explain each choice.
  3. Rewrite with stronger words: "The boat went slowly into the harbour on a very cold night."
  4. Write one sentence where "light" is a noun and another where it is an adjective.
  5. "The lonely girl walked home." Is "lonely" an adverb? Explain.

Upgrade Match

ran very fast → sprinted · ate hungrily → devoured · very tired → exhausted · very big → enormous · very cold → freezing · looked hard at → stared at · very loud → deafening · walked angrily → stormed · very happy → overjoyed · very dirty → filthy · cried loudly → wailed · said quietly → whispered

Story Swap

Any word of the right class is correct; check each one fits its label (e.g. an adverb answers "how"). In the rewrite, look for exact, strong verbs and adjectives.

A. Error Hunt

The cake was real tasty.Describes tasty (how tasty), so an adverb: really tasty
She finished the race quick.Tells us how she finished: quickly
Me and Tom went to the match.Subject pronoun needed: Tom and I went
The dog wagged it's tail.it's = it is. The possessive is its tail
He was very very scared.Use one strong adjective: terrified
I seen the film last night.Past tense verb: I saw the film (or I have seen)

B. Exam-style questions

  1. e.g. noun: lighthouse · verb: stood · adjective: old or rusty · preposition: on or against · conjunction: and or whenever.
  2. e.g. "smashed": the waves attack the walls with force. "groaned": the door sounds old and in pain, adding to the eerie mood. (Credit: exact word quoted + what it shows.)
  3. e.g. The boat crept into the harbour on a freezing night.
  4. e.g. Turn on the light (noun). My bag is light (adjective).
  5. No. "Lonely" describes the girl (a noun), so it is an adjective, even though it ends in -ly.

Marking tip

In questions on a writer's language, credit the exact word quoted plus a clear point about its effect; naming the word class makes the point sharper. In students' own writing, adjective/adverb slips and weak, repeated words cost marks for accuracy and style.
