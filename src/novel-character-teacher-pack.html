Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

The novel 1: character

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R6, R7, R4, W8

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can name five ways a writer shows a character (STEAL), choose a trait word rather than a feeling, back it up with a short quote (point, quote, explain), and say how a character changes.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Trait Match cards (mingle) · p.3 STEAL Swap A/B cards (pairs) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Answer + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (works with any class novel). Time: about 60 minutes. All extracts are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Board: “Mia gave half of her lunch to the new boy.” Is Mia kind? How do you know, if the word isn't there? Elicit show, don't tell.
8 minGuided discovery. Show the Mia extract (poster or game). For each line, pairs decide: does Mia do, say or think it, or is it someone else? Build STEAL on the board.
12 minTrait Match (p.2, mingle). Hand out one card each (22 cards: 11 points, 11 proofs). Students read their card aloud and find the partner card, then explain the link in one sentence ("Grabbing the biggest slice shows he's selfish").
12 minSTEAL Swap (p.3, pairs, A/B). Each student reads their extract aloud line by line; the partner listens and labels each line S, T, E, A or L, plus the trait it shows. Then swap.
18 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Answer in pairs (what's missing? improve it), then the exam-style questions on “Mrs Keane” alone.
5 minExit ticket. One trait of a character from the class novel, with a quote and an explanation. Homework: play Character Clues at leim.ie/character-clues.html.
Trait Match pairs (point–proof): 8–9 · 20–11 · 14–12 · 3–18 · 15–17 · 2–10 · 22–4 · 6–7 · 1–13 · 5–19 · 16–21.
Errors to look for: feelings instead of traits · points with no quote · quotes that don't prove the point · retelling the plot · one quote for two traits. Support: the poster (p.4) on the desk. Fast finishers: write a 4-line extract that shows a character using all five STEAL methods.

B. Exam-style questions

Mrs Keane
Everyone on Main Street knew Mrs Keane. She was seventy-eight, wore the same green cardigan every day and ran the tiny shop beside the church. Children were a little afraid of her: she frowned over her glasses and never let anyone pay later. One wet Tuesday, Josh came in soaked, counting coins that didn't add up to the price of a sandwich. “Put those away,” Mrs Keane snapped, and she handed him the sandwich anyway, with a carton of milk. “And don't tell anyone,” she muttered, turning back to her newspaper. Josh never did. But from then on, he noticed the bread she left out for the birds, and the way she always kept the last paper for old Mr Walsh.
  1. What do we learn about Mrs Keane from her looks? Give one detail.
  2. How do the children react to Mrs Keane at first, and why?
  3. What kind of person is Mrs Keane really? Give two qualities, with a quote for each.
  4. Why do you think she says, “And don't tell anyone”?
  5. Would you like to have Mrs Keane as a neighbour? Give a reason based on the text.

A. Fix the Answer

Mia is kind.No proof. Add a quote: she “gave half of her lunch”.
Mia is happy because she gave half of her lunch.“Happy” is a feeling, not a trait. Say kind (or generous), and quote: “gave half of her lunch”.
Ciarán is selfish: “Ciarán grabbed the biggest slice of pizza before anyone else sat down.”Whole sentence copied and no explanation. Quote “grabbed the biggest slice” and say why it shows selfishness.
Does Leah change? Leah is shy: “stared at her shoes”.Only the start. Add the end: she is brave when she “walked on stage”.
Niamh is brave: “Got him!”The quote doesn't prove it. Use “waded in after it”: she goes into a river for the dog.
Aisling is bossy and clever: she “worked out which way was north”.Two traits, one quote. Bossy needs its own proof: “and nobody talks”.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. e.g. She is old (“seventy-eight”) and wears “the same green cardigan every day”: she is plain and set in her ways, not fussy about clothes.
  2. They are “a little afraid of her”, because she “frowned over her glasses” and never let anyone pay later: she seems strict and unfriendly.
  3. e.g. Generous: she “handed him the sandwich anyway, with a carton of milk”. Caring: she leaves “bread … for the birds” and keeps “the last paper for old Mr Walsh”. (Also accept modest or private, with proof.)
  4. e.g. She doesn't want praise, or she wants to keep her strict image: she is kind in private, not for show.
  5. Either answer, backed up: e.g. yes, because under the frown she quietly looks after people (“kept the last paper for old Mr Walsh”).

Marking tip

Credit a trait word (not a feeling), a short accurate quote, and a sentence explaining how the quote shows the trait. Retelling the plot without naming a trait is usually worth little.
