1. Every word has a job

The job a word does in a sentence is its word class (or part of speech). Knowing the jobs helps you write accurately and talk about a writer's language.

2. The big four

Nouna person, place, thing or idea: Maya, Cork, ball, courage
Verban action or a state: kicked, slept, is, seems
Adjectivedescribes a noun: the muddy ball, it was empty
Adverbhow, when, where, how often: ran quickly, never came

3. The glue words

Pronounstands in for a noun: I, you, she, it, we, them
Prepositionwhere or when: under the oak, after dinner
Conjunctionjoins ideas: and, but, so, because, although

4. Quick tests

the ___ ? → noun (the fox)  ·  they ___ ? → verb (they slept)
Describes a noun? → adjective  ·  Tells you how / when? → adverb (many end in -ly)

5. Same word, different job

The class depends on the job in that sentence.

I run every day (verb) · a long run (noun)
a fast car (adjective) · she drives fast (adverb)
turn on the light (noun) · a light bag (adjective)

6. Stronger words

One strong verb beats a weak verb + adverb. Swap tired words for exact ones.

ran very fast → sprinted  ·  ate hungrily → devoured
very tired → exhausted  ·  nice food → delicious food
went across the road → bolted across the road

7. Adjective or adverb?

Telling us how something was done? Use the adverb.

He played good well.  She sang beautiful beautifully.
Classic mistakes: "quick" for quickly · "good" for well · very + a weak word (very big) instead of one strong word (enormous) · calling every -ly word an adverb (friendly, lonely are adjectives).
Exam tip: when you write about a writer's language, quote the exact word and name it: "The verb 'howled' makes the wind sound like an animal."

Worked examples

2. slept"The fox slept": try "they slept". It works, and it's something the fox does, so it's a verb.
11. silent"Fear kept them silent": silent describes them (a pronoun standing in for people), so it's an adjective, even though it's at the end.
35. fast"She drives fast": fast tells us how she drives, so here it's an adverb. In "a fast car" it's an adjective.
28. them"Fear kept them silent": them stands in for the people, so it's a pronoun.
