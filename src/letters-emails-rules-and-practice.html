2. Faithfully or sincerely?

GreetingSign-off
Dear Sir/Madam, (no name)Yours faithfully,
Dear Ms Kelly, (a name)Yours sincerely,
Hi Aoife, / Dear Gran, (a friend)Talk soon, / Love,
Memory trick: Sir and sincerely never go together. Capital Y, small f or s, then a comma.

4. Emails

A short subject line (Subject: Lost jacket on the 8:15 bus). Then greeting, opening line, paragraphs, closing line, sign-off and full name, just like a letter. No addresses or date needed.
Classic mistakes: "Hi Manager" · Dear Sir/Madam with Yours sincerely · "Yours Sincerely" with a capital S · your name in your address · one "gonna" in a formal letter · no reason for writing in the first line.
Exam tip: read the question twice. Who are you writing to and why? That decides formal or informal, the layout and the sign-off. Then give one idea per paragraph.

F. Exam-style

  1. Your sports club wants a new minibus. Write the greeting, opening line, closing line and sign-off of a letter to Ms Doyle, the manager of a local business, asking her to sponsor it.
  2. Write a short informal email to a friend telling them about the minibus. Name two things that make it informal.

Answers

B9. Talk soon / Love · 10. Yours faithfully · 11. Yours sincerely · 12. Talk soon / Love · 13. Yours sincerely · 14. Yours faithfully

(Other formal versions are fine: full words, no slang, polite.)
E20. The date, under the address on the right (30 August 2026). · 21. The subject line (e.g. Subject: Rooms for a school trip).
F22. Sample: Dear Ms Doyle, / I am writing to ask if your business would sponsor a new minibus for Ballyduff Athletics Club. / I look forward to hearing from you. / Yours sincerely, + full name (a name was used, so sincerely).
23. Sample: Hi Sam, Guess what? We're getting a new minibus for the club! No more squashing into Dad's car. Talk soon, Cian. Informal: short forms (We're), a chatty greeting and sign-off, "!".
