Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

Paragraphs and topic sentences

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): W1, W10, W3

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can find and write a topic sentence, support it with details, cut a sentence that doesn't belong, put a paragraph in a logical order, know when to start a new paragraph (TiPToP: time, place, topic, person) and choose the right linking word.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Paragraph Jigsaw cards (cut-up) · p.3 Paragraph Swap A/B (speaking + listening) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Paragraph + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (useful revision in 2nd and 3rd). Time: about 60 minutes. All texts are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Read out four jumbled sentences about puffins. Which one could be the title for the others? Elicit: the topic sentence gives the main idea; the others give details and link back to it.
8 minGuided discovery. Show a paragraph with one off-topic sentence. Pairs find it and say why. Board: one paragraph = one main idea; topic sentence + supporting sentences + link. Then TiPToP and the linking-word families.
15 minParagraph Jigsaw (p.2, groups of 3–4, one set of 24 cards). Sort the cards into 6 paragraphs, put the topic sentence first and the rest in order. Fast groups: write a 7th paragraph of their own on blank cards.
12 minParagraph Swap (p.3, pairs). A reads "The Sleepover" aloud; B listens with the book closed and calls "New paragraph!" + the TiPToP reason. A checks against the // marks. Swap for "The Breakfast Club".
15 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Paragraph in pairs, then the exam-style questions on "The Day the River Rose" alone.
5 minExit ticket. Write a topic sentence for a paragraph about your favourite place, and one supporting sentence that starts "For example,". Homework: play Paragraph Builder at leim.ie/paragraph-builder.html.
Errors to look for: picking the most interesting detail as the topic sentence (it must sum up all the others) · a new paragraph for every sentence · one huge block · "However" with no contrast · starting every sentence with a linking word. Support: the poster (p.4) on the desk; give weaker groups the topic sentences of the jigsaw first. Fast finishers: rewrite "The Sleepover" with a fourth paragraph that starts with a new place.

Paragraph Swap A/B

↗ Léim

Each story is written with no paragraph breaks. Only the reader can see where they go (//). Listen for TiPToP: a new Time, Place, Topic or Person.

Student A · Paragraph Swap

1. Read your story aloud slowly, one sentence at a time. Your partner listens with their book closed and says "New paragraph!" + the reason (Time, Place, Topic or Person) when they hear one. Check against the // marks.

"The Sleepover" Ciara's sleepover was supposed to be the best night of the year. Six of us squeezed into her bedroom with sleeping bags and snacks. We watched two films and ate far too many crisps. // At midnight, the lights suddenly went out. [Time] Somebody screamed, and the popcorn went everywhere. // Down in the kitchen, we found a torch in a drawer. [Place] The whole street outside was dark. // Ciara's dad appeared at the back door with a lantern. [Person] He told us the storm had knocked down a power line. We spent the rest of the night telling ghost stories by lantern light.

2. Then listen to your partner's story, "The Breakfast Club". Say "New paragraph!" + the reason each time you hear one. How many did you get out of 3?

Student B · Paragraph Swap

1. Read your story aloud slowly, one sentence at a time. Your partner listens with their book closed and says "New paragraph!" + the reason (Time, Place, Topic or Person) when they hear one. Check against the // marks.

"The Breakfast Club" Our school should start a breakfast club. Many students arrive in the morning without eating anything. It is hard to concentrate in class on an empty stomach. // Another reason is that a breakfast club helps students make friends. [Topic] Students from different years would sit together and chat. // On the other hand, some people say it would cost too much. [Topic] The canteen already has a toaster and a kettle. Parents could donate cereal and fruit. // In addition, students who arrive early would have somewhere warm to wait. [Topic] The breakfast club deserves a try.

2. Then listen to your partner's story, "The Sleepover". Say "New paragraph!" + the reason each time you hear one. How many did you get out of 3?

Fix the Paragraph

↗ Léim

A. Fix the Paragraph

Each paragraph has one problem. Say what's wrong and how to fix it.

ParagraphWhat's wrong and the fix
The sliotar can travel at 150 km an hour. Hurling players need quick hands. Teams race from end to end in seconds.
Breakfast gives you energy for the morning. Students who eat breakfast concentrate better. My cousin lives in Sligo. Porridge is a cheap, filling breakfast choice.
Dogs are loyal pets. They wait by the door for you. Cats prefer to be left alone and sleep all day.
The test seemed easy at first. As a result, nobody finished the last question.
Phones are a big distraction in class.
We went to bed at ten. The next morning, the garden was covered in snow.

B. Exam-style questions: "The Day the River Rose"

Rain had been falling on Bandon for three days. By Friday, the river was brown and roaring. We kept checking the weather warnings on the radio. That night, the water crept over the riverbank. As a result, the road outside our house disappeared under the flood. Upstairs in my bedroom, I watched the water rise. Our car floated slowly away down the street. Mrs Daly from next door rowed up to our window in a little red boat. She was laughing and waving a flask of tea. I was too worried about our car to laugh.
  1. This text should be four paragraphs. Write the first two words of each new paragraph and the TiPToP reason.
  2. Write a topic sentence that could start the whole story.
  3. Find a linking word in the text. What job does it do?
  4. Write the next paragraph. Start it with a new time.
  5. Write one paragraph arguing that every town needs a library. Use a topic sentence, support and a linking word.

Paragraph Swap

"The Sleepover": new paragraphs at "At midnight" (Time), "Down in the kitchen" (Place), "Ciara's dad" (Person). "The Breakfast Club": "Another reason", "On the other hand", "In addition" (all Topic: a new point).

A. Fix the Paragraph

The sliotar can travel at 150 km an hour. Hurling players need quick hands. Teams race from end to end in seconds.No topic sentence. Add one first: Hurling is one of the fastest field games in the world.
Breakfast gives you energy for the morning. Students who eat breakfast concentrate better. My cousin lives in Sligo. Porridge is a cheap, filling breakfast choice.Off-topic sentence: cut My cousin lives in Sligo.
Dogs are loyal pets. They wait by the door for you. Cats prefer to be left alone and sleep all day.Two ideas: start a new paragraph (new topic) at Cats prefer…, with its own topic sentence and details.
The test seemed easy at first. As a result, nobody finished the last question.Wrong linking word: it's a contrast, not a result. However, nobody finished the last question.
Phones are a big distraction in class.A topic sentence on its own isn't a paragraph. Add details or examples: A single buzz can pull attention away from the lesson…
We went to bed at ten. The next morning, the garden was covered in snow.Time moves on: start a new paragraph at The next morning, (TiPToP: Time).

B. Exam-style questions

  1. "That night," (Time) · "Upstairs in" (Place) · "Mrs Daly" (Person).
  2. e.g. The summer our town flooded was the scariest week of my life.
  3. "As a result": it shows what happened because the water came over the bank (a result).
  4. Any paragraph that opens with a time phrase (e.g. "By morning, …" / "Two days later, …") and stays on one main idea.
  5. e.g. Every town needs a library. It gives everyone free books, films and computers. For example, students can print school projects there. As a result, it helps families who can't afford these things at home.

Marking tip

In students' own writing, credit a clear topic sentence, support that stays on the point and paragraphs that change at a sensible place. One idea per paragraph matters more than length.
