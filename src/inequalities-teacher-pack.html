Junior Cycle Maths · Teacher pack

Inequalities

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): AF.4, AF.7c, U.6

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can read and write the four inequality signs, show a solution on a number line (open and closed dots; dots for ℕ and ℤ, a shaded line for ℝ), solve linear inequalities, list or count whole-number solutions, read a solution from a graph, and form and solve an inequality from a real-life problem. Higher Level: dividing by a negative (flip the sign), double inequalities and fractions.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Shade Match mingle cards + Budget Swap A/B cards · p.3 Inequality Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 3rd year (Higher and Ordinary). Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: the height sign. "You must be at least 120 cm for this ride." Who can go: 119 cm? 120 cm? 135.5 cm? Write h ≥ 120 and draw it on a number line together. Elicit open vs closed dot from "under 12s go free".
8 minGuided discovery. Pairs start with 6 > 2 and do the same thing to both sides: + 3, − 10, × 2, ÷ 2, × (−1), ÷ (−2). Which ones are still true? Elicit the flip rule (Higher Level). Ordinary Level groups: solve 2x + 1 < 9 like an equation and test x = 3, 4, 5.
10 minShade Match mingle (p.2, 24 cards = 12 inequality cards + 12 number-line cards; one card each). Inequality card? Solve it, then find the number line that shows your answer. Number-line card? Find your inequality. Pairs check: dot, direction, dots or shaded line.
12 minBudget Swap (p.2, pairs, don't show your card). Each fact is on only ONE card. Ask your partner for what you're missing ("How much is the bus?"), write the inequality together, then solve and round the sensible way. Feedback: class trip → 29 students (round down); cake sale → 55 cakes.
10 minInequality Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, solve the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, and build the chain to FINISH.
12 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name it, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone. Question 5 is Higher Level.
3 minExit ticket. Solve 3x − 4 < 11 and list the values if x ∈ ℕ. (x < 5: {1, 2, 3, 4}.) Homework: play Number-Line Shade at leim.ie/number-line-shade.html.
Mingle key (inequality card → number-line card): 21 → 18 · 12 → 5 · 16 → 6 · 3 → 9 · 14 → 24 · 13 → 2 · 15 → 22 · 20 → 1 · 10 → 19 · 11 → 4 · 7 → 17 · 23 → 8.
Errors to look for: open and closed dots swapped · 0 in an ℕ answer · a shaded line for x ∈ ℤ · not flipping when dividing by a negative · only subtracting from two parts of a double inequality · rounding up for a limit or down for a target. Support: "Is the end number allowed? Yes → closed dot." Fast finishers: write an inequality whose ℕ answer is {1, 2, 3, 4, 5} in three different ways.

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. Solve 5x − 2 > 3x + 6, x ∈ ℝ, and show your answer on a number line.
  2. List the values of x for which 2(x + 1) ≤ 11, x ∈ ℕ.
  3. Liam has €40. Cinema tickets cost €9 each and he must also buy one €4 popcorn. (a) Write an inequality in t, the number of tickets. (b) What is the greatest number of tickets he can buy?
  4. The graph shows y = x − 2 and the dashed line y = 1. For which values of x is y > 1? Check with algebra.
  5. Higher Level: (a) solve 3 − 4x ≥ −13. (b) List the values of x for which −7 < 3x + 2 ≤ 8, x ∈ ℤ.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. 2x > 8 → x > 4: open dot at 4, shaded to the right with an arrow
  2. 2x + 2 ≤ 11 → x ≤ 4.5 → {1, 2, 3, 4}
  3. (a) 9t + 4 ≤ 40 (b) 9t ≤ 36 → t ≤ 4: 4 tickets
  4. The line meets y = 1 at x = 3 and is above it to the right: x > 3. Check: x − 2 > 1 → x > 3 ✓
  5. (a) −4x ≥ −16 → divide by −4, flip: x ≤ 4 (b) −9 < 3x ≤ 6 → −3 < x ≤ 2: {−2, −1, 0, 1, 2}

Marking tip

Give credit for each step, as the exam does: a correct first step, the solved inequality, and the number line or list. On a number line look for three things: the right dot, the right direction, and dots (ℕ, ℤ) or a shaded line (ℝ). In word problems insist on a sentence answer that has been rounded the sensible way.
