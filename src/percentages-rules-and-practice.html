Percentages

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): N.2a, N.2b. Play the game: leim.ie/percent-pop.html

1. Percent = out of 100

45% = 45/100 = 0.45. % → decimal: ÷ 100 (7% = 0.07, not 0.7). Decimal → %: × 100 (0.375 = 37.5%). Fraction → %: make the bottom 100, or divide and × 100 (3/20 = 15/100 = 15%). % → fraction: over 100, then simplify (35% = 7/20).

2. A percentage of an amount

Build it from 10% (÷ 10), 5% (half of 10%) and 1% (÷ 100). 35% of €80: 10% = €8, 30% = €24, 5% = €4, so 35% = €28. Calculator: 0.35 × 80. Swap trick: 8% of 25 = 25% of 8 = 2.

3. One amount as a % of another

Fraction × 100: 18 out of 24 = 18/24 × 100 = 75%. Same units first (350 g of 2 kg = 350/2000).

5. Percentage change

Change ÷ original × 100. €50 → €60: 10 ÷ 50 × 100 = 20% increase. Always divide by the starting amount.

6. Back to the start

After 20% off, a jacket costs €48. €48 is 80%, so 1% = €0.60 and 100% = €60 (or 48 ÷ 0.8).

Classic mistake: adding 20% back on: 48 × 1.2 = €57.60, not €60. The 20% was of the OLD price. Ask: "which amount is 100%?"
Exam tip: show the step ("10% = €8, so 30% = €24") because it earns marks even if the last line slips. Money has 2 decimal places (€57.60). Check the answer makes sense: a sale price must be less than the old price.

E. Exam-style questions (show your work)

  1. A school has 560 students. 45% walk to school, 1/4 cycle and the rest come by bus. (a) How many come by bus? (b) What percentage come by bus?
  2. A hoodie costs €95. Shop A takes 20% off. Shop B takes €15 off. (a) Which is cheaper, and by how much? (b) After the sale, Shop A puts its price back up to €95. Find the percentage increase.
  3. After a 20% increase, a ticket costs €78. (a) What did it cost before? (b) Why is 20% off €78 the wrong method?
  4. A €400 bike goes up by 10%, then the new price is cut by 10%. Is it back to €400? Explain.
