Comparing texts

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R6, R7, R9, W8. Play the game: leim.ie/compare-cards.html

A comparing question gives you two texts (two poems, two extracts, or two texts you studied) and asks how they are alike and how they are different. Write about both texts together, point by point, not one essay on each.

1. What to compare

Narratorfirst person (“I”) or third person (he / she)?
Settingwhere and when? by the sea, on a farm, at night …
Main characterkind, brave, determined, honest …? A quote from each text.
Mood and endinghappy or sad? Does the mood change?
Themewhat is each text about underneath? (friendship, loss, courage)

2. Linking words

AlikeDifferent
Both … · Similarly, … · Likewise, … · In the same way, … · also · tooHowever, … · In contrast, … · On the other hand, … · whereas · while · unlike

3. Compare, don't retell

Weak (two summaries)“The Calf” is about Ruth in a barn at night. She gives her coat to a calf. “Inis Oírr” is about Nuala on a ferry. She gives her seat to an old man.
Strong (point by point)Both main characters are kind: Ruth “shared her coat” and Nuala “offered her seat”. However, “The Calf” is set on a farm at night, whereas “Inis Oírr” is set by the sea in bright sunshine.

4. A frame that works

Both texts … (similarity + a short quote from each) → However, … or …, whereas … (difference + a quote from each) → Overall, … (your view, if the question asks).

Exam tip: every paragraph should mention both texts. One short quote from each beats three from one.

F. Exam-style: two original texts

Text A: The School BusEvery morning I sit at the back of the school bus with my headphones on. Nobody talks to me, and I like it that way. Outside, the wet green fields of Offaly roll past. When the bus finally stops at the gate, I'm the last one off.
Text B: The Walk to SchoolEvery morning Jack walks to school with his twin sisters, one on each hand. They chatter and skip and ask him a hundred questions. He answers every one. At the gate, they hug him goodbye and run off laughing.
  1. Name one way the texts are similar and one way they are different. Use a short quote from each text.
  2. Which character would you rather share the journey with? Refer to both texts.

Answers

A1. Both · 2. B · 3. Neither · 4. A · 5. B · 6. B · 7. A · 8. Neither · 9. Both · 10. B
B11. S · 12. S · 13. C · 14. C · 15. C · 16. S (S = a similarity word: Similarly, / Likewise, / In the same way, · C = a contrast word: However, / In contrast, / On the other hand,)
C17. b · 18. c (the others compare something untrue, mention only one text, or swap the facts)
D19. “Market Day” ends happily, whereas “The Window” ends sadly. · 20. “First Day” has a determined main character, whereas “The Football” has an honest main character.
F21. Similar: both are journeys to school “Every morning”, and both end “at the gate”. Different: Text A is told in the first person and the narrator is alone by choice (“Nobody talks to me, and I like it that way”), whereas Text B is told in the third person and Jack is surrounded by his sisters, who “chatter and skip”. · 22. Any choice, with a reason and a quote from each text (e.g. Jack: he is patient, “He answers every one”; the narrator of A wants quiet).
