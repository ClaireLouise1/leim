1. Volume or surface area?

Volume = the space inside (cm³, m³, litres). Surface area = the area of all the outside faces (cm², m²). 1 litre = 1,000 cm³ · 1 m³ = 1,000 litres.

2. Prisms and cylinders

Volume = area of the end × length. A triangular end: ½ × base × perpendicular height (not the sloping side). A cube: V = a³, SA = 6a². A closed cylinder: SA = 2πr² + 2πrh; no lid: πr² + 2πrh.

3. Nets

Cuboid: 6 rectangles
2 triangles + 3 rectangles
2 circles + 1 rectangle

The net's area = the surface area. The cylinder's rectangle is 2πr long.

4. Pack it, fill it, splash it

Boxes in a container: count along each edge, round DOWN, multiply (don't divide the volumes). Missing length: volume ÷ the other two. Water rising: rise = volume sunk ÷ base area (in a cylinder π cancels). Combined solids: add the parts.

5. π and units

Follow the instruction: π button, 3.14, 22/7 or in terms of π (36π stays 36π). r is the RADIUS: halve a diameter first. Round only at the end.

Higher Level: work backwards (⁴⁄₃πr³ = 288π → r³ = 216 → r = 6). Melting or recasting: the volumes are equal.

Classic mistakes: diameter used as r · forgetting a face (or the missing lid) · cm² for a volume · 1 litre = 100 cm³ · dividing volumes to count boxes · the sloping side as the height.
Exam tip: the cylinder and sphere formulas are in the Formulae and Tables booklet. Write the formula, put in the numbers, then the answer with units.

  1. The water trough. A trough is a cuboid 2 m long, 0.8 m wide and 0.5 m deep. (a) Find its volume in m³. (b) How many litres does it hold? (c) A hose fills it at 20 litres a minute. How long does it take?

  3. Higher Level. Melting. A metal sphere of radius 6 cm is melted down and made into a solid cylinder of radius 4 cm. (a) Find the height of the cylinder. (b) Find the total surface area of the cylinder, in terms of π.
