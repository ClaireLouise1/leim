Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

Spelling and commonly confused words

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): W11, R10

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can choose correctly between common mix-ups (there / their / they're, your / you're, to / too / two, could have, than / then …), apply the doubling, drop-the-e and y → i rules when adding endings, form regular and irregular plurals, and use memory tricks for tricky words.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Gap Match mingle cards + Spelling Swap A/B cards · p.3 Spelling Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (useful revision in 2nd and 3rd). Time: about 60 minutes. All sentences are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Board: “hopping / hoping”. Who is jumping? Who is wishing? Elicit that one letter changes the word.
8 minGuided discovery. Board hop → hopping, run → running, hope → hoping, ride → riding. Pairs: when do we double? when do we drop the e? Then happy → happiness, carry → carried, cry → crying, play → played: what happens to y? Quick board review of the swap test (they're = they are).
10 minGap Match mingle (p.2, 24 cards = 12 sentences with a gap + 12 words; one card each). Find your partner, then say the rule or the swap test that proves it.
12 minSpelling Swap (p.2, pairs, don't show your card). A reads a sentence and says the bold word twice; B writes it. 8 words each, then swap roles. Swap cards and check; each missed word is written 3 times with its memory trick (poster, p.4).
10 minSpelling Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the answer to the clue, find the domino with that word on its left. Build the chain to FINISH.
12 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs, then the exam-style questions alone.
3 minExit ticket. “Their going too the shop to buy to apples.” → They're going to the shop to buy two apples. Homework: play Spell Strike at leim.ie/spell-strike.html.
Mingle key (sentence card → word card): 22 → 14 · 18 → 10 · 16 → 8 · 15 → 23 · 9 → 20 · 12 → 21 · 11 → 5 · 13 → 3 · 17 → 1 · 6 → 2 · 4 → 24 · 7 → 19.
Errors to look for: could of · their / there / they're · to / too · then for than · hopeing / hoping · babys, potatos · i before e slips. Support: the poster (p.4) on the desk. Fast finishers: write one sentence with there, their and they're.

A. Error Hunt

I new the answer straight away.Past of know → knew (new = not old)
We where late for school.Past of are → were
Can you here the music?With your ear → hear
The team lost it's first match.Belonging to it, no apostrophe → its
I brought a new phone in the sale.Paid money, from buy → bought
She is to tired to play.More than enough → too

B. Exam-style questions

  1. Febuary → February · Their → There · definately → definitely · intresting → interesting · freind → friend · embarassed → embarrassed · should of → should have · then → than
  2. Their (should be There), of (should have), then (than). A spellchecker won't catch these, so read them yourself.
  3. After should, could, would, must and might we need the verb have. “Of” only sounds the same as the short form should've.
  4. prisoners, coats, classes, stories (class ends in s → es; story is consonant + y → ies)
  5. e.g. They're bringing their lunches, so leave the bags over there.

Marking tip

In students' own writing, spelling is part of accuracy. Mix-ups (there / their, could of) and words copied wrongly from the question count most against an answer; an occasional slip in a hard word matters less than a pattern of the same mistake.
