Junior Cycle Maths · Teacher pack

Problem solving & exam mix

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): U.5, U.6, U.7, U.8, U.9, U.10, U.13

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can break a problem in context into steps, choose the right topic for each step, find the hidden middle number (a perimeter, an area, a time), use the right units and rounding, check that an answer makes sense, and set out their work so it earns step marks. A good revision lesson for 3rd year before the exam.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Two-Step Match mingle cards + Problem Swap A/B cards · p.3 Mixed Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 2nd and 3rd year (Ordinary and Higher; one question marked Higher Level). Time: about 60 minutes. Calculators needed. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: what's the hidden number? Board: "Garden 10 m by 6 m. Fence €20 a metre. Cost?" Students write ONLY the number they need first (32 m). Elicit that most exam questions hide a middle step.
8 minGuided discovery. Pairs sort three problems (wall + tins, journey + arrival time, five test scores) by "what do I find first?". Elicit the four steps Read · Plan · Do · Check, and two traps: round tins UP, 2.5 h = 2 h 30 min.
10 minTwo-Step Match mingle (p.2, 24 cards = 12 problem cards + 12 answer cards; one card each; calculators allowed). Find your partner, then tell the class your hidden middle number.
12 minProblem Swap (p.2, pairs, don't show your card). Each fact is on only ONE card. Ask for what you're missing, then fill in the blanks together. Feedback: trip → 1 h 45 min, 11:00, 2 buses, €13.46; patio → 30 m², 23 m, 120 slabs, €384.
10 minMixed Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, and build the chain to FINISH. Every strand is in the chain.
12 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name it, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone. Question 5 is Higher Level.
3 minExit ticket. "A room is 5 m by 4 m. Carpet costs €25 per m². Cost?" (€500.) Which step is the hidden one? Homework: play Exam Mix at leim.ie/exam-mix.html.
Mingle key (problem card → answer card): 1 → 5 · 16 → 23 · 20 → 18 · 21 → 2 · 9 → 8 · 11 → 12 · 3 → 17 · 6 → 13 · 7 → 24 · 15 → 10 · 14 → 22 · 19 → 4.
Errors to look for: adding two percentage discounts · rounding tins or buses down · 2.5 h read as 2 h 50 min · cm³ given as litres · overtime paid at double. Support: a "which topic?" key-word list on the desk (fence → perimeter, cover → area, fill → volume). Fast finishers: write your own two-step problem for a partner.

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. A lawn is 12 m by 7.5 m. One box of seed covers 20 m² and costs €8.50. (a) Find the area. (b) How many boxes are needed? (c) Find the cost.
  2. Plan A costs €10 a month + €2 per GB. Plan B costs €4 per GB. (a) Find the cost of 4 GB on each plan. (b) For how many GB do they cost the same? (c) Which plan is cheaper for 8 GB?
  3. A 5 m ladder leans against a wall. Its foot is 1.4 m from the wall. (a) How high up the wall does it reach? (b) Find the angle it makes with the ground, to the nearest degree.
  4. In a survey of 40 students, 12 walk to school, 18 get the bus and the rest come by car. (a) What percentage come by car? (b) Find the pie-chart angle for bus. (c) One student is picked at random. Find P(walks).
  5. Higher Level: a cylinder-shaped tank has radius 0.5 m and height 1.2 m. Water flows in at 15 litres a minute. How long does it take to fill, to the nearest minute? (1 m³ = 1000 litres)

B. Exam-style questions

  1. (a) 12 × 7.5 = 90 m² (b) 90 ÷ 20 = 4.5 → 5 boxes (c) 5 × €8.50 = €42.50
  2. (a) A: €10 + €8 = €18; B: €16 (b) 10 + 2g = 4g → 5 GB (c) A: €26, B: €32, so Plan A
  3. (a) √(5² − 1.4²) = √23.04 = 4.8 m (b) cos A = 1.4 ÷ 5 = 0.28, A = 74°
  4. (a) 10 by car: 10 ÷ 40 = 25% (b) 18/40 × 360° = 162° (c) 12/40 = 3/10
  5. V = π × 0.5² × 1.2 = 0.942… m³ = 942.5 litres. 942.5 ÷ 15 = 62.8 → 63 minutes

Marking tip

Mark in steps, as the exam does: the hidden middle number (perimeter, area, time), the second step done with it, then units and rounding. If a student's first step is wrong but used correctly afterwards, give the later marks.
