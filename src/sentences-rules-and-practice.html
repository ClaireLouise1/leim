Sentences, fragments and run-ons

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): W10, W11, W12. Play the game: leim.ie/sentence-surgeon.html

1. What a sentence needs

Who or whatthe subject: The bus, Ciara, We
A verbdoing or being: was, laughed, missed
A complete thoughtmakes sense on its own; capital letter at the start, end mark at the end

2. Fragments: half a sentence

Because the bus was late. → Because the bus was late, we missed the film.
Running down the corridor. → I was running down the corridor.
The boy with the red scarf. → The boy with the red scarf waved.

3. Run-ons: two sentences jammed together

It was raining, we stayed in. ✗ (a comma can't hold two sentences)
→ It was raining. We stayed in.
→ It was raining, so we stayed in.

4. Four types of sentence

Statementgives information: The shop opens at nine.
Questionasks something: Where is my jumper?
Commandtells someone to do something: Close the door.
Exclamationstrong feeling: What a goal! How cold it is!

5. Simple, compound, complex

Simpleone clause: Rain fell all night. (Two names or two verbs can still be simple: Ciara and Jack walked to school.)
Compoundtwo main clauses + and / but / so / or: We knocked, but nobody answered.
Complexmain clause + a because / when / if / although / until … clause: We stayed in because it was raining.

6. Joining words

but contrast · so result · or choice · because reason · when / until / before / while time · if condition · although surprise

7. Commas with joining words

Comma before and / but / so / or when they join two sentences: I was tired, but I kept running.
"Because / when / if…" part first? Comma after it: If you hurry, you'll catch the train.

8. Sentences for effect

Mix short and long sentences. A very short sentence after longer ones creates tension or shock: Then the lights went out. Writers sometimes use a fragment on purpose (Too silent.), but in your own answers use full sentences.

Classic mistakes: a comma between two sentences (comma splice) · starting a sentence with "Because…" and never finishing it · forgetting the question mark · a small letter after a full stop.
Exam tip: read your answer back in your head. Where your voice drops and you pause, you need a full stop, not a comma.

Worked examples

2. Because the bus was late.Because… then what happened? There's no main part, so it's a fragment. Fix: Because the bus was late, we missed the film.
5. When the bell rang, everyone rushed outside."When the bell rang" can't stand alone, but it's joined to a main part (everyone rushed outside), so it's a sentence.
36. The children laughed and shouted.Two verbs, but one subject doing both: one clause, so simple.
34. We knocked, but nobody answered.Two main clauses (we knocked / nobody answered) joined by "but": compound.
