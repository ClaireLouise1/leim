Surds

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): N.1b, U.13. Play the game: leim.ie/surd-simplify.html

1. What is a surd?

A square root that never comes out exact: √2, √3, √5, √12 …  √9 = 3 and √25 = 5 are not surds. Leave answers "in surd form" (exact): √2, not 1.41.

2. Spot the pattern

√4 × √9 = 2 × 3 = 6√36 = 6
√4 × √25 = 2 × 5 = 10√100 = 10
√9 + √16 = 3 + 4 = 7√25 = 5 (not 7!)

√a × √b = √(ab), but √a + √b ≠ √(a + b).

3. The rules

Multiply: √a × √b = √(ab)√2 × √8 = √16 = 4
Divide: √a ÷ √b = √(a ÷ b)√50 ÷ √2 = √25 = 5
Square: (√a)² = a(√7)² = 7  ·  (3√2)² = 9 × 2 = 18

4. Simplifying

Find the biggest square number that goes in: 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64 …

√72 = √(36 × 2) = √36 × √2 = 6√2
√48 = √(16 × 3) = 4√3 (2√12 is not finished)
Backwards: 3√5 = √9 × √5 = √45

5. Adding and subtracting

Only like surds (same number under the root). Treat √2 like x: 3√2 + 5√2 = 8√2.

√8 + √18 = 2√2 + 3√2 = 5√2 (simplify first)
√50 − √8 = 5√2 − 2√2 = 3√2
√2 + √3 can't be simplified (it is not √5)

Multiply numbers and roots separately: 2√3 × 5√2 = 10√6.

6. Higher Level: brackets

Expand like algebra. √3 × √3 = 3.

√3(4 + √3) = 4√3 + 3 = 3 + 4√3
(5 + √2)(5 − √2) = 25 − 2 = 23 (middle terms cancel)
(1 + √2)² = 1 + √2 + √2 + 2 = 3 + 2√2
Classic mistakes: not using the biggest square (2√12) · √2 + √8 = √10 · 3√5 × 2√5 = 6√5 (it's 6 × 5 = 30) · (√6)² = 36 (it's 6) · (1 + √2)² = 1 + 2 (forgot the middle).
Exam tip: show the square you used: √72 = √36 × √2 = 6√2. "In the form a√b" means b must have no square factor left.
