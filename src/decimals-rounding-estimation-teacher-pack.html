Decimals & rounding

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): N.1e, N.2a (rounding and estimating come back in every measurement, money and statistics question)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can read and compare decimals using place value, multiply and divide by 10, 100 and 1000, switch between fractions and decimals, add, subtract, multiply and divide decimals by hand, round to decimal places, significant figures and the nearest 10/100/1000, and estimate to check whether an answer is reasonable.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Human Number Line cards + Estimation Auction · p.3 Rounding Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: Which is bigger? Board 0.5 and 0.45. Hands up for each. Ask a student who chose 0.45 why ("45 is bigger than 5"). Don't correct yet.
12 minHuman Number Line (p.2 cards; one each). Mark 0 and 1 at opposite ends of the room. Students stand where their number belongs, with no talking for the first minute, then they may move each other and must explain. Check left to right against the key. Feedback: how did you decide 0.405 goes before 0.45? Elicit the rule: same number of places first (0.405 vs 0.450). Revisit the lead-in.
8 minGuided discovery: rounding. Board 6.2849 and the four rounding instructions from the poster (p.4). Pairs try them before you show the rule. Ask: what's the difference between 2 decimal places and 2 significant figures? When do we need zeros as place holders?
10 minEstimation Auction (p.2, one sheet per team of 3–4, €100 each). Teams decide which statements are right and bid (min €10) on the ones they believe. Reveal one by one: right = double your money; wrong = lose it. The winning team explains how estimating (5 × 20 = 100) caught the decimal-point slip.
12 minRounding Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, and build the chain to FINISH. Answer chain below.
10 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name the rule, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone. Feedback on question 3: why does rounding too early lose marks?
3 minExit ticket. 0.0748 to 2 s.f. and 2.4 × 0.3 (0.075; 0.72). Homework: play Round It at leim.ie/round-it.html (Is it reasonable? round) and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Human Number Line key (smallest → biggest): 0.05, 0.07, 0.09, 0.099, 0.19, 0.3, 0.303, 0.33, 0.405, 0.45, 0.5, 0.54, 0.7, 0.707, 0.77, 0.9.
Errors to look for: "longer is bigger" (0.405 > 0.5) · "add a zero" for × 10 with decimals (3.470) · counting the decimal places wrongly when multiplying (0.3 × 0.3 = 0.9) · rounding by cutting off (5.38 → 5.3) · dropping place holders (28 for 28 000) · counting leading zeros as significant figures · rounding in the middle of a calculation · €9.8 instead of €9.80. Support: a place-value grid on the desk. Fast finishers: find a number that is 3.5 to 1 d.p. and 3.46 to 2 d.p. (e.g. 3.459); what are the smallest and biggest numbers that round to 4.7?

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. A taxi charges €4.20 plus €1.35 per km. How much is a 12.6 km journey?
  2. (a) Estimate (12.4 × 0.49) ÷ 2.1 by rounding each number to 1 significant figure. (b) Use a calculator to find the answer correct to 2 decimal places.
  3. A marathon is 42.195 km. Orla runs it in 3.5 hours. Find her average speed in km/h, correct to 1 decimal place. Why should she not round 42.195 to 42 first?
  4. Juice costs €0.85 a carton. Does €21 buy 24 cartons? Show your work.
  5. Best buy: 1.5 kg of rice for €6.75 or 2.4 kg for €10.20? Find the price per kg of each.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. 1.35 × 12.6 = 17.01, plus 4.20 = €21.21
  2. (a) 10 × 0.5 ÷ 2 = 2.5 (b) 6.076 ÷ 2.1 = 2.893… = 2.89 (close to 2.5, so reasonable)
  3. 42.195 ÷ 3.5 = 12.055… = 12.1 km/h. Rounding first (42 ÷ 3.5 = 12) gives a different answer: round at the end.
  4. 24 × €0.85 = €20.40, which is less than €21, so yes (60c change).
  5. €6.75 ÷ 1.5 = €4.50/kg; €10.20 ÷ 2.4 = €4.25/kg, so the 2.4 kg bag is better value.

Marking tip

Give credit for each step, as the exam does: the correct unrounded answer with a rounding slip still earns most of the marks. Insist on writing the estimate, and on 2 decimal places for money.
