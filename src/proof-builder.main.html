Theorems, congruent & similar triangles

Spot the pattern

Draw a parallelogram on paper (two pairs of parallel sides). Draw the diagonal from A to C, then cut the parallelogram out and cut along the diagonal.

  • Turn one triangle round. Does it fit exactly on top of the other?
  • Which side of one triangle lands on [AB]? Which lands on [BC]?
  • So what can you say about the opposite sides and the opposite angles of every parallelogram?
Show me

The two triangles always fit exactly: they are congruent. The angles with the same marks are alternate angles (equal), and [AC] is shared, so they are congruent by ASA. When they fit, [CD] lands on [AB] and [DA] lands on [BC]. So in every parallelogram the opposite sides are equal and the opposite angles are equal: that's Theorem 9, and you have just seen its proof.

The words of proof

Axiom: a statement we accept without proof, as a starting point (for example, the congruence tests SAS, ASA and SSS). Theorem: a statement that has been proved. Proof: a chain of steps, each with a reason, that shows a statement must be true. Corollary: a result that follows quickly from a theorem. Converse: swap the "if" and the "then" parts. Implies (⇒): "A ⇒ B" means "if A is true, then B is true".
A converse isn't always true! "If a shape is a square, then it has four right angles" is true, but its converse "if a shape has four right angles, then it is a square" is false (a rectangle). For the theorems on this page, the converses we use are true.

Parallelograms (Theorems 9 and 10)

Opposite sides are equal and opposite angles are equal (Theorem 9). Neighbouring angles add to 180° (co-interior angles).
The diagonals bisect each other (Theorem 10): each diagonal cuts the other exactly in half. They are not usually equal or at right angles.

Converses: a quadrilateral IS a parallelogram if its opposite angles are equal, or its opposite sides are equal, or its diagonals bisect each other. Higher Level: a diagonal divides a parallelogram into two congruent triangles (Corollary 1).

Circles

Every angle in a semicircle is a right angle (Corollary 3). If [BC] is a diameter, ∠BAC = 90°. Converse (Corollary 4): if the angle at A is 90°, then [BC] is a diameter.
Higher Level: the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circle standing on the same arc (Theorem 19). Angles at the circle on the same arc are equal (Corollary 2).
Higher Level: in a cyclic quadrilateral (all four corners on the circle) opposite angles add to 180° (Corollary 5).

Congruent triangles: the same shape AND the same size

  • SSS: all three sides match.
  • SAS: two sides and the angle between them (the included angle) match.
  • ASA: two angles and the side between them match.
  • RHS: both have a right angle, the same hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) and one other equal side.
  • Not tests: three angles (AAA) only gives the same shape, and two sides with an angle that is NOT between them can give two different triangles.
Naming matters: △ABC ≡ △PQR means A matches P, B matches Q and C matches R. So |BC| = |QR| and ∠ABC = ∠PQR. Once two triangles are congruent, all their matching parts are equal: that's how a congruence proof finishes.

Similar triangles: the same shape, maybe a different size

  • Triangles are similar if their angles match (equiangular). Two matching angles are enough, as the third must match too.
  • Theorem 13: if two triangles are similar, their sides are in proportion: |AD| : |AB| = |AE| : |AC| = |DE| : |BC|.
  • Scale factor = a side of the new triangle ÷ the matching side of the old one. Multiply every side by it.
  • A line parallel to one side of a triangle makes a small triangle similar to the big one (corresponding angles, and the angle at the top is shared).
  • Higher Level: a line parallel to one side cuts the other two sides in the same ratio (Theorem 12), and three parallel lines that cut equal pieces off one transversal cut equal pieces off any other (Theorem 11).

How to write a proof

  1. Write down what you're given and what you want to prove. Mark the diagram.
  2. Write one fact per line, with the reason in brackets: ∠BAC = ∠ACD (alternate angles).
  3. For congruence, find three matching facts and name the test: △ABD ≡ △ACD (SSS).
  4. Finish with "So …" and the thing you wanted to prove. Each line may only use facts from the lines before it.
  5. You won't be asked to write out the formal proofs of the theorems, but you need to use them and understand how they work.
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Geometry on both papers uses these facts. Expect to find missing angles and lengths in parallelograms, circles and triangles, to say which test proves two triangles congruent, to use similar triangles to find a length, and to write a short argument with a reason on every line. Higher Level adds the circle theorems (Theorem 19, Corollaries 2 and 5), Theorems 11 and 12, and more proof.

How marks are won and lost

  • A reason on every line. "∠ABC = 65°" on its own loses marks when the question says "give a reason" or "justify".
  • Name the test. In a congruence proof, the last line must say which test: SSS, SAS, ASA or RHS, and the three facts must fit it.
  • Match the letters. △ABC ≡ △PQR tells you which parts match. Getting the order wrong is the most common slip.
  • Use the scale factor the right way round. Big ÷ small to go from the small triangle to the big one.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first.

  1. A parallelogram. PQRS is a parallelogram with ∠PQR = 115°. Its diagonals meet at T. (a) Find ∠QRS and ∠RSP, giving a reason for each. (b) |PT| = 4.5 cm and |QS| = 14 cm. Find |PR| and |QT|.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) ∠QRS = 180° − 115° = 65° (co-interior angles: PQ ∥ SR). ∠RSP = 115° (opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal).
    (b) The diagonals bisect each other (Theorem 10): |PR| = 2 × 4.5 = 9 cm and |QT| = 14 ÷ 2 = 7 cm.

  2. A congruence proof. In the quadrilateral ABCD, |AB| = |AD| and ∠BAC = ∠DAC. (a) Prove that the triangles ABC and ADC are congruent. (b) Hence show that |BC| = |DC|.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) |AB| = |AD| (given)
    ∠BAC = ∠DAC (given)
    |AC| = |AC| (common side)
    So △ABC ≡ △ADC (SAS: the angle at A is between the two sides).
    (b) [BC] and [DC] are matching sides of the congruent triangles, so |BC| = |DC|.

  3. Similar triangles. In the triangle ABC, DE ∥ BC, |AD| = 6 cm, |DB| = 3 cm and |DE| = 8 cm. (a) Explain why the triangles ADE and ABC are similar. (b) Find x = |BC|. (c) |AE| = 5 cm. Find |EC|.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) ∠ADE = ∠ABC and ∠AED = ∠ACB (corresponding angles: DE ∥ BC), and the angle at A is shared. The triangles are equiangular, so they are similar.
    (b) |AB| = 6 + 3 = 9 cm. Scale factor = 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5, so x = 8 × 1.5 = 12 cm.
    (c) |AC| = 5 × 1.5 = 7.5 cm, so |EC| = 7.5 − 5 = 2.5 cm.

  4. Angles in a circle. [BC] is a diameter of the circle with centre O, and ∠ABC = 32°. (a) Find x, giving a reason. (b) Find y. (c) Higher Level: D is on the circle. Find z, giving a reason.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) x = 90° (the angle in a semicircle is a right angle).
    (b) y = 180° − (90° + 32°) = 58° (angles in a triangle).
    (c) z and y both stand on the arc AB, so z = y = 58° (angles at the circle on the same arc are equal).
