Junior Cycle Maths · Teacher pack

Constructions

↗ Léim

Learning outcome (2018 specification): GT.3a, constructions 1–15 (3 and 7 are Higher level only)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can carry out the Junior Cycle constructions accurately, put the steps in the right order, choose the right instruments, leave their construction arcs showing, and explain why a construction works (e.g. every point on a perpendicular bisector is the same distance from both ends).

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Step Sort cards · p.3 Construction Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster: the 15 constructions · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style constructions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st or 2nd year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

8 minLead-in: Same distance? Students construct the perpendicular bisector of any [AB], pick three points on it and measure to A and to B. "What do you notice? Why does the compass have to be more than half of |AB|?" Elicit: every point on it is equidistant from A and B.
12 minStep Sort (p.2, groups of 3–4). Cut out the 18 cards. Groups sort them into five constructions and put each one's steps in order, using the pictures (the newest part is in red). Check against the key, then each group demonstrates one construction at the board.
10 minConstruction Dominoes (p.3). Start at START, answer the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, build the chain to FINISH. Chain below.
22 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs, then the exam-style constructions alone with compass, ruler and protractor. Partners swap and check each other's lengths and angles with a ruler and protractor (within 1 mm and 1°).
8 minExit ticket. "Bisect this angle using only a compass and straight edge" (draw one on the board). Look for: arcs showing, same width from both points. Homework: play Construction Steps at leim.ie/construction-steps.html.
Step Sort key (card numbers in order): Bisector of an angle: 4 → 11 → 1 · Perpendicular bisector of a segment: 18 → 15 → 7 · Perpendicular at a point on l: 5 → 16 → 2 · Parallel line through a point: 8 → 3 → 14 → 12 → 9 · Triangle: SAS: 13 → 10 → 17 → 6.
Errors to look for: arcs rubbed out · a protractor or ruler used for constructions 1 and 2 · compass opened to half of |AB| or less · the same width kept in construction 4 (the arcs meet at P) · the compass width changed between the two arcs of the angle bisector · the wrong protractor scale (180° minus the angle) · SAS angle drawn at the wrong end · a "triangle" whose two shorter sides add to less than the longest.

Step Sort

↗ Léim

Cut out the 18 cards. Sort them into five constructions, then put each construction's steps in order. In each picture the newest part is drawn in red.

Card 1
Draw a line from B through F. It bisects ∠ABC: it cuts the angle exactly in half.
Card 2
Draw the line through P and Q. It's perpendicular to l at P.
Card 3
Put the compass point on Q and draw an arc that crosses l at R and the line QP at S.
Card 4
Put the compass point on B and draw an arc that crosses both arms. Call the crossing points D and E.
Card 5
Put the compass point on P and draw arcs that cross l on each side of P, at A and B.
Card 6
Join C to B.
Card 7
Draw the line through C and D. It's the perpendicular bisector of [AB].
Card 8
Draw a line through P that crosses l. Call the crossing point Q.
Card 9
Draw the line through P and U. It's parallel to l (corresponding angles are equal).
Card 10
At A, use a protractor to draw an angle of 63° with [AB] as one arm.
Card 11
Put the compass point on D, then on E, and draw two arcs with the same compass width. They cross inside the angle at F.
Card 12
Set the compass to the distance from R to S. Put the point on T and draw an arc that crosses the last arc at U.
Card 13
Draw [AB], 8 cm long, with a ruler.
Card 14
Keep the same compass width. Put the point on P and draw an arc that crosses the line QP above P, at T.
Card 15
With the compass point on A, and then on B (same width), draw arcs above and below [AB]. They cross at C and D.
Card 16
Open the compass wider. With the point on A, and then on B (same width), draw arcs above l that cross at Q.
Card 17
Set the compass to 6 cm. Put the point on A and draw an arc that crosses the new arm at C.
Card 18
Open the compass to more than half the length of [AB].

The 15 constructions

Leave your arcs showing!

1. Bisector of an angle
2. Perpendicular bisector of a segment
3. Perpendicular from a point not on l (HL)
4. Perpendicular at a point on l
5. Parallel line through a point
6. 2 or 3 equal parts
7. Any number of equal parts (HL)
8. Segment of a given length on a ray
9. Angle of a given size on a ray
10. Triangle: SSS
11. Triangle: SAS
12. Triangle: ASA
13. Right-angled: hypotenuse + side (RHS)
14. Right-angled: side + acute angle
15. Rectangle

1 and 2: compass and straight edge only · 3 and 7: Higher level only · Sharp pencil · Label every point

B. Exam-style constructions (use plain paper; leave all your arcs showing)

  1. Construct the triangle ABC with |AB| = 8 cm, |BC| = 6 cm and |AC| = 7 cm. Measure ∠ACB.
  2. Construct the rectangle PQRS with |PQ| = 9 cm and |QR| = 5 cm. Measure the diagonal |PR|.
  3. Draw [PQ] 11 cm long. Construct its perpendicular bisector, meeting [PQ] at M. Mark the point R on the bisector, 4 cm from M. Measure |RP| and |RQ|. What do you notice, and why?
  4. (a) Draw an angle of 64° with a protractor. (b) Bisect it using only a compass and straight edge. (c) What should each half measure? (d) Why are you not allowed to use the protractor in part (b)?
For the teacher

Answers

↗ Léim

Step Sort

Bisector of an angle: 4 → 11 → 1 · Perpendicular bisector of a segment: 18 → 15 → 7 · Perpendicular at a point on l: 5 → 16 → 2 · Parallel line through a point: 8 → 3 → 14 → 12 → 9 · Triangle: SAS: 13 → 10 → 17 → 6.

A. Error Hunt

Bisect ∠ABC (construction 1): Measured 64° with a protractor and marked 32°Construction 1 must use a compass and straight edge only: arcs, not a protractor
Perpendicular bisector of a 10 cm segment: Opened the compass to 4 cm4 cm is less than half of 10 cm, so the arcs don't cross. Use more than 5 cm
Perpendicular to l at P (P on l): Kept the same compass width for the arcs from A and BWith the same width they only meet at P. Open the compass wider
Triangle with sides 4 cm, 5 cm and 10 cm: The arcs didn't meet, so I joined the ends anywayIt's impossible: 4 + 5 = 9, less than 10. No such triangle exists
Draw an angle of 50°: Drew 130° using the outer scaleRead the scale that starts at 0° on the arm: 180 − 50 = 130 is the wrong-scale slip
Divide [AB] into 3 equal parts: Drew lines through the marks perpendicular to [AB]The lines must be parallel to the line joining the last mark to B

B. Exam-style constructions

  1. SSS: draw [AB] 8 cm; arcs of 7 cm from A and 6 cm from B cross at C. ∠ACB ≈ 75.5° (accept 74°–77°).
  2. Draw [PQ] 9 cm; perpendiculars at P and Q; arcs of 5 cm give S and R; join SR. |PR| ≈ 10.3 cm (accept 10.2–10.4 cm).
  3. |RP| = |RQ| ≈ 6.8 cm. They are equal because every point on the perpendicular bisector of [PQ] is the same distance from P and Q.
  4. (c) 64 ÷ 2 = 32°. (d) The task says compass and straight edge only: the construction must work without measuring (it shows the bisector is exact, not measured).

Marking tip

As in the exam, accuracy and visible construction lines both matter. A correct-looking answer with no arcs should not get full marks. Check lengths with a ruler (within about 1 mm) and angles with a protractor (within about 1°).
