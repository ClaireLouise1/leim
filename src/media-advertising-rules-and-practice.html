Every advert wants you to buy or do something. Ask three questions: Who is it for? What is it selling? How does it try to persuade me?

1. Six tricks

Star powerFamous chef Gráinne Lynch cooks with Crispa oil.
Free giftFree delivery on all Plantly orders.
BandwagonAll your friends are wearing Kicks trainers.
Statistic9 out of 10 dogs prefer Waggo biscuits.
ExaggerationThe greatest pizza in the universe!
HurryWhile stocks last. Today only.

Ask about every statistic: who counted, and how many? "9 out of 10 dogs" might mean 10 dogs.

2. Who is it for?

Young childrentoys, cartoons, bedtime, "ask your mum or dad"
Teenagersexams, study, your mates, gaming
Parentsyour kids, the school run, family meals
Older peopleretirement, grandchildren, reading glasses
Sporty peopletraining, the gym, a personal best

3. Fact or opinion?

Fact: it can be checkedSunny Bay ice cream costs €2.50 a cone.
Opinion: a judgement nobody can proveSunny Bay ice cream is the tastiest treat around.

4. A good slogan

is short, names the brand and is catchy: alliteration (Fizzpop: feel the fizz!) or rhyme (Sparkle: no mess, no stress.).

5. Pictures and layout

The picture sends a message before you read a word: fruit and runners = healthy; diamonds and gold = luxury; a family at a table = home; balloons and rides = fun; trees and bees = natural. The eye goes to the biggest thing first, usually the picture or the headline.

Exam tip: for "How does this advert persuade?" name the trick, quote it and explain its effect on the reader. Talk about the picture and the layout too, not only the words.
