1. Word ladders: shades of meaning

Words that mean nearly the same can be weaker or stronger. Weakest at the bottom, strongest at the top:

heatwarm → hot → sweltering
angerannoyed → angry → furious
tirednesssleepy → tired → exhausted
sizebig → huge → gigantic
speedwalk → jog → sprint
raindrizzle → rain → downpour
Cut the “very”very hot → sweltering · very hungry → starving · very funny → hilarious. Only use the top word when you mean it.

2. Synonyms and antonyms

synonymsame meaning: huge = enormous, begin = start
antonymopposite: huge ↔ tiny, ancient ↔ modern
thesaurusa list of synonyms. Check a new word in a dictionary before you use it.

3. Dictionary skills

ABC orderSame first letter? Look at the 2nd letter, then the 3rd: brand, brave, bread.
Guide wordsThe first and last word on the page. fish … flag: five is on the page, fly is not.
More than one meaningbat, bank, match, light: read the whole sentence to choose.
An entry gives youthe meaning, how to say it, the word class (n, v, adj, adv) and an example.

4. Prefixes: at the start

un-, dis-, in-not: unhappy, dishonest, incorrect
im-not, before p or m: impossible, immature
il-not, before l: illegal, illogical
ir-not, before r: irregular, irrelevant
re-again: rebuild, rewrite
mis-wrongly, badly: misspell, misbehave
pre-before: preheat, preschool
Keep the whole prefix AND the whole word: il + legal = illegal, mis + spell = misspell.

5. Suffixes and word families

noun-ness, -ment, -tion, -th: happiness, decision, strength
adjective-ful, -ous, -ive, -able: careful, dangerous, creative
adverb-ly: carefully, happily (how?)
verb-en, -ify: strengthen, simplify
NounVerbAdjectiveAdverb
happiness–happyhappily

Which one fits?After the / her / my → a noun. After is / was / very → an adjective. Says how something is done → an adverb.
Exam tip: asked what a word suggests? Give its meaning AND the feeling it creates. In your own writing, one precise word beats “very” + a weak word, but don't use a fancy word you're not sure of.
