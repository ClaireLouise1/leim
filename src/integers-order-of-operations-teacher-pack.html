Integers & the order of operations

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): N.1a, N.1b, N.1c, U.1, U.2

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can order integers on the number line, add, subtract, multiply and divide with negative numbers, explain why a negative × a negative is positive, and use BIMDAS (including indices and negatives in brackets) to evaluate expressions and spot errors in worked solutions.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Find Your Match / Human Number Line cards · p.3 Integer Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: Warmer or colder? Board: Dublin 4 °C · Mullingar −3 °C · Cavan −7 °C · Cork 6 °C. Pairs put the towns in order coldest first and say how many degrees warmer Cork is than Cavan (13). Draw the vertical thermometer as a number line.
8 minGuided discovery. Write the pattern 3 × (−2) = −6, 2 × (−2) = −4 … 0 × (−2) = 0. Pairs continue it to −1 × (−2) and −2 × (−2) and state the rule in their own words. Same idea for subtraction: 5 − 2, 5 − 1, 5 − 0, 5 − (−1)… Check with the poster (p.4).
12 minFind Your Match → Human Number Line (p.2). Give each student one card (24 cards = 12 pairs; for smaller classes remove pairs). Students work out their card in their head, then mingle to find the partner whose card has the same value. Pairs then line up across the room in order of value, smallest at the left. The class checks the line, pair by pair, reading out both cards.
12 minInteger Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Each domino has an answer on the left and a new calculation on the right. Start with START, find the domino whose left side answers the calculation, and build the chain to FINISH. Answer chain below.
15 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name the rule, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone. Feedback: which slip was the most common?
5 minExit ticket. On a mini-whiteboard: (−3)² − 2 × (−5) (answer 19). Homework: play Integer Rush at leim.ie/integer-rush.html (Order of operations mode) and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Find Your Match key (smallest first, the order of the human number line): −10 − 6 = (−4)² × (−1) = −16 · (−3) × 4 = −20 + 8 = −12 · (−2)³ = −4 − 4 = −8 · 1 − 8 = (−21) ÷ 3 = −7 · 7 − 10 = (−12) ÷ 4 = −3 · −6 + 3 × 2 = 4 × 0 = 0 · (3 + 5) ÷ (−4) + 4 = −6 − (−8) = 2 · −15 ÷ (−3) = 2 − (−3) = 5 · (−1) × (−9) = 18 ÷ 2 = 9 · 2 + 3 × 4 = (−7) × (−2) = 14 · (−6) × (−6) ÷ 2 = 3 × 2² + 6 = 18 · (−5)² = 5 × 5 = 25.
Errors to look for: applying "two negatives make a positive" to addition ((−3) + (−7) = 10) · working left to right instead of BIMDAS (2 + 3 × 4 = 20) · (−4)² = −16 · 6 − (−2) = 4 · 24 ÷ 4 × 2 = 3 (× before ÷ instead of left to right) · thinking −8 is bigger than −2. Calculators: use the (−) key and type the brackets; a calculator gives −4² = −16 because it squares first. Support: keep a number line −20 to 20 on the desk. Fast finishers: using −2, 3, −4 and 5 once each with any operations and brackets, make as many integers from −10 to 10 as you can.

Integer rules

+ × + = +6 × 3 = 18
− × − = +(−6) × (−3) = 18
+ × − = −6 × (−3) = −18
− × + = −(−6) × 3 = −18

The same rules work for ÷: (−20) ÷ (−4) = 5 · 20 ÷ (−4) = −5

− (−) = +4 − (−3) = 4 + 3 = 7
+ (−) = −6 + (−9) = 6 − 9 = −3

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. The table shows the lowest temperature one night in four towns: Birr −4 °C · Athlone −9 °C · Galway 2 °C · Carlow −1 °C.
    (a) Which town was coldest? (b) How many degrees warmer was Galway than Athlone? (c) The next night, every temperature was 3 degrees lower. What was the temperature in Carlow?
  2. Niamh's bank balance is −€28. She is paid €75 and then spends €19. What is her balance now?
  3. Evaluate 5a − b² when a = −2 and b = −3.
  4. Insert one pair of brackets to make each statement true: (a) 12 − 4 × 2 + 1 = 17   (b) 12 − 4 × 2 + 1 = 0
  5. Use the distributive law to work out 8 × 99 without a calculator. Show your method.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. (a) Athlone (−9 °C is the smallest). (b) 2 − (−9) = 11 degrees. (c) −1 − 3 = −4 °C.
  2. −28 + 75 − 19 = €28.
  3. 5(−2) − (−3)² = −10 − 9 = −19. (Watch: (−3)² = 9, then subtract it.)
  4. (a) (12 − 4) × 2 + 1 = 17 (8 × 2 + 1)   (b) 12 − 4 × (2 + 1) = 0 (12 − 4 × 3 = 12 − 12). Without brackets the answer is 5.
  5. 8 × 99 = 8 × (100 − 1) = 800 − 8 = 792.

Marking tip

Give credit for each correct step, as the exam does: a correct method with one sign slip should still earn most of the marks. Ask students to circle the step where their answer went wrong before they correct it.
