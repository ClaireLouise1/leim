1. Six techniques

Rhetorical questionWho wants a town with nowhere safe to play?
Rule of threeSport teaches teamwork, patience and respect.
StatisticOur survey found that 8 in 10 students skip breakfast.
Emotive languageInnocent animals are left cold and frightened.
Direct addressYour vote can change this town.
RepetitionWe need action. We need it now.

Use real facts, or say where a number comes from ("our class survey"). Never make up a fact and pretend it is official.

2. Back it up

WeakWe should get a skate park because it would be good.
StrongA skate park would give teenagers a safe place to meet, so that fewer would skate on busy roads.

Evidence = a number, an example ("for example…", "such as…") or a result ("which means…"). Insults win no one over.

3. Build a speech

1 Hooka question, a fact or a short story
2 Pointsone per paragraph, each backed up
3 Answer backSome people say… However…
4 Call to actiontell them exactly what to do

4. Answer the other side

Some people say phones help students stay safe. However, students can stay safe with a phone kept in a locker until home time.

State their point fairly, use However / But / Yet, and deal with their point. Stay polite.

5. End with a call to action

WeakThat's all I have to say. Thanks.
StrongSign our petition today and help us plant fifty new trees.

Start with a doing word: Sign… Join… Vote… Bring… Write to…

Exam tip: for "How does the writer try to persuade?" questions: name the technique, quote it and explain its effect on the reader. For writing tasks, check who the audience is: a speech to the council is formal, polite but firm.

B. Which reason backs up the point best? Circle a letter

  1. Point: Mobile phones should be banned in class.
    • (a) Many families have a dog or a cat at home.
    • (b) Only idiots would keep their phones in class.
    • (c) Phones should be banned because they should not be allowed in any classroom at any time of the day, and everyone knows that perfectly well.
    • (d) Phones distract students: for example, our class survey found 7 in 10 checked messages during lessons.
  2. Point: Our school should start a coding club.
    • (a) We should start a coding club because a coding club is something we really should start this year.
    • (b) Coding is useful in many jobs, such as medicine, farming and music.
    • (c) Kids who can't code are hopeless.
    • (d) The hockey team won on Saturday.
  3. Point: Our library should open on Saturdays.
    • (a) Anyone who disagrees is a fool.
    • (b) The swimming pool is very warm.
    • (c) The library should open on Saturdays because Saturday is a good day for the library to open its doors to everyone in the whole town who wants to visit.
    • (d) Many students have no quiet place to study at home, for example those who share a bedroom, so a Saturday library would help.

C. Which is the best answer to the other side? Circle a letter

  1. The other side says: Some argue that plastic bottles are more convenient.
    • (a) I agree, plastic bottles are easier.
    • (b) However, a refillable bottle is just as convenient and saves money too.
    • (c) However, the school play was a great success and the cast deserve a party at the end of term.
    • (d) However, anyone who buys plastic is a pig.
  2. The other side says: Some argue that uniforms stop bullying.
    • (a) I agree, uniforms stop bullying.
    • (b) However, people who love uniforms are boring.
    • (c) However, bullying happens online too, where uniforms make no difference.
    • (d) However, the canteen should sell more fresh fruit and fewer fizzy drinks at break and at lunchtime.
  3. The other side says: Some argue that homework teaches discipline.
    • (a) I agree that homework teaches discipline.
    • (b) But the bus that brings us to school is always late on cold, wet winter mornings, which is not fair.
    • (c) But people who like homework are weird.
    • (d) But discipline can also be learned through sport, music or a part-time job.

E. Exam-style

  1. Write the opening of a speech to your school: "Our school should have a water fountain on every corridor." Use a rhetorical question and the rule of three.
  2. Name three techniques in this extract and explain how one works: "Who wants to eat lunch standing up? Every day, 120 students queue for 30 tables. The canteen is crowded, noisy and too small. You deserve somewhere to sit."

Answers

A1. Rhetorical question · 2. Direct address · 3. Direct address · 4. Statistic · 5. Rhetorical question · 6. Rule of three · 7. Rule of three · 8. Repetition · 9. Statistic · 10. Emotive language · 11. Repetition · 12. Emotive language
B13. (d) · 14. (b) · 15. (d): the right reason sticks to the point and gives evidence. The others insult, go off the topic or go round in circles.
C16. (b) · 17. (c) · 18. (d): the best answer starts with However / But / Yet, deals with their point and stays polite.
DSample answers: 19. Leave your phone in your bag at lunchtime and talk to the person beside you. · 20. Write to the council this week and tell them we need a skate park. · 21. Vote for me on Thursday and I will make your voice heard. · 22. Put every can in the green bin and let's make recycling a habit.
(Any line that tells the audience exactly what to do is fine.)
E23. Sample: Good morning, everyone. How many of you have sat through double PE with nothing to drink? A fountain on every corridor would be cheap, healthy and good for the planet.
24. Rhetorical question ("Who wants to eat lunch standing up?"), statistic ("120 students queue for 30 tables"), rule of three ("crowded, noisy and too small"), direct address ("You deserve…"). The statistic makes the problem sound real: 120 students for 30 tables is hard to argue with.
