The novel: plot, setting and theme

A novel is long, but its plot has the same shape as a short story, just stretched out. Once you can see the shape, you can find the key moments in any novel.

Spot the pattern

🐦 The Robin

AAt last, he opened the box, and the robin flew to the top of the hedge.

BIts wing was broken and it couldn't fly.

COn a frosty January morning, Kevin found a robin on his doorstep in Tralee.

DAfterwards, Kevin fed the robin every day for three weeks.

EJust then, his sister came home with a shoebox and a warm towel.

Put the five lines in order. Which line sets the scene? Which line is the turning point? Which words at the start of each line helped you?

Show me

C → B → E → D → A. C sets the scene: who, where and when (“a frosty January morning”, “Tralee”). B is the trouble. E is the turning point: “Just then…”. D is what happens after it: “Afterwards…”. A is how it ends: “At last…”.
The time words and the first few words of each line do most of the work. Writers use them to move a plot along, and you can use them to find your way around a novel.

The five stages of a plot

🏠 ExpositionMeet the characters, the place and the time.
📈 Rising actionTrouble starts and gets worse.
💥 ClimaxThe turning point: “Suddenly…”, “Just then…”.
📉 Falling actionWhat happens next: “After that…”, “Slowly…”.
🏁 ResolutionHow it ends: “In the end…”, “From that day on…”.

In a novel, each stage can last for chapters, and there are smaller ups and downs inside each one. The climax is the moment the whole book has been building towards.

Setting, mood, theme and change

📍 SettingWhere and when. Writers show it with details, not labels.
🌦️ MoodThe feeling the setting creates: 😨 tense, 😌 peaceful, 🎉 joyful, 😔 gloomy. Look at sounds, weather and light.
💡 ThemeThe big idea about life. The topic is “a dog”; the theme is “friendship can be found anywhere”.
🔄 ChangeCompare a character at the start and at the end. What happened in between to change them?

Common themes: friendship, courage, loss, belonging, growing up, fairness, forgiveness. Most novels have more than one.

Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Your novel comes up in the studied texts part of the paper. You may be asked about a key moment, the setting, a theme, how a character changes, or what you liked about the novel. You answer from memory, so learn a few short quotes for each key moment and theme.

How marks are won and lost

  • Name the moment: say what happens and where it comes in the plot (“the climax, when…”).
  • Back up every point with a short quote or a close reference to the text.
  • Explain why it matters: what it shows about the character or the theme.
  • Don't just retell the story. A summary of the plot with no point earns very little.
  • Use the name of your novel and the author in your first sentence.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). The extract is from an imaginary novel, so you can practise without knowing it. "Where the marks would go" is our guide, not an official marking scheme.

From the novel The Keeper's Daughter
Grey rain dripped from the bare trees as Nell climbed the path to the empty lighthouse. Since her father's boat had gone down in the spring, nobody in Rossmore had come up here. The door creaked open. Inside, everything was just as he had left it: his coat on the hook, his cup on the table, the great lamp cold. Nell had promised herself she would never come back. But tonight the fishing boats were still out, the fog was rolling in, and someone had to light the lamp. Her hands were shaking as she struck the first match.
  1. Describe the setting and the mood it creates. Quote two details.
    Show a model answer

    The extract is set at an empty lighthouse above Rossmore on a wet, foggy night. The mood is gloomy and lonely: “grey rain dripped from the bare trees” and the lighthouse is “empty”, with the lamp “cold”. The details make us feel how sad and abandoned the place has become since Nell's father died.
    Where the marks would go: place and time, the mood named in one word, two short quotes, and a sentence on what they make the reader feel.

  2. Which theme is shown in this extract? Use the text to support your answer.
    Show a model answer

    The extract shows the theme of courage. Nell “had promised herself she would never come back”, and her “hands were shaking”, so she is clearly afraid. But she goes in anyway because “someone had to light the lamp” for the fishing boats. Courage here means doing the right thing even when you are frightened. The theme of loss is there too, in her father's coat and cup left “just as he had left it”.
    Where the marks would go: the theme named, two references that show it, and an explanation of what the extract says about it.

  3. Where in the plot do you think this moment comes? Explain your answer.
    Show a model answer

    I think it comes near the climax. The exposition has already happened, because we know about her father's boat going down “in the spring”. Now the tension is at its highest: the boats are out, the fog is coming in, and Nell has to face the place she has avoided. Striking the first match feels like a turning point, the moment the whole story has been building towards.
    Where the marks would go: a stage named, a reason from the text, and an idea of what has come before and what might come next.

  4. Your novel: choose a key moment where a character changes. Describe the moment and explain how the character is different afterwards.
    Show a model answer

    A plan that works for any novel: (1) name the novel, the author, the character and the moment in one sentence; (2) describe the character before it, with a short quote; (3) describe the moment itself, saying where it comes in the plot; (4) describe the character after it, with a quote; (5) say what caused the change and what it shows about a theme.
    Where the marks would go: a clear moment (not the whole plot), before and after both described, a quote or close reference for each, and a point about why the change matters.
