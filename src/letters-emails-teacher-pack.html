Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

Letters and emails

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): W4, W12, R13

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can lay out a formal letter and email correctly, match the sign-off to the greeting (faithfully / sincerely), tell formal from informal language, and rewrite casual sentences in a formal register for a reader they don't know.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Letter Jigsaw cards (cut-up) · p.3 Register Swap A/B (speaking) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Letter + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 2nd year. Time: about 60 minutes. All letters are original; names and businesses are made up.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Write “Hey, gimme my money back asap!” on the board. Who could you send this to? Who couldn't you? Why not? Elicit "register": how formal you are depends on your reader.
8 minGuided discovery. Show a note to Gran and a letter to a manager from the same writer (the game's "Spot the pattern"). Students list the differences: their address, Dear Sir/Madam, the reason in line 1, Yours faithfully, full name. Elicit the faithfully / sincerely rule.
12 minLetter Jigsaw (p.2, groups of 3, one set of 24 cards). Sort the cards into three letters, then lay each letter out on the desk as it would look on the page (addresses and date in the right places). First group to check against the key wins.
12 minRegister Swap (p.3, pairs). A reads a casual line; B finds and reads the formal version; then swap. Together they circle the words that made each line casual.
18 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Letter in pairs, then the exam-style questions on "Library Hours" alone.
5 minExit ticket. Write the greeting and sign-off for a letter to (1) the Principal, Mr Hayes, (2) The Manager of a cinema, (3) your cousin. Homework: play Format Fixer at leim.ie/format-fixer.html.
Errors to look for: Dear Sir/Madam + Yours sincerely · capital F or S in faithfully / sincerely · the writer's name in their address · "Hi" or "Hey" in a formal letter · short forms (I'm, can't) and "!!" in a formal letter · no reason for writing in the first line · an email with no subject line. Support: the poster (p.4) on the desk. Fast finishers: write a reply from the manager to Letter 1.
Groups of 3 · cut up the 24 cards

Dear Sir/Madam,
I am writing to ask if the library could open later on weekday evenings.
At the moment it closes at five o'clock, which is before most students get home from sports training. A quiet place to study until seven would help many of us, especially in exam years.
I would be grateful if you could consider this request.
Yours faithfully,
Ruth Barry
  1. Who is Ruth writing to, and why? Use the opening line.
  2. Is the letter formal or informal? Give two features that show it.
  3. Why does Ruth sign off “Yours faithfully” and not “Yours sincerely”?
  4. Give one reason Ruth uses to persuade the library.
  5. Write a short informal email to a friend asking them to sign Ruth's petition. Then name two differences from Ruth's letter.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. The librarian at Clonakilty Library, to ask if the library could open later on weekday evenings.
  2. Formal: Dear Sir/Madam / Yours faithfully; full words ("I am writing"); polite request ("I would be grateful"); both addresses and the date; no slang.
  3. She did not use a name in the greeting (Dear Sir/Madam), so the sign-off is Yours faithfully.
  4. It closes at five, before students get home from training; a quiet place to study would help, especially in exam years.
  5. Credit a friendly greeting and sign-off, short forms or chatty words, a clear request. Differences: no addresses or date; first names; informal words.

Marking tip

Reward the right register for the reader first, then layout and sign-off, then a clear purpose in the first line and one point per paragraph. Our guide, not an official marking scheme.
