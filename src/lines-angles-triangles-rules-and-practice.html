Lines, angles & triangles

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): GT.3b, GT.3c. Play the game: leim.ie/angle-chase.html

1. Angle words

Acute < 90° · right angle = 90° (little square) · obtuse between 90° and 180° · straight angle = 180° · reflex between 180° and 360°.

∠ABC is the angle at B: the middle letter is the vertex.

Parallel lines never meet (arrows) · perpendicular lines meet at 90° · a transversal cuts across other lines · a line segment has two end points; a ray goes on forever one way.

2. Triangles

By sides: scalene (all different) · isosceles (two equal, marked with dashes) · equilateral (all equal, every angle 60°).

By angles (look at the biggest): acute-angled (all < 90°) · right-angled (one = 90°) · obtuse-angled (one > 90°).

3. The angle facts (learn the names: they are your reasons)

Angles on a straight line add to 180°
a + b = 180°
Angles at a point add to 360°
a + b + c = 360°
Vertically opposite angles are equal (Theorem 1)
a = b
Angles in a triangle add to 180° (Theorem 4)
a + b + c = 180°
Isosceles triangle: base angles are equal (Theorem 2)
a = b
Exterior angle = sum of the two interior opposite angles (Theorem 6)
e = a + b
Alternate angles are equal (Theorem 3)
a = b
Corresponding angles are equal (Theorem 5)
a = b
Co-interior angles add to 180°
a + b = 180°

4. How to chase an angle

Mark what you know. Find a fact that joins a known angle to an unknown one. Write each step on its own line with the reason: y = 180° − 72° = 108° (straight line). Keep going until you reach the angle you want. Check that each triangle adds to 180°.

Exam tip: the reason earns marks. Write alternate and corresponding, never "Z" or "F". Exam diagrams usually aren't drawn to scale, so don't measure: use the facts.
