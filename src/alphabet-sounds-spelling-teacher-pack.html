Junior Cycle Spanish · Teacher pack

Alphabet, sounds & spelling

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes: 1.9, 2.1, 2.4 (2017 spec, exams 2027–2028) · 2.1, 2.5 (2025 spec, 1st years from Sept 2026)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can say the Spanish letter names, spell and understand names, towns and emails letter by letter (¿Cómo se escribe? Se escribe jota, o…), link the tricky sounds to their spellings (j / ge, gi · z / ce, ci · h · ll / y · ñ · rr · b / v) and use written accents, ¿ ¡ and lower-case days, months and languages correctly.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Spell It Out (A/B info gap) · p.3–4 Spelling Dominoes (cut-up) · p.5 Wall poster: El alfabeto · p.6 Worksheet + answers. Level: 1st year, or 3rd-year exam revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All texts are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: Sound sort. Board: gato · gente · jamón · guitarra · girasol · gol. Students say them in pairs, then sort into two columns by the sound of the g. Ask: what decides the sound? (the next letter).
8 minGuided discovery. Repeat with casa · cine · queso · zumo · cena · cosa. Pairs work out the rule for c / qu / z, then check with the poster (p.5). Drill the alphabet in chunks of seven; the class shouts the next letter name. Focus on ge / jota, e / i, be / uve, hache.
15 minSpell It Out (p.2). Pairs, A and B cards. A is the summer-camp receptionist and fills in B's details as B spells them; then swap. Insist on letter names only, no English. Check by asking a few students to read back their partner's surname.
10 minSpelling Dominoes (p.3–4), groups of 3–4. Each domino has a word on the left and the spelling of another word on the right. Read the spelling aloud; whoever has the word puts it down. Answer chain below.
12 minWorksheet (p.6): read Pablo's email, A alone, B–C in pairs. Feedback: why does Pablo say "sapato"?
5 minExit ticket. Spell your name, a town in Spain and one word with an accent, letter by letter, to the teacher at the door. Homework: play Sound Match at leim.ie/sound-match.html (try the Listening mode) and copy five lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Spell It Out answers: A: Begoña Jiménez Ruiz · Jerez de la Frontera · calle Higuera 7 · bego.jr@ejemplo.es · murciélago (it has all five vowels). B: Javier Gutiérrez Vallejo · Zaragoza · calle Hoyos 12 · javi.gv@ejemplo.es · pingüino (it has a ü).
Errors to listen for: English letter names, especially "jay" for J and "gee" for G (→ jota, ge) · e and i swapped · ache (→ hache) · be for V (→ uve) · forgetting con tilde · pronouncing the h in hola · one r in perro. Language awareness: Irish learners already know the loch sound (Irish ch, as in loch, deoch): it's the Spanish j. Irish also marks long vowels with a síneadh fada (Seán, Ciarán), but the Spanish tilde marks stress, not length. Culture: the Spanish alphabet has 27 letters (ñ); since 2010 the Real Academia Española doesn't count ch and ll as letters. In Latin America and the Canaries, z and ce / ci sound like s (seseo). Support: leave the poster up. Fast finishers: find five Spanish words that sound exactly the same but are spelled differently (e.g. vaca / baca, hola / ola).

Worksheet

Pablo's arrival

↗ Léim
¡Hola, familia O'Sullivan! Me llamo Pablo Gutiérrez Vela y soy vuestro estudiante de intercambio. Llego a Cork el sábado, 14 de marzo, a las diez y veinte de la noche. Mi vuelo sale de Gran Canaria y hago escala en Madrid. Os escribo mi apellido letra por letra, porque en los aeropuertos siempre hay problemas: se escribe ge, u, te, i, e con tilde, erre, erre, e, zeta. ¡Muchos irlandeses escriben "Gutierres"! Tengo quince años y vivo en Las Palmas, en las islas Canarias. Hablo español e inglés, pero mi pronunciación de la "th" inglesa es fatal. En Canarias no decimos la zeta como en Madrid: decimos "sapato" en vez de "zapato" y "sine" en vez de "cine". ¡Es el acento canario! Me encantan el fútbol y la música, pero no como pescado. Mi correo es pablo.gv@ejemplo.es y mi madre se llama Rocío: erre, o, ce, i con tilde, o. Una pregunta: ¿cómo se pronuncia "O'Sullivan"? Hasta pronto, Pablo

A. Answer in English

  1. When exactly does Pablo arrive in Cork?
  2. Where does his flight leave from, and where does he change?
  3. Why does he spell his surname?
  4. How do many Irish people write it?
  1. How old is he, and where does he live?
  2. What is "fatal" about his English?
  3. How do people in the Canaries say zapato and cine?
  4. What does he love, what doesn't he eat, and what is his mum's name?

B. Find the Spanish in the email

  1. letter by letter
  2. I change planes in Madrid
  1. instead of
  2. How do you pronounce…?

C. Write the word, then add the accent if it needs one

  1. ce, a, ene, ce, i, o, ene
  2. hache, e, ele, a, de, o
  1. eme, u, ese, i, ce, a
  2. jota, o, uve, e, ene

D. Now you

Reply to Pablo as one of the O'Sullivans (50–60 words): spell your surname for him letter by letter, tell him how to say O'Sullivan, say who will meet him at the airport and ask him a question.

Answers. A: 1 Saturday 14 March at 10.20 pm. 2 Gran Canaria; Madrid. 3 There are always problems at airports. 4 "Gutierres". 5 Fifteen; Las Palmas, in the Canary Islands. 6 His pronunciation of the English "th". 7 "sapato" and "sine" (an s sound). 8 Football and music; fish; Rocío. · B: 9 letra por letra 10 hago escala en Madrid 11 en vez de 12 ¿cómo se pronuncia…? · C: 13 canción (ends in n, stress on the last syllable → accent) 14 helado 15 música (third-last syllable → accent) 16 joven (ends in n, stress on the second-last → no accent).
Watch out: vuestro = your (to a group, Spain) · hacer escala = to stop over · en vez de = instead of · hasta pronto = see you soon.
