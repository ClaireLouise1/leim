Punctuation 1: capitals, full stops, commas and apostrophes

Punctuation tells the reader where to pause, stop and look. Small marks, big difference.

Spot the pattern

How many dogs in each one?

the dog's bone
the dogs' bone
the children's toys
Show me

dog's = one dog. dogs' = more than one dog.
The trick: write the owner first (dog, dogs, children), then add the apostrophe. If the owner word already ends in s, just add '. If not, add 's. So: children → children's.

Capitals

✅ Capitalfirst word · I · names · places · days · months · languages · festivals
On Monday, Aoife and I speak French in Cork at Easter.
❌ No capitalseasons · most school subjects · mum/dad after my
my mum loves summer and maths

Full stops and end marks

  • . ends a statement. ? ends a question. ! shows strong feeling: What a goal!
  • Careful: She asked if I was hungry. reports a question, so it's a full stop.
  • One idea finished = one full stop. Don't glue two sentences with a comma.

Commas

Listseggs, milk and bread
no comma before the last “and”
Opening wordHowever, the bus was late.
NamesLiam, are you coming?
Thanks, Dad.
Yes / No / WellYes, I'd love to.
Extra informationMr Byrne, our maths teacher, was off.
take it out: the sentence still works

Apostrophes

Missing lettersdo not → don't · it is → it's · we are → we're
Belongingone girl → girl's · two girls → girls' · men → men's
Neverfor plurals: apple's for sale → apples for sale
in its, your, their, whose (belonging words)

It's or its? Say “it is” or “it has”. If that works → it's. If not → its. (The cat licked its paw.)

Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Nobody asks you to name a comma, but punctuation counts in every piece you write: accuracy is part of how writing is judged, and careless slips (no capitals, its/it's, missing full stops) make an answer harder to read and cost marks.

How marks are won and lost

  • Full stops between sentences: the most common slip is joining two sentences with a comma.
  • Capitals for names, places and I, every time.
  • Apostrophes: its/it's, your/you're, their/they're are noticed straight away.
  • Leave 3 minutes at the end to read your answer back and fix punctuation.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). "Where the marks would go" is our guide, not an official marking scheme.

  1. Rewrite this with correct capitals, full stops, commas and apostrophes: “on saturday aoife and i went to kinsale we bought chips ice cream and a kite its always windy there aoifes mum collected us at six”
    Show a model answer

    On Saturday Aoife and I went to Kinsale. We bought chips, ice cream and a kite. It's always windy there. Aoife's mum collected us at six.
    Where the marks would go: capitals (On, Saturday, Aoife, I, Kinsale, We, It's, Aoife's), three full stops between the sentences, one list comma, apostrophes in it's and Aoife's.

  2. Add the commas: “Before the race Niamh our best runner ate a banana drank some water and stretched.”
    Show a model answer

    Before the race, Niamh, our best runner, ate a banana, drank some water and stretched.
    Where the marks would go: a comma after the opening phrase, two commas around the extra information (our best runner), one comma in the list of actions.

  3. Add the apostrophes: “The girls coats were in the teachers office.” (a whole class of girls, one teacher)
    Show the answer

    The girls' coats were in the teacher's office. Many girls → girls'. One teacher → teacher's.

  4. Correct the two mistakes: “Its clear the school loves it's new pitch.”
    Show the answer

    It's clear the school loves its new pitch. “It is clear” works, so it's. “It is new pitch” doesn't, so its.
