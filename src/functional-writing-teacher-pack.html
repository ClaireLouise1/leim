Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

Functional writing

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): W4, W3, R13

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can tell a report, review, article, blog and speech apart by their features, lay out a report (title, terms of reference, findings, recommendations, sign-off), name the parts of a review, write a short headline that names the main fact, and match their tone to the reader.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Format Sort cards (cut-up) · p.3 Headline & Report Swap A/B (speaking) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Answer + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 2nd year. Time: about 60 minutes. All stories and titles are made up.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Read the skate-park news four ways (free sheet, box 3). Which is the report, article, blog and speech? What gave each one away? List the clues on the board.
8 minGuided discovery. Label the St Brigid's report (free sheet, box 4): title, introduction, findings, recommendations, sign-off. Then build a feature table for the five text types together.
12 minFormat Sort (p.2, groups of 3, one set of 20 cards). Sort the lines into report, review, article, blog and speech, and underline the word or phrase that gave each one away.
12 minHeadline & Report Swap (p.3, pairs). A reads four news stories; B picks each headline from a box with two decoys. Then A reads five jumbled report lines; B names each part and puts them in order. Swap.
18 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Answer in pairs, then the exam-style questions on the Kilmore Youth Café alone.
5 minExit ticket. Write one headline and one report recommendation about something in your school. Homework: play Format Sort at leim.ie/format-sort.html.
Errors to look for: writing a story when the task asks for a report or article · a chatty tone in a report · headlines that are full sentences · reviews with no verdict or with spoilers · articles full of the writer's opinions and no facts or quotes · forgetting the sign-off and date. Support: the poster (p.4) on the desk. Fast finishers: rewrite one Format Sort card as a different text type.

B. Exam-style questions: Kilmore Youth Café

THE FACTS
The old library in Kilmore has reopened as a youth café for 12 to 18-year-olds.
It is open Tuesday to Saturday, 4 p.m. to 9 p.m., with board games, a music room and free Wi-Fi.
Of the 200 teenagers surveyed by the council, 150 said they had nowhere to go in the evenings.
Youth worker Cathal Byrne: "This is their space, and they designed it."
  1. Write a headline for a local newspaper article about the café. Explain why it works.
  2. Write the first paragraph of the article. Include who, what, where, when and a quote.
  3. The council asks for a report. Write the terms of reference and one finding.
  4. Write two recommendations for the report.
  5. You visit the café. Write a short review for your school magazine, with a verdict.

A. Fix the Answer

Report: Hi everyone, I'm going to tell you all about the canteen!Wrong tone and no format. Start with a title and terms of reference: Report on the School Canteen. This report was requested by the student council to ...
Headline: There was a really big storm in our town last night and lots of trees fell downFar too long, and a full sentence. Keep the main fact: Storm Brings Down Trees in Kilkee
Review: The film is good. I liked it.No details, no reasons, no verdict: Storm Island (12A, 98 minutes) is a gripping thriller. The acting is superb ... Four stars out of five.
Review: In the end, it turns out the teacher was the thief all along!A spoiler. Hint at the twist without giving it away: The ending will take you completely by surprise.
Article: I think the new pool is brilliant and everyone should go.A news article reports facts and quotes people: The new pool opened on Monday. "We had 300 swimmers on the first day," said manager Ann Ryan.
Report: The yard is a disgrace and someone should do something!!A report is calm and specific: Findings: the bins are full by 11 a.m. Recommendation: I recommend that four more bins are placed in the yard.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. E.g. Old Library Reborn as Youth Café: short, present tense, names the main fact and the place.
  2. E.g. Teenagers in Kilmore finally have somewhere to go in the evenings: the old library has reopened as a youth café for 12 to 18-year-olds, open Tuesday to Saturday from 4 p.m. "This is their space, and they designed it," said youth worker Cathal Byrne.
  3. This report was requested by the council to find out whether young people use the new youth café. Finding: of the 200 teenagers surveyed, 150 said they had nowhere to go in the evenings.
  4. E.g. I recommend that the café opens on Sundays. It is recommended that a homework room is added.
  5. Details (place, opening hours), praise and criticism with reasons, a verdict or star rating, a lively tone for students.

Marking tip

Reward answers that show the format from the first line, use the features the task asks for and match the tone to the reader. Our guide, not an official marking scheme.
