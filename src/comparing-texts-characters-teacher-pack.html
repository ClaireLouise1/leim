Comparing Texts

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R6, R7, R9, W8

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can find similarities and differences between two texts (narrator, setting, main character, ending, theme), use similarity and contrast linking words correctly, and write point-by-point comparison sentences with evidence from both texts.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Venn Sort (4 text cards + 20 statement cards) · p.3 Compare Swap A/B (pair listening task) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Comparison + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 3rd year (works from 2nd year), Higher and Ordinary. Time: about 60 minutes. All texts are original and the skill works with any two texts.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Show the weak and strong answers from the poster (p.4): two summaries vs point-by-point. Ask: which one is comparing? Underline the linking words.
12 minVenn Sort (p.2, groups of 3–4). Each group gets one set (X or Y): 2 text cards + 10 statement cards. Draw a large Venn diagram (A, Both, B, with “Neither” outside) and place every card. Groups then swap sets.
15 minCompare Swap (p.3, pairs). Each reads a short text aloud twice; both fill a grid for the two texts, then write a “Both” sentence and a “whereas” sentence together.
5 minLinking-word race. Read out pairs of facts; students hold up S (similarity word) or C (contrast word).
18 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Comparison in pairs, then the exam-style questions on “The Cup” and “The Empty Stand” alone.
5 minExit ticket. One comparison sentence on two studied texts (e.g. two poems). Homework: play Compare Cards at leim.ie/compare-cards.html.
Errors to look for: two separate summaries · evidence from one text only · a contrast word between two matching facts (or a similarity word between two different ones) · comparing different things (setting in one, character in the other) · retelling · facts that aren't true of the text. Support: give students the grid from p.3 for any two texts. Fast finishers: add a theme comparison (friendship, loss, courage) with a quote from each text.

A. Fix the Comparison

Each line comes from a weak answer on the texts in the game. Say what's wrong and write a better version.

B. Exam-style questions: two original texts

Text A: The CupThe final whistle blew at Croke Park and I sank to my knees. Thirty years my town had waited for this cup. Around me, men who had cried at the last final were hugging strangers. I held the cup above my head and laughed until I couldn't breathe.
Text B: The Empty StandLong after the match, Tom sat alone in the empty stand. The floodlights clicked off one by one. His team had lost the final by a single point, the same as last year. He folded his flag carefully and walked out into the dark.
  1. Name one way the two texts are similar. Use a short quote from each.
  2. Name two ways the texts are different.
  3. Join these into one sentence with a contrast word: Text A ends with laughter. Text B ends in the dark.
  4. Which text made you feel more? Refer to both texts.
  5. Plan a comparison of two texts you studied on one theme: a similarity, two differences, evidence from each.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. Both are about the end of a final: A “The final whistle blew”, B “Long after the match”.
  2. Any two: first person (“I sank to my knees”) vs third person (Tom); a win vs a loss (“lost the final by a single point”); crowded and joyful (“hugging strangers”) vs alone (“sat alone”); daylight vs the dark.
  3. Text A ends with laughter, whereas Text B ends in the dark. (Or: …laughter. However, Text B …)
  4. Any view with a quote from each text and a reason that compares them.
  5. Reward a similarity and two differences, evidence from both texts, and clear linking words.

Marking tip

Comparing answers are rewarded for points that link both texts, supported by reference to each. Our guide, not an official marking scheme.
