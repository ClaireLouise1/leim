Units, time & timetables

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): GT.1 (units and time come back in timetables, speed, area, volume and money questions; speed, distance and time are taught later)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can convert between metric units of length, mass and capacity, compare and add amounts in mixed units, switch between the 12-hour and 24-hour clock, change between minutes and decimal hours, find time differences by counting on, and read timetables to plan a journey.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Unit Line-Up cards + Timetable Swap A/B cards · p.3 Time Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions and timetables are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: True or false? Board "1.5 hours = 1 hour 50 minutes" and "1.5 km = 1500 m". Hands up for each. Ask why they're different. Don't settle it yet.
10 minUnit Line-Up (p.2 cards; one each, 16 lengths in mm, cm and m). Students line up from shortest to longest without talking for the first minute, then they may move each other and must explain. Check against the key. Feedback: how did you compare 112 cm and 1180 mm? Elicit "same unit first" and "small unit → bigger number".
8 minGuided discovery: hours aren't decimals. Board 0.5 h, 0.25 h, 0.1 h, 1.5 h, 2.75 h. Pairs turn them into minutes (a clock face helps). What's the rule? (× 60.) Back to the lead-in. Then 08:47 → 11:15 by counting on, and why 1115 − 847 doesn't work.
12 minTimetable Swap (p.2, pairs: A and B each have half the times of the same bus timetable; don't show your card). Ask each other for the missing times ("When does Bus 2 get to Blackpool?"), fill the table, then answer the three questions on your card and check your partner's answers.
10 minTime Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, and build the chain to FINISH. Answer chain below.
12 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name the rule, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone. Feedback on question 4: why do you need 7 cans and not 6?
3 minExit ticket. 1.25 h in hours and minutes, and 6:40 pm in 24-hour clock (1 h 15 min; 18:40). Homework: play Clock Dash at leim.ie/clock-dash.html (Bus timetables round) and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Unit Line-Up key (shortest → longest): 60 cm, 0.65 m, 0.7 m, 0.8 m, 90 cm, 950 mm, 1 m, 1025 mm, 105 cm, 112 cm, 1180 mm, 1.2 m, 125 cm, 1.35 m, 1.45 m, 1500 mm.
Errors to look for: 1.5 h = 1 h 50 min · subtracting times like ordinary numbers (2 h 68 min) · 3:20 pm = 13:20 · 19:05 = 9:05 pm · 12:30 am = 12:30 · × 100 instead of × 1000 (0.35 kg = 35 g) · dividing when going to a smaller unit · adding amounts in different units · answers with no unit. Support: a clock face and a "km → m → cm → mm" unit ladder on the desk. Fast finishers: how many seconds are in a week? Which is longer: 0.3 of a day or 7 hours?

Units & time

1 km = 1000 mlength
1 kg = 1000 gmass
1 l = 1000 mlcapacity

Big → small: × (3.4 km = 3400 m) · small → big: ÷ (250 g = 0.25 kg) · 1 m = 100 cm · 1 cm = 10 mm · 1 t = 1000 kg

24-hour clockpm: add 12 → 2:35 pm = 14:35
12:40 am = 00:40
19:05 = 7:05 pm
Hours ≠ decimals60 min in an hour
0.5 h = 30 min · 0.1 h = 6 min
45 min = 45 ÷ 60 = 0.75 h
Count on08:47 → 09:00 = 13 min
09:00 → 11:00 = 2 h
11:00 → 11:15 = 15 min

Row = one stop

A. Error Hunt

3:20 pm in 24-hour clock = 13:20Added 10, not 12: 15:20
08:45 to 10:20 = 1 h 75 minTook away like ordinary numbers. Count on: 15 min + 1 h 20 min = 1 h 35 min
1 h 30 min as a decimal = 1.3 hMinutes ÷ 60: 30 ÷ 60 = 0.5, so 1.5 h
2.4 h in hours and minutes = 2 h 40 min0.4 × 60 = 24: 2 h 24 min
0.35 kg in grams = 35 g1 kg = 1000 g, so × 1000: 350 g
1.2 l + 300 ml = 301.2 mlSame units first: 1200 ml + 300 ml = 1500 ml (1.5 l)

B. Exam-style questions

  1. 16:48 → 17:00 = 12 min; 17:00 → 17:31 = 31 min: 43 min
  2. 12 × 400 = 4800 m = 4.8 km
  3. 1.75 h = 1 h 45 min; 20:10 + 1 h 45 min = 21:55
  4. 2 l = 2000 ml; 2000 ÷ 330 = 6.06…; 6 cans = 1980 ml isn't quite full, so 7 cans
  5. 80% to charge; 80 × 54 = 4320 s = 72 min = 1 h 12 min

Marking tip

Give credit for each step, as the exam does: a correct method with one slip (e.g. counting on correctly but adding wrongly) still earns most of the marks. Insist on units in every answer, and on 24-hour times written with four digits (09:05).
