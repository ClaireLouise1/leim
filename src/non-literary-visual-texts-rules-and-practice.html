Non-literary and visual texts

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R3, R9, R12, OL8. Play the game: leim.ie/fact-or-opinion.html

Non-literary texts are everyday texts: posters, leaflets, adverts, reviews, reports, articles, blogs, web pages, podcasts. Visual texts use pictures, charts, colour and layout. The exam asks what they say, how they say it, and whether you can trust them.

1. Fact or opinion?

FactOpinion
Can be checked or proved: numbers, dates, times, prices, places.Someone's view: judgement words like best, worst, tastiest, amazing, boring, too, better, should, I think.
The Greenway is 42 kilometres long.The Greenway is the most beautiful cycle in Ireland.

Ask: could I check this? Adverts and reviews mix the two on purpose: a fact builds trust, then an opinion slips in beside it.

2. Reading a chart

  • Read the title and the axis labels first.
  • Read across from the top of each bar. Halfway between two lines = the number in between.
  • “How many more?” = bigger − smaller.
  • Quote the numbers in your answer.

3. Parts of a poster or leaflet

Headlinebig, bold words at the top to grab attention
Pictureshows the event or product, sets the mood
Captionthe small line under a picture that explains it
When & whereday, date, time, place
Contact detailsphone, website, email: what to do next

4. Writing about a visual text

WeakThe poster has a picture and it is good.
Strong (name it + what it does)The photo of children laughing in the pool makes the club look fun and friendly, so young readers want to join.
Exam tip: name the feature, give the words or the number, then say what it does for the reader.

The headline “FRESH FROM THE FIELD!” is in capitals with an exclamation mark, so it sounds lively and makes you think of fresh food; the photo of a smiling stallholder with apples makes the market look friendly and local, and the caption names her, so it feels personal.
