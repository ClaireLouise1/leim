Pythagoras' theorem

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): GT.3b, GT.3e, U.6

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can name the hypotenuse, use Pythagoras' theorem (Theorem 14) to find the hypotenuse or a shorter side (with square roots, to a given accuracy), use the converse (Theorem 15) to decide whether a triangle is right-angled, and solve real-life problems: ladders, diagonals, isosceles triangles and journeys.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Ladder Lab mingle cards + Triangle Swap A/B · p.3 Pythagoras Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 2nd year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

8 minLead-in: square the sides. Pairs draw right-angled triangles on squared paper with shorter sides 3 & 4, 6 & 8 and 5 & 12 squares, measure the longest side, and fill a table with a², b² and c². "What do you notice?" Then one triangle that ISN'T right-angled: does it still work? Build c² = a² + b² and the word hypotenuse.
5 minThe rope trick (the converse). Tie 12 equally spaced knots in a loop of string. Three students pull it into a triangle with sides 3, 4 and 5 knots. What angle appears? Builders still use the 3-4-5 check: if (longest side)² = a² + b², the triangle is right-angled (Theorem 15).
10 minLadder Lab mingle (p.2, 24 cards = 12 question/answer pairs; one card each, calculators allowed). Question card? Work it out and find your answer. Answer card? Find your question. Pairs say which side is the hypotenuse and whether they added or subtracted.
12 minTriangle Swap (p.2, pairs, one A and one B card). Don't show your card. Each fact is on only one card: ask your partner for what you're missing, sketch each triangle, mark the hypotenuse and fill every blank on both cards.
12 minPythagoras Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4, calculators allowed. Start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, and build the chain to FINISH.
10 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone.
3 minExit ticket. Is a triangle with sides 7 cm, 8 cm and 11 cm right-angled? Show why. Homework: play Ladder Lab at leim.ie/ladder-lab.html and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. A wheelchair ramp rises 0.9 m over a horizontal distance of 4 m (diagram). Find the length of the ramp's sloping surface.
  2. Triangle KLM has |KL| = 16 cm, |LM| = 30 cm and |KM| = 34 cm. Show that it is right-angled and name the right angle.
  3. (a) A TV screen is 88 cm wide and 50 cm high. Find its diagonal, to 1 d.p. (b) Another screen has a 120 cm diagonal and is 104 cm wide. Find its height, to 1 d.p.
  4. A kite's string is 50 m long and tight. The kite is directly above a point 30 m along the ground from Aoife's hand. (a) How high is the kite? (b) She lets the string out to 65 m and the kite stays at the same height. How far along the ground is it now, to 1 d.p.?
  5. A farm gate is 3 m long and 1.2 m high. (a) Find the length of the diagonal brace, to 1 d.p. (b) Planks come in 3 m and 3.5 m lengths. Which length must Liam buy? Explain.

Answers

↗ Léim

Triangle Swap

Ladder: √(6.5² − 2.5²) = 6 m · Roof: √(5² − 4²) = 3 m · Path: √(12² + 9²) = 15 m · Kite: √(50² − 30²) = 40 m · TV: √(96² + 54²) = √12,132 = 110.1 cm · Shed: 3² + 4² = 25, so a square corner needs 5 m; the gap is 5.1 m, so no: the angle is more than 90°.

A. Error Hunt

Legs 6 cm and 8 cm. Find the hypotenuse. 6 + 8 = 14 cmSquare first: √(6² + 8²) = √100 = 10 cm
Hypotenuse 13 cm, one side 5 cm. Find the other side. √(13² + 5²) = 13.9 cmA shorter side: SUBTRACT. √(13² − 5²) = √144 = 12 cm
Legs 5 m and 7 m. Find the hypotenuse to 1 d.p. 5² + 7² = 74 mForgot the square root: √74 = 8.6 m
Is a triangle with sides 10, 24, 26 right-angled? 10² + 26² = 776 ≠ 24², so noTest the LONGEST side: 10² + 24² = 676 = 26², so yes, it is
Isosceles: base 12 cm, equal sides 10 cm. Find the height. 12² − 10² = 44, h = 6.6 cmUse HALF the base: √(10² − 6²) = √64 = 8 cm
A 4 m ladder, foot 1 m from the wall. Height to 1 d.p.? √(4² − 1²) = √15 = 3.8 m√15 = 3.872…, which rounds UP: 3.9 m

B. Exam-style questions

  1. The ramp is the hypotenuse: √(4² + 0.9²) = √16.81 = 4.1 m.
  2. 16² + 30² = 256 + 900 = 1156 and 34² = 1156. They are equal, so the triangle is right-angled (Theorem 15). The right angle is opposite the longest side [KM]: ∠KLM.
  3. (a) √(88² + 50²) = √10,244 = 101.2 cm. (b) √(120² − 104²) = √3584 = 59.9 cm.
  4. (a) √(50² − 30²) = √1600 = 40 m. (b) √(65² − 40²) = √2625 = 51.2 m.
  5. (a) √(3² + 1.2²) = √10.44 = 3.2 m. (b) 3.5 m: the brace is longer than 3 m.

Marking tip

Give credit for each step, as the exam does: the formula with the numbers substituted, the correct squares, the square root, then rounding and units. In "show that" questions both sides must be worked out and compared, with a conclusion that names the theorem or the right angle.
