Units of measure, time & timetables

Spot the pattern

Look at these. What do you notice?

  • 0.5 h = 30 min  ·  0.25 h = 15 min
  • 1.5 h = 1 h 30 min  ·  2.75 h = 2 h 45 min
  • 0.1 h = 6 min  ·  1.4 h = 1 h 24 min

Why isn't 1.5 h the same as 1 h 50 min? What would 3.2 h be?

Show me

An hour has 60 minutes, not 100, so the part after the point is a fraction of 60. 0.5 h = half of 60 = 30 min. To change the decimal part to minutes, × 60: 0.2 × 60 = 12, so 3.2 h = 3 h 12 min. Going the other way, minutes ÷ 60: 45 min = 45 ÷ 60 = 0.75 h.

Metric units

  • Length: 1 km = 1000 m · 1 m = 100 cm · 1 cm = 10 mm (so 1 m = 1000 mm)
  • Mass: 1 kg = 1000 g · 1 tonne (t) = 1000 kg
  • Capacity: 1 litre (l) = 1000 ml

Big unit → small unit: multiply (you need more of them): 3.4 km = 3.4 × 1000 = 3400 m. Small unit → big unit: divide: 250 g = 250 ÷ 1000 = 0.25 kg. Check: the number should get bigger when the unit gets smaller.

Same units first. To add, compare or divide amounts, change them to the same unit: 1.2 kg + 350 g = 1200 g + 350 g = 1550 g (= 1.55 kg). How many 250 ml glasses from a 1.5 l jug? 1500 ÷ 250 = 6.

The 24-hour clock

  • am times keep their hour, with a 0 in front if needed: 7:05 am = 07:05. Midnight is 00:00, so 12:20 am = 00:20.
  • pm times: add 12 to the hour: 2:35 pm = 14:35. But 12:40 pm (just after midday) stays 12:40.
  • Back to 12-hour: hours 13–23, take away 12 and write pm: 19:05 = 7:05 pm (not 9:05).

How long is it? Count on

08:47 to 11:15: count on to the next hour, then whole hours, then the rest. 08:47 → 09:00 is 13 min; 09:00 → 11:00 is 2 h; 11:00 → 11:15 is 15 min. Total 2 h 28 min.

The classic mistake: taking away like ordinary numbers: 1115 − 847 = 268 → "2 h 68 min". There's no such thing: there are only 60 minutes in an hour. Count on instead.

Reading a timetable

Each column is one bus; each row is a stop. Read down a column to follow one bus. A dash (—) means that bus doesn't stop there. To find the latest bus that gets you somewhere on time, look along the arrival row for the last time that's early enough, then go up the column to see when it leaves.

Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Units and time are the hidden part of lots of questions: timetables and journeys, speed, distance and time (later), area and volume (changing cm to m), recipes and money. Questions usually start with reading a table or a clock, then ask you to work out a time difference or plan something.

How marks are won and lost

  • Hours aren't decimals: 1 h 30 min is 1.5 h, not 1.3 h. Divide minutes by 60.
  • Count on for time differences; don't subtract times like ordinary numbers.
  • Same units first, and write the unit in your answer (450 g, not 450).
  • Read the question twice: "leaves by", "arrives by" and "the latest bus" are different things.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first.

  1. The 220 bus. Part of a bus timetable is shown.
    Ballincollig07:4008:0508:30
    Bishopstown07:52—08:43
    UCC08:0108:2008:53
    City Centre08:1208:2909:06
    (a) How long does the 07:40 bus take to get from Ballincollig to the City Centre? (b) Ciara has a class in UCC at 08:30. It's a 5-minute walk from the bus stop. What's the latest bus she can catch from Ballincollig? (c) Which bus is quickest from Ballincollig to the City Centre, and why?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 07:40 → 08:00 is 20 min, 08:00 → 08:12 is 12 min: 32 min.
    (b) She must be at the UCC stop by 08:25. The 08:05 bus gets there at 08:20 ✓; the 08:30 bus gets there at 08:53 ✗. So the 08:05.
    (c) 07:40: 32 min; 08:05: 24 min; 08:30: 36 min. The 08:05 is quickest: it doesn't stop at Bishopstown.

  2. Baking day. A recipe needs 1.2 kg of flour. Flour is sold in 500 g bags. (a) How many bags must you buy? (b) How many grams of flour are left over?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 1.2 kg = 1200 g. 1200 ÷ 500 = 2.4, so 2 bags isn't enough: 3 bags.
    (b) 3 × 500 = 1500 g; 1500 − 1200 = 300 g left over.

  3. Cinema trip. A film starts at 19:45 and lasts 2 h 25 min. (a) What time does it end? (b) The last bus home leaves at 22:20. How long is there to get to the bus stop? (c) The cinema website says the film is "2.4 hours long". Is that the same? Explain.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 19:45 + 2 h = 21:45; + 25 min = 22:10.
    (b) 22:10 → 22:20 = 10 min.
    (c) 0.4 h = 0.4 × 60 = 24 min, so 2.4 h = 2 h 24 min. Almost, but not quite (one minute shorter). 2 h 25 min is 2.41666… h.

  4. Sunday dinner. A chicken needs 45 minutes per kg plus an extra 20 minutes. It weighs 1.8 kg. Dinner is at 18:30. What's the latest time it can go in the oven?
    Show the worked answer

    45 × 1.8 = 81 min; + 20 = 101 min = 1 h 41 min. Count back from 18:30: 18:30 − 1 h = 17:30; − 41 min = 16:49.
