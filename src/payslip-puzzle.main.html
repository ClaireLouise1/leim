Money: income tax, net pay & compound interest

Spot the pattern

Two friends each put €1,000 away for 3 years at 10% a year. Aoife's account pays 10% of the first €1,000 every year. Cian's account pays 10% of whatever is in the account at the start of each year.

End of yearAoifeCian
1€1,100€1,100
2€1,200€1,210
3€1,300€1,331

Why does Cian pull ahead, and why does the gap get bigger every year? What single number could you multiply €1,000 by three times to get Cian's €1,331?

Show me

Cian earns interest on his interest too: in year 2 he gets 10% of €1,100 (€110), not 10% of €1,000. That's compound interest. Adding 10% is the same as multiplying by 1.1, so €1,000 × 1.1 × 1.1 × 1.1 = €1,000 × 1.1³ = €1,331.

Gross pay and net pay

  • Gross pay = what you earn before anything is taken off. Hourly pay: hours × rate (38 hours at €15.50 = €589).
  • Overtime: "time-and-a-half" = 1.5 × the normal rate; "double time" = 2 × the normal rate. Only the extra hours get the overtime rate.
  • Weekly, monthly, yearly: 52 weeks in a year, 12 months in a year. Never mix them up (a month is not 4 weeks).
  • Net pay (take-home pay) = gross pay − all the deductions (income tax, USC, PRSI, pension, union fees…).

Income tax

1. Gross taxGross income × the tax rate. In the exam the standard rate is usually 20%: 20% of €40,000 = €8,000.
2. Tax payableGross tax − tax credit. With a €3,750 tax credit: €8,000 − €3,750 = €4,250.
3. Net incomeGross income − tax payable (and any other deductions): €40,000 − €4,250 = €35,750.

A tax credit is taken off the tax, never off the income. Going backwards: tax payable + tax credit = gross tax, and gross tax ÷ 0.2 (or × 5) = gross income.

Two rates of tax Higher Level
Income up to the standard rate cut-off point is taxed at 20%; everything above it is taxed at the higher rate (40% in the exam). Earns €52,000, cut-off point €44,000: 20% of €44,000 = €8,800, plus 40% of €8,000 = €3,200. Gross tax €12,000, then take away the tax credits.

Other deductions: USC (Universal Social Charge), PRSI (social insurance) and pension. At Ordinary Level the exam gives you these as amounts. Higher Level they can be given as a % of gross pay: PRSI at 4% of €750 = €30.

Compound interest

  • Each year, the interest is worked out on the amount at the start of that year, then added on.
  • Quick way: r% a year means × (1 + r/100) each year. 4% → × 1.04; 2.5% → × 1.025. For 3 years at 4%: × 1.04³.
  • Different rate each year? Use each year's own multiplier: × 1.03 × 1.04 × 1.05.
  • Interest earned = final value − the amount you started with.
  • Loans work the same way. If a repayment is made at the end of a year, take it off after adding that year's interest, then carry on.
Working backwards Higher Level
Worth €4,410 after 2 years at 5%: P × 1.05² = €4,410, so P = €4,410 ÷ 1.1025 = €4,000. Finding the rate: €5,000 grew to €6,050 in 2 years → 6,050 ÷ 5,000 = 1.21 = (1 + r)², and √1.21 = 1.1, so r = 10%.
The classic mistakes: taking the tax credit off the income; adding the tax credit; using 1.5 for 5% (it's 1.05); working out interest on the first amount every year; giving the interest when the question asks for the value; and taking 5% off twice to go backwards (divide by 1.05² instead).
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Wages, tax and savings are a favourite real-life setting: a payslip or a person's yearly income with a tax credit, a family's savings or a loan over two or three years, often as one part of a longer money question. The early parts are usually straightforward; the later parts ask you to go backwards, compare two options, or explain someone's mistake.

How marks are won and lost

  • Write each step with a label: gross tax, tax payable, deductions, net pay. Each line can earn marks even if a later one goes wrong.
  • Compound interest year by year (or with the multiplier): show the value at the end of each year. A table is a great way to set it out.
  • Give money to the nearest cent unless the question says otherwise.
  • Read what's asked: "how much interest" is not "how much is it worth"; "net" is not "gross".
  • "Explain" parts need a sentence with numbers in it, not just "he's wrong".

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first.

  1. The payslip. Ciara is paid €16.40 an hour for a basic 39-hour week, and time-and-a-half for overtime. One week she works 45 hours. (a) Find her basic pay. (b) Find her overtime pay. (c) Her deductions that week are income tax €78.40, USC €21.15 and PRSI €31.49. Find her net pay.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 39 × €16.40 = €639.60.
    (b) Overtime rate = 1.5 × €16.40 = €24.60. 6 hours × €24.60 = €147.60.
    (c) Gross = €639.60 + €147.60 = €787.20. Deductions = €78.40 + €21.15 + €31.49 = €131.04. Net = €787.20 − €131.04 = €656.16.

  2. Income tax. Darragh earns €41,500 a year. His tax credit is €4,000 and the standard rate of income tax is 20%. (a) Find his gross tax. (b) Find the tax he pays. (c) He also pays USC of €1,240 and PRSI of €1,660. Find his net income. (d) Darragh says "I pay 20% of my income in income tax." Is he right? Explain.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 20% of €41,500 = €8,300.
    (b) €8,300 − €4,000 = €4,300.
    (c) €41,500 − €4,300 − €1,240 − €1,660 = €34,300.
    (d) No. The tax credit brings his tax down to €4,300, and €4,300 ÷ €41,500 × 100 ≈ 10.4% of his income, not 20%.

  3. The savings account. Róisín invests €5,000 for 3 years. The account pays compound interest of 3% in year 1, 4% in year 2 and 5% in year 3. (a) Find the value of her investment at the end of each year. (b) How much interest does she earn altogether? (c) Her brother says "3% + 4% + 5% = 12%, and 12% of €5,000 is €600, so that's the interest." Explain why his answer is too small.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) Year 1: €5,000 × 1.03 = €5,150. Year 2: €5,150 × 1.04 = €5,356. Year 3: €5,356 × 1.05 = €5,623.80.
    (b) €5,623.80 − €5,000 = €623.80.
    (c) He has worked out every year's interest on the first €5,000. With compound interest, years 2 and 3 earn interest on the interest already added (4% of €5,150 and 5% of €5,356), so the total is more than €600.

  4. Higher Level (a) Aoife earns €62,000 a year. Her standard rate cut-off point is €44,000, the standard rate is 20%, the higher rate is 40% and her tax credits are €4,000. Find the income tax she pays. (b) Aoife's savings grew from €12,500 to €13,520 in two years at the same rate of compound interest each year. Find the rate.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 20% of €44,000 = €8,800. €62,000 − €44,000 = €18,000 at 40% = €7,200. Gross tax = €16,000. Tax payable = €16,000 − €4,000 = €12,000.
    (b) €13,520 ÷ €12,500 = 1.0816 = (1 + r)². √1.0816 = 1.04, so the rate is 4% a year. (Not 8.16% ÷ 2 = 4.08%.)
