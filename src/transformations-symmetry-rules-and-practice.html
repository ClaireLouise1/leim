Transformations & symmetry

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): GT.6a, GT.6b. Play the game: leim.ie/mirror-move.html

1. The four transformations

The new shape is the image (S′, "S dash"). All four keep the same shape and size. In the picture:

Translation (slide), S → T: every point moves 6 left and 2 up. Not turned, not flipped.

Axial symmetry (mirror), S → A: in the x-axis. Same distance from the line on the other side; the shape is flipped.

Central symmetry (half turn), S → C: in (0, 0). Go to the centre, then the same again. Same as a rotation of 180°.

Rotation (turn), S → R: 90° anticlockwise about (0, 0). Trace it and turn the page a quarter turn.

2. The quick rules

In the x-axis(x, y) → (x, −y)
In the y-axis(x, y) → (−x, y)
In the origin(x, y) → (−x, −y)
Translationadd the move to x and y
90° anticlockwise about (0, 0)(x, y) → (−y, x)
90° clockwise about (0, 0)(x, y) → (y, −x)

3. Other lines and centres

A line m: count the squares straight across to the line (at right angles), then the same number on the other side. A point ON the line is its own image.

A centre C: P(1, 4), C(2, 1): 1 right and 3 down to C, then 1 right and 3 down again → (3, −2).

A translation from two points: (2, 1) → (5, −3) is 3 right and 4 down. Do the same to every point. Backwards (which point maps onto…?): do the opposite move.

4. Symmetry in shapes

An axis of symmetry folds a shape exactly onto itself. A centre of symmetry is a point where a half turn fits it back on itself.

Square4 axes · centre
Rectangle, rhombus2 axes · centre
Parallelogram0 axes · centre
Kite, isosceles trapezium1 axis · no centre
Isosceles / equilateral triangle1 / 3 axes · no centre
Regular polygon, n sidesn axes · centre if n is even

A rectangle's diagonals are not axes of symmetry (fold one and see).

Classic mistakes: changing the wrong sign · clockwise v anticlockwise · thinking a half turn flips the shape · translating backwards · counting to m along the grid instead of straight across · "4 axes" for a rectangle.
Exam tip: name a transformation fully ("axial symmetry in the y-axis", "a rotation of 90° clockwise about (0, 0)"), move the corners one at a time, label the image, and use a ruler.

E. Exam-style questions (show your work)

  1. Triangle T has corners (1, 1), (4, 1) and (2, 3). (a) Write the corners of its image under axial symmetry in the x-axis. (b) Write the corners of its image under the translation 5 units left and 1 unit up. (c) Find the image of (4, 1) under a rotation of 90° anticlockwise about (0, 0). (d) Which single transformation maps the image in (a) onto the image of T in the y-axis?
  2. The logo. A logo is a regular hexagon. (a) How many axes of symmetry does it have? (b) Does it have a centre of symmetry? (c) Name a quadrilateral with exactly 2 axes of symmetry and a centre of symmetry.
  3. Centre and slide. A is (−2, 3) and C is (1, 1). (a) Find A′, the image of A under central symmetry in C. (b) Describe the translation that maps A onto A′. (c) Find the image of (0, 0) under that translation.
  4. Spot the slip. Ciara says the image of (3, 1) under a rotation of 90° clockwise about (0, 0) is (−1, 3). Explain her mistake and give the right answer.
