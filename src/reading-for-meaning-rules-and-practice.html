Reading for meaning

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R1, R2, R3, OL4. Play the game: leim.ie/clue-hunt.html

1. Four ways to read

Skimfor the big idea: title, first line, last line. What's it about? Who's it for?
Scanfor one fact: run your eye down the text looking for one kind of word.
Inferread between the lines: the text shows a feeling without naming it.
Prove itback up every point with a short quote.

2. Question words: what to hunt for

When? What time?days, dates, times: at dawn, the 14th, May
How many? How much?numbers, prices: over 600, €95
Who? Where?names and places (capital letters!)
Why?because, so, as, since

3. Read between the lines

hands shaking, frozescared
stomach in knotsnervous
face went redembarrassed
sighing, nothing to dobored
grinning, jumped on herhappy
writing to complainangry

4. Point, quote, explain

Point: Rua is scared.
Quote: “her hands started shaking”
Explain: shaking hands show fear: every rattle of the roof frightens her.

Keep quotes short (3–6 words) and put them in quotation marks. Use your own words for the point.

5. Fact or opinion?

Fact: can be checked. The tickets cost €12.
Opinion: what someone thinks. The tickets are too expensive.
Opinion clue words: best, worst, boring, amazing, should, too, I think.

6. The best-title test

A good title covers the whole text. A wrong one usually fits just one line: it's a detail, not the main idea.

7. Traps

Wrong answers are often real words from the text, from the wrong line (the bus trip was on Saturday, but the new date is the 14th). Read the whole line around your answer.

Classic mistakes: a point with no quote · a quote with no explanation · copying out a whole paragraph · answering a different question · giving one reason when the question asks for two.
Exam tip: read the questions before the text. Then you know what you're hunting for, and you read with a purpose.

A. Three short texts

Information

1Skellig Michael is a rocky island off the coast of Kerry.

2Monks lived there more than a thousand years ago.

3Visitors climb over 600 stone steps to reach the top.

4Boats usually sail there only from May to September, when the sea is calmer.

  1. How many steps? Which line?
  2. When do boats sail? Which line?
  3. “The climb is hard work.” Which line proves it? Quote it.
  4. “The sea is rough in winter.” Which line proves it? Quote it.
  5. Best title? a How to build stone steps   b Visiting an old monks' island   c A storm off Kerry   d Boat trips around Ireland
Story

1Aoife jumped on the bus without looking up from her phone.

2Twenty minutes later, she saw fields where her school should have been.

3Her face went red, and she pressed the bell twice.

4The driver laughed and told her the right bus stopped across the road.

  1. How long until she noticed? Which line?
  2. Bell pressed how often? Which line?
  3. “Aoife wasn't paying attention.” Which line proves it? Quote it.
  4. “Aoife was far from school.” Which line proves it? Quote it.
  5. How does Aoife feel? Give the clue.
  6. Best title? a A new phone   b A day on a farm   c A friendly driver   d Aoife gets on the wrong bus
Advert

1Splash Camp runs every weekday in July at Youghal beach.

2Children aged 8 to 14 can try kayaking, surfing and snorkelling.

3Camp costs €95 a week, and wetsuits are included.

4Book before 1 June to get 10% off!

  1. Cost a week? Which line?
  2. Ages? Which line?
  3. “You don't need to bring a wetsuit.” Which line proves it? Quote it.
  4. “Booking early saves money.” Which line proves it? Quote it.
  5. Best title? a How to surf   b A water sports camp for children   c Buying a wetsuit   d A trip to the beach

A1. over 600 (line 3) · 2. May to September (line 4) · 3. line 3: “600 stone steps” · 4. line 4: “calmer” · 5. b (Visiting an old monks' island)
A6. Twenty minutes (line 2) · 7. twice (line 3) · 8. line 1: “without looking up” · 9. line 2: “fields” · 10. embarrassed: “face went red” (line 3) · 11. d (Aoife gets on the wrong bus)
A12. €95 (line 3) · 13. 8 to 14 (line 2) · 14. line 3: “wetsuits are included” · 15. line 4: “10% off” · 16. b (A water sports camp for children)
B17. F · 18. O · 19. F · 20. F · 21. F · 22. O · 23. O · 24. O
C25. e.g. Aoife is easily distracted: she got on the bus “without looking up from her phone”, so she didn't check where it was going. (Or: she's embarrassed: “her face went red”.)
26. Kind: he “laughed” but he “told her the right bus stopped across the road”, so he helped her.

Worked examples

Skellig: “The sea is rough in winter.”The text never says “rough” or “winter”. But line 4 says boats only sail “when the sea is calmer” (May to September), so the rest of the year it must be rougher. That's an inference.
Bus: best title?“A new phone” only fits line 1; “A friendly driver” only fits line 4. “Aoife gets on the wrong bus” covers the whole story: that's the main idea.
Camp: “€95” or “€10”?Both are in the text, but “10% off” is a discount, not the price. The cost is in line 3: “€95 a week”. That's a trap: always read the whole line.
Fact or opinion?“Hurling is played with a hurley and a sliotar” can be checked: fact. “Hurling is the most exciting sport” is what someone thinks: opinion.
