Junior Cycle Maths · Teacher pack

Area, perimeter & scale drawings

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): GT.2a, GT.2c (volume, surface area and nets come later, in topic 33)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can tell perimeter from area, find the area and perimeter of rectangles, L-shapes, triangles, parallelograms, circles, semicircles and shapes made from them (with the π button, 3.14, 22/7 or in terms of π), solve real-life floor problems (cost, packs, tiles, skirting), and read and draw scaled plans and maps.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Floor Planner mingle cards + Plan Swap A/B cards · p.3 Area Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 2nd year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: the perimeter trap. Board two rooms: 5 m × 5 m and 9 m × 1 m. "Both have a perimeter of 20 m. Do they need the same amount of flooring?" Students vote, then check (25 m² v 9 m²). Which word goes with skirting board, and which with flooring?
8 minGuided discovery. Pairs get an 8 cm × 4 cm rectangle, a triangle and a parallelogram with the same base and height on squared paper. Cut the triangle from corner to corner of the rectangle: what fraction is it? Cut a triangle off the parallelogram and slide it across: what shape appears? Build A = ½bh and A = bh, and ask why the sloping side never appears.
10 minFloor Planner mingle (p.2, 24 cards = 12 question/answer pairs; one card each). Question card? Work it out and find the person with your answer. Answer card? Find your question. Pairs check each other's working.
12 minPlan Swap (p.2, pairs, don't show your card). Each fact is on only ONE card. Ask your partner for what you're missing ("What is the scale?"), then both fill every blank. Feedback: floor 22 m², 9 packs, €495.00; patio paving €785.40, edging €102.10.
10 minArea Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, and build the chain to FINISH.
12 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name the rule, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone.
3 minExit ticket. A circle has a radius of 6 cm: area in terms of π? A 7 m wall on a 1 : 50 plan? (36π cm²; 14 cm.) Homework: play Floor Planner at leim.ie/floor-planner.html and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Mingle key (question card → answer card): 12 → 1 · 8 → 23 · 2 → 14 · 13 → 19 · 3 → 10 · 24 → 9 · 18 → 17 · 7 → 5 · 15 → 21 · 20 → 6 · 4 → 22 · 11 → 16.
Errors to look for: the sloping side as the height · forgetting ½ · the diameter in πr² · no straight edge on a semicircle · m for an area · rounding packs down · plan area × the scale. Support: a formula card with each shape drawn and its height marked. Fast finishers: a 20 m loop of rope: which rectangle encloses the most area? Can a circle beat it? (5 m × 5 m = 25 m²; circle ≈ 31.8 m²)

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. The diagram shows an L-shaped hall. (a) Find the two sides that are not labelled. (b) Find the perimeter. (c) Find the area. (d) Carpet costs €18 per m². Find the cost of carpeting the hall.
  2. A round table top has a diameter of 1.2 m. (a) Find its area in terms of π. (b) Find its circumference, to 2 decimal places.
  3. A wheel has a diameter of 64 cm. How many complete turns does it make in 1 km?
  4. A wall 2.4 m wide and 1.5 m high is covered with square tiles 15 cm by 15 cm. (a) How many tiles are needed? (b) Tiles come in boxes of 12. How many boxes are needed?
  5. A map has a scale of 1 : 50,000. (a) A lake is 3.6 cm long on the map. How long is it in real life, in km? (b) How long is a 4.5 km walk on the map?

A. Error Hunt

Triangle: base 10 cm, sloping side 6 cm, ⊥ height 5 cm. Area? ½ × 10 × 6 = 30 cm²Use the perpendicular height: ½ × 10 × 5 = 25 cm²
Circle with diameter 10 cm. Area (π button, 1 d.p.)? π × 10² = 314.2 cm²Use the radius, 5: π × 5² = 78.5 cm²
Semicircle with diameter 6 m. Perimeter (π = 3.14)? ½ × 3.14 × 6 = 9.42 mAdd the straight edge: 9.42 + 6 = 15.42 m
Rectangle 6 m by 4 m. Area? 6 + 4 + 6 + 4 = 20 m²That's the perimeter (20 m). Area = 6 × 4 = 24 m²
Floor 17 m². One pack covers 2 m². Packs? 17 ÷ 2 = 8.5, so 8 packs8 packs leave a gap: round UP to 9 packs
Plan 1 : 50: room 8 cm by 6 cm. Real area? 8 × 6 × 50 = 2,400 cm² = 0.24 m²Lengths first: 4 m × 3 m = 12 m²

B. Exam-style questions

  1. (a) 6 − 2.5 = 3.5 m and 8 − 3 = 5 m (b) 28 m (c) 8 × 6 − 3 × 2.5 = 40.5 m² (d) 40.5 × 18 = €729.00
  2. (a) π × 0.6² = 0.36π m² (b) π × 1.2 = 3.77 m
  3. 1 km = 100,000 cm. One turn = π × 64 = 201.06 cm. 100,000 ÷ 201.06 = 497.4 → 497 complete turns
  4. (a) 240 ÷ 15 = 16 and 150 ÷ 15 = 10: 160 tiles (b) 160 ÷ 12 = 13.3 → 14 boxes
  5. (a) 3.6 × 50,000 = 180,000 cm = 1.8 km (b) 450,000 cm ÷ 50,000 = 9 cm

Marking tip

Give credit for each step, as the exam does: the missing lengths, the formula, the substitution and the answer with the right units each earn marks. Accept answers in terms of π only when the question asks for them; otherwise check the rounding instruction. For "explain" parts insist on a sentence with numbers in it.
