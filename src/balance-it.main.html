Linear equations

Spot the pattern

An equation is like a balance: the two sides weigh the same. Each bag holds x counters. Here are some scales, and a move made to each one:

  • 3 bags + 2 counters = 14 counters. Take 2 counters off both sides → 3 bags = 12 counters.
  • 3 bags = 12 counters. Split both sides into 3 equal groups → 1 bag = 4 counters.
  • 5 bags + 1 counter = 2 bags + 10 counters. Take 2 bags off both sides → 3 bags + 1 = 10.
  • 3 bags + 2 counters = 14 counters. Take 2 counters off the left only → 3 bags = 14 counters?

Which move doesn't keep the scales level? What's the one rule every move has to follow?

Show me

The last one tips the scales: 12 ≠ 14. The rule is do the same thing to both sides. Then the two sides stay equal, and "=" still means "has the same value as". In algebra: 3x + 2 = 14 → 3x = 12 → x = 4. Check: 3(4) + 2 = 14 ✓.

Undo, in reverse order

To get x on its own, undo what was done to it, the last thing first. Use the opposite operation on both sides.

4x − 7 = 21
4x = 28  (add 7 to both sides)
x = 7  (divide both sides by 4)
Check: 4(7) − 7 = 21 ✓
  • + undoes −, × undoes ÷. x + 9 = 4 → x = −5.  −3x = 12 → x = −4.  x/5 = 6 → x = 30.
  • Answers can be negative. That's fine: just check them.

Letters on both sides

Move the x terms to one side and the numbers to the other (do the same to both sides each time).

5x + 3 = 2x + 18
3x + 3 = 18  (subtract 2x from both sides)
3x = 15  (subtract 3 from both sides)
x = 5  (divide by 3). Check: 28 = 28 ✓

Shortcut you'll see in class: a term that moves across the = changes its sign (+2x on the right becomes −2x on the left). It's the same as doing it to both sides.

Brackets: expand first

3(x − 4) = 2(x + 1)
3x − 12 = 2x + 2  (multiply out: every term)
x − 12 = 2  (subtract 2x)
x = 14  (add 12). Check: 3(10) = 30 and 2(15) = 30 ✓

Fractions: clear them first

Multiply every term on both sides by the lowest common multiple (LCM) of the bottom numbers.

x/2 + x/3 = 10  (LCM of 2 and 3 is 6)
3x + 2x = 60  (× 6: 6 × x/2 = 3x, 6 × x/3 = 2x, 6 × 10 = 60)
5x = 60, so x = 12. Check: 6 + 4 = 10 ✓
  • (x + 2)/5 = 3 → x + 2 = 15 → x = 13. Keep the top in a bracket when you multiply.
  • (x + 1)/3 = (x − 1)/2 → cross-multiply: 2(x + 1) = 3(x − 1) → 2x + 2 = 3x − 3 → x = 5.
  • The classic slip: multiplying the fractions but forgetting the number on its own (the 10 must become 60).

Forming equations from words

  1. Say what x stands for ("Let x = the price of one child's ticket").
  2. Write the other amounts using x (an adult ticket costs €6 more: x + 6).
  3. Find the two things that are equal and write the equation.
  4. Solve, then answer the question in words, with units.
"I think of a number, multiply it by 4 and add 7. The answer is 31."
4x + 7 = 31 → 4x = 24 → x = 6. Check in the words: 6 × 4 + 7 = 31 ✓
  • Three consecutive numbers: n, n + 1, n + 2.
  • Angles in a triangle add to 180°; a perimeter adds all the sides.
  • "5 more than" → + 5; "3 times as many" → 3x; "the same as" → =.

Always check

Put your answer back into the original equation. Both sides must give the same number. If they don't, look for a sign slip or a term you forgot to multiply.

Two equations at once (simultaneous equations), equations with x², and inequalities (<, >) come later in Junior Cycle Maths.

Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Solving a linear equation is one of the most common parts on the paper, and it hides inside bigger questions too: a perimeter or angle question, a word problem about prices or ages, the last step of a pattern or a graph question. Many questions say "write an equation" first: that line earns marks on its own.

How marks are won and lost

  • Show every step, one line each. Method marks are given for correct steps even if the final answer slips.
  • Do the same to both sides, and to every term (fractions: the whole number too).
  • Watch the signs when you expand a bracket or move a term across the =.
  • Word problems: say what x is, write the equation, solve, then answer in words with units.
  • Check by putting your answer back in. It takes 20 seconds and catches most slips.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first.

  1. Solve. (a) 5x − 7 = 2x + 11  (b) 4(x − 3) = 2(x + 5)  (c) (x + 5)/4 + (x − 1)/2 = 6
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 3x − 7 = 11 → 3x = 18 → x = 6. Check: 23 = 23 ✓
    (b) 4x − 12 = 2x + 10 → 2x = 22 → x = 11. Check: 4(8) = 32, 2(16) = 32 ✓
    (c) Multiply every term by 4: (x + 5) + 2(x − 1) = 24 → x + 5 + 2x − 2 = 24 → 3x + 3 = 24 → 3x = 21 → x = 7. Check: 12/4 + 6/2 = 3 + 3 = 6 ✓

  2. The triangle. An isosceles triangle has sides of x + 4 cm, x + 4 cm and 2x − 1 cm. Its perimeter is 39 cm. (a) Write an equation in x. (b) Solve it. (c) Find the length of each side.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) (x + 4) + (x + 4) + (2x − 1) = 39, so 4x + 7 = 39.
    (b) 4x = 32 → x = 8.
    (c) 12 cm, 12 cm and 15 cm. Check: 12 + 12 + 15 = 39 ✓

  3. Cinema tickets. A family buys 2 adult tickets and 3 child tickets for €62. An adult ticket costs €6 more than a child ticket. Let x = the price of a child ticket. (a) Write an expression for the price of an adult ticket. (b) Write an equation and solve it. (c) How much is each ticket?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) x + 6.
    (b) 2(x + 6) + 3x = 62 → 2x + 12 + 3x = 62 → 5x + 12 = 62 → 5x = 50 → x = 10.
    (c) Child €10, adult €16. Check: 2(16) + 3(10) = 32 + 30 = 62 ✓

  4. Spot the mistake. Niamh solved 3(x − 2) = x + 8 like this. Line 1: 3x − 2 = x + 8. Line 2: 2x = 10. Line 3: x = 5. (a) Show that x = 5 is not the solution. (b) Which line has the mistake? (c) Solve the equation correctly.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 3(5 − 2) = 9, but 5 + 8 = 13. 9 ≠ 13, so x = 5 is wrong.
    (b) Line 1: the 3 must multiply both terms: 3(x − 2) = 3x − 6.
    (c) 3x − 6 = x + 8 → 2x = 14 → x = 7. Check: 3(5) = 15 and 7 + 8 = 15 ✓
