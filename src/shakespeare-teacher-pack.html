Shakespeare

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R6, R7, R8, R4, OL8

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can match key quotes to characters and moments, read Shakespeare's old words (thou, wherefore, hath…), name common techniques (oxymoron, simile, rhyming couplet, rhetorical question, repetition, pun), plan the staging of an extract, and answer with a point, a short quote and an explanation.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Quote Match (24 mingle cards) · p.3 Director's Chair A/B (pair staging task) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Answer + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 3rd year Higher Level. Time: about 60 minutes. Quotes from Romeo and Juliet, The Merchant of Venice and Julius Caesar, checked against the text; the skills work with any play.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Write “O Romeo, Romeo! wherefore art thou Romeo?” on the board. Ask: where is Juliet asking Romeo to be? (Nowhere: wherefore = why.) Introduce thou / thee / thy and the old-words table (poster, p.4).
12 minQuote Match (p.2, whole class). Each student gets one card: a quote, or a “who + when” card. Find your partner, then together say what the quote shows about the speaker.
15 minDirector's Chair (p.3, pairs). Each plans the staging of an extract (positions, lighting, tone of voice, a prop) and reads it aloud; the partner says who has the power and how it made them feel.
5 minTechniques. Read out three quotes from the studied play: students name the technique and its effect.
18 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Answer in pairs, then the exam-style questions on Antony's speech alone.
5 minExit ticket. One character from the studied play + one short quote + what it shows. Homework: play Who Said It? at leim.ie/who-said-it.html.
Errors to look for: long quotes with no explanation · misquoting (learn a few words exactly) · “wherefore” read as “where” · retelling the plot · missing irony (Antony's “honourable”) · no act or scene · staging answers with no effect on the audience. Support: give the poster as a word bank and a modern-English version of the extracts. Fast finishers: find one example of each technique in the studied play.

B. Exam-style questions: Julius Caesar, Act 3, Scene 2

Brutus has just told the crowd why Caesar had to die. Now Antony speaks at Caesar's funeral.Antony: Friends, Romans, countrymen, lend me your ears; I come to bury Caesar, not to praise him. … The noble Brutus Hath told you Caesar was ambitious: If it were so, it was a grievous fault, And grievously hath Caesar answer'd it. … For Brutus is an honourable man; So are they all, all honourable men-- Come I to speak in Caesar's funeral. He was my friend, faithful and just to me: But Brutus says he was ambitious; And Brutus is an honourable man.
  1. Antony says he has come “to bury Caesar, not to praise him”. Is that true? Explain.
  2. Why does Antony keep repeating that Brutus is “an honourable man”? What effect does it have on the crowd?
  3. Find one word or phrase that shows how Antony felt about Caesar. Explain your choice.
  4. How would you stage this speech? Think about where Antony stands, Caesar's body, the crowd and his tone of voice.
  5. Choose a key speech from the Shakespeare play you studied. Explain how the speaker uses language to persuade or move the audience.

Director's Chair

A: Capulet has the power: Juliet kneels (“on my knees”), he stands over her; harsh light, his voice rising on “Hang thee” and “disobedient wretch”; the Nurse steps between them. B: the power swings from Shylock to Portia on “Tarry a little”: Shylock frozen with the knife raised, a pause, the light on Portia; Gratiano mocks (“O learned judge!”); Shylock's short, stunned “Is that the law?”. Accept any staging with a reason and an effect.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. No: he says he won't praise Caesar, then does exactly that (“my friend, faithful and just to me”) while turning the crowd against the killers.
  2. Repetition: each time he lists something good Caesar did, “honourable” sounds less true, until the crowd hears it as sarcasm (irony). It turns them against Brutus without Antony ever attacking him directly.
  3. For example: “my friend, faithful and just to me”: three warm words, personal and loyal, in contrast with Brutus's cold “ambitious”.
  4. Reward a specific idea with its effect: for example, standing over Caesar's body, quiet and grieving at first, then louder; a sarcastic pause before each “honourable”; the crowd slowly turning.
  5. Reward: a specific speech; two techniques (repetition, rhetorical question, imagery…) named, quoted briefly and explained; the effect on the listeners and the audience.

Marking tip

Answers on the studied play are rewarded for knowledge of the text, short accurate quotes and a clear, supported response to the question. Our guide, not an official marking scheme.
