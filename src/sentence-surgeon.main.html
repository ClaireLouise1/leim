Sentences: types, fragments and run-ons

Clear sentences are the base of every answer you write. Get them right and your writing is easier to read and earns more marks.

Spot the pattern

Read each one out loud. Which is a complete sentence?

1. The bus was late.
2. Because the bus was late.
3. The bus was late we missed the film.

Only one feels finished. What's missing from 2? What's too much in 3?

Show me

1 is a sentence: who (the bus) + verb (was) + a complete thought.
2 is a fragment: because… then what? It needs a main part: Because the bus was late, we missed the film.
3 is a run-on: two sentences jammed together. Fix: The bus was late. We missed the film. or The bus was late, so we missed the film.

What a sentence needs

👤 Who or whatthe subject
The bus · Ciara · We
🏃 A verbdoing or being
was · laughed · missed
✅ A complete thoughtmakes sense on its own
Capital letter at the start, end mark at the end.

Fragments and run-ons

🧩 Fragmenthalf a sentence
Running down the corridor.
→ I was running down the corridor.
🚧 Run-ontwo sentences jammed together
It was raining, we stayed in.
→ It was raining. We stayed in.

A comma can't hold two sentences together. Use a full stop, or a comma + and / but / so / or.

Four types of sentence

💬 Statementgives information
The shop opens at nine.
❓ Questionasks something
Where is my jumper?
📢 Commandtells someone to do something
Close the door.
😲 Exclamationstrong feeling
What a goal! How cold it is!

Simple, compound, complex

1️⃣ Simpleone clause
Rain fell all night.
➕ Compoundtwo main clauses + and / but / so / or
We knocked, but nobody answered.
🔀 Complexmain clause + because / when / if …
When the bell rang, we ran outside.
  • The "because / when / if" part can go first. Then put a comma after it: If you hurry, you'll catch the train.
  • Mix short and long sentences. A very short sentence after long ones creates tension: Then the lights went out.
  • Writers sometimes use a fragment on purpose for effect: The house was silent. Too silent. In your own answers, use full sentences.
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Every written answer is marked partly on how clearly and accurately you write. Run-ons and fragments are the most common slips. You may also be asked how a writer uses sentence length to create an effect.

How marks are won and lost

  • Comma splices ("It was late, we went home") cost accuracy marks in every piece.
  • Varied sentences (short for drama, longer to explain) make your writing stand out.
  • Name the effect when a writer uses a short sentence: tension, shock, a pause.
  • Read your work back in your head: where you pause and drop your voice, you need a full stop.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). "Where the marks would go" is our guide, not an official marking scheme.

  1. Correct the punctuation: “We got to the beach at ten the sun was shining. My brother ran into the sea, he screamed because it was freezing.”
    Show the answer

    We got to the beach at ten. The sun was shining. My brother ran into the sea. He screamed because it was freezing.
    (Or: …ran into the sea, and he screamed…)
    Where the marks would go: one mark-earning fix per run-on: a full stop + capital, or a comma + joining word.

  2. Turn each fragment into a complete sentence: (a) “Because the bus was late.” (b) “Running down the corridor.”
    Show a model answer

    (a) Because the bus was late, we missed the start of the film.
    (b) Jack was running down the corridor when the bell rang.
    Where the marks would go: each answer must have a main part (who + verb) that finishes the thought.

  3. “The house was silent. Too silent. Then, slowly, the cellar door began to open.” How does the writer use sentences to build tension?
    Show a model answer

    The writer uses very short sentences to slow the reader down. “Too silent.” is only two words; it sounds like a warning and makes us feel something is wrong. The last sentence is longer and slowed down by commas (“Then, slowly,”), so we wait anxiously to see what comes through the door.
    Where the marks would go: a point about sentence length, a short quote, and the effect on the reader (tension, fear, waiting).

  4. Join these three short sentences into one good sentence: “The match ended. The fans went home. They were disappointed.”
    Show a model answer

    When the match ended, the disappointed fans went home.
    (Or: The match ended, and the fans went home disappointed.)
    Where the marks would go: a correct joining word or a complex sentence, with all the information kept and correct punctuation.
