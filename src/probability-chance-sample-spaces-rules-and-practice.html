Probability: chance & sample spaces

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): SP.1a, SP.2a, SP.2b. Play the game: leim.ie/spin-and-predict.html

1. The probability scale

A probability is a number from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain). Write it as a fraction, a decimal or a percentage: 1/4 = 0.25 = 25%. It can never be below 0 or above 1.

2. Equally likely outcomes

A fair die, a fair coin, a spinner with equal sectors, a card or counter picked at random: every outcome has the same chance.

P(event) = outcomes that fit ÷ total outcomes

Roll a die: P(prime) = 3/6 = 1/2 (2, 3, 5). Bag of 2 red, 5 blue, 3 green: P(red or green) = 5/10 = 1/2. Always divide by the total, not by "the rest".

3. "Not"

An event either happens or it doesn't, so P(not A) = 1 − P(A). P(rain) = 0.3 → P(no rain) = 0.7. P(a 6) = 1/6 → P(not a 6) = 5/6.

4. Sample spaces

The sample space is the list of every possible outcome. Work in order. Two coins: {HH, HT, TH, TT}. HT and TH are different, so there are 4 outcomes, not 3.

5. Two-way tables

Two dice, scores added: 6 × 6 = 36 equally likely cells. A total of 7 is in 6 cells: P(7) = 6/36 = 1/6.

6. Tree diagrams

Toss a coin, then spin R, B, G. Each branch splits again: 2 × 3 = 6 outcomes. P(H and G) = 1/6.

Exam tip: "3 out of 8" and "3 : 5" are not probabilities: write 3/8. List outcomes in order so nothing is missed or written twice, and check every answer is between 0 and 1.

A. Impossible, unlikely, even chance, likely or certain?

  2. Rolling a number less than 7 on a fair die

  4. Rolling a 6 on a fair die
  5. Rolling a number bigger than 1 on a fair die
  6. Picking a red counter from a bag of 9 red and 1 blue

B. Equally likely outcomes (give fractions in their simplest form)

  1. You roll a fair die. Find P(a prime number).
  2. You roll a fair die. Find P(a multiple of 3).
  3. Cards numbered 1 to 20: find P(a multiple of 4).
  4. Cards numbered 1 to 20: find P(a prime number).
  5. A bag has 3 red, 5 blue and 2 green counters. Find P(blue).
  6. Same bag: find P(not green).
  7. Same bag: find P(red or green).
  8. The letters of BALLYBUNION are put on cards. Find P(B).

C. "Not" and "how many?"

  1. P(rain in Cork tomorrow) = 0.6. Find P(no rain).
  2. P(winning a raffle prize) = 3/7. Find P(not winning).
  3. P(the bus is late) = 12%. Find P(the bus is not late).
  4. A bag has 24 counters and P(red) = 3/8. How many are red?
  5. A spinner has 10 equal sectors and P(blue) = 3/10. How many sectors are blue?
  6. Which of these can NOT be a probability: 0.7, 5/4, 0, 85%?

  2. List the sample space when you spin a spinner with sectors R, B, then a spinner numbered 1 to 3.
  3. How many outcomes are there when you toss a coin and roll a die?
  4. Two dice are rolled and the scores added. How many cells are in the two-way table?

  6. Two dice: find P(both dice show the same number).
  7. Two dice: find P(a total greater than 9).
  8. Two dice: which total is most likely?

  10. A coin is tossed, then a spinner numbered 1 to 4 is spun. Use a tree diagram to find P(a head and an even number).

E. Exam-style questions (show your work)

  1. A bag has 5 red, 3 yellow and 4 green marbles. One is taken at random. (a) Find P(yellow). (b) Find P(not red). (c) How many green marbles must be taken out of the bag so that P(green) = 1/5?
  2. Spinner A is numbered 1 to 4 and spinner B is numbered 1 to 3 (equal sectors). Both are spun and the scores multiplied. (a) Draw a two-way table. (b) Find P(the product is 6). (c) Find P(the product is greater than 5).
  3. A fair coin is tossed three times. (a) Use a tree diagram to list the sample space. (b) Find P(exactly two heads). (c) Find P(at least one tail).
  4. Seán says: "The probability that Cork win their next match is 0.6, so the probability that they lose is 0.4." Explain why Seán may be wrong.
