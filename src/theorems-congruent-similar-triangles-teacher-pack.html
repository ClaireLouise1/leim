Theorems, congruent & similar triangles

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): GT.3b, GT.3c, GT.3d, GT.3e, U.11, U.12

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can use Theorems 9 and 10 (parallelograms), the angle in a semicircle (Corollaries 3 and 4), the congruence tests SSS, SAS, ASA and RHS and similar triangles (Theorem 13) to find angles and lengths, explain the words axiom, theorem, corollary, converse and implies, and order and justify the steps of a short proof. Higher Level: Theorems 11, 12 and 19, Corollaries 1, 2 and 5.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Proof Builder mingle cards + Proof Sort strips · p.3 Geometry Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 2nd year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: cut the parallelogram. Each pair draws a parallelogram on paper, draws one diagonal, cuts it out and cuts along the diagonal. "Do the two triangles fit on top of each other? Which sides land on which?" Elicit: congruent, so opposite sides and angles are equal (Theorem 9). Ask: "What made them fit?" (alternate angles + a shared side = ASA).
8 minGuided discovery: similar triangles. Pairs draw a triangle ABC, then a line DE parallel to BC (use a set square). Measure |AD|, |AB|, |DE| and |BC|. Compare |AD| ÷ |AB| with |DE| ÷ |BC|. What do you notice? Build Theorem 13 and the idea of a scale factor.
10 minProof Builder mingle (p.2, 24 cards = 12 question/answer pairs; one card each). Question card? Work it out and find your answer. Answer card? Find your question. Pairs explain the reason to each other ("opposite angles", "SAS").
10 minProof Sort (p.2, pairs). Cut the strips of one proof, put them in a correct order and read the proof aloud with the reasons. Discuss: which lines could swap places? (The three facts before "≡" can come in any order; the "So …" line is always last.)
12 minGeometry Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, and build the chain to FINISH. (Two dominoes are Higher Level: HL.)
12 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, give the right reason, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone.
3 minExit ticket. Write the converse of "If a quadrilateral is a parallelogram, then its diagonals bisect each other." Is it true? Homework: play Proof Builder at leim.ie/proof-builder.html and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.

Proof Sort key: The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other: D, C, A, B, E · The angle in a semicircle is a right angle: D, C, E, A, B (the first three facts of each can be in any order).
Errors to look for: SAS with the angle not between the sides · adding instead of multiplying with similar triangles · using part of a side · matching letters in the wrong order · thinking the diagonals of a parallelogram are equal. Fast finishers: is the converse of "every square has four equal sides" true?

Proof Sort: The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other

Given: ABCD is a parallelogram; its diagonals meet at E.

A |AB| = |CD| (Opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal)
B △ABE ≡ △CDE (ASA)
C ∠EBA = ∠EDC (Alternate angles)
D ∠EAB = ∠ECD (Alternate angles)
E So |AE| = |CE| and |BE| = |DE| (Matching parts of congruent triangles are equal)

Proof Sort: The angle in a semicircle is a right angle

Given: [BC] is a diameter, centre O, A on the circle; join O to A.

A x + y + (x + y) = 180° (Angles in a triangle add to 180°)
B So ∠BAC = x + y = 90° (Divide both sides by 2)
C ∠OBA = ∠OAB: call them x (Isosceles triangle: base angles are equal)
D |OA| = |OB| = |OC| (Radii of the same circle)
E ∠OCA = ∠OAC: call them y (Isosceles triangle: base angles are equal)

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work, with reasons)

  1. In a parallelogram, two neighbouring angles are (3x + 15)° and (2x + 15)°. Find x and the size of each angle.
  2. ABCD is a parallelogram with the diagonal [BD] (diagram). Prove that △ABD ≡ △CDB.
  3. In △ABC, D is on [AB], E is on [AC] and DE ∥ BC. |AD| = 5 cm, |AB| = 15 cm and |BC| = 18 cm. (a) Find |DE|. (b) |EC| = 8 cm. Find |AE|.

For the teacher

Answers

↗ Léim

Proof Sort

The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other: D, C, A, B, E · The angle in a semicircle is a right angle: D, C, E, A, B. Any order of the first three facts is correct; the congruence (or similarity) line comes after them and the "So …" line comes last.

A. Error Hunt

Parallelogram, ∠A = 64°. Find ∠C. 116°∠A and ∠C are opposite angles, so they are equal: 64°
[BC] is a diameter and ∠ABC = 40°. Find ∠ACB. 140°The angle at A is 90° (semicircle): 180° − (90° + 40°) = 50°
|AB| = |PQ|, |BC| = |QR| and ∠BAC = ∠QPR. "Congruent by SAS." SASThe angle at A is NOT between [AB] and [BC]: that's SSA, not a test
△ABC ≡ △PQR. "So |AC| = |QR|." |AC| = |QR|Match the letters in order: [AC] ↔ [PR]
Similar triangles: 4 cm ↔ 6 cm. A 10 cm side becomes? 12 cmMultiply, don't add: scale factor 1.5, 10 × 1.5 = 15 cm
Parallelogram ABCD, diagonals meet at O, |AO| = 5 cm. "So |BD| = 10 cm." 10 cm[AO] is half of [AC], not [BD]: |AC| = 10 cm; |BD| is unknown

B. Exam-style questions

  1. Neighbouring angles add to 180° (co-interior): 5x + 30 = 180, so x = 30. The angles are 105° and 75°.
  2. |AB| = |CD| and |AD| = |CB| (opposite sides of a parallelogram); |BD| = |DB| (common side). So △ABD ≡ △CDB (SSS). (Or ASA with alternate angles.) This is Corollary 1.
  3. (a) Scale factor 15 ÷ 5 = 3: |DE| = 18 ÷ 3 = 6 cm. (b) |AE| : |AC| = 1 : 3, so |EC| is 2 parts = 8 cm: |AE| = 4 cm.
  4. ∠PRQ = 90° (angle in a semicircle), so 2x + x + 15 = 90: x = 25. ∠RPQ = 50° and ∠RQP = 40°.
  5. ∠BAD = 130° ÷ 2 = 65° (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circle, Theorem 19). ∠BCD = 180° − 65° = 115° (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral, Corollary 5).

Marking tip

Give credit for each step, as the exam does: each correct value, each correct reason, and in a proof the three facts and the named test. A number without a reason, or "≡" without SSS/SAS/ASA/RHS, is incomplete. For "explain" parts insist on a sentence with a reason in it.
