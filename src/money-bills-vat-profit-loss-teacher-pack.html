Money: bills, VAT, profit & loss

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): N.2c, U.6 (income tax, net pay and compound interest come next, in Money 2; currency in the speed, distance and time topic)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can add up bills (meter readings, standing charges, phone plans, call-out charges), add VAT and find the price before VAT, find sale prices and prices before a sale, work out profit, loss and % profit on the cost price, and judge value for money. Higher Level: mark-up and margin.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Market Day mingle cards + Bill Swap A/B cards · p.3 Money Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 2nd year (Higher and Ordinary), or 3rd-year revision. Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: the VAT trap. Board "A bike costs €615 with VAT at 23%. What was it before VAT?" Students write an answer alone, then compare. Collect both answers (usually €473.55 and €500) without saying which is right.
8 minGuided discovery. Pairs add 23% to €100, €200, €500, then take 23% off each answer. Why don't they get back? (The second 23% is of a bigger number.) €123 is what % of €100? So how do we go back? (÷ 1.23.) Settle the lead-in: €500.
10 minMarket Day mingle (p.2, 24 cards = 12 question/answer pairs; one card each). Question card? Work it out and find the person with your answer. Answer card? Find your question. Pairs check each other's working.
12 minBill Swap (p.2, pairs, don't show your card). Each fact on the two bills is on only ONE card. Ask your partner for what you're missing ("What was the present reading?"), then both fill every blank. Feedback: compare totals (electricity €301.28, phone €50.50).
10 minMoney Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, and build the chain to FINISH.
12 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs (find the mistake, name the rule, fix it), then the exam-style questions alone.
3 minExit ticket. A coat costs €184.50 including VAT at 23%: price before VAT? Bought €60, sold €72: % profit on the cost? (€150; 20%.) Homework: play Shop Keeper at leim.ie/shop-keeper.html and copy three lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Market Day key: €50 + VAT at 23% → €61.50 · €246 incl. 23% VAT. Before VAT? → €200.00 · 25% off €36 → €27.00 · Bought €20, sold €26. % profit? → 30% · Cost €40, sold at 15% profit → €46.00 · Bought €50, sold €45. % loss? → 10% · Units: 12 450 → 12 850 → 400 units · 400 units at 30c → €120.00 · 500 g for €2.10. Per 100 g? → 42c · 3 for 2, €7 each. Cost of 3? → €14.00 · Higher Level: €40 → €50. Margin? → 20% · 9% VAT = €9. Price before VAT? → €100.00.
Errors to look for: taking 23% off a VAT-inclusive price · adding the % back on to undo a sale · dividing by the selling price for % profit on the cost · using the meter reading instead of the units used · forgetting the standing charge · charging every minute, not just the extras · comparing pack prices instead of price per 100 g. Support: write "100% → 123%" over every VAT question. Fast finishers: a shop adds a 50% mark-up; what is its margin? (33⅓%)

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. A gas meter read 37 145 at the start of the period and 37 945 at the end. Units cost 31c and the standing charge is €28.60. VAT at 9% is added. Find (a) the units used, (b) the bill before VAT, (c) the total bill to the nearest cent.
  2. A builder quotes €1,450 before VAT for a new wall. VAT is charged at 13.5%. Find the total price.
  3. A club shop buys 24 jerseys for €12.50 each. It sells 20 for €22 each and the rest at half price. Find (a) the total cost, (b) the total income, (c) the profit as a percentage of the cost price, to one decimal place.
  4. Washing powder comes in a 750 g box for €4.80 or a 1.2 kg box for €7.20. Which is better value? Show your work.
  5. Higher Level A café makes a smoothie for €0.90 and sells it for €2.25. Find the mark-up and the margin.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. (a) 800 (b) 800 × €0.31 = €248.00, + €28.60 = €276.60 (c) VAT €24.89, total €301.49
  2. €1,450 × 1.135 = €1,645.75
  3. (a) 24 × €12.50 = €300.00 (b) 20 × €22 + 4 × €11 = €484.00 (c) profit €184.00; 184 ÷ 300 × 100 = 61.3%
  4. Per 100 g: €4.80 ÷ 7.5 = 64c; €7.20 ÷ 12 = 60c. The 1.2 kg box is better value.
  5. Profit €1.35. Mark-up 1.35 ÷ 0.90 = 150%; margin 1.35 ÷ 2.25 = 60%

Marking tip

Give credit for each step, as the exam does: units used, cost of units, subtotal and VAT each earn marks even if a later line slips. For "which is better value?" insist on the price of the same amount for each AND a conclusion.
