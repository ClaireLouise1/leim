Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

Exam Technique

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R3, R7, W1, W3, W4, W11

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can say what each command word asks for, plan their time on a 2-hour, 180-mark paper from the marks, pick out the form, reader and purpose of a writing task, and fix answers that lose marks by missing the question.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Command Word Sort (24 cards) · p.3 Exam Planner Swap A/B (pair listening task) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Answer + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 3rd year (works from 2nd year). Time: about 60 minutes.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Write “Identify”, “Explain”, “Compare” on the board. Students say what a full answer to each would look like. Which is shortest? Which needs two texts?
12 minCommand Word Sort (p.2, groups of 3–4). Sort the 24 question cards into six piles by the job they ask for. Then each group picks one card and plans a full answer in three bullet points.
15 minExam Planner Swap (p.3, pairs). Each reads their made-up paper aloud; the partner plans the minutes for each question (⅔ of a minute a mark). Swap and check that each plan adds up to 120 minutes.
5 minPoster (p.4). Read it. Time it. Answer it. Ask: what do you do when the time for a question runs out?
18 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Answer in pairs, then the exam-style questions alone.
5 minExit ticket. One question from an old class test: underline the command word, circle how many, box the focus. Homework: play English Exam Mix at leim.ie/english-exam-mix.html.
Errors to look for: answering “what” when the question asks “why” · comparing one text only · long answers to small questions and no time left for the big one · retelling the story · a writing task in the wrong form or register for its reader. Note: the sections and marks of the real paper change each year; the papers in this pack are made up. Our guide, not an official marking scheme.

A. Fix the Answer

Q: Explain why the girl hides the letter. A: She hides it under her bed.says what, not why: “She hides it because she is afraid her mother will read it, as she “glanced at the door” first.”
Q: Compare the two poems. A: The first poem is about winter and uses lots of similes.only one poem: every point needs both (“Both are about winter, but…”)
Q: Identify two facts from the leaflet. (6 marks) A: a whole page about why the leaflet is gooda 6-mark question gets about 4 minutes: two short facts, then move on
Q: Do you agree that the ending is happy? A: The ending is when they go home.no view and no reason: “I agree, because…” + evidence
Q: Describe the setting. A: It is nice.no detail: name the place, time and mood, with words from the text
Q: Write a letter to your principal… A: Hey Sir, guess what!!wrong register for the reader: “Dear Mr Byrne, I am writing to…”

B. Exam-style questions

  1. About 13, 27 and 40 minutes (20 × ⅔ = 13⅓, 40 × ⅔ = 26⅔, 60 × ⅔ = 40).
  2. Name (find two things: short) and explain (say how she feels and why, with a quote).
  3. Talk about both stories in every point: how the openings are alike and how they are different.
  4. Form: a speech. Reader: the students at your school assembly. Purpose: to persuade.
  5. Sample: “Good morning, everyone. Hands up if you could swim fifty metres right now, in cold water, in your clothes?” (talks to the audience, hooks them with a question).

Marking tip

Answers are rewarded for doing what the question asks: the right job for the command word, the right number of points, and evidence from the text. Our guide, not an official marking scheme.
