Personal & descriptive writing

Good writing lets the reader see, hear and feel what happened. Don't name the feeling: show it.

Spot the pattern

A

I was scared. The house was dark.

B

My heart pounded. I froze at the bottom of the stairs as the floorboards creaked above me.

Both are about the same moment. Which one lets you picture it? What does B never say, but you still know? Find the words that show the fear.

Show me

A tells: it names the feeling (scared) and gives a vague picture (dark).
B shows: it never says “scared”, but the body gives it away: heart pounded, froze. The sound floorboards creaked puts you in the house. The reader works out the feeling, and that's what makes it gripping.

Cut nice, thing, stuff.
  • Personal writing is about you: use “I”, real moments and how you felt. Descriptive writing paints a place or a moment so the reader is there.
  • Pick one strong verb instead of a weak verb + adverb: “ran quickly” → raced.
  • Don't pile on adjectives. Two sharp details beat five vague ones.
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

The Junior Cycle paper usually asks you to write: a personal piece, a description, a story or a diary entry, often linked to the paper's theme. Examiners reward writing that is vivid, clear and controlled, not long.

How marks are won and lost

  • Show feelings through actions and body language instead of naming them.
  • Use more than sight: one sound, smell or touch detail makes a scene real.
  • Precise words (strong verbs, specific nouns) win marks; nice, good, thing and stuff lose them.
  • Stick to the task (diary? description?) and plan: a clear start, middle and ending.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). "Where the marks would go" is our guide, not an official marking scheme.

  1. Rewrite this sentence so it shows the feeling: “I was nervous before my first match.”
    Show a model answer

    “I tied my laces three times. My stomach was full of butterflies and my hands were so sweaty that the ball slipped when the ref threw it in.”
    Where the marks would go: no feeling word, two or more clear body or action clues, and a detail that puts us at the match.

  2. “The beach was nice.” Rewrite it using three different senses.
    Show a model answer

    “Gulls screamed above the waves (sound). The sand was warm and soft under my feet (touch), and the air smelled of salt and suncream (smell).”
    Where the marks would go: three senses, each clearly different, and “nice” replaced by details.

  3. Replace the weak verbs: “I walked to the door. ‘Hello?’ I said, and looked into the dark hall.”
    Show a model answer

    “I crept to the door. ‘Hello?’ I whispered, and peered into the dark hall.”
    Where the marks would go: three strong verbs that all fit the tense, quiet mood (not “raced” or “shouted”).

  4. Write the opening paragraph of a personal essay called “The Last Day of Summer”.
    Show a model answer

    “The sun was already low over Inchydoney when I dragged my feet out of the sea for the last time. My towel was gritty with sand. Somewhere behind the dunes, Dad was beeping the car horn, and a lump rose in my throat. Tomorrow there would be uniforms, alarm clocks and the smell of new copybooks.”
    Where the marks would go: a clear setting, more than one sense, a feeling shown not told (dragged my feet, a lump in my throat), and a final line that hints at what comes next.
