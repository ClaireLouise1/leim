Junior Cycle Spanish · Teacher pack

Health and the body

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes: 1.11, 1.13 (2017 spec, exams 2027–2028) · 1.10, 1.12 (2025 spec, 1st years from Sept 2026)

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can name the main parts of the body, say what hurts with me duele / me duelen (and le duele for someone else), describe symptoms with tengo (fiebre, tos, gripe) and estoy (enfermo/a, resfriado/a, mareado/a), understand and give advice with tienes que / debes + infinitive, and cope at the doctor's and the chemist's.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Doctor, Doctor! (A/B role-play info gap) · p.3–4 Health Dominoes (cut-up) · p.5 Wall poster · p.6 Worksheet + answers. Level: 3rd year (revision for the exam). Time: about 60 minutes. All texts are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in: Simón dice. Tocaos la cabeza… la rodilla… el hombro… Students touch the body part; anyone who moves without "Simón dice" sits down. Then mime pain (hold your head, your back) and elicit ¿Qué me pasa?
8 minGuided discovery. Board: Me duele la cabeza. / Me duelen los pies. / A mi madre le duele la espalda. Pairs answer: why duele or duelen? which word shows who? where is "my"? Link to me gusta / me gustan. Then sort fiebre, enfermo, la garganta, tos, mareado, el estómago under Tengo / Estoy / Me duele with the poster (p.5).
15 minDoctor, Doctor! (p.2). Pairs, A and B cards. Students first write the doctor's questions (tú) from the prompts, then A is the patient and B the doctor; swap. The doctor finishes with two pieces of advice. Monitor the questions first. Extension: a third student plays the chemist: the patient asks ¿Tiene algo para…?
10 minHealth Dominoes (p.3–4), groups of 3–4. Answer chain below.
12 minWorksheet (p.6): read Paula's email, A alone, B–C in pairs. Feedback: which body part did she break? (her left arm)
5 minExit ticket. Write one problem and one piece of advice for it (Me duele… → Tienes que…). Homework: play ¿Qué te duele? at leim.ie/que-te-duele.html and copy five lines from the "Go back over these" list.
Doctor's questions: 1 ¿Cómo te llamas? 2 ¿Qué te pasa? 3 ¿Desde cuándo te duele? 4 ¿Tienes fiebre? 5 ¿Te duele algo más? 6 ¿Has tomado algo? / ¿Qué has hecho? 7 ¿Haces deporte? Answers: A (Carmen): earache since Saturday · 37.5° · sore throat too · took a painkiller, it doesn't help · swims every day. Advice e.g. Tienes que ir al médico. No debes nadar esta semana. B (Rubén): twisted ankle playing football, since yesterday afternoon · no temperature · knee hurts a bit · put ice on it · football five days a week. Advice e.g. Debes descansar. No debes jugar al fútbol dos semanas.
Errors to listen for: Me duele mi cabeza (→ la cabeza) · Me duele los pies (→ duelen) · Estoy fiebre (→ tengo) · Debes bebes (→ infinitive) · Tienes ir (→ tienes que). Language awareness: Irish Tá tinneas cinn orm / Spanish Me duele la cabeza: neither says "I have a head that hurts". Culture: Spanish chemists (green cross) give a lot of advice; one farmacia de guardia stays open at night; 112 is the emergency number in Spain and Ireland. Support: leave the poster up. Fast finishers: invent a third patient.

Worksheet

Paula's skiing accident

↗ Léim
¡Hola, Aisling! ¿Qué tal estás? Te escribo desde el sofá… ¡con el brazo escayolado! El sábado fui a esquiar a Sierra Nevada con mi club. Hacía un día precioso, pero en la última bajada me caí y me di un golpe muy fuerte. Me dolía muchísimo el brazo y no podía moverlo. Un monitor llamó a la ambulancia y fuimos a urgencias en Granada. Me hicieron una radiografía: ¡me he roto el brazo izquierdo! Ahora tengo que llevar una escayola seis semanas. Lo bueno es que escribo con la mano derecha, así que puedo hacer los deberes (¡qué pena!). Lo malo es que no puedo jugar al baloncesto hasta marzo y por la noche me duele un poco. El médico dice que tengo que tomar una pastilla para el dolor cada ocho horas y descansar mucho. Mi hermano Óscar es muy simpático: me ayuda a vestirme y me prepara el desayuno. ¿Y tú? ¿Has tenido un accidente alguna vez? ¿Qué deportes haces en invierno? Un beso, Paula

A. Answer in English

  1. Where is Paula writing from? Why?
  2. Where did she go on Saturday, and with whom?
  3. What was the weather like?
  4. What happened on the last run?
  1. How did she get to hospital?
  2. What did the X-ray show? For how long is the cast?
  3. Give one good thing and one bad thing.
  4. What did the doctor tell her to do? Who helps her?

B. Find the Spanish in the email

  1. my arm hurt a lot
  2. we went to A&E
  1. I have to wear a cast
  2. every eight hours

C. Give advice (tienes que / debes / no debes…)

  1. Me duele la cabeza.
  2. Tengo mucha tos.
  1. Me duelen los pies.
  2. Estoy muy cansada.

D. Now you

Reply to Paula as Aisling (50–60 words): say you're sorry, give her two pieces of advice, tell her about a time you were ill or hurt, and answer one of her questions. Finish with ¡Que te mejores!

Answers. A: 1 From the sofa; she has her arm in a cast. 2 Skiing in Sierra Nevada, with her club. 3 A beautiful (sunny) day. 4 She fell and hit herself very hard; her arm really hurt and she couldn't move it. 5 An instructor called an ambulance; they went to A&E in Granada. 6 She has broken her left arm; six weeks. 7 Good: she writes with her right hand, so she can do her homework. Bad: no basketball until March / it hurts a bit at night. 8 Take a painkiller every eight hours and rest a lot; her brother Óscar (helps her get dressed, makes her breakfast). · B: 9 me dolía muchísimo el brazo 10 fuimos a urgencias 11 tengo que llevar una escayola 12 cada ocho horas · C (examples): 13 Debes tomar una aspirina y descansar. 14 Debes tomar un jarabe. 15 Debes ponerte zapatillas más cómodas. 16 Debes acostarte más temprano.
Watch out: escayolado = in a plaster cast; la bajada = the run (downhill); me di un golpe = I hit myself / took a knock; el monitor = instructor; la radiografía = X-ray.
