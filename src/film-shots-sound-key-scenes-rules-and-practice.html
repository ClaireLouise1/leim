Every shot in a film is a choice. In the exam you name the technique, point to the moment, and explain the effect on the audience.

1. Shot sizes: how close is the camera?

Extreme long shot
Long shot
Medium shot
Close-up
Extreme close-up

The further away, the more we see of the place (an extreme long shot often opens a scene: the establishing shot). The closer, the more we see of the feelings.

2. Camera angles

Low anglecamera looks up: big, powerful, frightening
High anglecamera looks down: small, weak, alone
Eye levelnormal, like meeting the person
Bird's-eye viewright above, looking straight down
Point-of-view shotwe see through a character's eyes

3. Sound and music

Diegeticfrom inside the story: voices, footsteps, a radio. The characters can hear it.
Non-diegeticadded for us: soundtrack music, a voice-over. The characters can't hear it.

Music tells us how to feel: low, faster drums = tension; slow piano = sadness; bright fiddle = joy. Silence makes a moment stand out.

4. Editing

Quick cutsmany short shots: speed, panic
Slow motiona big moment lasts longer
Flashbacka jump back to the past
Fade to blackends a scene; time passes
Montageshort clips squeeze weeks into a minute

5. Writing about a key scene

Name it · describe it · say what it doesA low-angle shot looks up at the coach on the touchline, so he seems powerful and frightening, and we fear for the team.

One sentence of plot, then two or three techniques. Name the film and the director.

Exam tip: “the camera shows” wins nothing. “A close-up of her shaking hands shows she is terrified” wins the marks.

F. Exam-style

A key scene from The Last Ferry (an imaginary film). Dara (13) has missed the last ferry home from the island. Shot 1: a long shot of Dara running down the empty pier as the ferry pulls away. Shot 2: a high-angle shot of Dara alone at the end of the pier. Shot 3: a close-up of his face as the ferry's lights get smaller. Sound: the ferry's horn, then slow, soft piano on the soundtrack. Shot 4: a low-angle shot of an old fisherman stepping out of the dark: “Need a lift, son?”
  1. How does the director make Dara seem alone and in trouble in Shots 1 to 3? Refer to two techniques.
  2. Why do you think Shot 4 is a low-angle shot? How might the audience feel about the fisherman at this moment?

D15. N · 16. D · 17. D · 18. D · 19. N · 20. N

F26. For example: the long shot of the empty pier shows nobody is there to help; the high-angle shot looks down on Dara, so he seems small and powerless; the close-up as the lights get smaller shows his worry; the slow piano (non-diegetic) adds sadness. Reward two techniques named, each linked to the effect. · 27. The low angle makes the fisherman seem big and a little frightening as he steps out of the dark, so we are unsure at first whether to trust him, until his kind words (“Need a lift, son?”) bring relief. Reward: the angle explained + a feeling with a reason.
