Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

Personal and descriptive writing

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): W3, W4, W9, W7

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can show a feeling through actions and body language instead of naming it, use more than one sense in a description, choose strong verbs and specific details, and spot vague writing in their own drafts.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Show It Sort cards (cut-up) · p.3 Picture This A/B (speaking + listening) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Writing + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (useful revision in 2nd and 3rd). Time: about 60 minutes. All texts are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Board two versions: I was scared. The house was dark. / My heart pounded. I froze at the bottom of the stairs as the floorboards creaked above me. Which one lets you picture it? What does the second never say, but you still know?
8 minGuided discovery. Students find the words that show fear (pounded, froze, creaked). Elicit the toolkit: body clues, the five senses, strong verbs, zooming in. Model turning "I was bored" into a showing sentence together.
12 minShow It Sort (p.2, groups of 3–4, one set of 24 cards). Sort the cards into eight feelings (3 each). Then each student mimes one card for the group to guess.
12 minPicture This (p.3, pairs). A reads "The Beach at Dawn" aloud twice; B listens with eyes closed, then writes the words for each sense, the feeling and the best verb. Swap for "The School Canteen".
18 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Writing in pairs, then the exam-style questions on "Market Morning" alone.
5 minExit ticket. Describe the classroom right now in three sentences: no "nice", one sound, one feeling shown. Homework: play Show, Don't Tell at leim.ie/show-dont-tell.html.
Errors to look for: naming the feeling and adding a body clue ("I was scared and I froze") · sight only · nice / good / things · adverbs propping up weak verbs · piles of adjectives. Support: the poster (p.4) on the desk; give weaker groups the eight feeling labels. Fast finishers: write the same moment twice, once happy and once nervous, without naming either feeling.

B. Exam-style questions: "Market Morning"

The English Market was already busy when we squeezed through the gates. Bright red apples were piled high beside the fish stall, where silver mackerel glittered on the ice. Traders shouted prices over the noise. The air smelled of fresh bread and coffee. Mam handed me a warm doughnut and I bit into it: the sugar was sweet on my tongue. I grinned. This was the best part of Saturday.
  1. Find one detail the writer can see and one they can hear.
  2. Pick out a strong verb. Why is it better than a plainer verb?
  3. How does the writer show how they feel without naming the feeling?
  4. Find one detail that "zooms in". What does it help you picture?
  5. Write a paragraph about a busy place you know. Use three senses and show one feeling.

The Beach at Dawn: Sight: Pale pink light spread across the sky. · Sound: Waves rumbled against the rocks. · Smell: The air smelled of seaweed. · Taste: I could taste salt on my lips. · Touch: The sand was cold and damp under my feet. · Feeling shown: Happy (I grinned and raced towards the water.)

The School Canteen: Sight: Bright orange trays slid along the counter. · Sound: Chairs scraped and the whole room roared. · Smell: The canteen smelled of chips and gravy. · Taste: My chicken curry was spicy and delicious. · Touch: The sticky table clung to my elbows. · Feeling shown: Bored (I yawned and watched the clock until the bell.)

Best strong verbs: raced, rumbled, spread (A) · slid, scraped, roared, clung (B). Accept any well-chosen verb with a reason.

A. Fix the Writing

I was really scared.Tells the feeling. Show it: I froze. My heart pounded so hard I could hear it.
The food was nice.Vague. Zoom in: The lasagne was hot and cheesy, with crispy edges.
I walked slowly up the hill.Weak verb + adverb. One strong verb: I trudged up the hill.
It was a big, dark, old, scary, creepy house.Too many adjectives. Pick two and add a sense: The old house was dark, and the gate creaked in the wind.
“Hi,” I said. “Hello,” he said. “Where's Mam?” I said.“Said” three times. Vary it or cut it: “Hi,” I whispered. “Where's Mam?”
Then I woke up and it was all a dream.A let-down ending. End on a feeling or a final image: I still keep that shell on my windowsill.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. See: "bright red apples", "silver mackerel glittered on the ice". Hear: "traders shouted prices over the noise".
  2. "squeezed" (or "glittered", "shouted"): it shows how crowded the gates were, which "went" or "walked" would not.
  3. "I grinned" and "the best part of Saturday": the reader can tell they are happy from the action and the final thought.
  4. "silver mackerel glittered on the ice": colour, the kind of fish and the ice let us picture the stall exactly.
  5. Credit three different senses, specific details, strong verbs and a feeling shown through actions or body language.

Marking tip

Reward precision over length: a short paragraph with a shown feeling, two or three senses and strong verbs beats a long one full of "nice" and "really". Our guide, not an official marking scheme.
