Volume, surface area & nets

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2018 specification): GT.2b, GT.2d, GT.2e

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can draw and recognise the nets of cuboids, prisms and cylinders, and find the volume and surface area of cuboids, cylinders, triangular prisms, spheres, hemispheres and combinations of these, in real-life problems (litres, packing, water rising), with and without π.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Pack It mingle cards + Tank Swap A/B · p.3 Volume Dominoes (cut-up) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Error Hunt + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 3rd year (Higher and Ordinary). Time: about 60 minutes. All questions are original.

Procedure

8 minLead-in: layers. Build a 4 × 2 layer of cubes (or sugar cubes). How many in 1, 2, 3 layers? 10 layers? Students find volume = base area × height, then stretch it to any prism and a cylinder. Unfold an empty cereal box to show a net.
10 minPack It mingle (p.2, 24 cards = 12 question/answer pairs; one card each, calculators allowed). Question card? Work it out and find your answer. Answer card? Find your question. Pairs say the formula they used.
12 minTank Swap (p.2, pairs, one A and one B card). Don't show your card. Each fact is on only one card: ask your partner for what you're missing, sketch each solid and fill every blank.
12 minVolume Dominoes (p.3), groups of 3–4. Start with START, work out the clue, find the domino with that answer on its left, and build the chain to FINISH. Answers with π stay in terms of π.
15 minWorksheet (p.5). Error Hunt in pairs, then the exam-style questions alone. Optional: bring in a tin, measure it and check the litres on the label.
3 minExit ticket. A cylinder has radius 5 cm and height 10 cm. Find its volume in terms of π. Homework: play Pack It at leim.ie/pack-it.html.

B. Exam-style questions (show all your work)

  1. A swimming pool is a cuboid 25 m long, 10 m wide and 1.5 m deep. (a) Find its volume in m³. (b) How many litres is that? (c) A pump fills it at 500 litres a minute. How many hours does it take?
  2. A candle is a cylinder with radius 4 cm and height 12 cm. (a) Find its volume in terms of π. (b) How many whole candles can be made from 6,000 cm³ of wax?
  3. A water trough is a triangular prism 200 cm long. Its end is a right-angled triangle with sides 30 cm, 40 cm and 50 cm. Find its volume in cm³ and in litres.
  4. Three tennis balls, each of radius 3.3 cm, fit exactly in a cylinder-shaped tube (radius 3.3 cm, height 19.8 cm). (a) Find the volume of the tube in terms of π. (b) Find the volume of the three balls in terms of π. (c) What fraction of the tube is empty space?
  5. Higher Level. A sphere has a volume of 4,500π cm³. (a) Find its radius. (b) Find its surface area, in terms of π and to 1 d.p.

Tank Swap

Fish tank: 80 × 30 × 40 = 96,000 cm³ = 96 litres · Glass (no lid): 2,400 + 2 × 3,200 + 2 × 1,200 = 11,200 cm² · Water butt: π × 30² × 90 = 254,469 cm³ = 254.5 litres · Ball: ⁴⁄₃π × 6³ = 288π; 288π ÷ (π × 8²) = 4.5 cm · Gift box: ½ × 9 × 12 × 20 = 1,080 cm³ · Card: 2 × 54 + (9 + 12 + 15) × 20 = 828 cm².

A. Error Hunt

Cylinder, diameter 10 cm, height 8 cm: volume in terms of π. π × 10² × 8 = 800π cm³Used the diameter. Radius 5: π × 5² × 8 = 200π cm³
Cuboid 6 × 4 × 3 cm: total surface area. 6 × 4 × 3 = 72 cm²That's the volume. 2(24 + 18 + 12) = 108 cm²
Sphere, radius 6 cm: volume in terms of π. 4π × 6² = 144π cm³That's the surface area formula. ⁴⁄₃π × 6³ = 288π cm³
Tank 30 × 20 × 10 cm: how many litres? 6,000 ÷ 100 = 60 litres1 litre = 1,000 cm³: 6,000 ÷ 1,000 = 6 litres
How many 10 cm cubes fit in a 25 cm cube box? 15,625 ÷ 1,000 = 15.6, so 15Count along each edge: 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 (the gaps can't hold a cube)
Open box 10 × 8 × 5 cm (no lid): area of card. 2(80 + 50 + 40) = 340 cm²No lid: one 10 × 8 face only. 80 + 100 + 80 = 260 cm²

B. Exam-style questions

  1. (a) 25 × 10 × 1.5 = 375 m³ (b) × 1,000 = 375,000 litres (c) 375,000 ÷ 500 = 750 minutes = 12.5 hours.
  2. (a) π × 4² × 12 = 192π cm³ (= 603.2 cm³). (b) 6,000 ÷ 603.19 = 9.9…, so 9 candles (round down: the 10th isn't complete).
  3. End ½ × 30 × 40 = 600 cm². 600 × 200 = 120,000 cm³ = 120 litres.
  4. (a) π × 3.3² × 19.8 = 215.622π cm³ (b) 3 × ⁴⁄₃π × 3.3³ = 143.748π cm³ (c) (215.622 − 143.748) ÷ 215.622 = 71.874 ÷ 215.622 = ⅓. The balls fill two thirds of the tube, whatever the radius.
  5. (a) ⁴⁄₃πr³ = 4,500π → r³ = 3,375 → r = 15 cm (b) 4π × 15² = 900π cm² = 2,827.4 cm².

Marking tip

Give credit for each step, as the exam does: the right formula, the numbers substituted (radius, not diameter), the calculation, then rounding and units (cm³ for volume, cm² for area). Count the faces before adding.
