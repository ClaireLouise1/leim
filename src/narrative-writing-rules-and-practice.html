Narrative writing

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): W1, W3, W7, W9. Play the game: leim.ie/story-spine.html

1. Plan in five minutes: the story spine

StepWhat it does
Once upon a time…who, where, and what they want or fear
Every day…normal life, before anything happens
But one day…one problem or event changes everything
Because of that…what it leads to: the tension builds (can repeat)
Until finally…the ending: the problem is solved
Once upon a time, Fionn was afraid of the ball. Every day, he hid at the back of the pitch. But one day, the goalkeeper was sick. Because of that, Fionn had to play in goal in the final. Until finally, Fionn saved a penalty.
When you write, the spine words disappear and each step grows into a paragraph.

2. One problem

A short story has room for one problem and two or three characters. If an event has nothing to do with the problem, cut it.

3. Who's telling the story?

First personthe narrator is in the story: I, me, my, we, us, our
My heart thumped as I opened the envelope.
Third personthe narrator tells us about others: he, she, they, a name
Tom's heart thumped as he opened the envelope.

4. Keep it steady

Choose past (I ran) or present (I run) tense, and first or third person, and keep them all the way through.
Not: I opened the door and I see a fox. She grabs the brush.

5. Pay it off

The ending solves the problem you set up. Plan it before you start. Avoid It was all a dream and endings that come from nowhere.
Classic mistakes: no plan, so the story runs out of steam · too many events · switching tense · switching from I to she · an ending that has nothing to do with the problem.
Exam tip: spend five minutes on a spine before you write, and five minutes at the end reading it over for tense and narrator slips.

A. Story spine

  1. Put this story spine in order.

    A Until finally, Sammy became the best hunter in the whole harbour, and the fishermen cheered him.

    B Once upon a time, an old seal called Sammy lived under the pier in Howth.

    C But one day, a storm wrecked the fishing boats and the scraps stopped.

    D Every day, Sammy waited for the fishermen to throw him scraps.

    E Because of that, Sammy had to learn to catch his own dinner.

  2. Circle the line that fills the gap.

    1 Once upon a time, a tortoise called Speedy lived in a classroom in Galway.

    2 Every day, Speedy watched the children from his glass tank.

    3 ? But one day, …

    4 Because of that, Speedy set off on a slow adventure across the classroom floor.

    5 Until finally, the children found Speedy asleep in the teacher's handbag.

      a) But one day, someone left the lid of the tank open.
      b) But one day, a poster at the shop announced a charity bike race.
      c) But one day, the drummer in the youth band broke her wrist before the concert.
  3. Circle the line that fills the gap.

    1 Once upon a time, Leah played the drums so loudly that the neighbours complained.

    2 Every day, Leah practised the drums in the shed for two hours.

    3 But one day, the drummer in the youth band broke her wrist before the concert.

    4 Because of that, the band asked Leah to play at the concert.

    5 ? Until finally, …

      a) Until finally, Leah's loud drumming got the whole hall dancing.
      b) Until finally, the hens felt safe and laid more eggs than ever.
      c) Until finally, Bran led every sheep home and never slept on the job again.

B. Cut it

  1. The Lost Dog: which event doesn't belong?

    1 Max the dog runs off on Inch beach.

    2 My sister starts a new job in a bank.

    3 We search the sand dunes until dark.

    4 A lifeguard hears barking in the dunes.

    5 Max is found asleep in a rowing boat.

  2. The Painting: which event doesn't belong?

    1 Darragh paints a picture for the art competition.

    2 The swimming pool closes for the summer.

    3 His little brother spills juice on the painting.

    4 Darragh turns the juice stains into flowers.

    5 His painting wins first prize at the competition.

D. Keep it steady: circle the best next line (same tense, same narrator)

  1. We hear a knock at the window.
    a) We freeze under the blankets.   b) They freeze under the blankets.   c) They froze under the blankets.   d) We froze under the blankets.

    a) She saw a fox in the garden.   b) I saw a fox in the garden.   c) She sees a fox in the garden.   d) I see a fox in the garden.
  3. The lights go out in the hall.
    a) Someone screamed at the back.   b) Someone screams at the back.   c) I screamed at the back.   d) I scream at the back.

E. Pay it off: circle the ending that works

  1. The Power Cut: It was a stormy night in January. In the middle of our film, the power went out.
      a) In the end, my teacher turned into a frog and hopped away.
      b) In the end, we lit candles and Grandad told ghost stories until the power came back.
      c) Then I woke up. It was all a dream.
      d) In the end, a farmer fixed the tyre with his puncture kit, and we cycled on.
  2. The Cat on the Roof: It was a windy Sunday in our estate. Our cat climbed onto the roof and was too scared to come down.
      a) In the end, the whole town packed up and went on holiday to Spain.
      b) Then I woke up. It was all a dream.
      c) In the end, we patched the hole with tape and slept in our coats.
      d) In the end, Dad got the ladder and carried the cat down in a basket.
