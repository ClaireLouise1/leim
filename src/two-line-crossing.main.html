Simultaneous equations

Spot the pattern

Here are some pairs of numbers that fit each equation. Is there a pair that fits both?

x + y = 5
x01234
y54321
y = 2x − 1
x01234
y−11357
Show me

x = 2, y = 3 is in both tables. Each equation on its own has lots of answers, but only one pair fits both at the same time. That pair is the solution of the simultaneous equations. On a graph, it's the point where the two lines cross, (2, 3):

Method 1: elimination

Make the x's or the y's the same size, then add or subtract so one letter disappears.

Signs different → add① 3x + 2y = 16
② x − 2y = 0
① + ②: 4x = 16, so x = 4
Put x = 4 in ①: 12 + 2y = 16 → y = 2
Signs the same → subtract① 5x + y = 17
② 2x + y = 8
① − ②: 3x = 9, so x = 3
Put x = 3 in ②: 6 + y = 8 → y = 2
Multiply first① 2x + 3y = 12
② 5x − 2y = 11
① × 2: 4x + 6y = 24
② × 3: 15x − 6y = 33
Add: 19x = 57, so x = 3, y = 2

Always check in the equation you didn't use: 5x − 2y = 15 − 4 = 11 ✓

Method 2: substitution

When one equation already says y = … (or x = …), put it into the other equation. Keep the bracket.

① y = 2x − 3
② 3x + 2y = 15
3x + 2(2x − 3) = 15
3x + 4x − 6 = 15 → 7x = 21 → x = 3
y = 2 × 3 − 3 = 3. Check ②: 9 + 6 = 15 ✓

Method 3: a graph

Draw both lines. The solution is the point where they cross. Read it off, then check it in both equations. Revise drawing lines with Line Lab.

Word problems

  • Say what x and y stand for: x = number of adult tickets, y = number of child tickets.
  • Make one equation from each fact. 120 tickets: x + y = 120. Adults €8, children €5, takings €801: 8x + 5y = 801.
  • Solve, then answer the question in words: "67 adult tickets and 53 child tickets."
  • Check it makes sense: whole numbers of tickets, and 8 × 67 + 5 × 53 = 801 ✓

Higher Level: fractions

  • Fractions in the equation? Multiply every term by the common denominator first: x/2 + y/3 = 4 becomes 3x + 2y = 24 (× 6).
  • Answers can be fractions: 2x + 4y = 7 and 2x − 2y = 1 give y = 1 and x = 3/2.
The classic mistakes: adding when the signs are the same (you subtract); forgetting to subtract the numbers on the right as well; multiplying only the x term when you multiply an equation (multiply every term); dropping the bracket in substitution (2(2x − 3) is 4x − 6, not 4x − 3); a pair that fits only one equation; stopping after x and not finding y; not checking in the other equation.
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Simultaneous equations come up in most papers, often as "solve" with two equations, sometimes inside a word problem (tickets, prices, two numbers) or with a graph, where you read the crossing point and then check it with algebra. Higher Level can add fractions in the equations or answers that are fractions.

How marks are won and lost

  • Show every step: label the equations ① and ②, write what you did ("① × 2", "① + ②").
  • Find both letters. An answer with only x loses marks.
  • Check in the other equation and write the check down.
  • In a word problem, say what x and y stand for and answer in words, with units.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first.

  1. Elimination. (a) Solve 3x + 2y = 16 and x − 2y = 0. (b) Solve 2x + 3y = 12 and 5x − 2y = 11. Check each answer.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) Add: 4x = 16, so x = 4. Then 4 − 2y = 0, so y = 2. Check: 12 + 4 = 16 ✓
    (b) Multiply the first by 2 and the second by 3: 4x + 6y = 24 and 15x − 6y = 33. Add: 19x = 57, so x = 3. Then 6 + 3y = 12, so y = 2. Check: 15 − 4 = 11 ✓

  2. Two lines. The lines l and k are drawn on the grid.
    (a) Show that l is the line x − 2y = −2 and k is the line 2x + y = 6. (b) Use the graph to solve the simultaneous equations x − 2y = −2 and 2x + y = 6. (c) Solve them with algebra to check your answer.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) l goes through (0, 1) and (2, 2): 0 − 2 = −2 ✓ and 2 − 4 = −2 ✓. k goes through (0, 6) and (3, 0): 0 + 6 = 6 ✓ and 6 + 0 = 6 ✓.
    (b) The lines cross at (2, 2): x = 2, y = 2.
    (c) Multiply the second by 2: 4x + 2y = 12. Add to the first: 5x = 10, so x = 2. Then 4 + y = 6, so y = 2 ✓

  3. Concert tickets. 250 tickets were sold for a school concert. Adult tickets cost €10 and student tickets cost €6. The takings were €2,060. (a) Write two equations, saying what x and y stand for. (b) How many of each ticket were sold? (c) Check your answer.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) x = adult tickets, y = student tickets: x + y = 250 and 10x + 6y = 2060.
    (b) Multiply the first by 6: 6x + 6y = 1500. Subtract it from the second: 4x = 560, so x = 140 and y = 110: 140 adult and 110 student tickets.
    (c) 140 + 110 = 250 ✓ and 1400 + 660 = 2060 ✓

  4. Substitution and fractions. (a) Solve y = 2x − 3 and 3x + 2y = 15. (b) Higher Level: solve x/2 + y/3 = 4 and x − y = 3. (c) Higher Level: solve 2x + 4y = 7 and 2x − 2y = 1.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 3x + 2(2x − 3) = 15 → 7x − 6 = 15 → x = 3, y = 2 × 3 − 3 = 3. Check: 9 + 6 = 15 ✓
    (b) Multiply the first by 6: 3x + 2y = 24. From the second, x = y + 3: 3(y + 3) + 2y = 24 → 5y = 15 → y = 3, x = 6. Check: 3 + 1 = 4 ✓
    (c) Subtract: 6y = 6, so y = 1. Then 2x − 2 = 1, so 2x = 3 and x = 3/2. Check: 3 + 4 = 7 ✓
