Drama 1: how plays work

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R6, R7, R9, OL8

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can read a script (character names, dialogue, stage directions), say what a stage direction tells the actor, name the five backstage jobs, choose lighting and sound to suit a mood, name the conflict in a scene, and explain a staging choice.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Backstage Sort cards (cut-up) · p.3 Director's Chair A/B (speaking) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Answer + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 2nd year. Time: about 60 minutes. Works with any studied play: all scenes are made up.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Two volunteers say "I'm fine." twice: once (smiling, hugging her mam), once (slamming the bedroom door). Same words: what changed? Introduce stage directions.
8 minGuided discovery. Read the NUALA / DAN script on the free sheet. Label character names, dialogue, stage directions. Sort directions into say it / move / do / face. Draw the stage map (upstage, downstage, stage left and right from the actor's view).
12 minBackstage Sort (p.2, groups of 3, one set of 20 cards). Sort the production notes into set, costume, lighting, sound and props. Then each group adds one note of its own for a scene from the class play.
12 minDirector's Chair (p.3, pairs). A reads three scenes; B picks the lighting and sound and says why. Then "Say it like this": A acts out three lines with a secret stage direction; B names the feeling. Swap.
18 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Answer in pairs, then the exam-style questions on "The Empty Chair" alone.
5 minExit ticket. One key moment from your class play: one lighting choice, one sound, one stage direction, each with a reason. Homework: play Stage It at leim.ie/stage-it.html.
Errors to look for: quoting dialogue only and ignoring the stage directions · "good lighting" with no specific effect or reason · retelling the plot · mixing up stage left and right (it's the actor's left) · calling every disagreement "conflict" without saying who or what is in the way. Support: the poster (p.4) on the desk. Fast finishers: write a 6-line scene with three stage directions and swap it with a partner to stage.

B. Exam-style questions: "The Empty Chair"

THE EMPTY CHAIR (an extract from an imaginary play)
(A farmhouse kitchen on Christmas Eve. Warm, golden light. Paper chains hang from the ceiling. MAM sets the table. JACK, fifteen, stares at the empty chair at the end.)
JACK: (bitterly) You've set a place for him again.
MAM: (still smiling, not looking up) He might come.
JACK: (picking up the plate) He said that last year.
MAM: (stopping, her hands shaking) Put it back, Jack.
(A car's headlights sweep across the window. They both freeze.)
  1. Where and when is this scene set? What mood do the opening stage directions create?
  2. What is the conflict between Jack and Mam?
  3. What does "(stopping, her hands shaking)" tell us about Mam?
  4. You are directing. Choose the lighting and one sound for the final moment and explain.
  5. Would this scene work well on stage? Give two reasons.

A. Fix the Answer

The play is set in a room.Be precise and use the stage directions: It is set in a small ferry waiting room on an island, late in the evening, in a storm.
The stage directions are in brackets.Say what they tell us, not what they look like: "(her hands shaking)" shows that Mam is frightened.
There is a lot of conflict in the scene.Name it and say who or what is in the way: Person vs person: Jack is angry with Mam for still hoping Dad will come home.
I would use good lighting.Choose a specific effect and give a reason: A spotlight on the empty chair, to show that everyone is thinking about the missing father.
Then Jack picks up the plate and then Mam says put it back and then the lights come.Don't retell the plot. Pick one moment and explain why it works on stage.
Mam is sad.Back it up with evidence from the script: Mam is hopeful but fragile: she is "still smiling" but her hands shake when Jack takes the plate.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. A farmhouse kitchen on Christmas Eve. "Warm, golden light" and "Paper chains" create a cosy, festive mood, which makes the empty chair stand out even more.
  2. Person vs person: Jack is angry and hurt that Mam still sets a place for his father ("You've set a place for him again"); Mam still hopes he will come ("He might come").
  3. Mam is not as calm as her smile suggests: she is upset and afraid of losing hope. Her hands give her away.
  4. E.g. the warm light dims to one spotlight on the empty chair as the headlights sweep the room; the sound of a car engine stopping, then silence. It leaves the audience wondering if the father has come home.
  5. Any two reasons, e.g. strong conflict between two characters; a powerful prop (the plate, the empty chair); a cliffhanger ending with the headlights.

Marking tip

Reward answers that use stage directions as evidence and give a reason for every staging choice. Our guide, not an official marking scheme.
