Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

Persuasive writing and speeches

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): W3, OL5, OL7, OL10, R8

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can name and explain six persuasive techniques, back up a point with evidence, answer the other side politely, and end a speech with a clear call to action.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Technique Sort cards (cut-up) · p.3 Debate Swap A/B (speaking) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Speech + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 2nd year. Time: about 60 minutes. All texts are original; names, places and survey numbers are made up.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Read two openings for a skate-park speech: "Today I'm going to talk about the skate park. It would be good." and "Where can a teenager go in this town on a Saturday?…" (the game's "Spot the pattern"). Which would make the council listen? Why?
8 minGuided discovery. Students find three things the strong opening does. Elicit and name: rhetorical question, statistic, direct address. Add rule of three, emotive language, repetition with one example each.
12 minTechnique Sort (p.2, groups of 3, one set of 24 cards). Sort the lines into six groups of four. Then each group picks its favourite line and explains the effect.
12 minDebate Swap (p.3, pairs). A reads a point from the other side; B finds and reads the answer that deals with it; then swap. Together they underline the linking word.
18 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Speech in pairs, then the exam-style questions on the bike-shed speech alone.
5 minExit ticket. Write a one-line call to action for a speech asking for a longer lunch break. Homework: play Persuasion Power at leim.ie/persuasion-power.html.
Errors to look for: opinions with no reason ("it would be good") · made-up facts presented as official · insulting the other side · agreeing with the other side by mistake · a flat ending ("That's all, thanks") · naming a technique with no quote or explanation. Support: the poster (p.4) on the desk. Fast finishers: write the other side's speech against the bike shed.

B. Exam-style questions: "A Bike Shed for Kilbrook Community School"

Good morning, everyone. How many of you cycled to school today? In our class survey, only 4 of 28 students did, and most of the others said there is nowhere safe to lock a bike. A covered bike shed would be cheap, simple and safe. Some people say bikes would be stolen anyway. However, bikes locked in a shed beside the office would be in full view of staff all day. Think of the traffic at the gate every morning. Think of your own fitness. Please sign our petition at lunchtime and help us build the shed before spring. Thank you for listening.
  1. Who is the audience for this speech, and what is its purpose?
  2. Find a rhetorical question and a statistic in the speech.
  3. Quote the rule of three and explain why it works.
  4. How does the speaker deal with the other side's point?
  5. Is the ending effective? Explain your answer.

A. Fix the Speech

Today I'm going to talk about why we need a new library.Weak hook (it just announces the topic): Where in this town can you study in peace? Nowhere.
We should get a pool because it would be good.No evidence: A pool would let every first year learn to swim, which means safer summers at the beach.
Anyone who disagrees with me is an idiot.An insult wins nobody over: Some people may disagree, so let me explain why I think they should think again.
Loads of kids, like, really want it!!Slang in a formal speech: Many students have told us that they want it.
Some people say it costs too much. They are right.Agrees with the other side: Some people say it costs too much. However, local businesses could help to pay for it.
That's all I have to say. Thanks.Weak ending: Please sign our petition today and help us make it happen. Thank you for listening.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. Students (and staff) at the school assembly; to persuade them to support a covered bike shed and sign the petition.
  2. Rhetorical question: "How many of you cycled to school today?" Statistic: "only 4 of 28 students".
  3. "cheap, simple and safe": three short words sound complete and easy to remember, and they answer the main worries (cost, effort, safety).
  4. It states the other side fairly ("Some people say bikes would be stolen anyway"), then answers it with "However" and a reason (bikes locked beside the office would be in view of staff all day).
  5. Yes: it is a clear call to action (sign the petition, when and where) with a goal ("before spring"), and it thanks the audience politely. Repetition just before it ("Think of…") builds up to it.

Marking tip

Reward a technique named + quoted + explained over a list of names. In writing, reward evidence for each point and a fair answer to the other side. Our guide, not an official marking scheme.
