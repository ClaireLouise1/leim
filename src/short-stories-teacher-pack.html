Junior Cycle English · Teacher pack

Short stories

↗ Léim

Learning outcomes (2015 specification): R6, R8, R9, W7

Aims

By the end of the lesson students can name the setting, problem, climax and ending of a short story, recognise a strong first line (dialogue, action, mystery), find the clue words that show a setting, and tell a fair twist (set up by a clue) from a cheat ending.

In this pack

p.1 Teacher notes · p.2 Story Jigsaw cards (cut-up) · p.3 Twist Swap A/B (speaking + listening) · p.4 Wall poster · p.5 Worksheet: Fix the Story + exam-style questions · p.6 Answers. Level: 1st year (useful for any short story on your course). Time: about 60 minutes. All stories are original.

Procedure

5 minLead-in. Read three lines: A parcel arrived. It ticked quietly. Grandad had been talking about his broken alarm clock. Give two endings: an alien bomb / a new alarm clock Grandad ordered. Which is the better surprise? Why?
8 minGuided discovery. Elicit the four parts (setting, problem, climax, ending) and the signal words for each. Then the three kinds of first line, and the rule for a twist: surprising, but set up by a clue.
12 minStory Jigsaw (p.2, groups of 3–4, one set of 24 cards). Rebuild the 6 mini-stories in order: setting, problem, climax, ending. Fast groups: choose one story and write a twist ending for it.
15 minTwist Swap (p.3, pairs). A reads the story aloud and stops before the ending; B fills the grid and guesses the ending; A reads the real ending; B finds the clue. Swap.
15 minWorksheet (p.5). Fix the Story in pairs, then the exam-style questions on "The Last Bus" alone.
5 minExit ticket. Write a first line and a planted clue for a story called "The Locked Box". Homework: play Twist Detector at leim.ie/twist-detector.html.
Errors to look for: retelling the plot instead of naming the part · calling any surprise a twist · "It was all a dream" · naming the setting without quoting clue words · too many characters in students' own stories. Support: the poster (p.4) on the desk; give weaker groups the 6 setting cards of the jigsaw first. Fast finishers: rewrite one jigsaw story with a mystery first line.

Student A · Twist Swap

1. Read your story aloud twice, slowly. Stop before the ending. Let your partner guess the ending, then read it out.

"The Haunted Classroom"
It was a dark November evening, and Jamie had stayed late at school to finish a project. On the way in, he had noticed a mop and bucket outside Room 9. Then the lights in the corridor went out, and the door of Room 9 was locked. Suddenly, a low moaning sound came from inside, and the handle began to turn.

B. Exam-style questions: "The Last Bus"

It was a foggy Friday night in Skibbereen, and Niamh had just missed the last bus home. Her phone was dead, and the only other person at the stop was an old man in a long grey coat. He smiled and said, “Don't worry. Someone always comes for the Friday girl.” Just then, headlights cut through the fog, and a battered red van pulled up beside them. The driver leaned out, and Niamh gasped. It was her grandad, who drove the hospital night shift and passed the stop every Friday.
  1. Where and when is the story set? Quote two words or phrases that help you picture it.
  2. What is Niamh's problem?
  3. Which sentence is the climax? Explain your choice.
  4. The old man's words are a clue. What do they set up?
  5. Write a different twist ending for the story, set up by a clue that is already in the text.

Twist Swap

The Haunted Classroom: Setting: dark November evening · Problem: Then the lights in the corridor went out, and the door of Room 9 was locked. · Climax: Suddenly, a low moaning sound came from inside, and the handle began to turn. · Clue: On the way in, he had noticed a mop and bucket outside Room 9. · Twist: It turned out the cleaner had been singing along to her headphones while she mopped.

The Footprints: Setting: frosty January morning · Problem: The footprints led to the shed, but the shed door was locked from the inside. · Climax: All at once, the door creaked open and something with claws stepped out. · Clue: Her little brother had asked for giant slippers with claws for his birthday. · Twist: It turned out to be her brother, wearing his brand-new monster slippers.

A. Fix the Story

“This is a story about a boy called Jack who went to the beach.”Flat opening. Start with dialogue, action or mystery: Jack ran straight into the waves, still wearing his runners.
“We were at the beach. It was nice.”Setting told, not shown. Use clue words: Gulls screamed and the wet sand squelched under our feet.
A story with eleven characters in two pages.Too many characters for a short story. Keep two or three and give each one a job.
“…and then I woke up. It was all a dream.”A cheat ending: nothing was at stake. Plan a real ending and set it up.
“It turned out the teacher was an alien.” (no clue anywhere)An ending from nowhere. Plant a clue early, e.g. Ms Doyle never ate lunch and her eyes glowed in the dark.
A whole page of the friends chatting, and nothing goes wrong.No problem, so no story. Give the characters something to solve.

B. Exam-style questions

  1. A bus stop in Skibbereen on a foggy Friday night. "foggy Friday night", "headlights cut through the fog", "battered red van" (any two).
  2. She has missed the last bus home, her phone is dead and she is alone at the stop with a stranger.
  3. "Just then, headlights cut through the fog…": "Just then" signals the big moment, and the problem is about to be solved.
  4. That someone comes every Friday: the stranger already knows the grandad passes the stop, so the ending makes sense when you look back.
  5. Credit an ending that follows on, a surprise, and a clue already in the text (the grey coat, "the Friday girl", the dead phone).

Marking tip

In reading answers, reward a clear point + a short quote + a sentence on its effect. In students' own stories, reward a strong first line, one clear problem and an ending that was planned, more than length. Our guide, not an official marking scheme.
