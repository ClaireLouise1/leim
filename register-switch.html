Register & tone

Register is how formal your words are. Tone is the feeling in them. Both change with your reader (who is it for?) and your purpose (why are you writing or speaking?).

Spot the pattern

📱 Text to a friendMatch's off Saturday, pitch is flooded. Gutted!! See ya Monday.
📧 Email to the principalI am writing to let you know that Saturday's match has been cancelled because the pitch is flooded.
📻 Announcement for listenersGood morning. We regret that Saturday's under-15 match in Kanturk has been cancelled due to a flooded pitch.

The same news, three times. What stays the same? What changes? Look at the short forms, the “!!”, the greetings and how much detail each reader is given.

Show me

The facts stay the same: no match, flooded pitch. The register changes. The text uses short forms (Match's), slang (Gutted, See ya) and “!!”: fine for a friend. The email uses full words (I am writing, has been cancelled) and gives the reason politely. The announcement is formal too, and adds the details strangers need (under-15, Kanturk).

Who is it for?

📨 FormalA principal, a manager, an editor, the council, an audience, judges, anyone you don't know. Full words (I am, I would like), polite, no slang.
😊 InformalFriends, family, your team chat. Short forms (I'm, can't), chatty words, “!” are fine.
🎯 PurposeTo complain, to ask, to thank, to persuade, to entertain. The purpose decides how much detail and how firm you are.

Polite or blunt?

🙄 BluntAn order: Give me… Move! I want… now!
😊 PoliteA question: Could you…? Would you mind…? Excuse me… Sorry… Thank you.
💡 Watch out“Move, please!” is still an order. Turn it into a question: “Excuse me, could I get past?”

Tone

😄 Cheerfulbrilliant, thrilled, can't wait
😢 Sadmiss, lonely, tears, goodbye
😠 Angryfurious, fed up, unacceptable, slammed
😟 Worriedwhat if, nervous, anxious, afraid

To name a tone in the exam, quote the words that create it: “The word ‘furious’ shows an angry tone.”

Slang to swap in formal writing

deadly / epic / awesome→ excellent, very enjoyable
loads / tons→ many, a lot of
wrecked→ exhausted, badly damaged
gonna / wanna→ going to, want to
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Every writing task names a reader and a purpose: a talk to your class, a letter to a newspaper, a blog for teenagers, a speech to the council. The marks reward writing that suits that reader. Reading questions also ask about the tone of a text or who it is aimed at, and how you can tell.

How marks are won and lost

  • Name the reader and purpose before you write: it decides formal or informal.
  • Stay in one register: one “gonna” in a speech to the council loses marks.
  • Tone questions: name the tone, then quote the words that create it and explain.
  • Polite but firm is the best tone for a complaint: no insults, no “!!”.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). "Where the marks would go" is our guide, not an official marking scheme.

  1. Your school's sports day has been moved to Friday. Write it as (a) a text to your friend and (b) an announcement for the school intercom.
    Show a model answer

    (a) Sports day's moved to Friday!! Bring your runners, it's gonna be class.
    (b) Good morning, everyone. Please note that sports day has been moved to Friday, 12 June. All students should bring a PE kit and a water bottle.
    Where the marks would go: the same facts in both; short forms, slang and “!!” in the text only; full words, a greeting and the details everyone needs in the announcement.

  2. Read the two lines below. Which is more suitable for a letter to a newspaper? Give two reasons.
    A: The new car park is a total joke, like, nobody wanted it!!
    B: I wish to express my concern about the new car park, which few residents asked for.
    Show a model answer

    Line B. (1) It uses full, formal words (“I wish to express my concern”), while A uses slang (“total joke”, “like”). (2) It is polite but firm and makes the point calmly; A uses “!!” and exaggerates (“nobody”).
    Where the marks would go: the right choice, plus two reasons, each backed up with words from the lines.

  3. Rewrite this note to a neighbour so that it is polite but still clear: “Stop parking in front of our gate. We can't get the car out!!”
    Show a model answer

    Sorry to bother you, but would you mind not parking in front of our gate? We sometimes can't get our car out in the mornings. Thank you.
    Where the marks would go: the order turned into a polite request (would you mind…?); softening words (Sorry to bother you, Thank you); the reason kept; no “!!”.

  4. What is the tone of this passage? Quote two words that show it.
    Orla checked her phone again. Still nothing. What if the ferry had turned back? She paced the pier, her stomach in knots, and kept thinking about the storm warning.
    Show a model answer

    A worried (anxious) tone. “What if” shows she imagines the worst; “kept thinking” and “stomach in knots” show she can't stop worrying.
    Where the marks would go: one mark for naming the tone; a mark for each quote with a short explanation of how it creates the tone.
