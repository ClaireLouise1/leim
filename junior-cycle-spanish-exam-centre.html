The paper explained

Junior Cycle Spanish is one written paper, at one level for everyone, lasting up to 2 hours in June of 3rd year. It is marked out of 360. There is no Higher or Ordinary choice: every student sits the same paper.

The big message: listening and reading together are worth almost 80% of the paper. You win this exam by understanding Spanish, not just by writing it.

How to use your time

  • Listening: the recordings run at a fixed pace. Use every pause to read the next question.
  • After the listening, split the time left roughly: about 55% on Reading, 35% on Writing, and the last 10% checking.
  • Never leave a question blank. A sensible guess can earn marks; a blank earns none.

Answer in the right language

Each question tells you whether to answer in English, in Spanish, or by ticking, matching or writing a number or letter. Read the instruction first: a correct answer in the wrong form can lose marks.

A Distinction needs 306 out of 360 (85%), and every 18 marks is 5% of the grade. These are the new grade bands used since the 2025 exams.

Listening and Reading (280 marks)

Marks go to correct details. Most questions want one specific fact: a time, a price, a place, a reason, a feeling.

  • Give exactly what is asked. If the question asks for two things, give two. A wrong extra can cancel out a right one.
  • Be precise. "In the afternoon" is not "at 4 o'clock". Numbers, days, times and prices must be exact.
  • Don't contradict yourself. Two different answers "just in case" usually earn nothing.

Writing (80 marks)

Marks come from two things: doing the whole task, and the quality of your Spanish.

  • Cover every point. If the task lists four things, mention all four, clearly. A missed point can't be won back with lovely Spanish elsewhere.
  • Get the message across. The reader should understand everything first time.
  • Show some range. More than one tense where it fits, opinions with reasons, and linking words like porque, pero, también, además.
  • Keep it accurate. Simple and correct beats complicated and confusing.

Exam strategies

The students who score highest prepare for listening every week, not just in May.

Listening: 140 marks

  1. Read ahead. In every pause, read the next question and underline the key word: when, where, how much, why.
  2. Predict. A time? Listen for numbers. A feeling? Listen for words like contento, nervioso, cansado.
  3. Note first, answer later. Jot short notes the first time; write the full answer on the next play.
  4. Watch for traps. Speakers change their mind: "a las cinco… no, mejor a las seis". The last answer is usually the right one.
  5. Practise little and often. Ten minutes of Spanish audio a day beats two hours the week before.

Words that catch students out: numbers above 20, days and months, times (y cuarto, menos cuarto, y media), weather, food, school subjects, places in town.

Reading: 140 marks

  1. Read the question before the text, so you know what to hunt for.
  2. Skim, then scan: the general idea first, then the exact detail.
  3. Use the clues: titles, pictures, prices and layout.
  4. Watch for negatives: no, nunca, nada, nadie turn the meaning upside down.

Writing: 80 marks

  1. Tick off each point. Number the points in the task and tick each one as you cover it.
  2. Use the right layout. An email needs a greeting and a sign-off; a text message is short and friendly.
  3. Use your toolkit: ready phrases for opinions, reasons, time words and sign-offs.
  4. Use three tenses where they fit: past (fui), present (voy), future (voy a ir).
  5. Leave 3 minutes to check verb endings, adjective endings (-o/-a) and accents.

The mistakes that cost the most marks

  • Leaving answers blank.
  • Answering in the wrong language.
  • Giving extra answers that contradict a right one.
  • Missing a point in the writing task.
  • Running out of time on one hard question. Move on and come back.

Speaking: the oral CBA

There is no speaking in the June exam. Speaking is assessed in class, in the Oral Communication CBA: about 3–4 minutes in Spanish (a presentation, an interview, a role-play or a conversation about a picture), with the teacher's questions. For exams in 2027 and 2028 it's done by the end of 2nd year and it's the one compulsory Spanish CBA. It gets a descriptor on the JCPA, not marks, and it doesn't change the exam grade. It still pays off in June: students who speak Spanish regularly find the listening (39% of the exam) much easier.

Model answer

An original Léim writing task (not an exam question) with a top-grade answer and the reasons it scores.

Text message: invite a friend out

Task: Write a text message to your Spanish friend Lucía. (1) Invite her to the cinema on Saturday. (2) Say the time and where to meet. (3) Suggest something to do afterwards. (4) Ask her to reply.

¡Hola Lucía! ¿Qué tal? ¿Quieres ir al cine conmigo el sábado? Hay una película de acción nueva que parece genial. Podemos quedar a las cinco en la puerta del cine, en la calle principal. Después, si quieres, podemos ir a comer pizza. ¡Contéstame pronto! Un abrazo, Aoife

Why it scores:

  • All four points covered, in order: invite, time and place, afterwards, reply.
  • Short and friendly, which is right for a text.
  • Gives a reason (parece genial) and uses si quieres, a natural, polite touch.

Phrases to steal: ¿Quieres…? · Podemos quedar a las… · Después… · ¡Contéstame pronto!

For parents

Your child's Spanish grade comes almost entirely from one June exam, and you can help even if you don't speak Spanish.

How the grade works now

  • The June exam gives the state grade: Distinction, Higher Merit, Merit, Achieved, Partially Achieved or Not Graded (see Where the marks are).
  • Classroom-Based Assessments (CBAs) are done in class and graded by the teacher. Students sitting the exam in 2027 and 2028 complete at least one CBA (most schools do one, and it must be the oral one), and the written Assessment Task is not examined (gov.ie). The CBA result appears on the Junior Cycle Profile of Achievement, separately from the exam grade. CBAs explained, with examples.
  • There is one level. Every student sits the same paper.

How to help at home (no Spanish needed)

  1. Ten minutes of listening a day. Spanish songs, cartoons or YouTube with Spanish subtitles all count. Listening is 39% of the exam.
  2. Ask your child to teach you five new words at dinner. Explaining is one of the best ways to remember.
  3. Time a past paper. Download one from examinations.ie and let your child do one section against the clock.
  4. Check the writing checklist. After a practice writing task, ask: "Did you cover every point? Show me where."
  5. Keep it calm. Short, regular practice beats long, stressful cramming. The free games are a good 10-minute start.
