Look at the verbs in the second column. Have you seen those forms before? Who do they usually mean? Think about it, then check.

Show me

Spanish has two ways to say "you" to one person. Tú is friendly: friends, family, people your age, children, pets. Usted is polite: adults you don't know, older people, officials, formal emails. Usted uses the él / ella form of the verb (está, se llama, es, tiene, puede), because it began as a title, like "Your Honour". Write it Ud. for short. Not sure? Use usted with adults: it's never rude.

The teacher's instructions end in -d (abrid, escuchad, mirad) because they're for vosotros: the whole class. To one student: abre, escucha, mira.

Culture: in Spain, when you're introduced, friends and family usually greet with dos besos (two kisses, one on each cheek). Formal meetings: a handshake. Adults will often say tú to you because you're young, but you say usted to them until they say ¡Tutéame! (Call me tú!). In many Spanish secondary schools, students say tú to their teachers and call them by first name: in the exam, usted is always safe. Irish link: Irish and English don't have a polite "you"; Irish has tú (one person) and sibh (more than one), just like Spanish tú and vosotros. Dia duit · Dia is Muire duit · Conas atá tú? = Hola · Hola · ¿Cómo estás?
Exam tip: In Listening, short meetings and introductions are a favourite opener: catch the name (it's often spelled out), the age (tengo trece or tengo treinta?), where they're from and how they feel. Usted, señor, señora and buenos días tell you it's a formal situation, like a hotel or a shop. In Writing, start a note or email to a friend with ¡Hola, Pablo! ¿Qué tal? and introduce yourself in three lines: Me llamo Ciara, tengo catorce años y soy de Cork. Vivo con mi familia en Douglas. Soy simpática y hablo inglés e irlandés. Finish with ¡Hasta pronto!
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Greetings and introductions are how most Listening dialogues start: someone meets a receptionist, a host family or a new friend, and you have to catch the name (often spelled out), the age, where they are from and how they feel. In Writing, every email and letter (Q17 and Q18) opens with a greeting and closes with a sign-off, and many ask you to introduce yourself. There is no speaking in the June exam, but the Oral CBA starts the same way: Buenos días, me llamo…

How marks are won and lost

  • Answer in the language asked. Listening and Reading questions are usually answered in English (or by ticking): write 14 or fourteen, not catorce.
  • Spelled names: write the letters down as you hear them. Know the tricky ones: ge = G, jota = J, i = I, e = E, erre = R, uve = V.
  • Formal or informal? usted, señor, señora, le, buenos días = formal. tú, ¿qué tal?, hola = informal. Give the clue word as your reason.
  • Traps: soy de (where you're from) and vivo en (where you live) can be two different places.
  • In Writing, cover every point, then check tengo … años (never soy) and -o / -a endings (encantado / encantada).

  1. Listening 1: at a hotel in Seville. You will hear a conversation at a hotel reception. Answer in English.
    Recepcionista and Cliente
    1. How many nights is the booking for?
    2. Write down the surname she spells.
    3. Where is she from? Give two details.
    4. What is her room number?
    5. Is this conversation formal or informal? Give one word that tells you.
    Show a model answer

    (a) Three nights. (b) Brennan. (c) Galway, in the west of Ireland. (d) Room 14. (e) Formal: the receptionist says usted (also: señora, le, su, buenas tardes).
    Where the marks would go: one mark for each detail; the number must be exact (14, not 4 or 40); the surname must be spelled right; for (e), "formal" plus a clue word from the conversation.

  2. Listening 2: first day at a summer camp. Two teenagers meet on the first day of a summer camp in Spain. Answer in English.
    Javier and Niamh
    1. How does Niamh feel, and why?
    2. How old is Javier?
    3. Where is Javier from, and where does he live now?
    4. Who does Javier introduce, and what is he like?
    Show a model answer

    (a) A bit nervous, because it is her first day. (b) 15. (c) He is from Valencia but lives in Madrid. (d) His friend Raúl, who is very nice / friendly.
    Where the marks would go: one mark for each detail; (a) and (c) need both parts. The trap is (c): soy de and vivo en are different places. Javier is 15, not 14 (that is Niamh).

  3. Reading: a message from your exchange partner. Seán is going to Spain on an exchange. Read the message from his exchange partner, Lucía, and answer the questions in English.
    ¡Hola, Seán! ¿Qué tal? Me llamo Lucía y soy tu compañera de intercambio. Tengo trece años y vivo en Salamanca con mis padres y mi hermano pequeño.Mañana vamos juntos al instituto. Las clases empiezan a las ocho y media. Mi profesora de inglés se llama Carmen: es muy simpática, pero habla muy rápido. Si no entiendes, di: «¿Puede repetir, por favor?».En mi instituto, los alumnos hablan de tú a los profesores. ¡No te preocupes!¡Hasta mañana! Un abrazo, Lucía
    1. Who does Lucía live with?
    2. What time do classes start?
    3. Give two details about Carmen.
    4. What should Seán say if he doesn't understand?
    5. What does Lucía say about how students speak to teachers?
    Show a model answer

    (a) Her parents and her little brother. (b) At half past eight (8:30). (c) Any two: she is the English teacher; she is very nice; she speaks very fast. (d) "Can you repeat that, please?" (e) Students say tú to their teachers (they speak to them informally).
    Where the marks would go: one mark for each detail. In (a), "brother" alone is not enough if the question has two parts: give both. pequeño here means younger / little.

  4. Writing: an email to your exchange partner (like Q17). You are going on an exchange to Spain. Write an email in Spanish to your exchange partner, Lucía. Include all five points:
    1. Greet her and say your name and age.
    2. Say where you are from and where you live.
    3. Describe yourself (one or two details).
    4. Say which languages you speak.
    5. Ask her two questions, and sign off.
    Show a model answer

    ¡Hola, Lucía! ¿Qué tal? Me llamo Seán y tengo catorce años. Soy irlandés, de Cork, y vivo en Ballincollig con mi familia. Soy simpático y un poco tímido. Hablo inglés e irlandés, y ahora estudio español. ¿Tienes hermanos? ¿Qué te gusta hacer los fines de semana? ¡Hasta pronto! Un abrazo, Seán
    Where the marks would go: every one of the five points covered (a missed point can't be made up elsewhere); the message clear first time; correct verbs (me llamo, tengo, soy, vivo, hablo); e instead of y before irlandés; questions with ¿ ?; a friendly greeting and sign-off.
