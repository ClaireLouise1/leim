Spot the pattern: nationalities

Look at the table. What letter do nationalities start with in Spanish? What happens when the masculine form ends in a consonant (español, irlandés)? Which one never changes for a girl? Think about it, then check.

Show me

1. Nationalities and languages take no capital letter in Spanish: Soy irlandesa. Hablo inglés. Countries and cities do: Irlanda, Cork. 2. -o words change to -a for a girl (peruano → peruana). 3. If the masculine ends in a consonant, add -a (español → española), and the accent disappears because the stress is now in the normal place (irlandés → irlandesa, inglés → inglesa). 4. Words ending in -e are the same for boys and girls (costarricense, estadounidense). 5. A group of boys, or boys and girls together, uses the masculine plural: Mis primos (Pablo y Ana) son colombianos.

Talking about a festival

SpanishEnglish
Las Fallas se celebran en Valencia, en marzo.Las Fallas are celebrated in Valencia, in March.
San Fermín empieza el 6 de julio.San Fermín starts on 6 July.
La gente se viste de blanco y baila en la calle.People dress in white and dance in the street.
Es típico comer churros con chocolate.It's traditional to eat churros with chocolate.
Se come pan de muerto. · Se comen doce uvas.People eat "bread of the dead". · People eat twelve grapes.
Me gustaría ir a la Tomatina porque parece muy divertida.I'd like to go to La Tomatina because it looks great fun.

Watch out: la gente (people) is singular: la gente baila, not bailan. With se + verb, the verb agrees with the thing: se come una sopa / se comen doce uvas. Months take no capital letter: en marzo, el 6 de julio.

Comparing Spain and Ireland

PatternExampleEnglish
más + adjective + queEn España se cena más tarde que en Irlanda.In Spain people have dinner later than in Ireland.
menos + noun / adjective + queEn Irlanda hace menos calor que en España.In Ireland it's less hot than in Spain.
tan + adjective + comoLa paella es tan rica como la tortilla.Paella is as tasty as Spanish omelette.
mejor / peor queEl clima de Málaga es mejor que el clima de Cork.The weather in Málaga is better than in Cork.
mayor / menor queMi hermana es mayor que yo.My sister is older than me.

The adjective still agrees: Las playas de Cádiz son más bonitas que… Never say más bueno or más malo to compare quality: use mejor and peor. It's más… que but tan… como.

Culture: in Spain the main meal is around 2–3 pm and dinner is often after 9 pm. Children get presents from los Reyes Magos on 6 January, and at midnight on 31 December people eat doce uvas, one for each chime of the clock. Remember: one festival or habit is not "all Spanish people". Not everyone dances flamenco (it comes from Andalucía), it rains a lot in the green north, and it can snow in Madrid. Irish link: Galicia, in the north-west, has Celtic music with the gaita (bagpipes), and Halloween comes from the Irish festival of Samhain. Just as Spain has catalán, gallego and euskera, Ireland has two official languages: an Ghaeilge and English.
Exam tip: In Reading, culture texts (a festival poster, a blog about a trip to Mexico) test dates, months, places and what people do or eat: underline every number and month, and look for se celebra, la gente, es típico. In Listening, nationalities come fast: listen for the ending (peruano / peruana). In Writing, describe an Irish festival for a Spanish friend and compare: El Día de San Patricio es el 17 de marzo. Hay desfiles en todas las ciudades y la gente se viste de verde. Es más tranquilo que San Fermín, ¡pero es muy divertido!
