Percentages

Spot the pattern

Look at these. What do you notice?

  • 8% of 25 = 2  ·  25% of 8 = 2
  • 12% of 50 = 6  ·  50% of 12 = 6
  • 36% of 75 = 27  ·  75% of 36 = 27

Does it always work? Why? So what's the easy way to find 16% of 25?

Show me

x% of y is always the same as y% of x, because both are x × y ÷ 100. So 16% of 25 = 25% of 16 = a quarter of 16 = 4. When one way looks hard, swap it round.

Percent means "out of 100"

45% = 45/100 = 0.45. To change between the three:

  • Percentage → decimal: ÷ 100 (digits move 2 places right): 7% = 0.07, not 0.7. 120% = 1.2.
  • Decimal → percentage: × 100: 0.375 = 37.5%.
  • Fraction → percentage: make the bottom 100, or divide then × 100: 3/20 = 15/100 = 15%; 3/8 = 0.375 = 37.5%.
  • Percentage → fraction: put it over 100 and simplify: 35% = 35/100 = 7/20.
Learn these: 1/2 = 0.5 = 50% · 1/4 = 0.25 = 25% · 3/4 = 0.75 = 75% · 1/5 = 0.2 = 20% · 1/10 = 0.1 = 10% · 1/20 = 0.05 = 5% · 1/8 = 0.125 = 12.5% · 1/3 = 0.333… ≈ 33.3%. To compare, change them all to percentages first.

Finding a percentage of an amount

Build it from 10% (÷ 10), 5% (half of 10%) and 1% (÷ 100). For 35% of €80: 10% = €8, so 30% = €24; 5% = €4; 35% = €28. On a calculator: 0.35 × 80.

One amount as a percentage of another: write it as a fraction, then × 100. 18 out of 24 → 18/24 × 100 = 75%.

Increase and decrease

  • Decrease by 20%: you keep 80%, so × 0.8. €60 → 0.8 × 60 = €48.
  • Increase by 15%: you have 115%, so × 1.15. €40 → 1.15 × 40 = €46.
  • Percentage change = change ÷ original × 100. €50 → €60: 10 ÷ 50 × 100 = 20% increase.

Back to the start (finding the original)

After 20% off, a jacket costs €48. The €48 is 80% of the old price, so 1% = €0.60 and 100% = €60. (Or €48 ÷ 0.8 = €60.)

The classic mistake: adding 20% back on. 48 × 1.2 = €57.60, not €60, because the 20% was taken off the old price, not the new one. Always ask: "100% is which amount?"
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Percentages turn up all over the paper: money questions (sales, price rises, and later VAT, interest and tax), statistics (what percentage of a survey said yes), probability and ordering fractions, decimals and percentages. Questions often build up: (a) a simple percentage of an amount, (b) a percentage change, (c) "find the original price".

How marks are won and lost

  • Divide by the original for a percentage change, never by the new amount.
  • Don't add the % back on to find an old price: work out what 1% is, or divide by the multiplier.
  • Show the step: "10% = €8, 30% = €24" earns marks even if the last line slips.
  • Money has 2 decimal places (€57.60, not €57.6), and check the answer makes sense: a sale price must be less than the old price.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first.

  1. In order. Write these in order, starting with the smallest: 0.4, 3/8, 38%, 0.35.
    Show the worked answer

    Change them all to percentages: 0.4 = 40%, 3/8 = 37.5%, 38%, 0.35 = 35%. So the order is 0.35, 3/8, 38%, 0.4.

  2. Getting to school. A school has 640 students. 35% walk to school, 1/4 cycle and the rest come by bus. (a) How many walk? (b) How many come by bus? (c) What percentage come by bus?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 10% = 64, 30% = 192, 5% = 32, so 35% = 224.
    (b) 1/4 of 640 = 160 cycle. Bus: 640 − 224 − 160 = 256.
    (c) 256/640 × 100 = 40%. Check: 35% + 25% + 40% = 100% ✓.

  3. Two shops. A bike costs €480. Shop A has 30% off. Shop B has €150 off. (a) Which shop is cheaper, and by how much? (b) When Shop A's sale ends, the price goes back up from the sale price to €480. Find the percentage increase, correct to 1 decimal place. Why isn't it 30%?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) Shop A: 0.7 × 480 = €336. Shop B: 480 − 150 = €330. Shop B is cheaper by €6.
    (b) Increase = 480 − 336 = €144. 144 ÷ 336 × 100 = 42.857…% = 42.9%. It isn't 30% because the increase is worked out on the sale price (€336), which is a smaller amount than €480.

  4. Back to the start. After a 15% increase, a concert ticket costs €69. (a) What did it cost before the increase? (b) Seán says, "Just take 15% off €69." What answer does he get, and why is it wrong?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) €69 is 115% of the old price. 1% = 69 ÷ 115 = €0.60, so 100% = €60. (Or 69 ÷ 1.15 = 60.)
    (b) 0.85 × 69 = €58.65. It's wrong because the 15% was worked out on the old price (€60), not on €69.
