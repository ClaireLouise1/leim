Probability: chance & sample spaces

Spot the pattern

Work out each pair of probabilities, then add them.

  • Roll a fair die: P(a 6) = 1/6 · P(not a 6) = 5/6
  • A bag of 3 red and 7 blue counters: P(red) = 3/10 · P(not red) = 7/10
  • A spinner with 8 equal sectors, 5 of them green: P(green) = 5/8 · P(not green) = 3/8

What do you get every time? Why must that be true?

Show me

Every pair adds up to 1. Something either happens or it doesn't, and one of the two is certain. So P(not A) = 1 − P(A). If the chance of rain is 0.3, the chance of no rain is 1 − 0.3 = 0.7.

The probability scale

A probability is a number from 0 to 1. You can write it as a fraction, a decimal or a percentage: 1/4 = 0.25 = 25%. Nothing can have a probability below 0 or above 1.

Equally likely outcomes

When every outcome has the same chance (a fair die, a fair coin, a spinner with equal sectors, a counter picked at random):

P(event) = number of outcomes that fit ÷ total number of outcomes
  • Roll a fair die. P(a prime number): the primes are 2, 3, 5, so P = 3/6 = 1/2.
  • A bag has 2 red, 5 blue and 3 green counters. P(red or green) = (2 + 3) ÷ 10 = 5/10 = 1/2.
  • Letters of MALLOW on cards: P(L) = 2/6 = 1/3 (count the repeated L twice).

Always divide by the total. 2 red and 8 blue gives P(red) = 2/10, not 2/8.

Sample spaces: list every outcome

The sample space is the list of every possible outcome. Work in order so you don't miss any.

Listing. Toss two coins: {HH, HT, TH, TT}. HT and TH are different outcomes, so there are 4, not 3.
Two-way table. Roll two dice and add: 6 rows × 6 columns = 36 equally likely cells. A total of 7 is in 6 cells, so P(total 7) = 6/36 = 1/6.
Tree diagram. Toss a coin, then spin a spinner with 3 equal sectors (R, B, G). The coin gives 2 branches, each splits into 3: 6 outcomes: HR, HB, HG, TR, TB, TG. P(H and G) = 1/6.
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Probability comes up every year, often as the first question on the paper. Expect to place events on a probability scale, find simple probabilities from a bag, spinner, die or cards, list a sample space, and fill in or read a two-way table or a tree diagram.

How marks are won and lost

  • Write it as a probability. "3 out of 8" or "3 : 5" are not probabilities; write 3/8 (or 0.375 or 37.5%).
  • Divide by the total, not by "the rest". 3 red and 5 blue: P(red) = 3/8.
  • List in order so nothing is missed or written twice. HT and TH both count.
  • Fill in the whole table before you count, then count cells, not numbers.
  • Check it's between 0 and 1. An answer like 8/5 is always wrong.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first.

  1. A bag of sweets. A bag has 4 red, 6 blue and 2 white sweets. One is taken at random. (a) Find P(red). (b) Find P(not blue). (c) Is picking a white sweet unlikely, even chance or likely? (d) How many extra red sweets must be added to make P(red) = 1/2?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 12 sweets, 4 red: 4/12 = 1/3.
    (b) Not blue = 4 + 2 = 6: 6/12 = 1/2.
    (c) P(white) = 2/12 = 1/6, less than 1/2: unlikely.
    (d) Try adding red ones: +2 gives 6/14, +4 gives 8/16 = 1/2 ✓. So 4 extra red sweets.

  2. Two dice. A red die and a blue die are rolled and the scores are added. (a) Draw a two-way table of all the totals. (b) How many outcomes are there? (c) Find P(total = 8). (d) Find P(total of 10 or more). (e) Which total is most likely?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) Rows 1–6 (red), columns 1–6 (blue), each cell = row + column.
    (b) 6 × 6 = 36.
    (c) Totals of 8: 2+6, 3+5, 4+4, 5+3, 6+2: 5/36.
    (d) 10, 11 or 12: 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 cells: 6/36 = 1/6.
    (e) 7: it's in 6 cells, more than any other total.

  3. A tree diagram. Cian tosses a fair coin and then spins a fair spinner numbered 1, 2, 3. (a) Draw a tree diagram and list the sample space. (b) Find P(a head and a 3). (c) Find P(a tail and an odd number). (d) Find P(the spinner shows 2).
    Show the worked answer

    (a) H1, H2, H3, T1, T2, T3 (6 outcomes).
    (b) 1 of 6: 1/6.
    (c) T1 and T3: 2/6 = 1/3.
    (d) H2 and T2: 2/6 = 1/3 (the same as spinning the spinner on its own).

  4. True or false? (a) Niamh says "the probability that it rains tomorrow is 0.3 and the probability that it doesn't is 0.6". Explain why she must be wrong. (b) Darragh says "rolling a 6 is less likely than rolling a 1, because 6 is a big number". Is he right? (c) Put these on a 0–1 scale: A: rolling an even number, B: rolling a 7, C: picking a red counter from a bag of 9 red and 1 blue.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 0.3 + 0.6 = 0.9. It either rains or it doesn't, so the two must add to 1: P(no rain) = 0.7.
    (b) No. On a fair die every number has the same probability, 1/6.
    (c) B = 0 (impossible), A = 1/2 (even chance), C = 9/10 (very likely, close to 1).
