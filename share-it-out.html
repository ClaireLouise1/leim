Ratio & proportion

Spot the pattern

Aoife and Ben always share money in the ratio 2 : 3. Look at what Aoife gets each time. What do you notice?

  • Share €20 → Aoife €8, Ben €12
  • Share €45 → Aoife €18, Ben €27
  • Share €100 → Aoife €40, Ben €60

What fraction of the money does Aoife always get? So how much would she get from €350?

Show me

2 : 3 means 5 parts altogether, and Aoife has 2 of them, so she always gets 2/5 of the total (and Ben gets 3/5). 2/5 of €350 = €140. The bottom of the fraction is the total number of parts (5), not Ben's 3.

What a ratio means

A ratio compares amounts in order. "Girls to boys is 3 : 4" means 3 girls for every 4 boys. Boys to girls would be 4 : 3.

  • Simplify by dividing both numbers by the same number (the HCF): 12 : 18 = 2 : 3 (÷ 6).
  • Same units first: 50 cm : 2 m = 50 cm : 200 cm = 1 : 4.
  • Equivalent ratios come from multiplying (or dividing) both numbers by the same number: 3 : 5 = 6 : 10 = 12 : 20. Adding the same number to both does not work: 3 : 5 is not 4 : 6.

Sharing in a ratio

  • Share €60 in the ratio 1 : 2 : 3. 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 parts. One part = €60 ÷ 6 = €10. Shares: €10, €20, €30. Check: they add to €60 ✓.
  • You know one share. Red to blue = 3 : 5 and there are 21 red. 3 parts = 21, so 1 part = 7 and blue = 5 × 7 = 35.
  • You know the difference. Ratio 2 : 7 and Ben gets €35 more. 7 − 2 = 5 parts = €35, so 1 part = €7 and the total is 9 × €7 = €63.

Proportion: the unitary method

Find one first, then multiply. 4 tickets cost €18. One costs €18 ÷ 4 = €4.50, so 7 cost 7 × €4.50 = €31.50.

  • Recipes: 300 g of flour for 6 people → 50 g each → 9 people need 450 g. Don't just add 3 to the amount.
  • Map scales: 1 : 25 000 means 1 cm on the map is 25 000 cm in real life. 6 cm → 150 000 cm = 1 500 m = 1.5 km (÷ 100 for metres, then ÷ 1 000 for km).
  • Best buy: work out the price of one (or of 100 g) for each pack. 6 for €4.20 is 70c each; 10 for €6.50 is 65c each, so the 10-pack is better value.

Absolute vs relative comparison

  • Absolute = how many, or how much it changed: Class B had 15 passes, Class A had 12. "Prices went up by €5."
  • Relative = as a fraction or percentage of the whole: Class A had 12 out of 20 = 60%, Class B had 15 out of 30 = 50%. So Class A did better, even though B had more passes.
The classic mistake: saying "B is better because 15 is bigger than 12". When the groups are different sizes, compare fractions or percentages. For a relative change, divide by the start amount.
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Ratio and proportion usually come in a real-life setting: sharing prize money, scaling a recipe, mixing paint or concrete, reading a map scale, choosing the better value pack, or deciding which group "did better". They also sit inside other questions: scale drawings, similar triangles, statistics and probability.

How marks are won and lost

  • Keep the order: "boys to girls" and "girls to boys" are different ratios.
  • Show the "1 part" step (€840 ÷ 12 = €70): it earns marks even if a later line slips.
  • Check your shares add up to the total. It takes five seconds.
  • Change to the same units before simplifying or using a map scale, and give the units the question asks for (km, not cm).
  • "Which is better?" needs a reason: show the price of one, or the percentages, and say which is better.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first.

  1. Prize money. Niamh, Ola and Tadhg win €840 in a quiz. They share it in the ratio 2 : 3 : 7. (a) How much does each person get? (b) Tadhg then gives some of his money to Niamh so that the two of them have the same amount. How much does he give her?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 2 + 3 + 7 = 12 parts. One part = €840 ÷ 12 = €70. Niamh 2 × 70 = €140, Ola 3 × 70 = €210, Tadhg 7 × 70 = €490. Check: 140 + 210 + 490 = 840 ✓.
    (b) Together they have €140 + €490 = €630, so they each need €315. Tadhg gives 490 − 315 = €175.

  2. Pancakes. A recipe for 6 people uses 240 g of flour and 450 ml of milk. (a) How much flour and milk are needed for 9 people? (b) Seán has 400 g of flour and plenty of milk. What is the largest number of people he can make pancakes for? (c) Flour costs €1.60 for a 1 kg bag or €0.70 for a 400 g bag. Which bag is better value? Show your work.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) For 1 person: 240 ÷ 6 = 40 g flour and 450 ÷ 6 = 75 ml milk. For 9: 360 g of flour and 675 ml of milk.
    (b) 400 ÷ 40 = 10 people.
    (c) Price per 100 g: 1 kg bag €1.60 ÷ 10 = 16c; 400 g bag €0.70 ÷ 4 = 17.5c. The 1 kg bag is better value.

  3. The map. A walking map has a scale of 1 : 50 000. (a) Two car parks are 7 cm apart on the map. How far apart are they in real life, in km? (b) A lake is 2.5 km long. How long is it on the map, in cm?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 7 × 50 000 = 350 000 cm = 3 500 m = 3.5 km.
    (b) 2.5 km = 2 500 m = 250 000 cm. 250 000 ÷ 50 000 = 5 cm.

  4. Which school did better? In School A, 84 of the 120 students got Merit or higher. In School B, 126 of the 200 students did. (a) Which school had more students with Merit or higher? (b) Which school did better relatively? Show your work. (c) Explain the difference between your answers to (a) and (b).
    Show the worked answer

    (a) School B (126 is more than 84).
    (b) School A: 84/120 = 70%. School B: 126/200 = 63%. School A did better relatively.
    (c) (a) is an absolute comparison (how many students); (b) is a relative comparison (what fraction of each school). School B is bigger, so it can have more students with Merit while a smaller proportion of its students got it.
