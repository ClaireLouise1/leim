Volume, surface area & nets

Spot the pattern

Each layer is 4 × 2 = 8 cubes of 1 cm³.

1 layer: 8 cm³
2 layers: 16 cm³
3 layers: ?

How many cubes in 3 layers? What about 10 layers? Can you write a rule using the area of the base?

Show me

3 layers = 3 × 8 = 24 cm³; 10 layers = 80 cm³. Volume = area of the base × height. The same idea works for every prism and cylinder: area of the end × length.

Volume or surface area?

  • Volume = the space inside (how much it holds). Units: cm³, m³, or litres.
  • Surface area = the area of all the outside faces (card, paint, wrapping). Units: cm², m².
  • 1 litre = 1,000 cm³ and 1 m³ = 1,000 litres.
CuboidV = l × w × h
SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)
Cube: V = a³, SA = 6a²
PrismV = area of the end × length
SA = 2 ends + the rectangles
CylinderV = πr²h
Curved SA = 2πrh
Closed: + 2πr²
SphereV = ⁴⁄₃πr³
SA = 4πr²
HemisphereV = ⅔πr³
Curved SA = 2πr²
Solid: + πr² = 3πr²

The formulas for the cylinder and sphere are in the Formulae and Tables booklet you get in the exam. Know where they are, and know that r is the RADIUS.

Nets

A net is the flat shape that folds up into the solid. Its area = the surface area.

Cuboid: 6 rectangles
Triangular prism: 2 triangles + 3 rectangles
Cylinder: 2 circles + 1 rectangle
  • The cylinder's rectangle wraps round the circle, so its length is the circumference 2πr.
  • Faces, edges, vertices: cuboid 6, 12, 8; triangular prism 5, 9, 6.

Pack it, fill it, splash it

  • How many boxes fit? Count along each edge and round DOWN: 50 ÷ 12 → 4. Then multiply. Dividing the two volumes can give too many, because gaps can't hold a whole box.
  • Missing length: 240 cm³ ÷ (10 × 6) = 4 cm.
  • Water rising: the rise × base area = the volume of what sank in. 2,000 cm³ in a 40 × 25 tank: 2,000 ÷ 1,000 = 2 cm. In a cylinder, π cancels: a ball of radius 3 in a tin of radius 6 → ⁴⁄₃ × 27 ÷ 36 = 1 cm.
  • Combined solids: add the parts. Silo = cylinder + hemisphere.
  • π: follow the instruction (π button, 3.14, 22/7 or in terms of π). 36π stays as 36π.
  • Higher Level work backwards. Sphere volume 288π: r³ = 288 × 3 ÷ 4 = 216, r = ∛216 = 6. Melting/recasting: the volume stays the same, so set the two volumes equal.
The classic mistakes: using the diameter as r; adding instead of multiplying; using the sloping side for the triangle's height; forgetting a face (or counting the lid of an open box); cm² for a volume; 1 litre = 100 cm³ (it's 1,000); dividing volumes to count boxes; rounding early.
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Usually a real-life object: a tank, a tin, a box, a silo, a ball. The parts build up: a volume, then litres, then surface area (card, paint, glass), then a cost, a time to fill, or how far the water rises. Higher Level often asks you to work backwards to a radius or height, or to melt one solid into another.

How marks are won and lost

  • Write the formula (from the Formulae and Tables booklet), put the numbers in, then the answer with units.
  • Radius, not diameter. Halve it first.
  • Follow the π instruction and round only at the end.
  • Surface area: list the faces so none are missed, and check for a lid.
  • Litres: ÷ 1,000 from cm³.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first.

  1. The fish tank. A glass tank (no lid) is 60 cm long, 40 cm wide and 50 cm high.
    (a) Find its volume in cm³. (b) How many litres does it hold when full? (c) It is filled to 5 cm below the top. How many litres of water is that? (d) Find the area of glass in the tank, in m². (e) A tap pours 6 litres a minute. How long does it take to fill the tank to 5 cm below the top?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 60 × 40 × 50 = 120,000 cm³.
    (b) 120,000 ÷ 1,000 = 120 litres.
    (c) Depth 50 − 5 = 45 cm: 60 × 40 × 45 = 108,000 cm³ = 108 litres.
    (d) No lid, so 5 faces: base 60 × 40 = 2,400, front and back 2 × 60 × 50 = 6,000, ends 2 × 40 × 50 = 4,000. Total 12,400 cm² = 1.24 m² (÷ 10,000).
    (e) 108 ÷ 6 = 18 minutes.

  2. The grain silo. A silo is a cylinder with a hemisphere on top. The radius is 1.5 m and the cylinder is 4 m high.
    (a) Find the volume of the cylinder, in terms of π. (b) Find the volume of the hemisphere, in terms of π. (c) Find the total volume, to 2 decimal places. (d) The curved side and the dome are painted. Find this area, to 2 decimal places. (e) One tin of paint covers 12 m². How many tins are needed?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) πr²h = π × 1.5² × 4 = 9π m³.
    (b) ⅔πr³ = ⅔ × π × 1.5³ = 2.25π m³.
    (c) 9π + 2.25π = 11.25π = 35.34 m³.
    (d) Curved side 2πrh = 2 × π × 1.5 × 4 = 12π. Dome = half of 4πr² = 2π × 1.5² = 4.5π. Total 16.5π = 51.84 m².
    (e) 51.84 ÷ 12 = 4.32, so 5 tins (4 is not enough).

  3. Splash! A cylinder of radius 10 cm holds water 12 cm deep.
    (a) Find the volume of the water, in terms of π. (b) How many litres is that, to 1 decimal place? (c) A ball of radius 6 cm sinks to the bottom and is covered by the water. Find the volume of the ball, in terms of π. (d) By how much does the water rise? (e) Higher Level All the water is poured into a sphere-shaped bowl that it fills exactly. Find the radius of the bowl, to 2 decimal places.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) π × 10² × 12 = 1,200π cm³.
    (b) 1,200π = 3,769.9 cm³ → ÷ 1,000 = 3.8 litres.
    (c) ⁴⁄₃ × π × 6³ = ⁴⁄₃ × 216π = 288π cm³.
    (d) π × 10² × rise = 288π, so rise = 288 ÷ 100 = 2.88 cm.
    (e) ⁴⁄₃πr³ = 1,200π → r³ = 1,200 × 3 ÷ 4 = 900 → r = ∛900 = 9.65 cm.

  4. The chocolate box. A box is a triangular prism. Its end is a right-angled triangle with sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm. It is 15 cm long.
    (a) Find the area of one end. (b) Find the volume of the box. (c) Sketch a net of the box and write the size of each face on it. (d) Find the total surface area. (e) A cuboid box has the same volume and a square base 6 cm by 6 cm. How tall is it?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) ½ × 6 × 8 = 24 cm² (the 10 cm side is the sloping side).
    (b) 24 × 15 = 360 cm³.
    (c) 2 triangles (sides 6, 8, 10) and 3 rectangles: 6 × 15, 8 × 15 and 10 × 15.
    (d) Triangles 2 × 24 = 48. Rectangles (6 + 8 + 10) × 15 = 360. Total 408 cm².
    (e) 360 ÷ (6 × 6) = 360 ÷ 36 = 10 cm.
