Trigonometry

Spot the pattern

Three right-angled triangles share the same 35° angle. Each one is bigger than the last.

TriangleOpposite (cm)Hypotenuse (cm)
A2.84.9
B4.98.5
C7.012.2
  • Work out opposite ÷ hypotenuse for each triangle. What do you notice?
  • Now type sin 35° into your calculator. Compare.
Show me

Every answer is about 0.57, and sin 35° = 0.5736. The triangles are similar, so the ratio of the sides depends only on the angle, not on the size. That fixed ratio is sin 35°. Cos and tan are the other two ratios.

Name the sides (from the angle θ)

Hypotenuse: opposite the right angle, the longest side.
Opposite: across from θ (it doesn't touch θ).
Adjacent: next to θ (the side that isn't the hypotenuse).
Move θ to the other angle and the opposite and adjacent swap.

SOH CAH TOA

SOHsin θ = O ÷ H
CAHcos θ = A ÷ H
TOAtan θ = O ÷ A

Pick the ratio that uses the side you have and the side you want. They're in the Formulae and Tables booklet, but you still need to label the sides.

Finding a side or an angle

x on top: MULTIPLYHypotenuse 12, angle 35°, want the opposite:
sin 35° = x ÷ 12
x = 12 × sin 35° = 6.9
x on the bottom: DIVIDEOpposite 8, angle 40°, want the hypotenuse:
sin 40° = 8 ÷ x
x = 8 ÷ sin 40° = 12.4
An angle: use ⁻¹ (SHIFT)Opposite 5, adjacent 8:
tan θ = 5 ÷ 8
θ = tan⁻¹(5 ÷ 8) = 32.0…° = 32°
Calculator in DEG. Check: sin 30° must give 0.5. If you get −0.988, it's in RAD mode.

Angles of elevation and depression

Elevation: looking up, measured from the horizontal.
Depression: looking down, measured from the horizontal (not from the cliff).
  • The angle of depression from the top = the angle of elevation from the bottom (alternate angles).
  • Eye height: if the angle is measured from someone's eyes, add their eye height at the end.
  • A clinometer measures angles of elevation. With a tape measure for the distance, you can find the height of anything.
  • Higher Level two angles from the same point (a flagpole on a tower): find both heights, then subtract. Or use one ratio to find another: tan A = 3/4 → sides 3, 4, 5 → sin A = 3/5.

Classic mistakes

  • Calculator in RAD mode.
  • Labelling from the wrong angle: opposite and adjacent depend on which angle you use.
  • Multiplying when x is on the bottom (8 × sin 40° instead of 8 ÷ sin 40°).
  • Typing sin instead of sin⁻¹ when you want an angle.
  • Measuring the angle of depression from the vertical cliff.
  • Rounding too early: keep the full calculator value until the last step.
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Trigonometry is a regular on both levels, often with Pythagoras in the same question: a ramp, a ladder, a tree measured with a clinometer, a boat seen from a cliff. Expect to find a side, find an angle to the nearest degree, and to explain whether an answer is reasonable.

How marks are won and lost

  • Write the ratio with the numbers in it. "tan 32° = h ÷ 18" earns marks even if you slip on the calculator.
  • DEG mode, every time.
  • Round only at the end, to the accuracy asked.
  • Units: metres for sides, degrees for angles.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first. The diagrams are sketches: use the numbers.

  1. A ramp. A wheelchair ramp is 4.5 m long and rises 0.35 m. (a) Find the angle the ramp makes with the ground, to the nearest degree. (b) The rule is that a ramp must be no steeper than 5°. Does this ramp follow the rule? (c) Find the horizontal length of the ramp, to 2 decimal places.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) The ramp is the hypotenuse, the rise is the opposite: sin θ = 0.35 ÷ 4.5, so θ = sin⁻¹(0.0777…) = 4.46…° = 4°.
    (b) Yes: 4.46° is less than 5°.
    (c) Pythagoras: √(4.5² − 0.35²) = √20.1275 = 4.49 m (or 4.5 × cos 4.46…°).

  2. The clinometer. Ciara stands 18 m from a tree. Using a clinometer, she measures the angle of elevation of the top of the tree as 32°. Her eyes are 1.5 m above the ground. (a) Find the height of the tree, to 1 decimal place. (b) Ciara walks towards the tree until she is 10 m from it. What will the angle of elevation be now, to the nearest degree?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) tan 32° = h ÷ 18, so h = 18 × tan 32° = 11.247… m. Add her eye height: 11.247… + 1.5 = 12.7 m.
    (b) The part above her eyes is still 11.247… m: tan θ = 11.247… ÷ 10, so θ = tan⁻¹(1.1247…) = 48°.

  3. A cliff. From the top of a cliff 60 m high, the angle of depression of a boat is 14°. (a) How far is the boat from the foot of the cliff, to 1 decimal place? (b) Later the angle of depression is 22°. How far has the boat moved towards the cliff?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) The angle at the boat is also 14° (alternate angles). tan 14° = 60 ÷ x, so x = 60 ÷ tan 14° = 240.6 m.
    (b) 60 ÷ tan 22° = 148.5… m. It moved 240.64… − 148.50… = 92.1 m.

  4. Sides and angles. In the triangle ABC, |∠ABC| = 90°, |AB| = 7 cm and |BC| = 24 cm. (a) Find |AC|. (b) Write tan ∠BAC as a fraction. (c) Find |∠BAC| to the nearest degree. (d) Write sin ∠BCA as a fraction.
    Show the worked answer

    (a) |AC|² = 7² + 24² = 625, so |AC| = 25 cm.
    (b) From A, the opposite side is [BC] and the adjacent side is [AB]: tan ∠BAC = 24/7.
    (c) tan⁻¹(24 ÷ 7) = 73.7…° = 74°.
    (d) From C, the opposite side is [AB]: sin ∠BCA = 7/25.
