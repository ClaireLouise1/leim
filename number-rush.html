Numbers, age, dates and time

Spot the pattern

Look at how these numbers are built:

  • 16 dieciséis · 17 diecisiete · 18 dieciocho
  • 22 veintidós · 25 veinticinco · 29 veintinueve
  • 35 treinta y cinco · 47 cuarenta y siete · 99 noventa y nueve

What changes after 29? Think about it, then check.

Show me

Up to 29, the number is one word: diez y seis became dieciséis, veinte y dos became veintidós. From 31 on, you write the tens, then y, then the unit, as three words: treinta y uno, ochenta y ocho. Watch the accents on dieciséis, veintidós, veintitrés, veintiséis.

Watch out: sesenta (60, s like seis) and setenta (70, t like siete). Quince (15) and cincuenta (50). Catorce (14) and cuarenta (40). Say cien for exactly 100 (cien euros) and ciento for 101–199.

Age: use tener, not ser

¿Cuántos años tienes?How old are you?
Tengo catorce años.I'm 14. (literally "I have 14 years")
Mi hermana tiene diez años.My sister is 10.
Mi padre tiene cuarenta y un años.My dad is 41. (uno → un before años: veintiún, treinta y un)

English says "I am 14", so it is tempting to write Soy catorce años. Don't! Spanish always uses tener.

Dates and birthdays

Pattern: el + number + de + month. Months have no capital letter.

enero · febrero · marzo · abrilmayo · junio · julio · agostoseptiembre · octubre · noviembre · diciembre
  • ¿Cuándo es tu cumpleaños? When is your birthday?
  • Mi cumpleaños es el tres de mayo. My birthday is on 3 May. (No word for "on"!)
  • The 1st: el uno de enero (Spain) or el primero de enero (Latin America).
  • Nací en dos mil trece. I was born in 2013.
Watch out: junio (June) / julio (July), and marzo (March) / mayo (May).

Telling the time

TimeSpanish
1:00Es la una (one o'clock is singular)
3:00Son las tres (en punto)
3:05 · 3:15 · 3:30Son las tres y cinco · y cuarto · y media
3:40 · 3:45 · 3:55Son las cuatro menos veinte · menos cuarto · menos cinco

After half past, go to the next hour and take minutes away with menos: 3:45 = "four minus a quarter". To say at a time, use a las (or a la una): El partido empieza a las ocho y media. Add de la mañana / de la tarde / de la noche for a.m. and p.m.

Spain and Ireland: same numbers, different habits

  • Dates go day then month, like in Ireland: 03/05 = el tres de mayo.
  • Prices use a comma: 2,50 € = dos euros cincuenta. A thousand is written with a point: 1.000.
  • Timetables, trains and TV use the 24-hour clock: 20:30 = las veinte treinta.
  • Meals are later: lunch around 14:00 (las dos), dinner around 21:00 (las nueve de la noche).
Exam tip: numbers, times, dates and prices are the classic details in the Listening section. Read the question first so you know which number you are waiting for, write it down in digits the moment you hear it, and listen out for menos, sesenta / setenta and quince / cincuenta. On the second listening, check it.
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Numbers are in almost every Listening question: times, prices, dates, ages, phone numbers, room and platform numbers. They are quick, easy marks if you catch them, and easy to lose if you mix up sesenta and setenta. In Reading, posters, adverts and timetables are full of dates and times. In Writing (Q17 and Q18) you will often give your age, your birthday or the time and date of a plan.

How marks are won and lost

  • Write digits, not words. In Listening, jot 68 the moment you hear sesenta y ocho, then answer in English.
  • Pairs that trip people up: sesenta 60 / setenta 70, catorce 14 / cuarenta 40, quince 15 / cincuenta 50, doce 12 / dos 2.
  • Times with menos: go back from the NEXT hour. Las doce menos diez = 11:50, not 12:10.
  • Dates go day, then month: el veintitrés de junio = 23 June. Don't mix up junio (June) and julio (July).
  • In Writing: tengo … años (never soy), el + number + de + month, no capital letters on months or days, and a las for "at" a time.

  1. Listening 1: buying a train ticket in Madrid. You will hear a conversation at a train station ticket office. Answer in English.
    Cliente and Empleada
    1. What kind of ticket does the customer want, and where to?
    2. What time does the next train leave?
    3. What time does it arrive?
    4. How much does the ticket cost?
    5. Which platform does the train leave from?
    Show a model answer

    (a) A return ticket to Toledo. (b) At 11:15 (quarter past eleven). (c) At 11:50 (ten to twelve). (d) €14.50. (e) Platform 7.
    Where the marks would go: one mark for each detail; times and prices must be exact. The trap is (c): las doce menos diez is 11:50, not 12:10. In (d), catorce is 14, not 40.

  2. Listening 2: a phone message about a party. Ciara's Spanish friend Marta leaves her a phone message. Answer in English.
    Marta
    1. How old will Marta be?
    2. Give the day and the date of the party.
    3. What time does the party start?
    4. What is Marta's house number?
    5. Write down her phone number.
    Show a model answer

    (a) 15. (b) Saturday 23 June. (c) At 7 pm (seven in the evening). (d) 68 (Calle Mayor). (e) 641 32 70 18.
    Where the marks would go: one mark for each detail; (b) needs the day and the date, and the month must be June (junio), not July. Listen for sesenta (60) in the address and setenta (70) in the phone number.

  3. Reading: a summer camp poster. Read this poster for a summer camp in Spain and answer the questions in English.
    Campamento de verano «Sol y Deporte» – MálagaPara chicos y chicas de doce a dieciséis años.Fechas: del uno al quince de julio.Horario: de lunes a viernes, de las nueve y media de la mañana a las dos de la tarde.Actividades: fútbol, natación, tenis y vela.Precio: ciento ochenta euros por las dos semanas. ¡Para hermanos, veinte euros menos!Inscripción antes del treinta de mayo. Teléfono: 952 47 60 13.
    1. Who is the camp for?
    2. Give the dates of the camp.
    3. On which days, and between what times, does the camp run?
    4. Name two activities.
    5. How much does it cost, and what is the special offer?
    6. What is the closing date for booking?
    Show a model answer

    (a) Boys and girls aged 12 to 16. (b) From 1 to 15 July. (c) Monday to Friday, from 9:30 am to 2 pm. (d) Any two: football, swimming, tennis, sailing. (e) €180 for the two weeks; €20 off for brothers and sisters. (f) 30 May.
    Where the marks would go: one mark for each detail; (c) and (e) need both parts. Ciento ochenta is 180 (not 108), and the camp is in julio (July) but booking closes in mayo (May).

  4. Writing: an invitation to your birthday party (like Q17). Write an email in Spanish to your Spanish friend, Pablo, inviting him to your birthday party. Include all five points:
    1. Greet him and say how old you will be.
    2. Say the date of your birthday and the day of the party.
    3. Say what time the party starts and where it is.
    4. Say two things you will do at the party.
    5. Ask him when his birthday is, and sign off.
    Show a model answer

    ¡Hola, Pablo! ¿Qué tal? El mes que viene voy a cumplir quince años. Mi cumpleaños es el doce de marzo, y hago una fiesta el sábado catorce de marzo. La fiesta empieza a las ocho de la tarde en mi casa, en Athlone. Vamos a comer pizza y a bailar. ¿Puedes venir? ¿Cuándo es tu cumpleaños? ¡Hasta pronto! Un abrazo, Clodagh
    Where the marks would go: every one of the five points covered (a missed point can't be made up elsewhere); dates as el + number + de + month (no capital letter on marzo or sábado); a las for the time; voy a / vamos a + infinitive for plans; questions with ¿ ?; a friendly greeting and sign-off.
