Food, drink & eating out

Spot the pattern

  • Quiero un bocadillo. (I want a roll.)
  • Quisiera un bocadillo, por favor. (I'd like a roll, please.)
  • ¿Me pone un bocadillo, por favor? (Can I have a roll, please?)
  • Para mí, un bocadillo. (For me, a roll.)

All four get you the same roll. Which one would you say to a waiter you don't know? Which one sounds a bit blunt? Think about it, then check.

Show me

Quiero is fine with por favor, but on its own it can sound blunt, like "I want". Quisiera (I'd like) is the polite one, and the safest in the exam. ¿Me pone…? is what people really say at a bar or café in Spain. Para mí is handy when you're ordering in a group. Always add por favor and gracias.

Three courses: de primero (starter) · de segundo (main course) · de postre (dessert). De primero, la sopa; de segundo, el pollo; de postre, un flan. Other handy lines: la carta (the menu) · ¿Qué hay de postre? (What is there for dessert?) · Tengo hambre (I'm hungry) · Tengo sed (I'm thirsty) · Soy vegetariano / vegetariana · No como carne (I don't eat meat) · Soy alérgico / alérgica a…

Watch out: la cuenta = the bill, but la carta = the menu. And the waiter speaks to you as usted (polite "you"): ¿Qué va a tomar? ¿Qué quiere? not vas / quieres.

Prices: ¿Cuánto cuesta? ¿Cuánto cuestan?

¿Cuánto cuesta el bocadillo? — Cuesta tres euros con cincuenta (€3.50).  ·  ¿Cuánto cuestan las patatas fritas? — Cuestan dos euros con cincuenta. One thing = cuesta, more than one = cuestan (just like gusta / gustan).

un euro (€1) · un euro con veinte (€1.20) · cuatro euros con cincuenta (€4.50) · doce euros (€12). In Spain prices are written with a comma: 3,50 €.

Meals and when Spain eats them

MealVerb (I…)When in Spain
el desayuno (breakfast)desayuno7:30–9:00, often light: tostadas, café
la comida (lunch, the big meal)comoabout 2:00–3:30 pm
la merienda (afternoon snack)meriendoabout 5:30–6:30 pm
la cena (dinner)cenoabout 9:00–10:00 pm
Culture: lunch is the main meal in Spain, and many restaurants offer a menú del día on weekdays: a fixed price for a starter, a main course, a dessert (or coffee), bread and a drink. Tapas are small plates to share with friends, for example croquetas, tortilla or patatas bravas. Compare with Ireland: what time do you have dinner?
Exam tip: In Listening, orders and prices are classic details: listen for what (bocadillo de jamón or de queso?), how many and how much (cuatro euros con cincuenta or catorce?). In Reading, menus and café signs come up: learn primero, segundo, postre, bebidas, la cuenta. In Writing, say what you eat and when, with an opinion: Normalmente desayuno a las ocho. Me encanta la tortilla española porque es muy rica, pero no me gusta el pescado.
