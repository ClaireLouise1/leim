Spelling and commonly confused words

Most spelling mistakes come from a handful of rules and a short list of mix-ups. Learn those and most of your slips disappear.

Spot the pattern

Why does one get two p's and the other only one?

hop → hopping run → running sit → sitting
hope → hoping ride → riding write → writing
Show me

Short word, short vowel, one consonant at the end (hop, run): double the last letter before -ing, -ed, -er, -est.
Word ending in a silent e (hope, ride): drop the e and add the ending. Only one consonant, so the vowel says its name: hoping.
That's why hopping (jumping) and hoping (wishing) are two different words.

Adding endings

Double itshort vowel + 1 consonant
stop → stopped · big → biggest
two syllables, stress at the end: begin → beginning
Drop the ebefore a vowel ending
make → making · use → using
keep it before a consonant: careful, lovely
y → iconsonant + y
happy → happiness · carry → carried
but keep y before -ing (crying) and after a vowel (played)

Plurals

+ smost words, and vowel + y
days · keys · photos

Tricky words: memory tricks

  • i before e, except after c: believe, friend, receive (but weird is weird).
  • Hidden words: definitely, sepa rate, environment, be lieve.
  • Silent letters: knife, island, answer, Wednesday.
  • Doubles: necessary (one collar, two sleeves), embarrassed, address, tomorrow.
  • Say it the silly way: Wed-nes-day, Feb-ru-ary, li-brar-y.
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

There's no separate spelling test, but spelling counts in every piece you write: accuracy is part of how writing is judged, and slips like “could of” or “there bags” are noticed straight away.

How marks are won and lost

  • Mix-ups (there/their/they're, your/you're, to/too) are the slips examiners notice first.
  • Spell key words from the question correctly: they're printed on the paper, so copy them carefully.
  • Not sure of a word? Use a simpler word you can spell rather than guessing.
  • Leave 3 minutes to read your answer back, one word at a time.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). "Where the marks would go" is our guide, not an official marking scheme.

  1. Correct the six spelling mistakes: “Yesterday their was a wierd noise outside are house. I was quiet scared, but my freind said it was probly just the wind.”
    Show a model answer

    Yesterday there was a weird noise outside our house. I was quite scared, but my friend said it was probably just the wind.
    Where the marks would go: one mark for each word found and fixed (6). Changing a word that was already right doesn't lose a mark, but wastes time.

  2. Fill the gaps with there, their or they're: “___ coming to collect ___ bags from over ___.”
    Show the answer

    They're coming to collect their bags from over there. They are coming → they're. Belonging to them → their. A place → there.

  3. Correct the two mistakes: “We should of left earlier then we did.”
    Show the answer

    We should have left earlier than we did. “Should of” is always wrong. “Earlier than” compares two times.

  4. Write the right form of each word in brackets: “The two (baby) were (cry) while the (lady) went (shop).”
    Show the answer

    The two babies were crying while the ladies went shopping.
    Where the marks would go: babies and ladies (consonant + y → ies), crying (keep the y before -ing), shopping (short vowel: double the p).
