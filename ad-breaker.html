Need a refresher?

Media 1: advertising

An advert has one job: to make you want something and buy it (or do something, like book, join or vote). To read an ad well, ask three questions: Who is it for? What is it selling? How does it try to persuade me?

Spot the pattern

🅰️ Ad ARefillo water bottle. Holds 500 ml. Made of steel. €12.
🅱️ Ad BOlympic swimmer Orla Breen never trains without her Refillo. Everyone on the team has one. The best bottle ever made! Today only: €12.

Same bottle, same price. Which ad makes you want it more? Find four tricks Ad B uses that Ad A doesn't.

Show me

Ad B uses star power (an Olympic swimmer), the bandwagon (“Everyone on the team has one”), exaggeration (“The best bottle ever made!”) and hurry (“Today only”). Ad A only gives facts you can check. Ad B tells you almost nothing new about the bottle: it works on your feelings.

Six tricks ads use

⭐ Star powerFamous chef Gráinne Lynch cooks with Crispa oil.
🎁 Free giftFree delivery on all Plantly orders.
👥 BandwagonAll your friends are wearing Kicks trainers.
📊 Statistic9 out of 10 dogs prefer Waggo biscuits.
🚀 ExaggerationThe greatest pizza in the universe!
⏰ HurryWhile stocks last. Today only.

Ask about every statistic: who counted, and how many? “9 out of 10 dogs” might mean 10 dogs.

Who's it for? Fact or opinion?

🎯 The audienceLook at the words: “bedtime” and “ask your mum” = young children; “exams” and “your mates” = teenagers; “the school run” = parents; “your grandchildren” = older people.
✅ FactCan be checked: €2.50 a cone, made in Donegal, opens at 6 am.
💭 OpinionA judgement nobody can prove: delicious, the best, really cool.

Slogans and pictures

🏷️ A good sloganis short, names the brand and is catchy: alliteration (“Fizzpop: feel the fizz!”) or rhyme (“no mess, no stress”).
🖼️ The picturesends a message before you read a word: fruit and runners = healthy; diamonds = luxury; a family at a table = home; balloons = fun.
🎨 LayoutThe eye goes to the biggest thing first: usually the picture or the headline. Colours set the mood (green = natural, gold = luxury).
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

The paper often includes a visual or media text: an advert, a poster, a web page or a leaflet. Questions ask who it is aimed at, how it tries to persuade, what the image adds, and whether it works. Writing tasks can ask you to create an ad, a slogan or a short promotional piece.

How marks are won and lost

  • Name it, quote it, explain it: “The ad uses star power: ‘Olympic champion…’. Readers trust a winner.”
  • Talk about the picture and the layout, not only the words.
  • Back up “who it's for” with words from the ad.
  • Say whether it works and why: a clear opinion, backed up, wins marks.
  • Don't just describe the ad. Explain the effect on the reader.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Every brand and place is made up. "Where the marks would go" is our guide, not an official marking scheme.

[Picture: three smiling teenagers on surfboards, riding one big wave, bright blue sky]
SEASPRAY SURF SCHOOL, KILBRACK STRAND
Catch the wave of your life!
Two-hour lessons for teens, Saturdays at 10 am. €25, wetsuit included.
Bring your mates: everyone's talking about Seaspray this summer.
Hurry, only 12 places a week!
  1. Who is this advert aimed at? Give two pieces of evidence.
    Show a model answer

    It is aimed at teenagers. It says the lessons are “for teens”, it tells them to “Bring your mates”, and the picture shows three teenagers surfing together.
    Where the marks would go: the audience named; two pieces of evidence, at least one quoted (the picture counts as evidence).

  2. Name two persuasive techniques in the advert and explain how one of them works.
    Show a model answer

    The bandwagon (“everyone's talking about Seaspray this summer”) and hurry (“Hurry, only 12 places a week!”). Hurry works because it makes the reader feel they could miss out, so they book now instead of thinking it over.
    Where the marks would go: each technique named with a quote; the rest for explaining the effect on the reader.

  3. Find one fact and one opinion in the advert.
    Show a model answer

    Fact: “€25, wetsuit included” (it can be checked). Opinion: “Catch the wave of your life!” (a claim nobody can prove).
    Where the marks would go: one mark each, with the right label.

  4. Write a slogan and a three-line advert for a new reusable water bottle, Refillo, aimed at teenagers. Use two tricks.
    Show a model answer

    Refillo: refill, reuse, rule the school.
    Everyone in your year is swapping plastic for Refillo. Keeps water cold for 12 hours. Get a free sticker pack this week only!
    Where the marks would go: a short, catchy slogan with the brand (here alliteration); the audience clear (“your year”, “school”); two tricks used (bandwagon, statistic, free gift, hurry); at least one real fact.
