The alphabet, sounds and spelling

Spot the pattern

Say these out loud: gato · guapo · gorra and then gente · girasol · gimnasio. What happens to the g? Now try casa · cosa · cuna and cine · cena · cielo.

Show me

The letter after g or c decides the sound. Before a, o, u: hard, like English (gato, casa). Before e, i: soft. ge, gi sound like the Spanish j, the "ch" in loch (gente = jamón). ce, ci sound like z, the "th" in think in most of Spain (cine = zumo). To keep the hard sound before e and i, Spanish adds a silent u: gue, gui (guitarra) and que, qui (queso).

Watch out: one r or two can change the word: pero (but) / perro (dog), caro (expensive) / carro (cart). Vowels are short and always the same: a "ah", e "eh", i "ee", o "oh", u "oo".

  • ¿Cómo se escribe? How do you spell it? ¿Cómo se escribe tu apellido? How do you spell your surname?
  • Se escribe jota, o, ese, e con tilde: José. con tilde = with an accent. Two l's: dos eles or doble ele.
  • Easy to mix up: ge (G) and jota (J) · e (E) and i (I) · be (B) and uve (V) · hache (H).

The Spanish alphabet has 27 letters: the extra one is ñ. Since 2010, the Real Academia Española no longer counts ch and ll as separate letters: they're pairs of letters.

Accents: when do you write one?

The word ends in…Stress falls on…No accent needed
a vowel, n or sthe second-last syllableca-sa, ha-blan, li-bros, jo-ven, e-xa-men
any other consonantthe last syllableha-blar, ciu-dad, es-pa-ñol

If a word breaks the rule, it needs a written accent to show the stress: canción, café, inglés, jamón, árbol, lápiz, fútbol. Words stressed on the third-last syllable always have one: música, teléfono, miércoles, química. When a word grows, the accent can come or go: joven → jóvenes, examen → exámenes, but canción → canciones, inglés → ingleses.

Spain and Ireland: different writing habits

  • Questions and exclamations open upside down: ¿Cómo estás? ¡Qué bien!
  • No capitals for days, months, languages and nationalities: el lunes, en mayo, hablo inglés, soy irlandesa. Countries and towns do have capitals: España, Sevilla, Irlanda.
  • On a computer or phone, hold down n for ñ and a vowel for á é í ó ú.
Exam tip: in the Writing section, a missing accent can change the word (papá is "dad", papa is "potato"; está is "is", esta is "this"), so read your answer back once just for accents, ñ and ¿ ¡. In Listening, names, streets and emails are sometimes spelled out: write the letters down as you hear them, and remember jota = J, ge = G, i = I, e = E.
