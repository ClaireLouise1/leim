Speaking and the oral CBA

The June exam has no speaking part. Speaking is assessed in class, in the Oral Communication CBA. Here's how it works, what your teacher listens for, and how to prepare, with Spanish you can listen to.

How the oral CBA works

QuestionAnswer
When?2017 specification (exams in 2027 and 2028): CBA 1, finished by the end of 2nd year in a set window. Students sitting the exam in 2027 and 2028 do one CBA in Spanish, and it must be this oral one. 1st years from September 2026 (2025 specification) do it as CBA 2, the Oral Communication Task, in 3rd year.

How long?About 3–4 minutes, including the teacher's questions.
Who with?Alone, in a pair or in a small group, as your teacher agrees.

What your teacher listens for

Teachers use the official Features of Quality, which look at three things. In plain English:

  1. Speaking: how clear you are, how well it flows, how accurate it is and how many different words and structures you use.
  2. Answering questions: whether you understand the teacher's questions and give answers that make sense.
  3. How Spanish works: verb endings, agreement, word order, and fixing your own slips.

To move up a level: speak from key words, not a script · use the present, past and future · give opinions with reasons (porque…) · answer in full sentences and add one detail · fix slips out loud (fui… perdón, fuimos) · ask for help in Spanish if you don't understand.

The four formats

  • Presentation on a topic, with pictures, slides or props, then the teacher's questions. Ideas: your sport or hobby, a festival in a Spanish-speaking country compared with Ireland, a city you'd love to visit.
  • Interview: the teacher asks about you, your family, school, free time, holidays and plans. In pairs, you also ask each other.
  • Role-play: a real-life situation such as a hotel, a host family, a shop or a café, then questions.
  • Conversation about a stimulus: a picture, short text, video clip or object starts the conversation.

Pick something you care about: it's easier to talk about, and easier to answer questions on.

A 3-week plan

  1. Before: Speak Spanish in class every week. Learn the question bank a topic at a time.
  2. Day 1: Choose your format and topic with your teacher. It must be one you haven't already practised or prepared, so use models like the one below to learn HOW, then make your own.
  3. Week 1: Research: two or three sources (a website, a video, a book). Write down key words. Make a mind map and a plan.
  4. Week 2: Build your talk or role-play in short, simple sentences. Practise aloud every day; record yourself on your phone and listen back. Prepare answers to 8–10 likely questions.
  5. Week 3: Practise with a friend or parent who asks you questions in a different order. Time it: about 3–4 minutes, including the questions. Use the survival phrases.
  6. After: Write your Student Reflection Note in your own words.

When you're stuck

Asking for help in Spanish is good interaction, not a failure.

SayIt means
¿Puede repetir, por favor?Can you repeat that, please?
Más despacio, por favor.More slowly, please.
No entiendo la pregunta.I don't understand the question.
¿Qué significa «…»?What does … mean?
¿Cómo se dice «…» en español?How do you say … in Spanish?
Perdón, quiero decir…Sorry, I mean… (fixing your own mistake)

Six phrases, said slowly

Linking words: porque · pero · también · además · por ejemplo · sin embargo · y luego
Opinions: En mi opinión… · Creo que… · Me encanta… · Lo mejor es… · Lo malo es… · Prefiero… porque…

Sounds to get right

  • Vowels a e i o u are always short and clear: me-sa, pe-lo. Never swallow them.
  • h is silent: hola = "ola", hago = "ago".
  • j, ge, gi a breathy sound from the throat: jugar, gente, Jorge.
  • ll and y sound like the y in "yes": me llamo, playa.
  • ñ like "ny" in "canyon": España, año.
  • r and rr one quick tap in pero (but); a roll in perro (dog) and at the start of a word: Roma.
  • z, ce, ci "th" in most of Spain, "s" in Latin America: cinco, zapato. Both are right; pick one.

Practise the sounds with Sound Match, and questions with Question Quick-Fire and Meet & Greet. For a culture presentation, try Fiesta Quiz.

Self-check before the day

  • I can speak for about 3–4 minutes (including the teacher's questions) without reading.
  • My talk has a clear start, middle and end.
  • I use at least three time frames: present, past and future (or me gustaría).
  • I give opinions with reasons (porque…).
  • I join sentences with y, pero, también, además.
  • I can answer my 10 likely questions with two sentences each.
  • I can ask for help in Spanish (¿Puede repetir, por favor?).
  • I've checked my pronunciation of the tricky words.
  • I've said it out loud to someone at home at least three times.
  • I've included something about a Spanish-speaking country or culture (if it fits).

For parents: how to help (no Spanish needed)

  • Be the audience. Let your child give the talk or do the interview with you, then ask two questions (read them from their notes as best you can).
  • Time it: about 3–4 minutes including questions.
  • Help them plan the weeks: research, a plan, then a practice run each day in week 3.
  • Don't write it for them, and don't let AI write it. It must be their own work, and teachers can tell.
  • Keep it calm. It's one line on the JCPA and it doesn't change the exam grade.
