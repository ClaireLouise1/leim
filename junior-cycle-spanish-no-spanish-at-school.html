Junior Cycle Spanish · For parents and students

No Spanish at your school?

Lots of schools in Ireland only offer French or German. Your child can still learn Spanish and sit the Junior Cycle Spanish exam. Here is how it works, in plain English.

The short answerStep by stepThe oral CBAThe gradeHome-schooledHow to studyQuestions

The short answer

  • Yes, they can sit the exam. The Department of Education's Junior Cycle rules allow for subjects studied outside school: a student can take up to 10 exam subjects, and that limit includes subjects studied outside a recognised school setting.
  • They learn Spanish outside school (a tutor, online classes, a parent, or self-study with a plan like Léim's) and their own school enters them for the June exam. They sit it in their school with everyone else.
  • The one thing they can't do is the oral CBA (the classroom speaking assessment), because it has to be done and assessed in school. It shows as "not reported" on the school's Junior Cycle report.
  • For exams in 2027 and 2028 that doesn't change the grade. The June paper decides 100% of the Junior Cycle Spanish grade in those years.

Do this first: talk to the school's principal or exams secretary early, ideally in 1st or 2nd year and no later than the autumn of 3rd year, and ask them to enter your child for Junior Cycle Spanish.

Step by step for parents

  1. Check the subject count. Junior Cycle students take between 8 and 10 exam subjects, and Spanish studied outside school counts towards the 10. Ask the school how many your child already has.
  2. Talk to the school. Ask the principal or exams secretary: "Can you enter my child for Junior Cycle Spanish? They're studying it outside school." Schools make the Junior Cycle exam entries for their own students. Ask what date they need to know by.
  3. Choose how they'll learn. Spanish takes about three years at Junior Cycle level, so start in 1st year if you can. Options: a qualified tutor or language school, online classes, a family member who speaks Spanish, or structured self-study (see How to study).
  4. Follow the course. Students sitting the exam in 2027 and 2028 follow the 2017 Junior Cycle Modern Foreign Languages specification. Students who started 1st year in September 2026 follow the revised 2025 specification (exam in 2029). Our free games cover every 2017 topic.
  5. Practise the exam. From 2nd year, do past papers (free on examinations.ie) and use the Exam Centre.
  6. Sit the June exam in school with the other students. Results come out in the autumn, like every other subject.

Fees: the Junior Cycle exam fee is one fee for all subjects, not per subject (medical-card holders are exempt), so adding Spanish shouldn't cost extra. Check the current fee with your school or examinations.ie.

The oral CBA: what it is and what happens if you're outside school

A CBA (Classroom-Based Assessment) is a task done in class and judged by the teacher. In Spanish, the speaking CBA is the Oral Communication CBA.

WhatThe details
When2017 specification (exams 2027 and 2028): CBA 1, towards the end of 2nd year. 2025 specification (1st years from September 2026): CBA 2, the Oral Communication Task, in 3rd year.
What students doOne of: an interview, a role-play, a presentation followed by questions, or a conversation about a picture, video or text. Usually about 3 to 4 minutes, with the teacher's questions afterwards. It can be done alone, in a pair or in a small group.
How many CBAsStudents sitting the exam in 2026, 2027 and 2028 do only one CBA per subject instead of two. In Spanish and the other foreign languages it's the oral one.
MarksNone. The teacher gives one of four descriptors: Exceptional, Above expectations, In line with expectations or Yet to meet expectations.
Where it shows upOn the school's Junior Cycle Profile of Achievement (JCPA), separately from the exam grade. It does not count towards the State exam grade.
If Spanish is studied outside schoolThe CBA can't be done (it has to be done in school, with the school's teacher), so the JCPA shows it as "not reported". The exam grade isn't affected.
Still practise speaking. Even without the CBA, speaking is how Spanish sticks, and it makes listening (39% of the exam) much easier. See the ideas under How to study.

Example: a 3-minute presentation (2nd-year level)

Hola, me llamo Ciara y hoy voy a hablar de mi ciudad, Cork. Cork está en el sur de Irlanda y es la segunda ciudad más grande del país. Hay muchas cosas que hacer: hay tiendas, cafeterías, un mercado muy famoso y un río, el río Lee. Mi lugar favorito es el Mercado Inglés, porque hay comida de todo el mundo y siempre hay mucho ambiente. Lo malo es que llueve mucho, pero me gusta vivir aquí porque la gente es muy simpática. El verano pasado fui a la playa de Garrettstown con mis amigos y lo pasamos genial. En el futuro me gustaría vivir en España, pero siempre voy a volver a Cork. ¡Muchas gracias!

Example: a role-play (in a café)

— Hola, buenas tardes. ¿Qué vas a tomar?
— Hola. Un bocadillo de queso y un zumo de naranja, por favor.
— ¿Algo más?
— Sí, ¿tienen tarta de chocolate?
— Sí, claro.
— Entonces una tarta también. ¿Cuánto es?
— Son ocho euros con cincuenta.
— Aquí tiene. ¡Gracias!

These examples are original Léim material for practice. The school's Spanish teacher sets and judges the real CBA.

The grade: what counts

  • Exams in 2027 and 2028: the written Assessment Task (normally 10% of the grade) is not examined, so the June paper is 100% of the grade: Listening 140, Reading 140, Writing 80, out of 360. A student who learned Spanish outside school is marked on exactly the same paper, in exactly the same way. See the paper explained.
  • Grades: Distinction (90%+), Higher Merit, Merit, Achieved, Partially Achieved, Not Graded. See where the marks are.
  • From 2029: the rules for the new specification and the Assessment Task are under review. Check with your school.
Why it's worth it: at the four NUI universities, many courses need a third language (as well as English and Irish) in the Leaving Cert, and Spanish counts. Junior Cycle Spanish is the best preparation for Leaving Cert Spanish. Our free parent guide explains the college entry rules.

Home-schooled students

Students who are home-schooled can sit the Junior Cycle final exams. They don't get a JCPA (it's a school report), but they can get a statement of their exam results. They can't do CBAs. Contact the State Examinations Commission (examinations.ie) about how to enter.

How to study Spanish without a class

  1. A little every day. 15 to 20 minutes a day beats one long session a week. Play one Léim game a day, in teaching order.
  2. Follow the topics in order. Each Léim topic has a game, the rules and a free revision sheet, tagged with the curriculum learning outcomes.
  3. Listen every day. Spanish songs, cartoons, YouTube with Spanish subtitles, and the Listening modes in our games. Listening is 39% of the exam.
  4. Speak every week. A tutor, an online conversation class, a Spanish-speaking relative or neighbour, or a language exchange. Or record yourself giving a 1-minute talk and listen back.
  5. Write every week. A short message, email or post, then check it against our model answers.
  6. From 2nd year, practise exam papers with the Exam Centre, past papers on examinations.ie and the Léim Exam Pack (a full mock paper with answers).

Questions parents ask

Will my child be at a disadvantage without the CBA?

Not in the exam grade for 2027 and 2028, which comes 100% from the June paper. The school's JCPA will show the CBA as "not reported".

Who do we register with?

The school. Schools enter their own students for the Junior Cycle exams. Home-schooled students should contact the State Examinations Commission.

Can the school say no?

Talk to them early. The Department's rules allow for subjects studied outside school, within the 10-subject limit. If there's a problem, ask the school to explain it in writing and check with examinations.ie.

Does my child need a qualified teacher?

No, but it helps a lot, especially for speaking and writing feedback. Many families combine a weekly tutor with daily self-study.
