Integers and the order of operations

Spot the pattern

Look at these. Each line goes down by one step.

3 × 2 = 63 × (−2) = −6
2 × 2 = 42 × (−2) = −4
1 × 2 = 21 × (−2) = −2
0 × 2 = 00 × (−2) = 0
−1 × 2 = ?−1 × (−2) = ?

The answers on the left go down by 2 each time. On the right they go up by 2. What comes next in each column?

Show me

Left: −1 × 2 = −2. Right: −1 × (−2) = 2. Keep the pattern going and a negative times a negative has to come out positive. That's where the signs rule comes from: it isn't magic, it's the pattern.

Integers and the number line

Integers are the whole numbers, positive and negative, and zero: … −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3 … (the set ℤ). Natural numbers are the counting numbers 1, 2, 3 … (the set ℕ). On a number line, numbers get bigger to the right. So −2 is bigger than −8, even though 8 looks bigger: −2 > −8.

Watch out: the coldest temperature is the smallest number. −8 °C is colder than −2 °C, so −8 < −2.

Multiplying and dividing

SignsAnswerExamples
Same signs: + and +, or − and −Positive(−6) × (−3) = 18 · (−20) ÷ (−4) = 5
Different signs: one + and one −Negative(−6) × 3 = −18 · 20 ÷ (−4) = −5

More than two numbers? Count the minus signs: an even number of them gives a positive answer, an odd number gives a negative one. (−2) × (−3) × (−1) = −6 (three minus signs).

Watch out: these rules are for × and ÷ only. (−3) + (−5) is −8, not +8. And squaring a negative gives a positive: (−4)² = (−4) × (−4) = 16.

The order of operations: BIMDAS

BBrackets first(2 + 3) × 4 = 5 × 4 = 20
IIndices (powers and roots)2 × 3² = 2 × 9 = 18, not 6² = 36
M D× and ÷, working left to right12 ÷ 3 × 2 = 4 × 2 = 8
A S+ and −, working left to right10 − 4 + 3 = 6 + 3 = 9

So 2 + 3 × 4 = 2 + 12 = 14. If you just go left to right you get 20, which is wrong. × and ÷ are equal partners, and so are + and −: whichever comes first, reading left to right, goes first.

Three laws you can use to make sums easier

  • Commutative: the order doesn't matter for + and ×. 7 + (−2) = (−2) + 7 · 4 × 25 = 25 × 4. (Not for − or ÷: 5 − 2 ≠ 2 − 5.)
  • Associative: how you group doesn't matter for + and ×. (2 × 5) × 9 = 2 × (5 × 9) = 90.
  • Distributive: multiply everything inside the brackets. 3 × (40 + 2) = 3 × 40 + 3 × 2 = 126. Handy for mental maths: 7 × 99 = 7 × 100 − 7 × 1 = 693.
Exam corner

Where this shows up in the exam

Integers and BIMDAS rarely get a whole question to themselves. They are inside almost every question on the paper: temperatures and bank balances, substituting into a formula, solving equations, coordinates and slopes. One sign slip early on can carry right through a question.

How marks are won and lost

  • Show every step. Examiners give part marks for correct work, so a right method with one slip can still earn marks. A wrong answer on its own earns nothing.
  • Put negatives in brackets when you substitute: if x = −3, then x² = (−3)² = 9, not −9.
  • On the calculator, use the (−) key for a negative number and type the brackets in. Then check the answer is sensible: a temperature drop should give a lower number.
  • Write the units (°C, €, m) and answer the question asked: "how much colder?" wants a difference, not the new temperature.

Try these exam-style questions

Written by Léim in the style of the exam (not taken from past papers). Try each one on paper first.

  1. The coldest night. One night the temperature in Mullingar was −6 °C and in Kinsale it was 3 °C.
    (a) How many degrees warmer was it in Kinsale?
    (b) By morning the Mullingar temperature had risen by 4 degrees. What was it in the morning?
    Show the worked answer

    (a) 3 − (−6) = 3 + 6 = 9 degrees warmer. (Count on the number line: from −6 up to 0 is 6, then 0 up to 3 is 3 more.)
    (b) −6 + 4 = −2 °C. Check: rising means a bigger number, and −2 is bigger than −6. ✓

  2. Finish the calculation. Aoife says 20 − 8 ÷ 4 × 2 = 6. Show that she is wrong and find the right answer.
    Show the worked answer

    Aoife went left to right with the subtraction first: 20 − 8 = 12, 12 ÷ 4 = 3, 3 × 2 = 6. But ÷ and × come before −.
    8 ÷ 4 = 2, then 2 × 2 = 4, then 20 − 4 = 16.

  3. Substitute. Find the value of 2a² − 3b when a = −3 and b = −5.
    Show the worked answer

    2(−3)² − 3(−5) = 2(9) − (−15) = 18 + 15 = 33.
    Two places to slip: (−3)² is +9, and taking away −15 is the same as adding 15.

  4. Brackets. Put one pair of brackets into 4 + 6 × 5 − 2 so that the answer is 48.
    Show the worked answer

    Try the brackets in different places: 4 + 6 × (5 − 2) = 4 + 18 = 22 ✗ · 4 + (6 × 5 − 2) = 4 + 28 = 32 ✗ · (4 + 6) × 5 − 2 = 50 − 2 = 48 ✓
    Answer: (4 + 6) × 5 − 2 = 48.
